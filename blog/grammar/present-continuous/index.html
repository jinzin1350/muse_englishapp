Present Continuous

A1 · A2 · B1 · C1

We use the present continuous for actions happening now or around now: I'm reading a book. She's studying for her exams this week.

Form it with am / is / are + verb-ing. At higher levels it is also used for future plans, changing situations and annoying habits.

A1 · Beginner

Form and use: I'm working (now) A1

FormExamples
Positivesubject + am / is / are + verb-ingI'm working. He's sleeping. They're playing.
Negativesubject + am not / isn't / aren't + verb-ingI'm not working. She isn't sleeping. We aren't playing.
QuestionAm / Is / Are + subject + verb-ing?Are you working? Is he sleeping?
Short answersYes, + subject + am / is / are. / No, + subject + 'm not / isn't / aren't.Yes, I am. / No, she isn't.
Wh- questionquestion word + am / is / are + subject + verb-ing?What are you doing?
Spelling: verb + -ingExamples
Most verbs: add -ingwork → working, read → reading, play → playing
Verb ends in -e: drop the emake → making, write → writing, come → coming
One vowel + one consonant at the end of a stressed syllable: double the consonantrun → running, sit → sitting, swim → swimming, stop → stopping
Verb ends in -ie: change ie to -yinglie → lying, die → dying, tie → tying

  • I'm cooking dinner right now. (now)
  • She isn't working today. (negative)
  • Are you listening to me? (question)
  • Look! It's snowing! (Look! + continuous)
  • He's sitting next to the window. (sit → sitting)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I working now. → ✅ I'm working now. (Don't forget am / is / are.)
  • ❌ She is work at the moment. → ✅ She is working at the moment. (Use verb-ing.)
  • ❌ He is runing. → ✅ He is running. (Run → running (double the n).)
  • ❌ What you are doing? → ✅ What are you doing? (Question order: are + subject + verb-ing.)

Practice: A1

A2 · Elementary

Around now and temporary situations A2

  • I'm reading a great book at the moment. (around now)
  • My cousin is staying with us this month. (temporary)
  • We're learning about volcanoes in class this week. (this week)
  • Ben is learning Japanese this year. (this year)
  • How is your new job going? (a current situation)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I read a really good book at the moment. → ✅ I'm reading a really good book at the moment. (Around now: present continuous.)
  • ❌ My sister stays with us this month. → ✅ My sister is staying with us this month. (A temporary situation: present continuous.)

Future arrangements: I'm meeting Sam tonight A2

  • I'm meeting Sam at 6 tonight. (arrangement)
  • We're flying to Rome on Monday. (tickets booked)
  • What are you doing this weekend? (asking about plans)
  • She's starting her new job next week. (fixed plan)
  • They aren't coming to the party. (negative)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I see the dentist tomorrow. (my appointment) → ✅ I'm seeing the dentist tomorrow. (Personal arrangements: present continuous.)
  • ❌ What do you do this weekend? → ✅ What are you doing this weekend? (Asking about plans: present continuous.)

Practice: A2

5. I <span class="gap">___</span> a really interesting book at the moment.
Around now: I'm reading.
6. My cousin <span class="gap">___</span> with us this month. She's looking for a flat.
A temporary situation: is staying.
7. We <span class="gap">___</span> a project about space in class this week.
This week (around now): are doing.
8. I <span class="gap">___</span> my grandparents on Sunday. We've booked a table at their favourite restaurant.
An arrangement: present continuous, I'm visiting.
9. What <span class="gap">___</span> this weekend? – Nothing special.
Asking about plans: What are you doing…?
10. Which sentence is about an arrangement?
A plan with a person and a time: I'm having lunch with Maria tomorrow.

B1 · Intermediate

Changing situations: It's getting colder B1

  • The weather is getting colder. (get + comparative)
  • Prices are rising again. (rise)
  • Your English is improving fast. (improve)
  • The city is growing every year. (grow)
  • I'm starting to enjoy my new job. (start to)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The population increases very fast at the moment. → ✅ The population is increasing very fast at the moment. (A change happening now: present continuous.)
  • ❌ It gets dark. Let's go home. → ✅ It's getting dark. Let's go home. (A change happening now: present continuous.)

always + continuous: He's always complaining B1

  • He's always complaining about something. (annoyed)
  • You're always losing your keys! (too often)
  • My neighbour is constantly playing loud music. (constantly)
  • She's forever borrowing my phone. (forever)
  • I always walk to work. (neutral habit: present simple)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ She always is interrupting me. → ✅ She's always interrupting me. (Word order: be + always + verb-ing.)
  • ❌ I'm always going to work by bike. (my normal habit) → ✅ I always go to work by bike. (For a neutral habit, use the present simple.)

Practice: B1

11. Look outside! It <span class="gap">___</span> dark.
A change happening now: it's getting dark.
12. Prices <span class="gap">___</span> again. Everything is more expensive this year.
A change in progress: are rising.
13. I didn't like my job at first, but I <span class="gap">___</span> to enjoy it now.
A change that has started: I'm starting to enjoy it.
14. My brother <span class="gap">___</span> my clothes without asking! It's so annoying.
be + always + verb-ing: he's always borrowing.
15. Which sentence shows that the speaker is annoyed?
Always + continuous = too often, annoying.
16. I <span class="gap">___</span> to work. It's only ten minutes from my house. (my normal habit)
A neutral habit: present simple, I always walk.

C1 · Advanced

In progress at a regular time; always for typical behaviour C1

  • At 7 a.m. I'm usually having breakfast. (in progress at that time)
  • Don't call between 6 and 7: we're usually eating then. (regular time)
  • On Saturday mornings I'm normally working in the garden. (routine in progress)
  • She's always helping other people. (positive behaviour)
  • Whenever I visit, he's watching TV. (whenever + continuous)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ At 8 I'm usually drive to work. → ✅ At 8 I'm usually driving to work. (be + verb-ing.)
  • ❌ She's always help people. → ✅ She's always helping people. (be + always + verb-ing.)

When not to use it for the future C1

  • Predictions about things nobody can arrange, like the weather: It's going to rain tonight. / It'll rain tonight. (not It's raining tonight)
  • Permanent future situations: People will live longer in the future. (not are living longer in the future)

  • I'm going to Toronto next week. (rather than going to go)
  • Are you coming home for lunch? (come)
  • It's going to snow tonight. (prediction: not continuous)
  • Experts say the population will grow. (prediction)
  • I'm not lending him my car again. (firm decision)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ It's raining tomorrow. → ✅ It's going to rain tomorrow. / It'll rain tomorrow. (The weather can't be arranged: use going to or will.)
  • ❌ In the future, people are living on other planets. → ✅ In the future, people will live on other planets. (Predictions about the future: will or going to.)

Practice: C1

Continuous = the action is in progress at that time.
18. Which sentence describes kind, typical behaviour?
Always + continuous can describe typical behaviour, here positive.
19. Whenever I phone him, he <span class="gap">___</span>.
In progress whenever I phone: he's watching TV.
20. Look at the forecast. It <span class="gap">___</span> tomorrow.
The weather can't be arranged: it's going to snow.
21. Which sentence is NOT correct?
A prediction about the future: use will or going to, not the present continuous.
22. Which sentence is a prediction, not an arrangement?
Nobody can arrange the sea level: it's a prediction (will).
