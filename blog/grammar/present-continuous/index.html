Present Continuous (and Present Simple vs Continuous)

Level: A1 · Free grammar guide

We use the present continuous for actions happening now or around now: I'm reading a book. She's studying for her exams this week.

Form it with am / is / are + verb-ing.

How to form the present continuous

FormExamples
Positivesubject + am / is / are + verb-ingI'm working. He's sleeping. They're playing.
Negativesubject + am not / isn't / aren't + verb-ingI'm not working. She isn't sleeping. We aren't playing.
QuestionAm / Is / Are + subject + verb-ing?Are you working? Is he sleeping?
Short answersYes, + subject + am/is/are. / No, + subject + 'm not/isn't/aren't.Yes, I am. / No, she isn't.
Wh- questionquestion word + am / is / are + subject + verb-ing?What are you doing?

In speaking we usually use contractions: I'm, you're, he's, she's, it's, we're, they're.

Spelling: verb + -ing

RuleExamples
Most verbs: add -ingwork → working, read → reading, play → playing
Verb ends in -e: drop the e and add -ingmake → making, write → writing, come → coming
One vowel + one consonant at the end of a stressed syllable: double the consonantrun → running, sit → sitting, swim → swimming, stop → stopping
Verb ends in -ie: change ie to -yinglie → lying, die → dying, tie → tying

We don't double the consonant when the last syllable isn't stressed: visit → visiting, open → opening. British and American spelling are different for some verbs ending in -l: travelling (UK) / traveling (US). Both are correct.

When do we use the present continuous?

  • Now, at the moment of speaking: Look! It's raining.
  • Around now, but not exactly at this moment: I'm reading a great book this week.
  • Temporary situations: My cousin is staying with us this month.
  • Situations that are changing: The weather is getting colder.

Common time words: now, right now, at the moment, today, this week, Look!, Listen!

You can also use the present continuous for future plans that are already arranged: I'm meeting Sam on Friday.

Present simple or present continuous?

Present simplePresent continuous
habits and factsnow or around now
I walk to work every day.I'm walking to work now.
She speaks three languages.She's speaking on the phone.
It rains a lot in April.Look! It's raining.

Some verbs describe states, not actions, and we don't usually use them in the continuous: know, understand, want, need, believe, remember, like, hate. Say I know the answer (not I'm knowing) and I want a coffee (not I'm wanting). Have for possession is the same: I have a car. But I'm having lunch is correct, because have lunch is an action.

More practice: Present Simple.

Examples

  1. I'm cooking dinner right now. (now)
  2. She isn't working today. (negative)
  3. Are you listening to me? (question)
  4. The children are playing in the garden. (plural: are)
  5. What are you doing? (wh- question)
  6. It's snowing! Let's go outside. (happening now)
  7. I'm learning Spanish this year. (around now)
  8. He's sitting next to the window. (sit → sitting)
  9. We aren't watching the film. You can turn it off. (negative)
  10. My phone is charging. (now)

Common mistakes

❌ I working now.

✅ I'm working now. (Don't forget am, is or are.)

❌ She is work at the moment.

✅ She is working at the moment. (Use verb + -ing.)

❌ He is runing.

✅ He is running. (Run → running: double the n.)

❌ I'm knowing the answer.

✅ I know the answer. (Know is a state verb. Use the present simple.)

❌ I'm wanting a coffee.

✅ I want a coffee. (Want is a state verb. Use the present simple.)

❌ What you are doing?

✅ What are you doing? (Questions: are + subject + verb-ing.)

❌ Look! It rains.

✅ Look! It's raining. (For something happening now, use the present continuous.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. Be quiet! The baby ___.
It's happening now: is + verb-ing.
2. I ___ dinner at the moment.
At the moment = now: am cooking.
3. They ___ TV right now.
Negative with they: aren't + verb-ing.
4. ___ she working today?
Questions with she: Is + she + verb-ing?
5. What ___ you doing?
With you, use are: What are you doing?
6. He's ___ in the sea.
Swim ends in one vowel + one consonant: double the m, swimming.
7. She's ___ a letter to her grandmother.
Verbs ending in -e: drop the e and add -ing, writing.
8. Look! It ___.
Look! = happening now: is snowing.
9. I ___ the answer.
Know is a state verb, so use the present simple: know.
10. My sister usually ___ to school, but today she's taking the bus.
Usually = a habit: present simple, walks.
11. We ___ a project about space in class this week.
This week = around now, a temporary activity: are doing.
12. Choose the correct question.
Know is a state verb: Do you know…? With you, use do.
13. The children ___ in the park now.
Now + plural subject: are playing.
14. I can't talk now. I ___.
It's happening now: am driving.
15. Listen! Someone ___ at the door.
Listen! = now. Someone is singular: is knocking.
16. He ___ coffee. He prefers tea.
Like is a state verb: present simple, doesn't like.
