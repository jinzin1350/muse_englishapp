Countable and Uncountable Nouns

A1 · A2 · B1 · B2 · C1

Countable nouns can be counted: one apple, two apples. Uncountable nouns can't: water, rice, advice. You can't say two rices or an advice.

The difference changes the words you use with them: a, some, much, many, a few, a little. See also Much, Many, A Few, A Little.

A1 · Beginner

Countable or uncountable? A1

CountableUncountable
a / an + singular: a banana, an eggno a / an: bread, milk (not a bread)
plural: bananas, eggsno plural: rice (not rices)
How many? many, a fewHow much? much, a little
some / any + plural: some eggssome / any: some milk

  • I'd like an orange and some bread. (countable / uncountable)
  • There is some cheese in the fridge. (singular verb)
  • How many eggs do we need? (countable)
  • How much sugar do you take? (uncountable)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I need some moneys. → ✅ I need some money. (money: no plural)
  • ❌ The rice are ready. → ✅ The rice is ready. (uncountable: singular verb)
  • ❌ I eat a bread every morning. → ✅ I eat bread every morning. (bread: no a)

Practice: A1

1. I'd like <span class="gap">___</span> milk in my tea, please.
Uncountable: some milk.
2. Which noun is uncountable?
Rice has no plural.
3. The water <span class="gap">___</span> (be) very cold. Don't swim!
Uncountable: singular verb.

A2 · Elementary

Tricky uncountable nouns: advice, information, news A2

Uncountable (no a, no plural)Countable alternative
advicea piece of advice, a tip
informationa piece of information, a fact
newsa piece of news, a news story
furniturea chair, a table, a piece of furniture
luggage / baggagea bag, a suitcase
homeworkan exercise, a task
worka job
travela trip, a journey
weatherno countable form: What lovely weather!

  • Can you give me some information about the course? (some + uncountable)
  • I have a lot of homework tonight. (a lot of)
  • Our luggage is in the car. (singular verb)
  • I'm looking for a job. I need work. (job / work)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ She gave me a good advice. → ✅ She gave me some good advice. (advice: no a)
  • ❌ We bought new furnitures. → ✅ We bought new furniture. (furniture: no -s)
  • ❌ The news are on at six. → ✅ The news is on at six. (news: singular verb)
  • ❌ I had a nice travel. → ✅ I had a nice trip. (travel: uncountable → trip)

Practice: A2

4. Can I give you <span class="gap">___</span> advice?
Uncountable: some advice.
5. I have <span class="gap">___</span> homework tonight.
Uncountable: a lot of homework.
6. The information on this website <span class="gap">___</span> (be) very useful. I use it every day.
Uncountable: singular verb.

  • Could I have a glass of water, please? (glass)
  • I bought two loaves of bread. (loaf → loaves)
  • She gave me a useful piece of advice. (piece)
  • We need a carton of milk. (carton)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Two breads, please. → ✅ Two loaves of bread, please. (bread: uncountable → loaf)
  • ❌ three bottles of waters → ✅ three bottles of water (the uncountable noun stays singular)

B2 · Upper-intermediate

Nouns that can be both: paper or a paper, coffee or a coffee B2

UncountableCountable
paper: the material: a sheet of papera paper: a newspaper or an essay
glass: the material: made of glassa glass: for drinking; glasses: for your eyes
hair: all the hair on your heada hair: one single hair
room: space: no room for a sofaa room: in a house
time: I don't have time.a time: an occasion: I've been there three times.
experience: knowledge from doing somethingan experience: something that happened to you
coffee, tea, juice: the drink in generala coffee: a cup or glass of it
chicken: the meata chicken: the animal
  • I need some paper to print this. (material)
  • I bought a paper at the station. (a newspaper)
  • Two coffees, please. (cups of coffee)
  • She has a lot of experience in marketing. (knowledge)
  • Travelling alone was an amazing experience. (an event)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I have a lot of experiences in sales. → ✅ I have a lot of experience in sales. (knowledge: uncountable)
  • ❌ She has long black hairs. → ✅ She has long black hair. (all the hair: uncountable)
  • ❌ Is there a room for one more person in the car? → ✅ Is there room for one more person in the car? (space: uncountable)

Practice: B2

10. Is there <span class="gap">___</span> room in your car for my bag?
Space: uncountable.
11. I've been to London three <span class="gap">___</span>.
A time = an occasion: three times.
12. She has ten years of <span class="gap">___</span> (experience) as a nurse.
Knowledge: uncountable, no -s.

C1 · Advanced

Formal uncountable nouns: research, evidence, feedback C1

UncountableCountable way to say one
researcha study, a piece of research
evidencea piece of evidence, a clue
feedbacka comment, a piece of feedback
progressa step forward, an improvement
accommodation (BrE)a room, a flat (AmE often says accommodations)
equipment / softwarea tool, a device / a program, an app
knowledgea good knowledge of (with an adjective)

  • There is no evidence that the drug works. (singular verb)
  • She's doing research into childhood memory. (no *a*)
  • Thank you for your feedback. (uncountable)
  • We tasted three local cheeses. (kinds)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Recent researches show that sleep matters. → ✅ Recent research shows that sleep matters. (research: uncountable)
  • ❌ We found an evidence. → ✅ We found a piece of evidence. (evidence: no a)
  • ❌ You've made a good progress. → ✅ You've made good progress. (progress: no a)

Practice: C1

13. Recent <span class="gap">___</span> shows that exercise improves mood.
Uncountable: research shows.
14. The police didn't find <span class="gap">___</span> evidence.
Uncountable: any evidence.
15. You've made very good <span class="gap">___</span> (progress) this term.
Uncountable: no -s.
