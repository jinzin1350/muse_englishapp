Much, Many, A Lot Of, A Few, A Little

A1 · A2 · B1 · B2 · C1

Use many and a few with plural countable nouns (many books, a few friends), and much and a little with uncountable nouns (much time, a little milk). A lot of works with both.

See also Countable and Uncountable Nouns and Some and Any.

A1 · Beginner

Much, many, a lot of; how much, how many A1

Countable (plural)Uncountable
questionHow many apples? Are there many people?How much water? Is there much traffic?
negativeThere aren't many cafés.I don't have much time.
positiveThere are a lot of cafés.I have a lot of time.

  • How many brothers do you have? (countable)
  • I don't have much money. (uncountable, negative)
  • There are a lot of people here. (positive)
  • How much is a coffee? (price)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ How much people came? → ✅ How many people came? (countable: many)
  • ❌ I don't have many time. → ✅ I don't have much time. (uncountable: much)
  • ❌ There is a lot of cars. → ✅ There are a lot of cars. (plural noun: are)

Practice: A1

1. How <span class="gap">___</span> sisters do you have?
Countable: How many?
2. We don't have <span class="gap">___</span> milk. Can you buy some?
Uncountable, negative: much.
3. How <span class="gap">___</span> money do you need? (one word)
Uncountable: How much?

A2 · Elementary

A few and a little: some, but not a lot A2

Countable (plural)Uncountable
a few eggs, a few days, a few peoplea little milk, a little time, a little money

  • I'll be ready in a few minutes. (countable)
  • Add a little salt. (uncountable)
  • We stayed in Paris for a few days. (countable)
  • She speaks a little French. (uncountable)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I have a little friends here. → ✅ I have a few friends here. (countable: a few)
  • ❌ Can I have a few milk? → ✅ Can I have a little milk? (uncountable: a little)

Uncountable: a little.

B1 · Intermediate

Few or a few? Little or a little? B1

Positive (some, enough)Negative (not enough, almost none)
I have a few friends here.Very few people came.
There's a little time left. Let's have a coffee.There's little time left. Hurry!

  • Few tourists visit this area in winter. (not many)
  • He has little patience with lazy students. (not much)
  • A few of my friends came to help. (some)
  • Quite a few people complained. (quite a lot)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Unfortunately, a few people came, so the party was boring. → ✅ Unfortunately, few people came, so the party was boring. (negative: few)
  • ❌ Don't hurry. There's little time. → ✅ Don't hurry. There's a little time. (positive: a little)

Practice: B1

7. The town is very quiet. <span class="gap">___</span> people live there now.
Negative (not many): few.
8. Don't worry, we have <span class="gap">___</span> time. Let's have a coffee.
Positive (some): a little.
9. Very <span class="gap">___</span> students failed the exam. It was quite easy. (one word)
Very few = almost none.

B2 · Upper-intermediate

Too much, too many, plenty of, a large number of B2

Countable (plural)Uncountable
too many peopletoo much noise
plenty of chairsplenty of time
a large number of studentsa great deal of money, a large amount of data
so many questionsso much work

  • There are too many cars in the city centre. (too many)
  • I spend too much time on my phone. (too much)
  • Help yourself. There's plenty of food. (plenty of)
  • The project requires a great deal of effort. (formal, uncountable)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ There's too many noise. → ✅ There's too much noise. (uncountable: too much)
  • ❌ A large number of money was stolen. → ✅ A large amount of money was stolen. (uncountable: a large amount of)
  • ❌ A great deal of people agree. → ✅ A large number of people agree. (plural: a large number of)

Practice: B2

10. I can't sleep. There's <span class="gap">___</span> noise outside.
Uncountable: too much.
11. Don't rush. We have <span class="gap">___</span> time.
More than enough: plenty of.
12. There were too <span class="gap">___</span> people on the bus, so I walked. (one word)
Countable: too many.

C1 · Advanced

Much in formal writing, many a, a great many C1

PatternExample
much in formal positive sentencesMuch research has been done on this topic. There is much debate about…
much / many as pronounsMuch of the city was destroyed. Many believe that…
many a + singular noun + singular verbMany a student has failed this exam. (literary)
a good / great many + pluralI've visited a great many countries.
as much / as many as + numberIt can cost as much as $5,000.

  • Much has changed since the 1990s. (much as a pronoun)
  • Many a traveller has got lost in these mountains. (many a)
  • She has written a great many articles. (a great many)
  • As many as 500 jobs could be lost. (as many as)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Many a students have tried. → ✅ Many a student has tried. (many a + singular noun + singular verb)
  • ❌ A great many people was there. → ✅ A great many people were there. (plural verb)
  • ❌ It can cost as many as $1,000. → ✅ It can cost as much as $1,000. (money: as much as)

Practice: C1

13. <span class="gap">___</span> has been written about climate change.
Formal pronoun with a singular verb: much.
14. <span class="gap">___</span> student has struggled with this exam.
Many a + singular noun.
15. The storm affected as <span class="gap">___</span> as 20,000 homes. (one word)
Countable: as many as.
