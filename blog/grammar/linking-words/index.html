Linking Words in English

Linking words (also called connectors or conjunctions) join ideas and show how they're related: adding (and), contrast (but, although), reason (because), result (so), time (when, after) and comparison (like, as if).

This page gives the big picture. Each topic below has its own page with rules, examples and exercises at every level.

Grammar to watch

  • Although + subject + verb, but despite / in spite of + noun or -ing: Although it rained… / Despite the rain…
  • Because + clause, but because of / due to + noun: because it was late / because of the traffic.
  • Use one linker, not two: Although it was late, I stayed. (not Although it was late, but I stayed.)
  • However and therefore usually start a new sentence and are followed by a comma.
  • Like + noun (like my mother), as + clause or role (as I said, as a teacher).

And, But, Or, So and Because

The basic linking words: and (adding), but (contrast), or (choice), so (result) and because (reason).

Time Conjunctions and Sequencing Words

Words that show when things happen and in what order: when, while, before, after, as soon as, until, then, finally.

Although, Despite and In Spite Of

Showing contrast: although + subject + verb, despite / in spite of + noun or -ing, even though, whereas, albeit.

Because, As, Since and Due To

Ways to give a reason: because, as, since, now that, seeing as, due to, owing to, thanks to, given that.

Like, As and As If

A2 B1 B2 C1

When to use like (similar to), as (in the role of, in the same way) and as if (it seems that).
