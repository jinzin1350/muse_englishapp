Have Got

A1 · A2 · B1

Have got means the same as have for possession, family, appearance and illness: I've got two sisters. She's got blue eyes. I've got a cold. It's very common in British English, especially in speech.

In American English, people usually say I have and Do you have…? Both are correct. For have to (obligation), see Must and Have To.

A1 · Beginner

I've got, she's got: forms A1

Full formShort formExample
I / you / we / they have got've gotWe've got a new car.
he / she / it has got's gotShe's got long hair.

  • I've got a brother and a sister. (family)
  • She's got green eyes. (appearance)
  • We've got a big garden. (possession)
  • He's got a headache. (illness)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ She have got a cat. → ✅ She has got a cat. / She's got a cat. (she has)
  • ❌ I got two brothers. (BrE) → ✅ I've got two brothers. (have got)

I haven't got, have you got…? A1

NegativeQuestionShort answer
I haven't got a car.Have you got a car?Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.
She hasn't got a car.Has she got a car?Yes, she has. / No, she hasn't.

  • I haven't got any money. (negative)
  • Have you got a pen? (question)
  • Has he got any brothers? ~ No, he hasn't. (question + short answer)
  • We haven't got time for lunch. (negative)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Do you have got a car? → ✅ Have you got a car? / Do you have a car? (no do with have got)
  • ❌ I don't have got a pen. → ✅ I haven't got a pen. / I don't have a pen. (negative)
  • ❌ Have you got a car? ~ Yes, I've got. → ✅ Have you got a car? ~ Yes, I have. (short answer)

A2 · Elementary

Have got or have? Have breakfast, had a car A2

  • I have lunch at one o'clock. (action: have)
  • We had a great time. (past: had)
  • Do you have any brothers? (have + do)
  • She's got a beautiful voice. (possession: have got)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I've got breakfast at 8. → ✅ I have breakfast at 8. (action: have)
  • ❌ When I was young, I had got a dog. → ✅ When I was young, I had a dog. (past: had)

B1 · Intermediate

Have got to, got and gotten B1

PatternExampleNote
have got to + verbI've got to finish this today.= have to (informal)
has got to + verbShe's got to leave early.= has to
gotta (very informal)I gotta go.spoken only
AmE gottenIt's gotten dark. / BrE It's got dark.past participle of get

  • I've got to call my mother tonight. (obligation)
  • We've got to be at the airport by six. (obligation)
  • Have you got to work tomorrow? (question)
  • It's gotten really cold. (AmE) (gotten)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I had got to leave early yesterday. → ✅ I had to leave early yesterday. (past: had to)
  • ❌ She's got go now. → ✅ She's got to go now. (got to + verb)
