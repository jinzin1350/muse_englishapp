Have Got and Has Got

Level: A1 · Free grammar guide

We use have got / has got to talk about things we own, family, how people look and illnesses: I've got a new phone. She's got two brothers.

It means the same as have: I've got a car = I have a car.

Form

I / you / we / theyhe / she / it
PositiveI have got (I've got) a car.She has got (She's got) a car.
NegativeI haven't got a car.She hasn't got a car.
QuestionHave you got a car?Has she got a car?
Short answersYes, I have. / No, I haven't.Yes, she has. / No, she hasn't.

Short answers don't use got: Have you got a pen? – Yes, I have. (not Yes, I have got.)

Have got or have?

For possessions, family, appearance and illness, both are correct: I've got a cold / I have a cold. Have got is very common in British English and in informal speech. In American English, have is more common, especially in questions: Do you have a pen?

Don't mix the two forms: Have you got a pen? or Do you have a pen? (not Do you have got).

For actions, use only have, not have got: have breakfast, have a shower, have a party, have fun. I have breakfast at 7. (not I've got breakfast)

Only in the present

Have got is a present form. For the past, use had: I've got a dog now. When I was a child, I had a cat. Past questions and negatives use did: Did you have a pet? We didn't have a car.

Examples

  1. I've got two sisters and a brother. (family)
  2. My dad has got a new car. (possession)
  3. She's got long dark hair and brown eyes. (appearance)
  4. I can't come today. I've got a cold. (illness)
  5. We haven't got a garden. (negative)
  6. Have you got any brothers or sisters? (question)
  7. Has he got a job? – Yes, he has. (short answer)
  8. They've got a big house in the country. (they)
  9. Hurry up! We've got ten minutes. (time)
  10. When I was young, I had a bike. (past: had)

Common mistakes

❌ She have got two cats.

✅ She has got two cats. (He / she / it → has got.)

❌ I have got not a car.

✅ I haven't got a car. (Negative: haven't got / hasn't got.)

❌ Do you have got a pen?

✅ Have you got a pen? / Do you have a pen? (Don't mix the two forms.)

❌ Has he got a car? – Yes, he has got.

✅ Yes, he has. (Short answers don't use got.)

❌ I've got breakfast at 8.

✅ I have breakfast at 8. (For actions, use have, not have got.)

❌ Last year we have got a dog.

✅ Last year we had a dog. (For the past, use had.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. I ___ two brothers.
I → have got.
2. ___ you got a bike?
Question: Have you got…?
3. She ___ long brown hair.
She → has got.
4. We ___ got a garden, but we have a balcony.
Negative with we: haven't got.
5. Has your sister got a car? – Yes, she ___.
Short answers don't use got: Yes, she has.
6. My grandparents ___ got a house by the sea.
They (my grandparents) → have got.
7. ___ got a terrible headache.
I've got = I have got.
8. How many cousins ___ got?
Question: How many… have you got?
9. In 'He's got a new job', 's means ___.
He's got = he has got.
10. Last year we ___ a dog called Max.
For the past, use had.
11. Choose the correct question.
She → Has she got…?
12. Have you got a pen? – Sorry, I ___ one.
Negative with I: haven't got.
13. We've got a test tomorrow. = We ___ a test tomorrow.
Have got = have.
14. I usually ___ breakfast at 7 o'clock.
For actions like breakfast, use have, not have got.
15. My brother ___ got a car, so he goes everywhere by bike.
He goes by bike, so negative: he hasn't got a car.
16. They ___ any children.
Negative with they: haven't got.
