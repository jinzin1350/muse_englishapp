Word Order in English

A1 · A2 · B1 · C1

English word order is quite fixed: subject + verb + object: Sara loves coffee. (not Sara coffee loves). Information about place and time usually comes at the end, in that order.

This page shows the basic order, where adverbs go, and how to use two objects.

A1 · Beginner

Subject + verb + object A1

SubjectVerbObjectOther
Iliketeavery much.
My brotherplaysfootballon Saturdays.
ShespeaksEnglishvery well.

  • We watched a film last night. (S + V + O)
  • I like this song a lot. (verb + object together)
  • My parents speak Persian at home. (S + V + O)
  • He drinks coffee every morning. (S + V + O)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I like very much chocolate. → ✅ I like chocolate very much. (verb + object together)
  • ❌ She speaks very well English. → ✅ She speaks English very well. (verb + object together)
  • ❌ Coffee I drink every day. → ✅ I drink coffee every day. (subject first)

A2 · Elementary

Place before time: at the station yesterday A2

Subject + verb (+ object) + mannerPlace (where)Time (when)
I met Aliat the stationyesterday.
We're goingto Spainnext week.
She worked hardat the officeall day.

  • I bought this jacket in Toronto last year. (place, time)
  • She goes to the gym every morning. (place, time)
  • Last night we ate at a Persian restaurant. (time at the start)
  • He drove carefully through the city. (manner, place)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I was yesterday at home. → ✅ I was at home yesterday. (place before time (at the end))
  • ❌ We arrived last night in Paris. → ✅ We arrived in Paris last night. (place before time)
  • ❌ I'm tomorrow working. → ✅ I'm working tomorrow. (time at the end)

B1 · Intermediate

Where do adverbs go? Also, probably, already, only B1

AdverbPositionExample
also, just, already, still, probably, definitely, even, only, almostbefore the main verbI also speak French.
after beShe's probably tired.
after the first auxiliaryThey've just left. I can definitely help.
probably, definitely + negativebefore won't / don'tHe probably won't come. (not won't probably)

  • I also have a sister. (before the main verb)
  • He's always late. (after be)
  • We've already finished. (after the auxiliary)
  • She probably won't come. (probably + negative)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I like also tea. → ✅ I also like tea. (before the main verb)
  • ❌ He won't probably come. → ✅ He probably won't come. (probably before won't)
  • ❌ They have finished already their homework. → ✅ They have already finished their homework. (not between the verb and its object)

Two objects: give me the book, give the book to me B1

PatternExample
verb + person + thingI sent my mum a card.
verb + thing + to + personI sent a card to my mum.
verb + thing + for + person (buy, make, get, cook)He bought flowers for his wife.
the thing is a pronounGive it to me. (Give me it is informal)

  • My father gave me some advice. (person + thing)
  • Can you lend your car to my brother? (thing + to + person)
  • I made a cake for my sister. (for)
  • Please explain the rules to me. (explain + to)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Can you explain me the problem? → ✅ Can you explain the problem to me? (explain + to)
  • ❌ She bought a present to her mother. → ✅ She bought a present for her mother. (buy + for)
  • ❌ Give me it, please. (careful English) → ✅ Give it to me, please. (pronoun thing: to)

C1 · Advanced

Fronting for emphasis and here comes the bus C1

PatternExample
object fronting (contrast)Some subjects I love; maths I hate.
here / there + verb + noun subjectHere comes the bus! There goes my money. (but: Here it comes.)
place + verb + subject (literary)On the hill stood an old castle.
adjective + as + subject + verbStrange as it may seem, I enjoyed it.

  • That film I'll never forget. (object fronting)
  • Here comes the train. (here + verb + noun)
  • In the corner sat an old man. (place + verb + subject)
  • Tired as she was, she kept working. (adjective + as)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Here comes it! → ✅ Here it comes! (pronoun: no inversion)
  • ❌ There is she! → ✅ There she is! (pronoun: no inversion)
  • ❌ Tired as was she, she kept going. → ✅ Tired as she was, she kept going. (adjective + as + subject + verb)
