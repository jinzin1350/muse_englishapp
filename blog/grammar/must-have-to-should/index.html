Must, Have To and Should

Level: A2 · Free grammar guide

We use must and have to for obligation (something is necessary), and should for advice (something is a good idea).

You must wear a seatbelt. I have to work on Saturday. You should see a doctor.

Must and should never take to or -s: She must go (not musts or must to). Have to changes like a normal verb: has to, had to, will have to.

Mustn't or don't have to?

These look similar but mean very different things:

  • mustn't = it's not allowed, don't do it: You mustn't smoke here.
  • don't have to = it's not necessary, but you can if you want: You don't have to pay. It's free.

Must or have to?

In the present positive, both are often possible. Must is common in rules and signs, and when the speaker decides: Passengers must show their tickets. I must call Mum tonight. Have to is common when the obligation comes from outside (a job, a law): I have to wear a uniform at work.

For the past and future, use have to: Yesterday I had to work late. Next year I'll have to take the exam again.

Examples

  1. You must wear a helmet on a motorbike. (rule)
  2. She has to get up at 5 for work. (external obligation)
  3. You mustn't use your phone during the exam. (not allowed)
  4. We don't have to wear a uniform at school. (not necessary)
  5. Do you have to work at weekends? (question)
  6. I had to wait for an hour yesterday. (past)
  7. You'll have to book a table. (future)
  8. You should try this cake. It's delicious. (advice)
  9. You shouldn't eat so much sugar. (negative advice)
  10. Should I call her? (asking for advice)

Common mistakes

❌ You must to wear a seatbelt.

✅ You must wear a seatbelt. (No to after must.)

❌ She have to work today.

✅ She has to work today. (He / she / it: has to.)

❌ Yesterday I must work late.

✅ Yesterday I had to work late. (Past: had to.)

❌ You mustn't pay. It's free.

✅ You don't have to pay. It's free. (Not necessary: don't have to.)

❌ Do I must go?

✅ Must I go? / Do I have to go? (Don't use do with must.)

❌ You should to see a doctor.

✅ You should see a doctor. (No to after should.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. You ___ smoke in the hospital. It's not allowed.
Not allowed: mustn't.
2. Tomorrow is Sunday, so I ___ get up early.
Not necessary: don't have to.
3. You look tired. You ___ go to bed early.
Advice: should.
4. She ___ wear a uniform at work.
She → has to.
5. Yesterday I ___ work until 9 p.m.
Past obligation: had to.
6. ___ we have to bring our own lunch?
Question with have to: Do we have to…?
7. You ___ pay for the museum. It's free.
Not necessary: don't have to.
8. You ___ eat so much sugar. It's bad for your teeth.
Advice (negative): shouldn't.
9. Next year, I'll ___ take the exam again.
Future: will have to.
10. Does he ___ work at weekends?
After does: have to.
11. Choose the correct sentence.
mustn't + base form.
12. I think you ___ apologise to her. You were rude.
Advice: should.
13. We ___ hurry. We have plenty of time.
Not necessary: don't have to.
14. Children ___ play with matches. It's dangerous!
Not allowed: mustn't.
15. Did you ___ wear a tie to the interview?
After did: have to.
16. Passengers ___ show their passports at the gate. (It's a rule.)
A rule: must.
