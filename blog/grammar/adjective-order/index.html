ترتیب صفت‌هاAdjective Order

In English, adjectives usually go before the noun (a red car) or after verbs like be, look and seem (The car is red).

When there are two or more adjectives, there's a usual order: opinion first, then facts like size, age, colour, origin and material: a beautiful old Persian carpet.

A2 · مقدماتی

Adjectives before nouns and after be A2

Adjectives go before a noun (a small flat) or after be, look, feel, seem, sound, taste, get (The flat is small). They don't take -s in the plural: two small flats.

PositionExample
before a nounShe has long hair. It's a cheap hotel.
after be, look, feel, seem, sound, taste, getYou look tired. This soup tastes great.
with plural nounssmall flats (not smalls flats)

A few adjectives are used only after the verb: afraid, alone, asleep, awake, alive: The baby is asleep. (Before a noun, say a sleeping baby.)

  • I bought a new phone. (before a noun)
  • Your idea sounds good. (after sound)
  • They live in a beautiful house. (before a noun)
  • The children are asleep. (only after be)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I have a car red. → ✅ I have a red car. (adjective before the noun)
  • ❌ They are very kinds people. → ✅ They are very kind people. (no -s on adjectives)
  • ❌ There was an afraid child in the corner. → ✅ There was a frightened child in the corner. (afraid: only after be)

Adjective before the noun.

No -s on adjectives.

Asleep comes after be.

B1 · متوسط

Opinion, size, age, colour: a lovely big old house B1

With more than one adjective, put opinion first, then facts, usually in this order: size → age → shape → colour → origin → material → purpose + noun: a lovely small old round wooden table (but two or three adjectives are more natural).

OrderTypeExamples
1opinionbeautiful, lovely, nice, ugly
2sizebig, small, tall, long
3ageold, new, young, ancient
4shaperound, square, flat
5colourred, blue, grey
6originPersian, Italian, Canadian
7materialwooden, silk, leather, plastic
8purpose (often a noun)running (shoes), coffee (table)

In real English, we rarely use more than three adjectives before a noun: a lovely old wooden house.

Opinion adjectives (nice, beautiful, terrible) come before fact adjectives (big, old, red): a nice big room (not a big nice room).

  • She has beautiful long black hair. (opinion, size, colour)
  • We bought a small round wooden table. (size, shape, material)
  • It's a lovely old Persian carpet. (opinion, age, origin)
  • He was wearing new white running shoes. (age, colour, purpose)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ a black big dog → ✅ a big black dog (size before colour)
  • ❌ a wooden old chair → ✅ an old wooden chair (age before material)
  • ❌ an Italian beautiful car → ✅ a beautiful Italian car (opinion first)

B2 · بالاتر از متوسط

And between adjectives: big and bright, black and white B2

After a verb, join two adjectives with and: The room was big and bright. With three, use commas and and: clever, kind and funny. Use and between colours of one thing: a black and white photo.

PatternExample
after a verb: two adjectivesThe weather was cold and wet.
after a verb: three adjectivesShe's clever, kind and funny.
before a noun: same typea long, boring meeting or a long and boring meeting
before a noun: different typesa small red car (no and, no comma)
colours of one thinga black and white photo, a red and white flag

Before a noun, you can put a comma or and between two adjectives of the same type (two opinions: a long, boring film), but not between adjectives of different types (a big black dog).

  • The hotel was clean and comfortable. (after a verb)
  • It was a long, tiring journey. (same type: comma)
  • She has a black and white cat. (colours)
  • My grandfather is wise, patient and kind. (three adjectives)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ The food was cheap delicious. → ✅ The food was cheap and delicious. (after a verb: and)
  • ❌ She's clever and kind and funny and. → ✅ She's clever, kind and funny. (three adjectives: commas + and)

تمرین سطح B2

C1 · پیشرفته

Adjectives after nouns: something new, the people involved C1

Some adjectives go after the noun: after something, anyone, nowhere (something interesting), with some participles and available (the people involved, the seats available), and with measurements (two metres long).

PatternExample
something / anyone / somewhere + adjectiveLet's do something different. Is there anyone famous here?
participles and available after the nounthe people involved, the problems mentioned, the tickets available
measurementsThe wall is three metres high. She's six years old.
meaning changethe people present (= there) / the present situation (= now); the people concerned (= involved) / a concerned mother (= worried)
adjective + phrasea bag full of money, people interested in art

When an adjective has its own phrase after it, the whole thing goes after the noun: a room full of people (not a full of people room).

  • I want to try something new. (something + adjective)
  • All the people involved were interviewed. (after the noun)
  • The river is 200 metres wide. (measurement)
  • Are there any seats available? (available after the noun)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I'd like to eat new something. → ✅ I'd like to eat something new. (something + adjective)
  • ❌ The involved people were questioned. → ✅ The people involved were questioned. (involved (= taking part): after the noun)
