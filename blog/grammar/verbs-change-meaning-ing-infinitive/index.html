Verbs That Change Meaning: -ing or To-Infinitive

Level: B2 · Free grammar guide

Most verbs take either -ing or to-infinitive. But a few verbs can take both, with a different meaning: I stopped smoking (I don't smoke now) vs I stopped to smoke (I stopped what I was doing in order to smoke).

Remember and forget

FormMeaningExample
remember / forget + -inga memory of something you did beforeI remember meeting her at a party. I'll never forget seeing the ocean for the first time.
remember / forget + tosomething you need to doRemember to lock the door. I forgot to buy milk.

Stop and try

FormMeaningExample
stop + -ingnot do something any moreHe stopped smoking last year.
stop + tostop one thing in order to do anotherWe stopped to have a coffee.
try + tomake an effort (it may be difficult)I'm trying to concentrate.
try + -ingdo something as an experiment, to see if it worksIf you can't sleep, try drinking warm milk.

Regret, go on, mean, need

FormMeaningExample
regret + -ingbe sorry about something you didI regret being rude to her.
regret + tobe sorry to give bad news (formal)We regret to inform you that the flight is cancelled.
go on + -ingcontinue the same activityHe went on talking for an hour.
go on + todo something new nextAfter the intro, she went on to explain the rules.
mean + tointendI didn't mean to upset you.
mean + -inginvolve, have as a resultThe new job means travelling a lot.
need + toit's necessary (active)I need to do more exercise.
need + -ingit needs to be done (passive meaning)The car needs washing. (= needs to be washed)

With like, love, hate, start, begin and continue, the meaning hardly changes: It started to rain / raining. See Gerund or Infinitive?.

Examples

  1. I remember watching this cartoon as a kid. (memory)
  2. Did you remember to call the dentist? (something to do)
  3. She stopped eating meat ten years ago. (doesn't do it now)
  4. He stopped to tie his shoelace. (in order to)
  5. I tried to open the jar, but it was too tight. (effort)
  6. Try turning it off and on again. (experiment)
  7. I regret not studying harder at school. (sorry about the past)
  8. We regret to announce that the store is closing. (formal bad news)
  9. Taking the job would mean moving to another city. (involve)
  10. The grass needs cutting. (passive meaning)

Common mistakes

❌ Remember locking the door when you leave.

✅ Remember to lock the door when you leave. (Something you need to do: remember to.)

❌ I stopped to smoke last year and feel great.

✅ I stopped smoking last year and feel great. (Not doing it any more: stop + -ing.)

❌ I didn't mean upsetting you.

✅ I didn't mean to upset you. (Intend: mean to.)

❌ I forgot buying bread, so we have none.

✅ I forgot to buy bread. (Something you needed to do: forget to.)

❌ The house needs to paint.

✅ The house needs painting / needs to be painted. (Passive meaning: need + -ing.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. I remember ___ this film when I was a child. I loved it.
A memory of the past: remember + -ing.
2. Please remember ___ the door when you leave.
Something you need to do: remember + to.
3. He stopped ___ ten years ago, and he feels much healthier now.
He doesn't smoke now: stop + -ing.
4. We were driving for hours, so we stopped ___ a coffee.
Stopped in order to have a coffee: stop + to.
5. Please be quiet. I'm trying ___.
Making an effort: try + to.
6. If you can't sleep, try ___ a glass of warm milk before bed.
An experiment to see if it works: try + -ing.
7. I'll never forget ___ the Pyramids for the first time.
A memory: forget + -ing.
8. Oh no! I forgot ___ milk. Can you get some on your way home?
Something I needed to do: forget + to.
9. I regret ___ so rude to her. I should apologise.
Sorry about something I did: regret + -ing.
10. We regret ___ you that the flight has been cancelled.
Formal bad news: regret + to.
11. After the break, the teacher went on ___ about the same topic for another hour.
Continued the same activity: go on + -ing.
12. After explaining the past simple, the teacher went on ___ the present perfect.
Moved on to something new: go on + to.
13. If you take this job, it will mean ___ to work every weekend.
Involve: mean + -ing.
14. I didn't mean ___ you. I'm sorry.
Intend: mean + to.
15. The car is really dirty. It needs ___.
Passive meaning (it needs to be washed): need + -ing.
16. I need ___ more exercise.
It's necessary for me (active): need + to.
