Verbs that Change Meaning with -ing or To

B1 · B2 · C1

Most verbs take either -ing or to, but some can take both. With some of them (like, start, begin), the meaning hardly changes. With others (remember, forget, stop, try, regret, mean), the meaning is very different: I stopped smoking (I don't smoke now) / I stopped to smoke (I stopped what I was doing so that I could smoke).

For the general patterns, see Gerund or Infinitive.

B1 · Intermediate

Like, love, start, begin, continue: little or no difference B1

  • In British English, like + -ing often means enjoy, and like + to can mean think it's a good idea: I like to see the dentist twice a year. (I don't enjoy it, but it's sensible.)
  • After would like / would love / would prefer, only to is possible: I'd like to go. See Would Like.
  • Avoid two -ing forms together: It's starting to rain (not It's starting raining).
  • It started raining / to rain at noon. (both correct)
  • I love swimming / to swim in the sea. (both correct)
  • I like to pay my bills on time. (it's sensible (BrE nuance))
  • The baby is beginning to walk. (not beginning walking)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I'd love going to Japan one day. → ✅ I'd love to go to Japan one day. (would love + to)
  • ❌ It's starting snowing. → ✅ It's starting to snow. (avoid -ing + -ing)

Practice: B1

1. Which sentence is NOT correct?
After would like, only to.
2. Look! It's beginning ___.
Avoid two -ing forms: beginning to snow.
3. She started ___ (learn) English when she was six.
Start + -ing or to: both correct.

B2 · Upper-intermediate

Remember and forget: -ing or to? B2

PatternMeaningExample
remember + -ingI did it, and I have the memoryI remember locking the door. (I locked it; I can remember it.)
remember + toI didn't forget to do itI remembered to lock the door. (I locked it, as I needed to.)
forget + -inglose the memory of something you did (often I'll never forget)I'll never forget meeting her.
forget + tonot do something you needed to doI forgot to buy milk.

  • I remember visiting my grandparents every summer. (memory)
  • Please remember to call your mother. (duty)
  • I forgot to send the email! (didn't do it)
  • I'll never forget seeing the sea for the first time. (memory)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Remember locking the door when you go out! → ✅ Remember to lock the door when you go out! (duty: remember to)
  • ❌ I remember to go to Paris when I was a child. → ✅ I remember going to Paris when I was a child. (memory: remember -ing)

Stop and go on: -ing or to? B2

PatternMeaningExample
stop + -ingfinish an activityI stopped eating meat.
stop + topause in order to do somethingWe stopped to eat lunch. (purpose)
go on + -ingcontinue the same thingShe went on talking.
go on + todo the next thingHe finished school and went on to study law.

  • He stopped working at 65. (didn't work any more)
  • On the way home, I stopped to buy some flowers. (paused in order to buy)
  • The teacher went on explaining for an hour. (continued)
  • After the introduction, she went on to describe the results. (next thing)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I've stopped to drink coffee. (meaning I don't drink it now) → ✅ I've stopped drinking coffee. (no more: stop -ing)
  • ❌ We stopped buying petrol on the motorway. (meaning we paused to buy) → ✅ We stopped to buy petrol on the motorway. (purpose: stop to)

Try and need: -ing or to? B2

PatternMeaningExample
try + tomake an effortI tried to open the window, but it was stuck.
try + -ingdo it as an experimentIf you can't sleep, try drinking warm milk.
need + toit's necessary for the subject to do itI need to clean my room.
need + -ingit needs to be done (passive meaning)My room needs cleaning. (= needs to be cleaned)

  • I tried to lift the box, but it was too heavy. (effort)
  • The printer didn't work, so I tried turning it off and on. (experiment)
  • I need to call the bank. (necessary for me)
  • Your hair needs cutting. (needs to be cut)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I tried calling, but I couldn't because my phone was dead. (making an effort) → ✅ I tried to call, but I couldn't because my phone was dead. (effort: try to)
  • ❌ The windows need to clean. → ✅ The windows need cleaning / to be cleaned. (passive meaning)

Regret and mean: -ing or to? B2

PatternMeaningExample
regret + -ingI did it and I'm sorryI regret telling him the secret.
regret + to (say / tell / inform)formal: I'm sorry to give bad newsWe regret to inform you that the flight is cancelled.
mean + -inginvolve, have as a resultA new job means moving to another city.
mean + tointendI meant to call you, but I forgot.
  • I regret not studying harder at school. (sorry about the past)
  • We regret to announce that the concert has been cancelled. (formal bad news)
  • Saving money means spending less. (involves)
  • Sorry, I didn't mean to hurt you. (intend)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ We regret informing you that your application was unsuccessful. (formal letter) → ✅ We regret to inform you that your application was unsuccessful. (bad news: regret to)
  • ❌ I didn't mean hurting you. → ✅ I didn't mean to hurt you. (intend: mean to)

Practice: B2

4. Did you remember ___ the windows before we left?
A duty: remember to.
5. I remember ___ the Eiffel Tower when I was six. It was amazing.
A memory: remember -ing.
6. Oh no! I forgot ___ (buy) bread.
Didn't do it: forget to.
7. I was tired, so I stopped ___ a rest.
Pause in order to: stop to.
8. My doctor told me to stop ___.
Not do it any more: stop -ing.
9. After winning the national title, she went on ___ (win) an Olympic medal.
The next thing: go on to.
10. I tried hard ___ the jar, but the lid was too tight.
Effort (it was difficult): try to.
11. The headache didn't go away, so I tried ___ a glass of water.
An experiment: try -ing.
12. This shirt needs ___ (iron).
Passive meaning: needs ironing / to be ironed.
13. I regret ___ so much money on clothes. Now I'm broke.
Sorry about the past: regret -ing.
14. I'm sorry, I didn't mean ___ you.
Intend: mean to.
15. Getting a dog means ___ (walk) it twice a day.
Involve: mean -ing.

C1 · Advanced

Afraid to or afraid of -ing? Sorry to or sorry for -ing? C1

PatternMeaningExample
afraid to + verbdon't want to do it (because of fear)She was afraid to jump. (so she didn't)
afraid of + -ingafraid something might happenShe was afraid of falling.
sorry to + verbsorry about the present situationI'm sorry to hear about your father.
sorry for / about + -ingapologising for something doneI'm sorry for being late.

  • I was afraid to say anything. (didn't say it)
  • Many people are afraid of losing their jobs. (worried it might happen)
  • I'm sorry to hear that. (sorry about now)
  • I'm sorry for shouting at you yesterday. (apology for the past)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I'm afraid of to fly. → ✅ I'm afraid of flying. / I'm afraid to fly. (afraid of + -ing / afraid to + verb)
  • ❌ I'm sorry for interrupt. → ✅ I'm sorry to interrupt. / I'm sorry for interrupting. (sorry to + verb / sorry for + -ing)

Practice: C1

16. The path was icy, so I walked slowly. I was afraid ___.
Afraid that it might happen: afraid of -ing.
17. I'm sorry ___ you, but your car is blocking mine.
Sorry about now: sorry to.
18. The child was afraid ___ (ask) the teacher for help, so he said nothing.
Didn't do it because of fear: afraid to.
