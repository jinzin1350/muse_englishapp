Fronting and Word Order for Emphasis

Level: C2 · Free grammar guide

Fronting means moving part of a sentence to the beginning to give it emphasis or to create a dramatic effect: A castle stood on the hill. → On the hill stood a castle.

Sometimes fronting changes the word order of the subject and verb too (inversion). It's common in storytelling, speeches and formal writing.

Place and direction + verb + subject

After a fronted place or direction, a simple verb (be, stand, lie, come, go, walk, run) can come before a noun subject: Into the room walked a tall man. Next to the door lay a pile of newspapers. Down came the rain.

In everyday English: Here comes the bus! There goes my last chance.

With a pronoun subject, don't invert: Here it comes. Here they are. (not Here comes it)

Adjectives, so and such

PatternExample
adjective / participle + be + subjectGone are the days when we could leave doors unlocked. Lucky are those who have good friends.
So + adjective + be / auxiliary + subject + thatSo loud was the music that we couldn't hear each other.
Such be + noun + thatSuch was the demand that tickets sold out in minutes.

For inversion after negative words (Never have I…), see Inversion After Negative Adverbials.

Fronting an object or a clause (no inversion)

We can front an object for contrast or emphasis. The rest of the sentence stays in normal order: Coffee I love; tea I can't stand. What he said next I will never forget. This I cannot accept.

Examples

  1. Here comes the bus! (here + verb + noun)
  2. Look, here he comes. (pronoun: no inversion)
  3. On the top of the hill stood an old castle. (place + verb + subject)
  4. Into the room walked a man in a black coat. (direction + verb + subject)
  5. Away ran the children, laughing. (direction)
  6. Gone are the days of cheap flights. (participle fronting)
  7. So difficult was the exam that only two students passed. (so… that)
  8. Such was his anger that he couldn't speak. (such… that)
  9. Lucky are those who love their jobs. (adjective fronting)
  10. Some things you just can't explain. (object fronting)

Common mistakes

❌ Here comes it!

✅ Here it comes! (With a pronoun subject, don't invert.)

❌ So loud the music was that we left.

✅ So loud was the music that we left. (So + adjective at the start: inversion.)

❌ Such his anger was that he left.

✅ Such was his anger that he left. (Such at the start: such + be + subject.)

❌ Gone is the days of cheap travel.

✅ Gone are the days of cheap travel. (The verb agrees with the plural subject (the days).)

❌ Into the room did walk a man.

✅ Into the room walked a man. (After place / direction, use the simple verb, not do.)

❌ What he said next, will I never forget.

✅ What he said next, I will never forget. (A fronted object doesn't cause inversion.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. Look! Here ___!
Here + verb + noun subject: here comes the bus.
2. Look, there's Tom. Here ___ now.
Pronoun subject: no inversion, here he comes.
3. On the top of the hill ___ an old castle.
Place + verb + subject: stood an old castle.
4. So loud ___ that we couldn't hear each other.
So + adjective at the start: was the music.
5. ___ the days when we could leave our doors unlocked.
Participle fronting, plural subject: Gone are the days.
6. Such ___ his anger that he couldn't speak.
Such + was + subject + that.
7. Into the room ___ a tall man in a black coat.
Direction + simple verb + subject: walked a tall man.
8. Coffee I love; tea ___.
Fronted object: normal order, I can't stand.
9. Choose the correct sentence.
Pronoun subject: no inversion, Here they are.
10. Lucky ___ those who have good friends.
Adjective fronting, plural subject (those): are.
11. Away ___, laughing.
Direction + verb + subject: ran the children.
12. Such was the demand ___ the tickets sold out in minutes.
Such… that.
13. What he said next, ___ never forget.
Fronted object: normal order, I will never forget.
14. Next to the door ___ a pile of old newspapers.
Place + verb + subject: lay (past of lie).
15. Down ___ the rain all afternoon.
Direction + verb + subject: down came the rain.
16. So difficult ___ that only two students passed.
So + adjective at the start: was the exam.
