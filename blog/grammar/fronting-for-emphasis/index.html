Fronting and Word Order for Emphasis

C1 · C2

Fronting means moving a part of the sentence to the start to give it emphasis, create contrast or link it to what came before: I can't accept this. → This I can't accept.

Sometimes the subject and verb also change places (inversion): Gone are the days when phones were just phones. For the basics, see Word Order.

C1 · Advanced

On the hill stood a castle; off they went C1

NormalFronted
An old castle stood on the hill.On the hill stood an old castle.
A huge dog ran out.Out ran a huge dog.
The rain came down.Down came the rain.
They went off.Off they went. (pronoun: no inversion)

  • At the end of the garden was a small pond. (place + be)
  • Into the room walked the president. (direction + verb)
  • Up went the prices. (up + verb)
  • Away they ran, laughing. (pronoun: no inversion)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Off went they. → ✅ Off they went. (pronoun: no inversion)
  • ❌ In the corner a piano stood old. → ✅ In the corner stood an old piano. (place + verb + subject)

This I can't accept: fronting objects and complements C1

NormalFronted (contrast / emphasis)
I can't accept this.This I can't accept.
I liked the food, but I didn't like the service.The food I liked; the service I didn't.
I'll never forget that day.That day I'll never forget.
They were strange people.Strange people they were. (literary)

  • Some of the questions I could answer; others I couldn't. (contrast)
  • That I really didn't expect. (emphasis)
  • What he said next I'll never forget. (long object fronted)
  • His name I can't remember, but his face I know. (contrast)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ This do I not believe. → ✅ This I don't believe. (fronted object: no inversion)
  • ❌ The first part I understood it, but not the second. → ✅ The first part I understood, but not the second. (no extra it)

Gone are the days, attached is my CV C1

NormalFronted (formal / stylish)
The days when we wrote letters are gone.Gone are the days when we wrote letters.
My CV is attached.Attached is my CV.
Breakfast and dinner are included in the price.Included in the price are breakfast and dinner.
Those who have good friends are lucky.Lucky are those who have good friends.

  • Gone are the days of cheap flights. (gone are)
  • Attached is a copy of the invoice. (formal email)
  • Hidden behind the trees was a small cottage. (description)
  • Included in the package are flights and hotel. (advert)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Attached are my CV. → ✅ Attached is my CV. (be agrees with the subject: singular)
  • ❌ Gone are the day when we were young. → ✅ Gone are the days when we were young. (plural: days)

Practice: C1

1. On the top of the hill ___ an old church.
Place + verb + noun subject.
2. The door opened and in ___.
Pronoun subject: no inversion.
3. Down ___ (come) the rain, and everyone ran for cover.
Direction + verb + subject.
4. Most of the book I enjoyed, but the ending ___.
Fronted object: normal order.
5. Which sentence uses fronting for contrast?
Fronted objects for contrast.
6. Chocolate I love, but sweets ___ can't stand. (one word)
Fronted object + normal order: sweets I can't stand.
7. ___ the days when everyone had a landline at home.
Gone are the days…
8. Attached ___ copies of my certificates.
Plural subject: are.
9. Attached ___ my CV. I look forward to hearing from you. (one word)
Singular subject: Attached is…

C2 · Proficiency

More important is, equally worrying was, such is C2

NormalFronted
Health is more important than money.More important than money is health.
The lack of staff was equally worrying.Equally worrying was the lack of staff.
The price is even more surprising.Even more surprising is the price.
The demand is so great that…Such is the demand that…

  • More worrying is the rise in prices. (evaluation + be)
  • The film was long. Worse still was the ending. (worse still)
  • Of greater concern are the side effects. (plural subject)
  • Such is her talent that she won at 15. (such is)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ More important is the facts. → ✅ More important are the facts. (plural subject: are)
  • ❌ Such the demand was that tickets sold out. → ✅ Such was the demand that tickets sold out. (such + be + subject)

Try as I might, come what may, suffice it to say C2

ExpressionExampleMeaning
Try as I mightTry as I might, I couldn't open the jar.although I tried hard
Come what mayI'll support you, come what may.whatever happens
Be it … or …Every job, be it big or small, is important.whether it is … or …
Suffice it to saySuffice it to say, we won't go there again.I'll only say this
So be itIf that's your decision, so be it.I accept it
Far be it from me toFar be it from me to criticise, but…I don't want to… (but I will)

  • Try as she might, she couldn't remember his name. (try as I might)
  • We'll finish this project, come what may. (come what may)
  • All visitors, be they young or old, must buy a ticket. (be they … or …)
  • Far be it from me to tell you what to do, but… (far be it from me)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Try as I tried, I couldn't sleep. → ✅ Try as I might, I couldn't sleep. (try as I might)
  • ❌ Suffice to say it, the trip was a disaster. → ✅ Suffice it to say, the trip was a disaster. (suffice it to say)

Practice: C2

10. The hotel was noisy. Worse still ___ the food.
Singular subject: was.
11. More important than the price ___ the quality of the service.
Singular subject: is.
12. Even more surprising ___ the fact that nobody complained. (one word)
Fronted evaluation + be.
13. ___ as I might, I couldn't understand the instructions.
Try as I might.
14. I'll be there for you, ___.
Come what may.
15. ___ it to say, the meeting didn't go well. (one word)
Suffice it to say.
