Modals of Deduction in the Past (must have, can't have)

Level: B2 · Free grammar guide

To make guesses (deductions) about the past, use modal + have + past participle: The ground is wet. It must have rained last night. She might have missed the bus. He can't have seen us; he didn't say hello.

How sure are you?

ModalMeaningExample
must have + past participleI'm almost sure it happenedYou must have been tired after the flight.
might / may / could have + past participleit's possible that it happenedThey might have taken the wrong road.
might not / may not have + past participleit's possible that it didn't happenShe may not have heard the phone.
can't / couldn't have + past participleI'm almost sure it didn't happenHe can't have finished already. He only started ten minutes ago.

In American English, must not have is also used for negative deductions: He must not have heard me.

For an action in progress, use modal + have been + -ing: She must have been sleeping when you called.

Could have: two meanings

Could have + past participle can be a past possibility (The keys could have fallen out of your bag) or something that was possible but didn't happen (You could have called me! = you were able to, but you didn't). More: Should Have, Could Have, Needn't Have.

For present deductions, see Modals of Deduction: Must, Might, Can't.

Pronunciation

In speech, must have sounds like must-uv and might have like might-uv. In writing, always write have, never of: It must have been difficult. (not must of)

Examples

  1. The lights are off. They must have gone to bed. (almost sure)
  2. I can't find my wallet. I might have left it at the restaurant. (possible)
  3. She can't have forgotten your birthday. She talked about it all week. (almost sure it didn't happen)
  4. He may have missed the train. (possible)
  5. You must have been really worried. (must have been)
  6. They couldn't have known about the surprise. (impossible)
  7. It might not have been his fault. (possible negative)
  8. Someone must have taken my umbrella by mistake. (almost sure)
  9. She must have been studying all night. She looks exhausted. (continuous)
  10. The window is broken. A ball could have hit it. (possible explanation)

Common mistakes

❌ He must have went home.

✅ He must have gone home. (Use the past participle: gone.)

❌ She can't have saw us.

✅ She can't have seen us. (Use the past participle: seen.)

❌ They might has forgotten.

✅ They might have forgotten. (Always have, never has, after a modal.)

❌ It must of been difficult.

✅ It must have been difficult. (Write have, not of.)

❌ You must be tired yesterday.

✅ You must have been tired yesterday. (A past deduction: must have + past participle.)

❌ She can have left already.

✅ She could (or may, might) have left already. (Use could, may or might for past possibility, not can.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. The ground is wet. It ___ last night.
Past deduction: must have + past participle.
2. She ___ you. She was in Paris all last week.
You're sure it didn't happen: can't have seen.
3. I can't find my keys. I ___ them at work.
A possibility: might have + past participle (left).
4. He ___ the email. He replied to it an hour ago.
He replied, so you're sure he read it: must have read.
5. I'm not sure why he's late. He ___ the bus.
I'm not sure = possible: may have missed.
6. You ___ exhausted after such a long flight.
Almost sure about the past: must have been.
7. They ___ about the party. It was a complete surprise.
It was a surprise, so it's impossible they knew: couldn't have known.
8. Choose the correct sentence.
Must have + past participle (gone).
9. She looks exhausted. She ___ all night.
An activity in progress in the past: must have been + -ing.
10. The window is broken. Someone ___ it.
Must have + past participle (broken).
11. Which sentence means 'Maybe she didn't hear the phone'?
May not have + past participle = possibly didn't.
12. It ___ easy for you to move to a new country.
Can't have been: always have + past participle after the modal.
13. He ___ the answer. Nobody had told him, and he'd never studied chemistry.
You're sure he didn't know: can't have known.
14. You ___ been very proud when your daughter graduated.
Must have + past participle. Never write must of.
15. Is it possible that she left early? – Yes, she ___ left before we arrived.
Past possibility: could have + past participle.
16. Where's my umbrella? Someone ___ it by mistake.
Might have + past participle (taken).
