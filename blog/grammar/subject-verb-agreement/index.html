Subject-Verb Agreement

A1 · B1 · B2 · C1

The verb must agree with its subject: a singular subject takes a singular verb (She works), and a plural subject takes a plural verb (They work).

This is easy with short subjects, but harder with words like everyone, each and one of, and with long subjects. For group nouns like team and family, see Plural Nouns.

A1 · Beginner

He works, they work; there is, there are A1

SubjectbeOther verbs (present)
Iamwork
he / she / it, singular nounisworks
you / we / they, plural nounarework

  • My sister lives in Toronto. (singular noun + -s)
  • My parents live in Shiraz. (plural noun)
  • There is a park near my house. (singular)
  • There are three bedrooms in our flat. (plural)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ My brother work in a bank. → ✅ My brother works in a bank. (singular noun: -s)
  • ❌ The children is in the garden. → ✅ The children are in the garden. (plural: are)
  • ❌ There is two cafés on my street. → ✅ There are two cafés on my street. (plural: there are)

Practice: A1

Singular noun: works.
2. There <span class="gap">___</span> two supermarkets near my flat.
Plural: there are.
3. These days my cat <span class="gap">___</span> (sleep) on my bed every night.
Singular noun: + -s.

B1 · Intermediate

Everyone is, each student has, one of my friends is B1

SubjectVerbExample
everyone, everybody, someone, nobody, everything, nothingsingularEverybody knows him. Nothing is impossible.
each / every + singular nounsingularEvery student has a laptop.
each of + pluralsingularEach of the rooms has a TV.
one of + plural nounsingularOne of my sisters is a doctor.
both, many, few, severalpluralBoth my brothers are tall.

  • Everyone is welcome. (singular)
  • Every child needs love. (every + singular)
  • One of my friends works at the airport. (one of)
  • Each of the hotels has a pool. (each of)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Everybody are tired. → ✅ Everybody is tired. (everybody: singular)
  • ❌ One of my cousins live in Dubai. → ✅ One of my cousins lives in Dubai. (one of: singular)
  • ❌ Every students has a book. → ✅ Every student has a book. (every + singular noun)

Practice: B1

One of + singular verb.
6. Every room in this hotel <span class="gap">___</span> (have) a balcony. Ours looks out over the sea.
Every + singular noun + singular verb.

B2 · Upper-intermediate

Long subjects: the list of names is, either… or B2

PatternExample
noun + of + nounThe quality of the photos is excellent.
noun + relative clauseThe people who live next door are very kind.
as well as, along with, together withThe teacher, as well as the students, is here.
either… or / neither… norNeither my sister nor my parents are coming. Either Ali or Sara is coming.
the number of / a number ofThe number of students is rising. A number of students are absent.

  • The price of these shoes is too high. (head noun: price)
  • The students in my class come from ten countries. (head noun: students)
  • A number of people have complained. (a number of: plural)
  • Neither the manager nor the staff were happy. (nearest subject)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The quality of the products are excellent. → ✅ The quality of the products is excellent. (head noun: quality)
  • ❌ The number of tourists are increasing. → ✅ The number of tourists is increasing. (the number of: singular)
  • ❌ The manager, as well as his assistants, are coming. → ✅ The manager, as well as his assistants, is coming. (as well as: first subject)

Practice: B2

7. The price of houses in this city <span class="gap">___</span> very high.
Head noun: price (singular).
8. A number of students <span class="gap">___</span> asked for extra help.
A number of + plural verb.
9. The people who work in this office <span class="gap">___</span> (be) very friendly.
Head noun: people (plural).

C1 · Advanced

Amounts, what-clauses and none of them is or are C1

PatternExample
amounts as one unitTwo hours is too long to wait. Five kilometres is a long way.
what-clausesWhat I want is a holiday.
none of + pluralNone of my friends has / have a car. (singular = more formal)
most / half / all of + nounagrees with the noun: Most of the cake is gone. Most of the students are here.
more than one + singular nounsingular: More than one person has complained.

  • Fifty dollars is a lot for a T-shirt. (amount)
  • What surprised me was his attitude. (what-clause)
  • Most of the information is out of date. (agrees with *information*)
  • More than one student has failed. (more than one)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Fifty kilometres are a long way to drive. → ✅ Fifty kilometres is a long way to drive. (amount as one unit)
  • ❌ The majority of the work are done. → ✅ The majority of the work is done. (agrees with work)
  • ❌ More than one person have called. → ✅ More than one person has called. (more than one + singular verb)

Practice: C1

10. Ten kilometres <span class="gap">___</span> a long way to walk.
Amount as one unit: singular.
11. What we really need <span class="gap">___</span> a bigger kitchen.
What-clause + singular complement (a bigger kitchen): singular verb.
12. Half of the students <span class="gap">___</span> (be) absent today because of the flu.
Half of + plural noun: plural verb.
