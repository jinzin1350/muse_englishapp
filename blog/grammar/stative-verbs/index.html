Stative Verbs

Level: B1 · Free grammar guide

Stative verbs (state verbs) describe states, not actions: thoughts, feelings, senses, possession and existence.

We don't normally use them in continuous tenses: I know the answer. (not I'm knowing) She wants a new phone. (not She's wanting)

Common stative verbs

TypeVerbs
thinking and knowingknow, believe, understand, remember, forget, mean, realise, recognise, suppose, doubt
feelings and wantslike, love, hate, prefer, want, need, wish, mind
senses and appearanceseem, appear, look (= seem), sound, taste, smell, hear, see
possessionhave (= own), own, belong, possess, include, contain
being and measuringbe, exist, weigh, cost, depend, matter, consist

Verbs with two meanings

Some verbs describe a state with one meaning and an action with another. Only the action meaning can be continuous:

VerbState (simple)Action (continuous is OK)
thinkI think it's a good idea. (opinion)I'm thinking about my holiday. (considering)
haveShe has two cats. (own)She's having lunch / a shower / a good time.
seeI see what you mean. (understand)I'm seeing the doctor tomorrow. (meeting)
tasteThis soup tastes great.He's tasting the soup. (trying it)
lookYou look tired. (seem)Why are you looking at me? (directing your eyes)
beHe is rude. (his character)He's being rude. (his behaviour right now)
weighThe box weighs 5 kilos.She's weighing the flour.

In informal speech, you will sometimes hear state verbs in the continuous for emphasis or a temporary feeling: I'm loving this weather! This is fine in conversation, but use the simple form in exams and formal writing.

Stative verbs in other tenses

The same rule applies to the other continuous tenses: I've known him for years. (not I've been knowing) She had wanted that job for a long time. (not had been wanting)

Examples

  1. I don't understand this question. (thinking)
  2. This bag belongs to my sister. (possession)
  3. Do you remember her name? (thinking)
  4. He seems very tired today. (appearance)
  5. I'm thinking of moving to Montreal. (action meaning: considering)
  6. We're having dinner at the moment. Can I call you back? (action meaning: eating)
  7. This coffee tastes a bit strange. (senses)
  8. How much does this jacket cost? (measuring)
  9. You're being very quiet today. Is everything OK? (behaviour right now)
  10. I've known Sam since primary school. (present perfect simple)

Common mistakes

❌ I'm knowing the answer.

✅ I know the answer. (Know is a state verb.)

❌ This book is belonging to me.

✅ This book belongs to me. (Belong is a state verb.)

❌ She's wanting to leave.

✅ She wants to leave. (Want is a state verb.)

❌ I'm not believing you.

✅ I don't believe you. (Believe is a state verb.)

❌ I'm having a car.

✅ I have a car. (Have meaning own is a state verb.)

❌ What do you think about? You look worried.

✅ What are you thinking about? You look worried. (For the activity of thinking right now, use the continuous.)

❌ I've been knowing her for years.

✅ I've known her for years. (Use the present perfect simple with state verbs.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. I ___ what you mean.
Understand is a state verb: understand.
2. Shh! I ___ about the problem. Give me a minute.
The activity of thinking right now: am thinking.
3. She ___ a new laptop for university.
Need is a state verb, and she takes -s: needs.
4. This phone ___ to my brother.
Belong is a state verb: belongs.
5. We ___ lunch right now. Can I call you later?
Have lunch is an action: are having.
6. I ___ this is a good idea.
An opinion: think (state).
7. This soup ___ delicious.
Taste as a quality is a state: tastes.
8. My uncle ___ three cars.
Have = own: state, has.
9. He ___ very rude today. That's not like him.
Behaviour right now (not his character): is being.
10. Choose the correct sentence.
Believe is a state verb: I don't believe you.
11. I ___ her for ten years.
State verb + for ten years up to now: 've known.
12. How much ___ these shoes cost?
Cost is a state verb in the present simple. These shoes = they: do.
13. Look! The cat ___ at the bird.
Look at = an action happening now: is looking.
14. Which sentence is correct?
Weigh as a measurement is a state: weighs.
15. You ___ tired. Did you sleep badly?
Seem is a state verb: seem.
16. I ___ the doctor at 3 o'clock tomorrow.
See = meet (an arrangement): 'm seeing.
