Stative Verbs

A2 · B1 · B2 · C1

Stative verbs (state verbs) describe states, not actions: thoughts, feelings, senses, possession and existence.

We don't normally use them in continuous tenses: I know the answer. (not I'm knowing) She wants a new phone. (not She's wanting)

A2 · Elementary

Verbs we don't use in the continuous A2

TypeVerbs
thinking and knowingknow, believe, understand, remember, forget, mean, realise, recognise
feelings and wantslike, love, hate, prefer, want, need
senses and appearanceseem, sound, hear
possessionhave (= own), own, belong
other statescost, contain, depend, matter

  • I don't understand this question. (understand)
  • This bag belongs to my sister. (belong)
  • Do you remember her name? (remember)
  • How much does this jacket cost? (cost)
  • We're having dinner at the moment. (have = eat: action)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I'm knowing the answer. → ✅ I know the answer. (Know is a stative verb.)
  • ❌ This book is belonging to me. → ✅ This book belongs to me. (Belong is a stative verb.)
  • ❌ I'm having a car. → ✅ I have a car. (Have = own: a state.)

B1 · Intermediate

Verbs with two meanings: think, have, see, taste B1

VerbState (simple)Action (continuous is OK)
thinkI think it's a good idea. (opinion)I'm thinking about my holiday. (considering)
haveShe has two cats. (own)She's having lunch / a shower / a good time.
seeI see what you mean. (understand)I'm seeing the doctor tomorrow. (meeting)
taste / smellThis soup tastes great.He's tasting the soup. (trying it)
lookYou look tired. (seem)Why are you looking at me? (directing your eyes)
weighThe box weighs 5 kilos.She's weighing the flour.

  • I'm thinking of moving to Montreal. (think = consider)
  • What do you think of my new haircut? (think = opinion)
  • We're having a great time. (have = experience)
  • This coffee tastes a bit strange. (taste = state)
  • Look! The cat is looking at the bird. (look = action)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I'm thinking this is a good idea. → ✅ I think this is a good idea. (Think = opinion: a state.)
  • ❌ What do you think about? You look worried. → ✅ What are you thinking about? You look worried. (Think = consider: an action.)
  • ❌ This soup is tasting delicious. → ✅ This soup tastes delicious. (Taste = the flavour it has: a state.)

Practice: B1

Have lunch is an action: we're having.
7. This soup <span class="gap">___</span> delicious.
Taste = the flavour it has (a state): tastes.
8. I <span class="gap">___</span> the doctor at 3 o'clock tomorrow.
See = meet (an arrangement): I'm seeing.

B2 · Upper-intermediate

He's being rude: be in the continuous, and states in perfect tenses B2

  • You're being very quiet today. Is everything OK? (behaviour now)
  • Stop it! You're being silly. (behaviour now)
  • She's a very patient person. (character: simple)
  • I've known Sam since primary school. (state: perfect simple)
  • We had only had the car for a month when it broke down. (state: past perfect simple)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I've been knowing her for years. → ✅ I've known her for years. (Know is a state: perfect simple.)
  • ❌ He's being tall for his age. → ✅ He's tall for his age. (Being + adjective is only for behaviour.)

Practice: B2

9. He <span class="gap">___</span> very rude today. That's not like him.
Behaviour right now: is being + adjective.
10. I <span class="gap">___</span> her for ten years.
Know is a state: perfect simple, I've known.
11. Which sentence is correct?
Being + adjective is only for behaviour, not for height.

C1 · Advanced

State verbs in the continuous for emphasis or change C1

  • We're loving our new flat! (informal, temporary)
  • You're looking great these days. (around now)
  • I'm regretting that I said yes. (growing feeling)
  • He's considering early retirement. (consider: usually continuous)
  • We're hoping to finish by Friday. (softer, more tentative)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I'm believing you now. → ✅ I believe you now. (Believe is very rarely continuous.)
  • ❌ This box is consisting of three parts. → ✅ This box consists of three parts. (Consist of is never continuous.)

Practice: C1

12. Which sentence is informal but natural English?
I'm loving… is common in informal English for a temporary feeling.
13. What does I'm regretting my decision suggest?
The continuous shows a feeling that is growing.
14. Which verb is very rarely used in the continuous, even in informal English?
Own stays simple: She owns two cars.
