بیان هدف (to، in order to، so that)Infinitive of Purpose (to, for, so that, in order to)

To say why someone does something, we usually use to + base verb: I went to the bank to get some money. This is called the infinitive of purpose.

We can also use for + noun, so that + clause, and the more formal in order to / so as to. This page shows when to use each one.

A2 · B1 · B2 · C1

A2 · مقدماتی

To + verb: I went to the shop to buy bread A2

Use to + base verb to say why: I'm saving money to buy a car. She called to say sorry.

The infinitive with to answers the question Why?: Why did you go to the shop? ~ To buy some bread.

It usually comes after the main action: I went to the library to study. We use a knife to cut bread.

Don't use for + verb to give a purpose: I came here to learn English. Not for learn or for to learn.

  • I'm learning English to get a better job. (purpose)
  • She went to the airport to meet her sister. (purpose)
  • We turned on the heating to keep warm. (purpose)
  • Why did you call? ~ To ask about the party. (short answer)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I went to the shop for buy milk. → ✅ I went to the shop to buy milk. (purpose: to + verb)
  • ❌ He came to Canada for study. → ✅ He came to Canada to study. (to + verb)

تمرین سطح A2

1. I went to the post office ___ a parcel.
Purpose: to + verb.
2. Why are you going to the gym? ~ ___ fit.
Purpose: To get fit.
3. She went to the market ___ (buy) some fruit.
Purpose: to buy.

B1 · متوسط

To or for? For + noun, for + -ing B1

to + verb for an action: I went out to get some milk. for + noun: I went out for some milk. for + -ing for what a thing is used for: a knife for cutting bread.

PatternExample
to + verb (why someone does something)I went to the shop to buy bread.
for + nounI went to the shop for some bread. / Let's go out for dinner.
for + -ing (what a thing is used for)This tool is for opening bottles.

For + -ing describes the general function of a thing: A thermometer is used for measuring temperature. For a person's purpose, use to: I used the thermometer to measure the baby's temperature.

For + person + to + verb: There's a bus for students to get to campus.

  • Let's go out for a walk. (for + noun)
  • I went to the café for breakfast. (for + noun)
  • These scissors are for cutting paper. (function)
  • I bought some scissors to cut the paper. (person's purpose)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I went to the shop for buying bread. → ✅ I went to the shop to buy bread. (person's purpose: to)
  • ❌ We went out for eat. → ✅ We went out to eat. / We went out for dinner. (for + noun, to + verb)

So that + can / could / will / would B1

Use so that + subject + can / could / will / would for a purpose, especially when the subject changes: I'll write it down so that I don't forget. She spoke slowly so that we could understand.

So that introduces a full clause with a purpose. It's especially useful when the second subject is different: I gave him my number so that he could call me.

Use can / will for the present and future, and could / would for the past: I'm leaving early so that I can catch the train. I left early so that I could catch the train.

That is often dropped in speech: Speak louder so everyone can hear.

For a negative purpose, so that … won't / don't is often more natural than to: I wrote it down so that I wouldn't forget. (not to not forget in careful English)

  • Turn the light on so that I can see. (different subject (I))
  • I set two alarms so that I wouldn't oversleep. (negative purpose, past)
  • She saved money so that her children could go to university. (different subject)
  • Close the door so the cat doesn't get out. (informal: so)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I gave her my number so that she call me. → ✅ I gave her my number so that she could call me. (so that + modal)
  • ❌ He spoke slowly so that we can understand. (past) → ✅ He spoke slowly so that we could understand. (past: could)

تمرین سطح B1

4. I went to the bakery ___ some bread.
For + noun.
5. A corkscrew is a tool ___ wine bottles.
Function of a thing: for + -ing.
6. Let's go out ___ a drink after work. (one word)
For + noun.
7. I'm going to bed early ___ I can get up at five.
So that + clause.
8. She wore a hat so that the sun ___ burn her face.
Past, negative purpose: wouldn't.
9. I'll leave the key under the mat so that you ___ (can) get in.
Future purpose: so that you can.

B2 · بالاتر از متوسط

In order to, so as to (and the negative) B2

in order to / so as to + verb are more formal ways to give a purpose. For a negative purpose, use in order not to / so as not to (not not to alone).

In order to and so as to mean the same as to, but they're more formal and make the purpose clearer: We left early in order to avoid the traffic.

For a negative purpose, use in order not to or so as not to: We walked quietly so as not to wake the baby. Simple not to alone usually sounds wrong: not We walked quietly not to wake the baby.

In order to can go at the start of a sentence for emphasis: In order to apply, you must be over 18.

  • In order to get a visa, you need a letter from your employer. (formal)
  • I took notes so as to remember the details. (purpose)
  • He spoke quietly so as not to disturb anyone. (negative purpose)
  • She left early in order not to miss the train. (negative purpose)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I went out quietly not to wake anyone. → ✅ I went out quietly so as not to wake anyone. (negative purpose: so as not to / in order not to)
  • ❌ In order to pass, you need study hard. → ✅ In order to pass, you need to study hard. (need + to)

تمرین سطح B2

10. We turned off our phones ___ disturb the other guests.
Negative purpose: so as not to.
11. ___ to register, please complete the form below.
In order to at the start of a sentence.
12. She left early in order ___ (not / miss) the last train.
In order not to + verb.

C1 · پیشرفته

With a view to, with the aim of, for fear of, lest C1

Formal purpose expressions: with a view to / with the aim of + -ing, for fear of + -ing / for fear that, and very formal lest (= so that … not).

ExpressionMeaningExample
with a view to + -ingwith the intention ofThey bought land with a view to building a hotel.
with the aim of + -ingwith the purpose ofThe course was designed with the aim of improving skills.
for fear of + -ing / nounbecause you're afraid thatShe didn't speak for fear of making a mistake.
for fear that + clausein caseHe hid the letter for fear that someone would read it.
lest + (should) verbso that … not (very formal / literary)He wrote it down lest he (should) forget.

Note that to in with a view to is a preposition, so it's followed by -ing: with a view to buying (not to buy).

Lest is rare in modern speech; it appears in formal or literary writing. In everyday English, use so that … not or in case.

  • She's taking evening classes with a view to changing careers. (intention)
  • The law was passed with the aim of reducing pollution. (purpose)
  • I didn't mention it for fear of upsetting her. (afraid of the result)
  • They spoke in whispers lest they be overheard. (literary)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ They bought the house with a view to sell it. → ✅ They bought the house with a view to selling it. (with a view to + -ing)
  • ❌ He didn't move for fear to wake her. → ✅ He didn't move for fear of waking her. (for fear of + -ing)

تمرین سطح C1

13. The company is hiring more staff with a view ___ into new markets.
With a view to + -ing.
14. She didn't ask any questions for fear ___ stupid.
For fear of + -ing.
15. The programme was set up with the ___ of helping young people find work. (one word)
With the aim of + -ing.
