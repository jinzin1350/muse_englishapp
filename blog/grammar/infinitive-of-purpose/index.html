Infinitive of Purpose (I went out to buy milk)

Level: A2 · Free grammar guide

We use to + verb to say why we do something: I went to the shop to buy some milk. (Why did I go? To buy milk.)

This is called the infinitive of purpose.

To + verb

She's learning English to get a better job. I called him to say thank you.

It's also a short answer to Why?: Why are you saving money? – To buy a car.

Don't use for before a verb: I came here to see you (not for see or for to see).

To + verb or for + noun?

PurposeFormExample
an actionto + verbI went out to get some food.
a thingfor + nounI went out for some food.
what something is used forfor + -ingThis knife is for cutting bread.

In order to and so as to

In order to and so as to mean the same as to, but they're more formal: We left early in order to avoid the traffic.

For a negative purpose, use in order not to or so as not to: Speak quietly so as not to wake the baby. (not to not wake in formal English)

Examples

  1. I went to the bakery to buy some bread. (to + verb)
  2. She's saving money to buy a car. (purpose)
  3. Why are you learning English? – To travel. (short answer)
  4. We went to the café for a coffee. (for + noun)
  5. He called me to ask about the meeting. (to + verb)
  6. I use this app to learn new words. (to + verb)
  7. This bag is for carrying my laptop. (for + -ing: use)
  8. They studied hard in order to pass the exam. (formal)
  9. Close the window so as not to let the cold in. (negative purpose)
  10. I'm going to the station to meet my aunt. (to + verb)

Common mistakes

❌ I came here for see you.

✅ I came here to see you. (Purpose + verb: to, not for.)

❌ I went to the shop for to buy milk.

✅ I went to the shop to buy milk. (Don't use for before to.)

❌ She went out to some bread.

✅ She went out for some bread. / She went out to buy some bread. (Before a noun, use for.)

❌ Why did you go? – For buy food.

✅ To buy food. (Short answer to Why?: to + verb.)

❌ We left early in order to not miss the train.

✅ We left early in order not to miss the train. (In formal English: in order not to.)
