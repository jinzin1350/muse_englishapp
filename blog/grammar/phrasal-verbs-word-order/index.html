Phrasal Verbs: Word Order (turn it off, look after them)

Level: B2 · Free grammar guide

A phrasal verb is a verb + a particle (up, off, out, after…): turn off, look after, give up.

Where the object goes depends on the type of phrasal verb. The most important rule: with separable phrasal verbs, a pronoun always goes in the middle: Turn it off (not Turn off it).

Four types

TypeWord orderExamples
No objectverb + particleThe car broke down. I woke up at 6.
Separablenoun: before or after the particle; pronoun: in the middleTurn the TV off / Turn off the TV. Turn it off.
Inseparableobject always after the particleLook after the kids. Look after them.
Three-partobject always at the endI'm looking forward to the trip. We've run out of milk.

Common separable phrasal verbs

turn on/off/up/down, put on, take off, pick up, give back, throw away, fill in, look up, put off (= postpone), switch off, write down, try on, wake (someone) up

With a long object, put it after the particle: Fill in the form that I sent you yesterday.

Common inseparable and three-part verbs

  • Inseparable: look after, look for, come across, run into, get over, deal with, take after
  • Three-part: look forward to, run out of, get on with, put up with, come up with, catch up with

A dictionary shows the type: turn sth off (separable) vs look after sb (inseparable).

Examples

  1. It's cold. Put your coat on. (separable, noun in the middle)
  2. Where's my coat? I want to put it on. (pronoun in the middle)
  3. Can you pick me up at 7? (separable)
  4. Don't throw those boxes away. I need them. (separable)
  5. Who's going to look after the dog? (inseparable)
  6. I ran into an old friend yesterday. (inseparable)
  7. We've run out of coffee. (three-part)
  8. I can't put up with this noise. (three-part)
  9. The plane took off on time. (no object)
  10. The meeting has been put off until Friday. (passive)

Common mistakes

❌ Turn off it, please.

✅ Turn it off, please. (Separable: a pronoun goes in the middle.)

❌ Can you pick up me at 8?

✅ Can you pick me up at 8? (Separable: a pronoun goes in the middle.)

❌ My grandmother looks us after.

✅ My grandmother looks after us. (Look after is inseparable.)

❌ I'm looking forward the weekend.

✅ I'm looking forward to the weekend. (Three-part verb: look forward to.)

❌ We've run out milk.

✅ We've run out of milk. (Three-part verb: run out of.)

❌ I looked the word up it.

✅ I looked the word up. / I looked it up. (Use the noun or the pronoun, not both.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. The music is too loud. Can you turn ___?
Separable + pronoun: turn it down.
2. I've lost my keys. Can you help me look ___?
Look for is inseparable: look for them.
3. This form is important. Please fill ___ carefully.
Separable + pronoun: fill it in.
4. My grandmother looks ___ when my parents are at work.
Look after is inseparable: look after us.
5. Your shoes are dirty. Take ___ before you come in.
Separable + pronoun: take them off.
6. We've run ___ milk. Can you buy some?
Three-part verb: run out of.
7. I'm really looking ___ the holidays.
Three-part verb: look forward to.
8. If you don't know the word, look ___ in a dictionary.
Separable + pronoun: look it up.
9. I ran ___ my old teacher at the supermarket yesterday.
Run into (= meet by chance) is inseparable: ran into my old teacher.
10. Choose the correct sentence.
Separable + pronoun: Turn it off.
11. How do you get ___ your new boss?
Three-part verb: get on with sb.
12. Don't throw those letters away! Give ___ to me.
Separable + pronoun: give them back.
13. The meeting was put ___ until next week.
Passive: was put off (= postponed).
14. I can't put ___ this noise any longer!
Three-part verb: put up with (= tolerate).
15. I picked my sister ___ from the airport.
Separable with a noun in the middle: picked my sister up.
16. My car broke ___ on the motorway.
No object: broke down.
