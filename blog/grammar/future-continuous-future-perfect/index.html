Future Continuous and Future Perfect

Level: B2 · Free grammar guide

The future continuous (will be + -ing) describes an action in progress at a time in the future: This time tomorrow, I'll be flying to Rome.

The future perfect (will have + past participle) describes an action completed before a time in the future: By 6 p.m., I'll have finished the report.

Form

TenseFormExample
Future continuouswill be + -ingI'll be working at 10 tomorrow.
Future perfectwill have + past participleShe'll have left by then.
Future perfect continuouswill have been + -ingBy June, I'll have been living here for five years.

Negatives: I won't be working. She won't have finished. Questions: Will you be using the car? Will they have arrived by 8?

Future continuous: uses

  • An action in progress at a future time: Don't call at 8. I'll be putting the kids to bed.
  • Something that will happen as part of the normal plan: I'll be seeing Tom at work tomorrow, so I can give him the message.
  • Polite questions about someone's plans: Will you be using the car tonight? (more polite than Will you use…?)

Future perfect: uses

Use it for something that will be finished before a future time, often with by (= not later than) or by the time: By 2030, scientists will have found a cure, I hope. By the time you wake up, we'll have left.

Use the future perfect continuous to say how long an action will have continued at a future time: By the end of the month, she'll have been working here for 20 years.

After by the time, when, before and until, use a present tense for the future: By the time you get home (not will get). See Future Time Clauses.

Examples

  1. This time next week, we'll be lying on a beach. (in progress)
  2. I'll be waiting for you at the station at 6. (in progress)
  3. Will you be coming to the meeting? (polite question)
  4. I'll be passing the post office anyway, so I can post your letter. (normal plan)
  5. By the time we get there, the film will have started. (completed before)
  6. I'll have finished this book by Friday. (by + time)
  7. They won't have arrived by 8. Their flight is late. (negative)
  8. Will you have finished by lunchtime? (question)
  9. Next month, I'll have been learning English for three years. (duration)
  10. By the time she retires, she'll have taught thousands of students. (total by a future time)

Common mistakes

❌ This time tomorrow I will lie on the beach.

✅ This time tomorrow I'll be lying on the beach. (In progress at a future time: future continuous.)

❌ By next year I will finish my degree. (completed before then)

✅ By next year I'll have finished my degree. (Completed before a future time: future perfect is clearest.)

❌ By the time you will arrive, we'll have eaten.

✅ By the time you arrive, we'll have eaten. (After by the time, use the present simple.)

❌ I will have finish soon.

✅ I will have finished soon. (Will have + past participle.)

❌ Next June I'll work here for five years.

✅ Next June I'll have worked / I'll have been working here for five years. (Duration up to a future time: future perfect (continuous).)
