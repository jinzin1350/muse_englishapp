زمان حال کامل (ماضی نقلی)Present Perfect (I have done)

We use the present perfect (have / has + past participle) to connect the past with now: I've lost my keys (I can't find them now). She's been to Japan three times (in her life, up to now).

The important thing is not when it happened but that it matters now. If you say when (yesterday, in 2020), use the past simple. See Present Perfect vs Past Simple.

A2 · مقدماتی

Form: have / has + past participle A2

have / has + past participle: I've finished. She hasn't called. Have you eaten?

I / you / we / theyhe / she / it
PositiveI have (I've) finished.She has (she's) finished.
NegativeI haven't finished.She hasn't finished.
QuestionHave you finished?Has she finished?
Short answersYes, I have. / No, I haven't.Yes, she has. / No, she hasn't.

The past participle of regular verbs ends in -ed, like the past simple: work → worked, play → played. Irregular verbs have their own form:

VerbPast simplePast participle
bewas / werebeen
gowentgone / been
dodiddone
seesawseen
eatateeaten
writewrotewritten
taketooktaken
havehadhad

He's can mean he is or he has. If the next word is a past participle, it's has: He's gone = he has gone. He's going = he is going.

  • We've bought a new car. (have + bought)
  • She hasn't done her homework. (negative)
  • Have you seen my phone? (question)
  • My parents have never eaten sushi. (irregular participle)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ She have finished. → ✅ She has finished. (she → has)
  • ❌ I have went to Spain. → ✅ I have been to Spain. (Use the past participle (been / gone), not the past simple.)
  • ❌ Have you ate breakfast? → ✅ Have you eaten breakfast? (eat → eaten)

Experiences: ever, never, before A2

Use the present perfect for life experiences up to now, without saying when: Have you ever been to Canada? I've never tried sushi.

When we talk about experiences in our life (at any time up to now), we use the present perfect. We don't say when:

  • I've been to Italy three times.
  • Have you ever ridden a horse? (ever = at any time in your life)
  • She's never seen snow. (never = at no time)
  • I've met him before.

Ever is used in questions; never makes the sentence negative (don't add not): I've never been there. Not I haven't never been there.

If you then give details (when, where, how), change to the past simple: I've been to Rome. I went there in 2019 with my family.

Been to = went and came back (an experience). I've been to Paris = I visited Paris at some time in my life.

  • Have you ever eaten Persian food? (ever: at any time)
  • I've never flown in a helicopter. (never)
  • We've seen this film before. (before)
  • How many countries have you visited? (how many: up to now)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I have never not been to Asia. → ✅ I have never been to Asia. (never already makes it negative)
  • ❌ Have you ever went to Rome? → ✅ Have you ever been to Rome? (Use the past participle: been.)
  • ❌ I have been to London in 2019. → ✅ I went to London in 2019. (With a past time: past simple)

Just, already and yet A2

just = a short time ago; already = sooner than expected; yet = up to now (in questions and negatives, at the end).

WordMeaningPositionExample
justa short time agobefore the participleI've just had lunch.
alreadybefore now, maybe sooner than expectedbefore the participle (or at the end)She's already left.
yetup to now (expected to happen)at the end; questions and negativesHave you finished yet? I haven't finished yet.

Yet in a question asks whether something we expect has happened. In a negative it means it hasn't happened, but we expect it to: The bus hasn't come yet.

In American English, the past simple is also common with these words: I just ate. Did you finish yet? In British English, the present perfect is the usual choice. Both are correct.

  • Your taxi's just arrived. (a moment ago)
  • I've already paid the bill. (sooner than you expected)
  • Has the film started yet? (question with yet)
  • We haven't decided yet. (negative with yet)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I've finished yet. → ✅ I've already finished. (yet is for questions and negatives)
  • ❌ Have you yet finished? → ✅ Have you finished yet? (yet goes at the end)
  • ❌ I've eaten just. → ✅ I've just eaten. (just goes before the participle)

You → Have you…?

It's still morning: have written (American English may also use wrote).

Already = before now.

B1 · متوسط

Been or gone? B1

She's gone to Paris = she's there now. She's been to Paris = she went and came back.

SentenceMeaning
Sara has gone to the bank.She is at the bank now (or on the way).
Sara has been to the bank.She went to the bank and she's back now.
Sara has been to Japan.She visited Japan at some time in her life.

Use gone when the person is still away. Use been when the person has returned, and for experiences.

Where have you been? = you were away and now you're back. Where has he gone? = he isn't here now.

  • My parents have gone to Mashhad for a week. (they're there now)
  • Have you ever been to Mashhad? (experience)
  • Where have you been? I've been waiting for an hour! (you're back now)
  • Ali isn't here. He's gone home. (he's at home now)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I've gone to Rome twice. → ✅ I've been to Rome twice. (An experience: been.)
  • ❌ Where's Reza? ~ He's been to the gym. (he's still there) → ✅ Where's Reza? ~ He's gone to the gym. (He's still there: gone.)

A past action with a result now, and news B1

The present perfect shows that a past action has a result now (I've lost my keys = I don't have them) and gives new information: The prime minister has resigned.

We often use the present perfect when a past action is important because of its result now:

  • I've lost my keys. (I can't get in now.)
  • Somebody has broken the window. (It's broken now.)
  • She's had a haircut. (Her hair looks different now.)

We also use it to give news: The police have arrested a man in connection with the robbery. When we add details (when, how), we usually switch to the past simple: He was arrested at his home last night.

Compare: I've lost my keys (I still haven't found them) and I lost my keys yesterday, but I found them later (finished story). See Present Perfect vs Past Simple.

  • The lift has broken down again. We'll have to use the stairs. (result now)
  • I've cut my finger. It's bleeding. (result now)
  • Scientists have discovered a new species of frog. (news)
  • Prices have gone up. Everything is more expensive. (change with a result now)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ Prices have went up. → ✅ Prices have gone up. (Past participle: gone.)
  • ❌ The president has resigned yesterday. → ✅ The president resigned yesterday. (With yesterday: past simple.)

Today, this week, recently, so far B1

Use the present perfect with periods of time that are not finished: today, this week, this year, recently, lately, so far, up to now.

When the time period includes now, use the present perfect:

  • I've drunk three coffees today. (Today isn't finished.)
  • We haven't had any rain this month.
  • She's been very busy recently / lately.
  • So far, 200 people have signed the letter.

Compare: I've seen her this morning (it's still morning) and I saw her this morning (it's now afternoon, the morning is finished).

Recently can go with both tenses: I've recently started yoga / I recently started yoga. Lately usually goes with the present perfect.

  • I haven't seen Ali this week. (this week isn't finished)
  • It's rained a lot this year. (this year isn't finished)
  • Have you been to the cinema lately? (lately)
  • Up to now, we haven't had any problems. (up to now)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I've seen her yesterday. → ✅ I saw her yesterday. (yesterday is finished: past simple.)
  • ❌ Lately I watched a lot of films. → ✅ Lately I've watched a lot of films. (lately → present perfect)

It's the first time… and the best… I've ever… B1

After It's the first / second time and after a superlative + ever, use the present perfect: It's the first time I've driven. It's the best film I've ever seen.

When we talk about now, we use the present perfect after these patterns:

  • It's the first time I've eaten sushi. Not It's the first time I eat sushi.
  • This is the third time he's been late this week.
  • She's the nicest person I've ever met.
  • That was the most boring film I've ever seen.

For a past situation, use the past perfect: It was the first time I'd ever flown. See Past Perfect.

Ever is optional with superlatives, but very common: the best meal I've (ever) had.

  • This is the first time I've visited Canada. (first time + present perfect)
  • It's the second time you've asked me that today. (second time)
  • This is the best coffee I've ever tasted. (superlative + ever)
  • He's the funniest person I've ever known. (superlative)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ It's the first time I see snow. → ✅ It's the first time I've seen snow. (first time + present perfect)
  • ❌ This is the best book I ever read. → ✅ This is the best book I've ever read. (superlative + present perfect (British English))

C1 · پیشرفته

The present perfect in news and reports C1

News and reports often introduce a topic with the present perfect and then give the details in the past simple: A storm has hit the coast. It arrived at 3 am and destroyed…

In news reports, the first sentence often uses the present perfect to present the event as new and relevant now. The following sentences, which give details of time, place and manner, use the past simple:

Firefighters have rescued a family of five from a burning building in Tabriz. The fire started in the kitchen just after midnight, and the family escaped through a window.

In academic and formal writing, the present perfect introduces recent research or a general state of knowledge, and the past simple reports specific studies: Several studies have shown a link between sleep and memory. In 2019, a team at Oxford found that…

Headlines often drop have / has and use the past participle or the present simple: Fire destroys factory. Missing child found safe.

Once you mention a specific past time, place or event, the past simple usually takes over. Going back to the present perfect is possible to sum up: So far, the fire has caused damage worth millions.

  • The central bank has raised interest rates. The decision was announced this morning. (news, then details)
  • Researchers have developed a new test for the disease. They tested it on 500 patients. (report, then details)
  • Rescue teams have found two more survivors. (news with a result now)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ A plane has crashed in the mountains. It has crashed at 6 am. → ✅ A plane has crashed in the mountains. It crashed at 6 am. (With the time detail: past simple.)
  • ❌ Smith has published a famous study in 1998. → ✅ Smith published a famous study in 1998. (A specific date: past simple.)

When I've finished…: the present perfect for the future C1

In time clauses about the future, the present perfect shows that one action will be complete before another: I'll call you when I've finished.

After when, after, as soon as, until, once, before + a future meaning, we don't use will. We use the present simple, or the present perfect to stress that the first action will be finished first:

  • I'll call you when I've finished the report. (first finish, then call)
  • You can't leave until you've answered all the questions.
  • Once you've tried it, you'll love it.

Often both tenses are possible with little difference: I'll call you when I get home / when I've got home. The present perfect is clearer when the first action takes time and must be completed.

Don't use the present perfect when the two actions happen at the same time: When I see her, I'll tell her. Not When I've seen her, I'll tell her (you tell her while you see her).

See Future Time Clauses for when, if, as soon as with the future.

  • As soon as I've saved enough money, I'm going to buy a car. (first save, then buy)
  • Don't send the email until you've checked it. (complete the check first)
  • After we've eaten, we'll go for a walk. (first eat)
  • When I arrive, I'll call you. (same time: present simple)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I'll call you when I will finish. → ✅ I'll call you when I've finished. (No will in the time clause.)
  • ❌ When I've seen him tomorrow, I'll give him the book. → ✅ When I see him tomorrow, I'll give him the book. (Same time: present simple.)

تمرین سطح C1
