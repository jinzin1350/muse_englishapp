Present Perfect (I have done)

A2 · B1 · C1

We use the present perfect (have / has + past participle) to connect the past with now: I've lost my keys (I can't find them now). She's been to Japan three times (in her life, up to now).

The important thing is not when it happened but that it matters now. If you say when (yesterday, in 2020), use the past simple. See Present Perfect vs Past Simple.

  • We've bought a new car. (have + bought)
  • She hasn't done her homework. (negative)
  • Have you seen my phone? (question)
  • My parents have never eaten sushi. (irregular participle)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ She have finished. → ✅ She has finished. (she → has)
  • ❌ I have went to Spain. → ✅ I have been to Spain. (Use the past participle (been / gone), not the past simple.)
  • ❌ Have you ate breakfast? → ✅ Have you eaten breakfast? (eat → eaten)

Experiences: ever, never, before A2

  • I've been to Italy three times.
  • Have you ever ridden a horse? (ever = at any time in your life)
  • She's never seen snow. (never = at no time)
  • I've met him before.

  • Have you ever eaten Persian food? (ever: at any time)
  • I've never flown in a helicopter. (never)
  • We've seen this film before. (before)
  • How many countries have you visited? (how many: up to now)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I have never not been to Asia. → ✅ I have never been to Asia. (never already makes it negative)
  • ❌ Have you ever went to Rome? → ✅ Have you ever been to Rome? (Use the past participle: been.)
  • ❌ I have been to London in 2019. → ✅ I went to London in 2019. (With a past time: past simple)

Just, already and yet A2

WordMeaningPositionExample

yetup to now (expected to happen)at the end; questions and negativesHave you finished yet? I haven't finished yet.

  • Your taxi's just arrived. (a moment ago)
  • I've already paid the bill. (sooner than you expected)
  • Has the film started yet? (question with yet)
  • We haven't decided yet. (negative with yet)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I've finished yet. → ✅ I've already finished. (yet is for questions and negatives)
  • ❌ Have you yet finished? → ✅ Have you finished yet? (yet goes at the end)
  • ❌ I've eaten just. → ✅ I've just eaten. (just goes before the participle)

B1 · Intermediate

Been or gone? B1

SentenceMeaning
Sara has gone to the bank.She is at the bank now (or on the way).
Sara has been to the bank.She went to the bank and she's back now.
Sara has been to Japan.She visited Japan at some time in her life.

  • My parents have gone to Mashhad for a week. (they're there now)
  • Have you ever been to Mashhad? (experience)
  • Where have you been? I've been waiting for an hour! (you're back now)
  • Ali isn't here. He's gone home. (he's at home now)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I've gone to Rome twice. → ✅ I've been to Rome twice. (An experience: been.)
  • ❌ Where's Reza? ~ He's been to the gym. (he's still there) → ✅ Where's Reza? ~ He's gone to the gym. (He's still there: gone.)

A past action with a result now, and news B1

  • I've lost my keys. (I can't get in now.)
  • Somebody has broken the window. (It's broken now.)
  • She's had a haircut. (Her hair looks different now.)

  • The lift has broken down again. We'll have to use the stairs. (result now)
  • I've cut my finger. It's bleeding. (result now)
  • Scientists have discovered a new species of frog. (news)
  • Prices have gone up. Everything is more expensive. (change with a result now)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Prices have went up. → ✅ Prices have gone up. (Past participle: gone.)
  • ❌ The president has resigned yesterday. → ✅ The president resigned yesterday. (With yesterday: past simple.)

Today, this week, recently, so far B1

  • I've drunk three coffees today. (Today isn't finished.)
  • We haven't had any rain this month.
  • She's been very busy recently / lately.
  • So far, 200 people have signed the letter.

  • I haven't seen Ali this week. (this week isn't finished)
  • It's rained a lot this year. (this year isn't finished)
  • Have you been to the cinema lately? (lately)
  • Up to now, we haven't had any problems. (up to now)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I've seen her yesterday. → ✅ I saw her yesterday. (yesterday is finished: past simple.)
  • ❌ Lately I watched a lot of films. → ✅ Lately I've watched a lot of films. (lately → present perfect)

It's the first time… and the best… I've ever… B1

  • It's the first time I've eaten sushi. Not It's the first time I eat sushi.
  • This is the third time he's been late this week.
  • She's the nicest person I've ever met.
  • That was the most boring film I've ever seen.

  • This is the first time I've visited Canada. (first time + present perfect)
  • It's the second time you've asked me that today. (second time)
  • This is the best coffee I've ever tasted. (superlative + ever)
  • He's the funniest person I've ever known. (superlative)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ It's the first time I see snow. → ✅ It's the first time I've seen snow. (first time + present perfect)
  • ❌ This is the best book I ever read. → ✅ This is the best book I've ever read. (superlative + present perfect (British English))

C1 · Advanced

The present perfect in news and reports C1

  • The central bank has raised interest rates. The decision was announced this morning. (news, then details)
  • Researchers have developed a new test for the disease. They tested it on 500 patients. (report, then details)
  • Rescue teams have found two more survivors. (news with a result now)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ A plane has crashed in the mountains. It has crashed at 6 am. → ✅ A plane has crashed in the mountains. It crashed at 6 am. (With the time detail: past simple.)
  • ❌ Smith has published a famous study in 1998. → ✅ Smith published a famous study in 1998. (A specific date: past simple.)

When I've finished…: the present perfect for the future C1

  • I'll call you when I've finished the report. (first finish, then call)
  • You can't leave until you've answered all the questions.
  • Once you've tried it, you'll love it.

  • As soon as I've saved enough money, I'm going to buy a car. (first save, then buy)
  • Don't send the email until you've checked it. (complete the check first)
  • After we've eaten, we'll go for a walk. (first eat)
  • When I arrive, I'll call you. (same time: present simple)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I'll call you when I will finish. → ✅ I'll call you when I've finished. (No will in the time clause.)
  • ❌ When I've seen him tomorrow, I'll give him the book. → ✅ When I see him tomorrow, I'll give him the book. (Same time: present simple.)

Practice: C1

22. Police <span class="gap">___</span> a man after a robbery in the city centre. He was arrested at his home on Tuesday.
The news is introduced with the present perfect; the details follow in the past simple.
23. In an essay: A number of researchers <span class="gap">___</span> this question in recent years.
A general state of recent research: present perfect.
24. A strong earthquake has shaken parts of Japan. It <span class="gap">___</span> (strike) at 4.15 local time.
A time detail: past simple struck.
25. You can watch TV after you <span class="gap">___</span> your homework.
Time clause, first action complete: have done.
26. I'll lend you the book when I <span class="gap">___</span> it.
The reading must be complete first: have read.
27. Once you <span class="gap">___</span> (fill) in the form, give it to the receptionist.
Once + present perfect (or present simple): no will.
