Comparatives and Superlatives

Level: A2 · Free grammar guide

We use a comparative to compare two things: My brother is taller than me. We use a superlative to say one thing is more than all the others in a group: He's the tallest person in our family.

Short adjectives add -er / -est (cheap, cheaper, the cheapest). Longer adjectives use more / the most (more expensive, the most expensive).

How to form comparatives and superlatives

AdjectiveRuleComparativeSuperlative
One syllableadd -er / -estcheap → cheaperthe cheapest
One syllable ending in -eadd -r / -stnice → nicerthe nicest
One vowel + one consonantdouble the last letterbig → biggerthe biggest
Two syllables ending in -yy → -ier / -iesteasy → easierthe easiest
Two or more syllablesmore / the mostexpensive → more expensivethe most expensive

More examples of doubling: hot → hotter, thin → thinner, sad → sadder, wet → wetter. We don't double w: new → newer, slow → slower.

Some two-syllable adjectives can use both forms: quiet → quieter / more quiet, simple → simpler / more simple, clever → cleverer / more clever. Both are correct; the -er form is more common.

Irregular comparatives and superlatives

AdjectiveComparativeSuperlative
goodbetterthe best
badworsethe worst
farfurther / fartherthe furthest / the farthest
much / manymorethe most
littlelessthe least

Well (= healthy) also becomes better: I was ill last week, but I feel better now.

How to use them in a sentence

  • Comparative + than: Trains are faster than buses.
  • the + superlative: It's the oldest building in the city. Use in with places and groups (in the world, in my class) and of with time periods and numbers (of the year, of the three).
  • Superlative + present perfect + ever: It's the best film I've ever seen.
  • less / the least is the opposite of more / the most: This phone is less expensive than that one.
  • as … as = the same; not as … as = less: My sister is as tall as me. The second film wasn't as good as the first.

To make a comparative stronger, use much, a lot or far: much cheaper, a lot more expensive, far better. Don't use very with comparatives.

Examples

  1. My new flat is bigger than my old one. (big → bigger: double the g)
  2. This is the most beautiful beach I've ever seen. (long adjective: the most)
  3. Today is hotter than yesterday. (hot → hotter)
  4. She's the best student in our class. (good → the best; in + group)
  5. English is easier for me than French. (easy → easier)
  6. My laptop is more expensive than yours, but it's slower. (more + long adjective; slow → slower)
  7. That was the worst meal of my life. (bad → the worst; of + time)
  8. The bus is cheaper than the train, but it's less comfortable. (less = the opposite of more)
  9. My brother isn't as tall as my sister. (not as … as)
  10. The Nile is one of the longest rivers in the world. (one of the + superlative + plural noun)

Common mistakes

❌ She is more tall than me.

✅ She is taller than me. (One-syllable adjectives take -er.)

❌ This book is more better.

✅ This book is better. (Better is already a comparative. Don't add more.)

❌ He is the most happiest person I know.

✅ He is the happiest person I know. (Use -est or most, not both.)

❌ My car is bigger then yours.

✅ My car is bigger than yours. (Use than, not then.)

❌ It's the biggest city of the world.

✅ It's the biggest city in the world. (Use in with places.)

❌ This test is very easier.

✅ This test is much easier. (Use much, a lot or far with comparatives, not very.)

❌ The weather is badder today.

✅ The weather is worse today. (Bad is irregular: bad → worse → the worst.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. My bag is ___ than yours.
Heavy ends in consonant + y: y → -ier. Use the comparative with than.
2. Everest is ___ mountain in the world.
We compare one mountain with all the others, so use the superlative: the highest.
3. This is ___ restaurant in town.
Good is irregular: good → better → the best.
4. Flying is ___ than driving, but it's more expensive.
Fast has one syllable: add -er.
5. This exam was ___ than the last one. I failed it!
Bad is irregular: bad → worse → the worst.
6. My new job is ___ than my old one.
Long adjectives use more + adjective + than.
7. I feel ___ today than yesterday. My cold has almost gone.
Well (= healthy) and good both become better.
8. Yesterday was ___ day of the year so far.
Hot ends in one vowel + one consonant, so double the t: the hottest.
9. This sofa is ___ comfortable than the old one.
Make a comparative stronger with much: much more comfortable. Don't use very.
10. Your phone is ___ mine.
New → newer (we don't double w). Use than, not then.
11. Choose the correct sentence.
Long adjective: the most + adjective.
12. The film wasn't ___ the book.
Use not as + adjective + as.
13. Is London ___ Paris?
Big → bigger (double the g) + than.
14. Neptune is ___ planet from the sun.
Far is irregular: far → further/farther → the furthest/the farthest.
15. It was ___ film I've ever seen.
Superlative + I've ever: funny → the funniest. Don't use most and -est together.
16. Train tickets are ___ than plane tickets.
Less + adjective + than is the opposite of more … than.
