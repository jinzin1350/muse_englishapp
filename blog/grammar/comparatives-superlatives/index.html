Comparatives and Superlatives

A2 · B1 · B2 · C1

Use comparatives to compare two things (Toronto is bigger than Ottawa) and superlatives to say which is the most in a group (It's the biggest city in Canada).

This page also covers as… as and how to make a comparison stronger or weaker (much bigger, a bit cheaper, by far the best).

A2 · Elementary

Bigger, more expensive, the biggest, the most expensive A2

AdjectiveComparativeSuperlative
one syllable: cheap, tallcheaper, tallerthe cheapest, the tallest
ending in -e: nice, largenicer, largerthe nicest, the largest
one vowel + one consonant: big, hotbigger, hotterthe biggest, the hottest
two syllables ending in -y: happy, easyhappier, easierthe happiest, the easiest
two or more syllables: modern, expensivemore modern, more expensivethe most modern, the most expensive

  • My flat is smaller than yours. (-er + than)
  • This is the most beautiful beach in Iran. (the most)
  • Today is hotter than yesterday. (double consonant)
  • Maths is easier than physics for me. (y → ier)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ She is more tall than her sister. → ✅ She is taller than her sister. (short adjective: -er)
  • ❌ This is the most cheap hotel. → ✅ This is the cheapest hotel. (short adjective: the -est)
  • ❌ He's older that me. → ✅ He's older than me. (than)
  • ❌ It's the biggest city of the world. → ✅ It's the biggest city in the world. (superlative + in (a place))

Good, better, best; bad, worse, worst; far, further A2

Adjective / adverbComparativeSuperlative
good / wellbetterthe best
bad / badlyworsethe worst
farfarther / furtherthe farthest / the furthest
much / manymorethe most
little (amount)lessthe least

  • Your English is better than mine. (good → better)
  • That was the worst meal I've ever had. (bad → the worst)
  • The station is further than I thought. (far)
  • She earns more money than me. (much → more)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ This film is more good than the book. → ✅ This film is better than the book. (good → better)
  • ❌ It was the baddest day of my life. → ✅ It was the worst day of my life. (bad → the worst)
  • ❌ I feel more bad today. → ✅ I feel worse today. (bad → worse)

B1 · Intermediate

As… as, not as… as, the same as B1

PatternExample
as… as (equal)My car is as fast as yours.
not as / not so… as (less)This hotel isn't as expensive as the other one.
the same (+ noun) asYour phone is the same as mine. She's the same age as me.
as much / as many… asI don't have as much time as you.
twice / three times as… asTheir house is twice as big as ours.

  • The new film is as good as the first one. (equal)
  • Ottawa isn't as big as Toronto. (less)
  • My bag is the same as yours. (the same as)
  • This laptop is twice as expensive as that one. (twice as… as)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ She's as taller as me. → ✅ She's as tall as me. (as + adjective + as)
  • ❌ My phone is the same like yours. → ✅ My phone is the same as yours. (the same as)
  • ❌ It isn't as cold than yesterday. → ✅ It isn't as cold as yesterday. (as… as)

Much bigger, a bit cheaper, by far the best B1

StrengthComparativeSuperlative
a big differencemuch / a lot / far cheaperby far the cheapest
a small differencea bit / a little / slightly cheaper
no differenceno cheaper (formal), not any cheaper
evenIt's even colder today. (and it was already cold)

  • The train is much faster than the bus. (much)
  • Could you speak a bit more slowly? (a bit)
  • This is by far the best café in town. (by far)
  • Today is even colder than yesterday. (even)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ My new flat is very bigger. → ✅ My new flat is much bigger. (not very + comparative)
  • ❌ This is far the best hotel. → ✅ This is by far the best hotel. (by far + superlative)

B2 · Upper-intermediate

The more, the better; colder and colder B2

PatternExample
the… the…The sooner we leave, the better.
The more I read, the more I learn.
-er and -erThe days are getting longer and longer.
more and more + adjectiveLife is becoming more and more expensive.
less and lessI see him less and less.

  • The more you sleep, the better you feel. (the… the…)
  • The faster you drive, the more dangerous it is. (the… the…)
  • My son is getting taller and taller. (-er and -er)
  • Online shopping is becoming more and more popular. (more and more)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ More you practise, better you get. → ✅ The more you practise, the better you get. (the… the…)
  • ❌ It's getting more and more cold. → ✅ It's getting colder and colder. (short adjective: -er and -er)
  • ❌ Prices are getting more expensive and more expensive. → ✅ Prices are getting more and more expensive. (more and more + long adjective)

Practice: B2

Continuing change: colder and colder.

The more…, the more…

C1 · Advanced

Superior to, nowhere near as, twice the size of, the latter C1

PatternExample
superior / inferior / preferable toThis model is superior to the old one. (not superior than)
nowhere near / not nearly as… asThe sequel is nowhere near as good as the original.
twice / three times the + noun + ofTheir house is twice the size of ours.
the former / the latterI visited Rome and Paris. The latter was my favourite. (= Paris)
more + adjective + than + adjectiveHe's more lucky than clever. (two qualities of one person)

  • Walking is preferable to driving in the city centre. (preferable to)
  • The new version is nowhere near as fast as we hoped. (nowhere near as)
  • The new stadium is twice the size of the old one. (twice the size of)
  • I like tea and coffee, but the former is better for my sleep. (the former = tea)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ This method is superior than the old one. → ✅ This method is superior to the old one. (superior to)
  • ❌ The sequel is nowhere near good as the original. → ✅ The sequel is nowhere near as good as the original. (nowhere near as… as)

Practice: C1

16. In my opinion, natural light is far superior <span class="gap">___</span> artificial light.
Superior to.
17. The second season was <span class="gap">___</span> as good as the first. I stopped watching.
Much less: nowhere near as… as.
18. I was offered jobs in Toronto and Calgary. I chose the <span class="gap">___</span>, because it's closer to the mountains. (one word)
The second of two: the latter.
