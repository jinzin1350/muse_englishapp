Present Tenses: Simple and Continuous

English has two present tenses that learners use every day: the present simple (I work) and the present continuous (I'm working).

This page shows the big picture and helps you choose. Each topic below has its own page with rules, examples and exercises at every level.

The two present tenses at a glance

Present simplePresent continuous
FormI work / she worksI'm working / she's working
Main usehabits, facts, permanent situationsnow, around now, temporary situations
Future usetimetables: The train leaves at 8.arrangements: I'm meeting Sam at 8.
Typical wordsevery day, usually, never, on Mondaysnow, at the moment, this week, Look!

Which one do I need?

  • Is it happening now or around now? → present continuous: She's studying for her exams.
  • Is it a habit, a fact or something permanent? → present simple: She studies law.
  • Is the verb a state (know, want, like, belong)? → present simple, even for now: I know the answer.
  • Is it a future arrangement with people? → present continuous: We're having dinner with Tom tonight.
  • Is it a future timetable? → present simple: The film starts at 9.
  • Is it a change in progress or an annoying habit? → present continuous: It's getting colder. He's always complaining.
