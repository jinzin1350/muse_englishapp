Indirect Questions (Do you know where…?)

A2 · B1 · B2 · C1

An indirect question is a question inside another sentence: Where is the station? → Do you know where the station is? Indirect questions are more polite and very common in conversation.

The rule: after the question word (or if / whether), use statement word order and no do / does / did. For direct questions, see Question Words and Question Forms.

A2 · Elementary

Do you know where…? I don't know what… A2

Direct questionIndirect question
Where does she live?Do you know where she lives?
What time is it?Do you know what time it is?
Why did he leave?I don't know why he left.
How much does it cost?I'm not sure how much it costs.

  • Do you know where the post office is? (statement order)
  • I don't know what her name is. (statement order)
  • I have no idea why he said that. (no did)
  • Can you remember when the film starts? (no does)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Do you know where is the station? → ✅ Do you know where the station is? (statement order)
  • ❌ I don't know where does he work. → ✅ I don't know where he works. (no does)
  • ❌ Do you know what time is it? → ✅ Do you know what time it is? (statement order)

Practice: A2

1. Do you know where ___?
Statement order: the bathroom is.
2. I don't know what time ___.
Statement order, no does.
3. Where does Ali work? → Do you know where Ali ___ (work)?
Statement order: Ali works.

B1 · Intermediate

If and whether: indirect yes/no questions B1

  • Do you know if the bank is open today? (yes/no question)
  • I wonder whether she got my message. (whether)
  • I'm not sure if I locked the door. (not sure if)
  • It depends on whether we have time. (preposition + whether)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Do you know is the shop open? → ✅ Do you know if the shop is open? (if + statement order)
  • ❌ I wonder if does she like me. → ✅ I wonder if she likes me. (no does after if)
  • ❌ It depends on if it rains. → ✅ It depends on whether it rains. (preposition + whether)

Could you tell me…? Polite indirect questions B1

Direct (more direct)Indirect (more polite)
Where is the station?Could you tell me where the station is?
What time does it close?Do you happen to know what time it closes?
How much is this?Can I ask how much this is?
Why was my card refused?I was wondering why my card was refused.

  • Could you tell me where the lifts are? (polite)
  • Do you happen to know if there's a pharmacy near here? (very polite)
  • Would you mind telling me what your name is? (polite (would you mind + -ing))
  • I'd like to know why the flight was cancelled. (formal complaint)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Could you tell me where is the exit? → ✅ Could you tell me where the exit is? (statement order)
  • ❌ I was wondering if could you help me? → ✅ I was wondering if you could help me. (statement order; it's a statement)

Practice: B1

4. Is the museum open on Mondays? → Do you know ___ open on Mondays?
If + statement order.
5. It depends ___ we can get tickets.
After a preposition: whether.
6. Did she pass the exam? → I don't know ___ she passed the exam. (one word)
Yes/no: if or whether.
7. Excuse me, could you tell me ___?
Statement order inside the question.
8. Which is the most polite?
Do you happen to know…? is very polite.
9. Could you tell me how much this jacket ___ (cost)?
Statement order: this jacket costs.

B2 · Upper-intermediate

What to do, whether to go: question word + to-infinitive B2

  • I don't know what to wear to the wedding. (what I should wear)
  • Can you tell me how to get to the station? (how I can get)
  • We couldn't decide whether to stay or go. (whether + to)
  • She showed me where to sign. (where I should sign)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I don't know what should I do. → ✅ I don't know what to do. / I don't know what I should do. (question word + to)
  • ❌ I'm not sure if to accept. → ✅ I'm not sure whether to accept. (whether + to (not if))
  • ❌ I don't know why to go. → ✅ I don't know why I should go. (not why + to)

Practice: B2

10. I've never used this machine. Can you show me ___ it?
How to + verb.
11. He can't decide ___ to take the job or not.
Whether to + verb (not if to).
12. I didn't know what ___ (say), so I said nothing.
What to + verb.

C1 · Advanced

What he said is true: question-word clauses as subjects and objects C1

  • as subject: What you need is a holiday. How he did it is a mystery. Whether we win doesn't matter.
  • as object: I can't believe what she told me. I understand why you left.
  • after prepositions: It depends on how much it costs. We talked about where to live.

  • What happened next was amazing. (subject)
  • Why she left remains unclear. (subject (formal))
  • I can't remember where I parked. (object)
  • It depends on whether the weather improves. (after a preposition)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ What did he say is true. → ✅ What he said is true. (statement order)
  • ❌ Where does she live is a secret. → ✅ Where she lives is a secret. (statement order)
  • ❌ I can't believe what did she tell me. → ✅ I can't believe what she told me. (statement order, no did)

Practice: C1

13. ___ is a mystery to everyone.
A clause as subject: statement order.
14. I don't agree with ___.
Object clause: statement order.
15. ___ (whether / we / go) depends on the weather.
A clause as subject: Whether we go…
