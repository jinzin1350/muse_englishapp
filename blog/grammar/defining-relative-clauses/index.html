Defining Relative Clauses

Level: B1 · Free grammar guide

A defining relative clause tells us which person or thing we mean. Without it, the sentence isn't clear: The woman who lives next door is a doctor.

We don't use commas around defining relative clauses.

Relative pronouns

PronounUse it forExample
whopeopleThe man who called you is my uncle.
whichthings and animalsThe bike which I bought was cheap.
thatpeople or things (very common in speech)The man that called you… / The bike that I bought…
whereplacesThat's the café where we met.
whosepossession (his, her, their)She's the girl whose brother is a pilot.
whentimesI remember the day when we met.

In American English, that is much more common than which in defining clauses. Both are correct.

When can you leave out who, which or that?

You can leave out the pronoun when it is the object of the clause, that is, when another subject comes after it: The book (that) I'm reading is great. You can't leave it out when it is the subject: The man who lives next door… (not The man lives next door…).

  1. The film (that) we watched was boring. (object: that is optional)
  2. The people who work here are friendly. (subject: who is necessary)

Don't repeat the pronoun

The relative pronoun replaces he, she, it or them, so don't add another pronoun: The bike which I bought was cheap. (not The bike which I bought it…)

Where, whose and prepositions

Where = in / at which: the hotel where we stayed = the hotel that we stayed in. In everyday English, prepositions usually go at the end: the person (who) I spoke to. In formal English: the person to whom I spoke.

Whose replaces his, her, its, their: I know a man. His car is electric. → I know a man whose car is electric.

Examples

  1. The teacher who helped me is from Ireland. (who for people)
  2. Is this the bus that goes to the airport? (that for things)
  3. The phone (that) I bought last week has stopped working. (object: that is optional)
  4. That's the restaurant where we had our first date. (where for places)
  5. I have a friend whose dad is a famous chef. (whose for possession)
  6. People who read a lot usually have a big vocabulary. (subject: who is necessary)
  7. The keys (which) you lost are on the table. (object: which is optional)
  8. Do you know the name of the song that is playing? (subject: that is necessary)
  9. She's the woman (who) I met at the conference. (object: who is optional)
  10. Sunday is the day when I relax. (when for times)

Common mistakes

❌ The man which lives next door is a doctor.

✅ The man who lives next door is a doctor. (Use who or that for people.)

❌ The book who I read was great.

✅ The book that I read was great. (Use which or that for things.)

❌ The film that I watched it was boring.

✅ The film that I watched was boring. (Don't repeat the object.)

❌ The woman lives next door is friendly.

✅ The woman who lives next door is friendly. (You can't leave out a subject pronoun.)

❌ That's the hotel where we stayed in.

✅ That's the hotel where we stayed. (Where already means in which. (Or: the hotel that we stayed in.))

❌ The boy who his bike was stolen is upset.

✅ The boy whose bike was stolen is upset. (Use whose for possession.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. The woman ___ lives next door is a nurse.
For people, use who (or that).
2. This is the phone ___ I bought yesterday.
For things, use which (or that).
3. That's the café ___ we first met.
For places, use where (= in which).
4. He's the boy ___ dog bit me.
Possession (his dog): whose.
5. In which sentence can you leave out *that* or *who*?
That is the object (I is the subject after it), so you can leave it out: The book I'm reading is great.
6. I don't like people ___ are rude.
For people, use who.
7. Choose the correct sentence.
Use that for things and don't repeat it.
8. Is this the bus ___ goes to the city centre?
For things, use that (or which).
9. Choose the correct sentence.
Who is the subject of lives, so you need it, and you don't add he.
10. That's the shop ___ I bought my shoes.
A place: where (= in which).
11. I met a woman ___ son plays for the national team.
Possession (her son): whose. Who's = who is.
12. The keys ___ you were looking for are in the car.
For things, use that (or which). The preposition for stays at the end.
13. Do you remember the year ___ we moved here?
For times, use when.
14. The people ___ I work with are very friendly.
For people, use who.
15. This is the hotel ___ we stayed in last summer.
With in at the end, use which (or that). Where already means in which.
16. A dentist is a person ___ looks after your teeth.
For people, use who.
