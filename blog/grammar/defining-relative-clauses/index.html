Defining Relative Clauses (the man who…, the book that…)

A2 · B1 · B2 · C1

A defining relative clause tells us which person or thing we're talking about: The man who called you is my uncle. Without the clause, the sentence isn't complete or clear.

Defining clauses don't use commas. For clauses that add extra information (My uncle, who is a doctor, …), see Non-Defining Relative Clauses.

A2 · Elementary

Who, which and that A2

PronounUsed forExample
whopeopleThe woman who works here is very kind.
whichthings, animalsThe bus which goes to the airport is the 10.
thatpeople or things (informal)The man that called you… / The bus that goes…

  • I know a girl who speaks five languages. (people: who)
  • Where's the cake that was in the fridge? (things: that)
  • The film which we saw last night was boring. (things: which)
  • People who exercise live longer. (people: who)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The man which lives next door is a doctor. → ✅ The man who lives next door is a doctor. (people: who / that)
  • ❌ The phone who I bought is broken. → ✅ The phone that / which I bought is broken. (things: which / that)
  • ❌ The woman who she called me is my aunt. → ✅ The woman who called me is my aunt. (don't repeat the subject)

Leaving out who, which or that: the people we met A2

  • The cake (that) you made was delicious. (object: can leave out)
  • The people (who) we met on holiday were lovely. (object: can leave out)
  • The man who lives next door is a pilot. (subject: can't leave out)
  • Is this the jacket (which) you wanted? (object: can leave out)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The woman lives next door is a doctor. → ✅ The woman who lives next door is a doctor. (subject: keep who)
  • ❌ The book I'm reading it is great. → ✅ The book I'm reading is great. (don't add it)

Practice: A2

1. The boy <span class="gap">___</span> won the race is my cousin.
People: who (or that).
2. I lost the watch <span class="gap">___</span> my father gave me.
Things: that (or which).
3. Do you know the woman <span class="gap">___</span> is talking to Ali? (one word)
People: who or that.
4. In which sentence can you leave out that?
That I bought: the pronoun is the object.
5. Which sentence is correct?
The pronoun can be left out (object), no it.

The pronoun is the subject: you can't leave it out.

B1 · Intermediate

Whose, where and when B1

WordReplacesExample
whosehis / her / their / itsThe girl whose bag was stolen called the police.
wherein / at which (place)This is the house where I was born.
whentimeI remember the day when we first met.

  • That's the man whose dog bit me. (whose + noun)
  • The café where we first met has closed. (place)
  • Summer is the time when most people travel. (time)
  • I have a friend whose father is a famous actor. (whose)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The man who's car was stolen called the police. → ✅ The man whose car was stolen called the police. (whose (not who's = who is))
  • ❌ The hotel which we stayed was lovely. → ✅ The hotel where we stayed was lovely. / The hotel which we stayed at was lovely. (place: where or which … at)

Prepositions in relative clauses: the house I live in B1

  • That's the girl I told you about. (preposition at the end)
  • Is this the book you were looking for? (preposition at the end)
  • The hotel we stayed at was by the sea. (preposition at the end)
  • The committee to which we applied rejected the plan. (formal)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The person to who I spoke was rude. → ✅ The person (who) I spoke to was rude. / The person to whom I spoke was rude. (preposition + whom (formal))
  • ❌ The house in that I live is old. → ✅ The house (that) I live in is old. / The house in which I live is old. (not preposition + that)

B2 · Upper-intermediate

Who or whom? B2

  • The doctor whom I saw was very kind. (formal object)
  • The doctor (who) I saw was very kind. (everyday)
  • The colleague with whom I share an office is on holiday. (after a preposition)
  • The woman who called you is here. (subject: who)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The man whom lives next door is noisy. → ✅ The man who lives next door is noisy. (subject: who)
  • ❌ The friend with who I travelled was ill. → ✅ The friend with whom I travelled was ill. (after a preposition: whom)

What or that? Everything that, all that, what you need B2

  • What I love about Toronto is the people. (= the thing that)
  • Everything that you told me was true. (not everything what)
  • The only thing (that) I need is a coffee. (not the only thing what)
  • She didn't understand what I said. (= the thing that)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Everything what he said was a lie. → ✅ Everything (that) he said was a lie. (after everything: that)
  • ❌ The thing what I like most is the music. → ✅ The thing that I like most is the music. / What I like most is the music. (after a noun: that)

Reduced relative clauses: the woman talking to Tom, a car made in Japan B2

Full clauseReduced clause
The woman who is talking to Tom is my aunt.The woman talking to Tom is my aunt.
Anyone who wants to come is welcome.Anyone wanting to come is welcome.
The car which was stolen yesterday was found.The car stolen yesterday was found.
Phones that are made in Korea…Phones made in Korea…

  • Who's the boy wearing the red hat? (active: who is wearing)
  • Most of the people invited to the party came. (passive: who were invited)
  • Students wishing to join should email the club. (active: who wish)
  • The new bridge, built in 2020, is very modern. (passive (non-defining))

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The man stood at the door is my father. → ✅ The man standing at the door is my father. (active: -ing)
  • ❌ A letter writing in English is easier to read. → ✅ A letter written in English is easier to read. (passive: past participle)

Practice: B2

C1 · Advanced

The first to arrive, nothing to do, the reason why, the way C1

  • Neil Armstrong was the first person to walk on the Moon. (the first + to)
  • I've got a lot of work to do today. (noun + to)
  • Is there anywhere to park near here? (anywhere + to)
  • The reason (why) I called is to say thank you. (the reason why)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ She was the first woman who to fly across the Atlantic. → ✅ She was the first woman to fly across the Atlantic. (the first + to)
  • ❌ I don't like the way how he speaks. → ✅ I don't like the way (that) he speaks. (not the way how)

Practice: C1

The last + to + verb.
23. I'm bored. There's nothing <span class="gap">___</span>.
Nothing + to + verb.
24. Is there anything <span class="gap">___</span> (eat) in the fridge?
Anything + to + verb.
