Past Continuous (I was doing)

A2 · B1 · B2 · C1

We use the past continuous (was / were + -ing) for an action that was in progress at a particular time in the past: At nine o'clock last night I was reading.

It often appears with the past simple: I was reading when the phone rang. That contrast has its own page: Past Simple vs Past Continuous.

A2 · Elementary

Form and use: in the middle of an action A2

Subjectwas / were-ingExample
I / he / she / itwasverb + -ingShe was cooking at 7 pm.
you / we / theywereverb + -ingWe were waiting for the bus.

  • At 7 o'clock this morning I was running in the park. (in progress at 7)
  • The children were playing in the garden all afternoon. (in progress for a period)
  • Sorry I missed your call. I was driving. (in the middle of it)
  • What were you doing at midnight? (question)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ At 9 pm I was watch TV. → ✅ At 9 pm I was watching TV. (was + -ing)
  • ❌ They was waiting outside. → ✅ They were waiting outside. (they → were)
  • ❌ I was knowing the answer. → ✅ I knew the answer. (know is a stative verb.)

Negatives and questions A2

FormExample
Negativewasn't / weren't + -ingI wasn't listening.
QuestionWas / Were + subject + -ing?Were you sleeping?
Short answersYes, … was / were. No, … wasn't / weren't.Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't.
Wh-questionquestion word + was / were + subject + -ing?What were you doing?

  • I wasn't feeling well, so I stayed at home. (negative)
  • Was it raining when you left? ~ Yes, it was. (question + short answer)
  • Who were you talking to? (wh-question)
  • We weren't expecting so many people. (negative)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Did you sleeping? → ✅ Were you sleeping? (No did in the past continuous.)
  • ❌ What you were doing? → ✅ What were you doing? (were before the subject)
  • ❌ I didn't listening. → ✅ I wasn't listening. (negative: wasn't + -ing)

B1 · Intermediate

Two actions in progress at the same time: while B1

  • While we were having dinner, it was raining outside. (two actions in progress)
  • Some students were writing notes, and others were looking at their phones. (a scene)
  • I was working while my flatmate was sleeping. (while in the middle)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ While I was cooking, he watching TV. → ✅ While I was cooking, he was watching TV. (Past continuous needs was / were.)
  • ❌ During I was studying, she was working. → ✅ While I was studying, she was working. (while + clause (during + noun))

Setting the scene in a story B1

  • The sun was shining and the birds were singing. (background)
  • We were sitting in the café when a strange man came in. (background + event)
  • Everyone was laughing and having a good time. (the situation at that time)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ When I arrived, everyone danced and sang. → ✅ When I arrived, everyone was dancing and singing. (To describe the scene when you arrived: past continuous.)
  • ❌ The music was playing, and suddenly the lights were going out. → ✅ The music was playing, and suddenly the lights went out. (Sudden event: past simple.)

B2 · Upper-intermediate

Was always doing: annoying past habits B2

  • My brother was always taking my phone. (annoying)
  • She was constantly checking her messages during the meeting. (too often)
  • Our old car was forever breaking down. (it happened again and again)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ He always was complaining. → ✅ He was always complaining. (always goes after was)
  • ❌ She was always borrow my pen. → ✅ She was always borrowing my pen. (was always + -ing)

C1 · Advanced

Temporary periods, repeated actions and duration C1

  • When I was at university, I was working in a café at weekends. (temporary routine)
  • It was snowing all day, so the roads were closed. (duration + result)
  • In those days we were seeing each other every weekend. (temporary arrangement)
  • I called you three times last night! (a counted series: past simple)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I was calling you three times. → ✅ I called you three times. (Counted actions: past simple.)
  • ❌ I was knowing her for years. → ✅ I had known her for years. (know is stative; up to a past time: past perfect.)

I was wondering if…: polite requests and past plans C1

  • I was wondering if I could leave early today. (polite request about now)
  • We were hoping you could come to dinner on Saturday. (polite invitation)
  • She was nervous because she was starting a new job the next day. (plan seen from the past)
  • I was thinking we could try that new restaurant. (soft suggestion)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I was wondering if you can help me. → ✅ I was wondering if you could help me. (Keep the past form: could.)

Practice: C1

16. Which sentence is NOT correct?
A counted series uses the past simple: I rang the bell four times.
17. Which sentence presents the situation as temporary?
The past continuous presents it as a temporary arrangement.
18. I <span class="gap">___</span> (ring) the doorbell three times, but nobody answered.
A counted series: past simple rang.
19. Which is the most polite way to ask your manager?
I was wondering if… is the softest form.
20. I didn't go to the party because I was flying to Rome early the next morning. When was the flight?
It was a plan: the flight was the next morning.
21. She couldn't come to the party because she <span class="gap">___</span> (work) early the next morning.
A plan seen from the past: was working.
