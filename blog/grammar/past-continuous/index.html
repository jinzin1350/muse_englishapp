زمان گذشتهٔ استمراریPast Continuous (I was doing)

We use the past continuous (was / were + -ing) for an action that was in progress at a particular time in the past: At nine o'clock last night I was reading.

It often appears with the past simple: I was reading when the phone rang. That contrast has its own page: Past Simple vs Past Continuous.

A2 · مقدماتی

Form and use: in the middle of an action A2

was / were + -ing: the action was in progress at a past moment. At 8 I was having dinner.

Subjectwas / were-ingExample
I / he / she / itwasverb + -ingShe was cooking at 7 pm.
you / we / theywereverb + -ingWe were waiting for the bus.

The past continuous shows that an action had started and hadn't finished at a time in the past. We were in the middle of it.

Typical time phrases: at 6 o'clock yesterday, at this time last week, all morning, when you called.

The -ing spelling rules are the same as for the present continuous: make → making, sit → sitting, lie → lying. Stative verbs (know, want, like) are not usually used in the continuous: I knew the answer (not was knowing). See Stative Verbs.

  • At 7 o'clock this morning I was running in the park. (in progress at 7)
  • The children were playing in the garden all afternoon. (in progress for a period)
  • Sorry I missed your call. I was driving. (in the middle of it)
  • What were you doing at midnight? (question)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ At 9 pm I was watch TV. → ✅ At 9 pm I was watching TV. (was + -ing)
  • ❌ They was waiting outside. → ✅ They were waiting outside. (they → were)
  • ❌ I was knowing the answer. → ✅ I knew the answer. (know is a stative verb.)

Negatives and questions A2

Negative: wasn't / weren't + -ing. Question: Was / Were + subject + -ing?

FormExample
Negativewasn't / weren't + -ingI wasn't listening.
QuestionWas / Were + subject + -ing?Were you sleeping?
Short answersYes, … was / were. No, … wasn't / weren't.Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't.
Wh-questionquestion word + was / were + subject + -ing?What were you doing?

Don't use did in past continuous questions: Were you working? Not Did you working?

  • I wasn't feeling well, so I stayed at home. (negative)
  • Was it raining when you left? ~ Yes, it was. (question + short answer)
  • Who were you talking to? (wh-question)
  • We weren't expecting so many people. (negative)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ Did you sleeping? → ✅ Were you sleeping? (No did in the past continuous.)
  • ❌ What you were doing? → ✅ What were you doing? (were before the subject)
  • ❌ I didn't listening. → ✅ I wasn't listening. (negative: wasn't + -ing)

B1 · متوسط

Two actions in progress at the same time: while B1

Two longer actions happening at the same time: While I was cooking, he was watching TV.

When two longer actions were in progress at the same time, we can use the past continuous for both, often with while: While I was cooking, my husband was setting the table.

While can go at the start or in the middle: She was reading while the baby was sleeping. Use a comma when the while clause comes first.

Use while (or as) with longer actions. For a short action that interrupts a longer one, use when + past simple: I was cooking when the lights went out. See Past Simple vs Past Continuous.

  • While we were having dinner, it was raining outside. (two actions in progress)
  • Some students were writing notes, and others were looking at their phones. (a scene)
  • I was working while my flatmate was sleeping. (while in the middle)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ While I was cooking, he watching TV. → ✅ While I was cooking, he was watching TV. (Past continuous needs was / were.)
  • ❌ During I was studying, she was working. → ✅ While I was studying, she was working. (while + clause (during + noun))

Setting the scene in a story B1

Use the past continuous for the background of a story; use the past simple for the events.

At the start of a story, the past continuous describes the situation: what was happening around the main events. It was raining. People were hurrying home. A man was standing at the corner.

Then the past simple tells the events: Suddenly he turned and walked towards me.

This contrast is the heart of storytelling in English. For the complete set of story tenses, see Narrative Tenses.

  • The sun was shining and the birds were singing. (background)
  • We were sitting in the café when a strange man came in. (background + event)
  • Everyone was laughing and having a good time. (the situation at that time)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ When I arrived, everyone danced and sang. → ✅ When I arrived, everyone was dancing and singing. (To describe the scene when you arrived: past continuous.)
  • ❌ The music was playing, and suddenly the lights were going out. → ✅ The music was playing, and suddenly the lights went out. (Sudden event: past simple.)

B2 · بالاتر از متوسط

Was always doing: annoying past habits B2

always / constantly + past continuous for repeated past behaviour, often annoying: He was always losing his keys.

Just like the present continuous with always (You're always complaining!), the past continuous with always, constantly, forever describes behaviour that happened again and again, usually with a feeling of annoyance or surprise.

My old flatmate was always borrowing my things without asking. = He did it very often, and it annoyed me.

Compare: He always borrowed my things is a neutral fact. He was always borrowing my things adds feeling: it happened too often. For the present version, see Present Continuous.

  • My brother was always taking my phone. (annoying)
  • She was constantly checking her messages during the meeting. (too often)
  • Our old car was forever breaking down. (it happened again and again)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ He always was complaining. → ✅ He was always complaining. (always goes after was)
  • ❌ She was always borrow my pen. → ✅ She was always borrowing my pen. (was always + -ing)

تمرین سطح B2

C1 · پیشرفته

Temporary periods, repeated actions and duration C1

The past continuous can show a temporary routine (That summer I was working nights) or stress duration (I was cleaning all morning).

Temporary situations and routines. We use the past continuous for something that was true for a limited period: That year I was cycling to work every day. She was living with her parents while her flat was being repaired.

Duration. With all day, all morning, all week, the past continuous stresses that the activity filled the time, often to explain a result: I was gardening all afternoon, so I'm exhausted.

Not for a counted series. When we say how many times, we use the past simple: I phoned her five times. Not I was phoning her five times.

The past simple is also possible in many of these sentences (That year I cycled to work). The past continuous adds the idea that the situation was temporary or in progress.

  • When I was at university, I was working in a café at weekends. (temporary routine)
  • It was snowing all day, so the roads were closed. (duration + result)
  • In those days we were seeing each other every weekend. (temporary arrangement)
  • I called you three times last night! (a counted series: past simple)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I was calling you three times. → ✅ I called you three times. (Counted actions: past simple.)
  • ❌ I was knowing her for years. → ✅ I had known her for years. (know is stative; up to a past time: past perfect.)

I was wondering if…: polite requests and past plans C1

I was wondering if you could help is a polite request about now. She was leaving the next day is a plan seen from the past.

Politeness. With verbs like wonder, hope, think (of), the past continuous makes a request or suggestion softer and less direct. The meaning is now: I was wondering if you could help me. We were hoping you might join us. I was thinking we could go to the coast.

Plans in the past. The past continuous can describe an arrangement that was in the future at that time: I couldn't stay late because I was flying to Paris the next morning. See Future in the Past.

The degree of politeness, from direct to soft: I wonder if… → I wondered if… → I was wondering if… (softest).

  • I was wondering if I could leave early today. (polite request about now)
  • We were hoping you could come to dinner on Saturday. (polite invitation)
  • She was nervous because she was starting a new job the next day. (plan seen from the past)
  • I was thinking we could try that new restaurant. (soft suggestion)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I was wondering if you can help me. → ✅ I was wondering if you could help me. (Keep the past form: could.)
