Second Conditional

Level: B1 · Free grammar guide

Use the Second Conditional to talk about unreal or imaginary situations in the present or future — things that are unlikely or impossible. The form is: If + Past Simple, would + base verb. The Past Simple here does not mean past time; it shows the situation is unreal.

How to form the second conditional

Form: If + Past Simple, would + base verb

  • If I had more time, I would learn Italian.
  • If she knew the answer, she would tell us.

Both orders work:

  • I would travel the world if I were rich. (result first, no comma)
  • If I were rich, I would travel the world. (condition first, comma)

"Were" for all persons: After if, I / he / she / it often take were instead of was in formal English: If I were you, I would accept. In everyday speech, If I was you is also widely used and accepted. Both are correct; were is more formal.

Other modals: Instead of would, you can use could (ability) or might (possibility): If I had a car, I could drive to work. If it stopped raining, we might go for a walk.

Zero vs First for general truths: Both can describe general truths. If you heat water, it boils (zero — a scientific fact). If you heat the water, it will boil (first — the same fact, framed as a prediction). The difference is emphasis, not correctness.

First vs Second: If I have time, I will help you (real — I might have time). If I had time, I would help you (unreal — I probably won't have time). Choose based on how likely you think the condition is.

Examples

  1. If I won the lottery, I would buy a house by the sea. (Unreal — I probably won't win.)
  2. If she spoke French, she could work in Paris. (She doesn't speak French.)
  3. What would you do if you lost your job? (Imaginary question.)
  4. If I were taller, I would play basketball. (I'm not taller — impossible to change.)
  5. If it didn't rain so much here, we would spend more time outside. (It does rain a lot.)
  6. I would be happier if I had more free time. (Result first, no comma.)
  7. If they invited us, we would definitely go. (They probably won't invite us.)
  8. If I knew his number, I would call him right now. (I don't know it.)
  9. She would travel more if she didn't have a dog to look after. (She does have a dog.)
  10. If I were you, I'd take the job. (Advice — the classic "If I were you" pattern. I'd = I would.)

    11. Common mistakes

    ❌ If I would have more time, I would learn Italian.

    ✅ If I had more time, I would learn Italian. (In the if clause, use Past Simple, not would.)

    ❌ If I was you, I will accept.

    ✅ If I were you (or: If I was you), I would accept. (Use would in the result, not will. After if, was and were are both accepted; were is more formal.)

    ❌ If she would know the answer, she would tell us.

    ✅ If she knew the answer, she would tell us. (Would does not go in the if clause.)

    ❌ If I had more money, I will buy a car.

    ✅ If I had more money, I would buy a car. (Unreal condition → would, not will.)

    1. If I ___ more money, I ___ a bigger flat.
    Second conditional: If + Past Simple, would + base verb.
    2. If she ___ the truth, she ___ us.
    *Knew* (Past Simple) in the if-clause, *would tell* in the result. *Would* never goes in the if-clause.
    3. What ___ you do if you ___ your job?
    Imaginary question → Second Conditional. *Lost* is the Past Simple of *lose*.
    4. If I ___ you, I ___ that offer.
    "If I were you" is the standard advice pattern ("If I was you" is also common in speech).
    5. I ___ happier if I ___ less.
    Result first, no comma. *Would be* + If + Past Simple.
    6. The forecast says it will probably rain. If it ___ tomorrow, we ___ the picnic.
    Rain is likely → real possibility → First Conditional. "If it rained, we would cancel" would mean you think rain is unlikely.
    7. If you ___ ice, it ___.
    A scientific fact, always true → Zero Conditional: If + Present Simple, Present Simple.
    8. If he ___ a car, he ___ to work.
    Unreal → Second Conditional. *Could* expresses ability in the result clause.
    9. If I ___ the answer, I ___ you.
    *Knew* in the if-clause, *would tell* in the result.
    10. She ___ abroad if she ___ English better.
    Unreal (her English isn't good enough yet) → Second Conditional.
    11. Choose the correct sentence:
    *Were* in the if-clause (not *would be*), *would travel* in the result.
    12. They don't know our address. If they ___ it, they ___ us an invitation.
    They don't know it now → unreal present → Second Conditional.
    13. If I ___ a bird, I ___ to Paris.
    Impossible situation → Second Conditional. *Were* is the formal choice.
    14. What would you do if someone ___ you a million pounds?
    After *if* in a Second Conditional, use Past Simple: *gave*.
    15. If I ___ more time, I ___ the guitar.
    Unreal → If + Past Simple, would + base verb.
    16. He would be a great teacher if he ___ more patience.
    Unreal (he doesn't have enough patience) → if + Past Simple: *had*.
