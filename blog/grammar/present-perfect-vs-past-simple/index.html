Present Perfect vs Past Simple

A2 · B1 · B2 · C1

Both tenses talk about the past. The past simple (I lost) is about a finished time. The present perfect (I've lost) connects the past with now.

The most important question is: do you say or mean a finished time? If yes, use the past simple. This page goes from that basic rule to the cases where both are possible.

A2 · Elementary

Finished time or up to now? A2

Past simple (finished time)Present perfect (up to now / no time given)
I saw Sara yesterday.I've never seen a ghost.
We went to Rome in 2019.We've been to Rome twice.
She left two hours ago.She's just left.
When did you arrive?Have you ever been to Rome?

  • I lost my phone last week. (finished time)
  • I've lost my phone. Can you call it? (result now, no time)
  • When did you buy that dress? (When → past simple)
  • Have you bought the tickets? (no time, result now)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I've seen that film last night. → ✅ I saw that film last night. (last night is finished: past simple)
  • ❌ When have you got home? → ✅ When did you get home? (When → past simple)
  • ❌ I have visited my aunt on Sunday. → ✅ I visited my aunt on Sunday. (on Sunday is finished: past simple)

Have you ever…? Yes, I went there in… A2

  • I've met a famous actor. I met him at the airport in Dubai. (experience + details)
  • Have you tried this café? ~ Yes, I had lunch there on Monday. (question + details)
  • She's won a lot of prizes. She won her first one when she was ten. (general + specific)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Have you ever been to Japan? ~ Yes, I've been there in 2018. → ✅ Have you ever been to Japan? ~ Yes, I went there in 2018. (Details with a time: past simple.)
  • ❌ I've seen the match. It has been fantastic. → ✅ I've seen the match. It was fantastic. (Details about the match: past simple.)

Practice: A2

1. I <span class="gap">___</span> my grandparents last weekend.
Last weekend = finished time.
2. <span class="gap">___</span> in a hot-air balloon?
Life experience: Have you ever flown…?
3. We <span class="gap">___</span> (move) to this flat in 2020.
In 2020 = finished time.
4. Have you ever eaten Korean food? ~ Yes, I <span class="gap">___</span> it in Seoul last year.
Details with a time: past simple.
5. A: I've been to India. B: Oh really? When <span class="gap">___</span>?
When → past simple.
6. I've seen that film. I <span class="gap">___</span> (watch) it on the plane.
Details about one occasion: past simple.

B1 · Intermediate

Still true now, or finished? B1

Present perfect (still true)Past simple (finished)
He's worked at the bank for five years. (he still does)He worked at the bank for five years. (he doesn't now)
We've been married since 2010.We were married for ten years. (not now)

  • My grandfather lived in Tabriz all his life. (he has died)
  • My parents have lived in Tabriz all their lives. (they still live there)
  • Picasso painted thousands of works. (no longer alive)
  • She's already written three books, and she's only 25. (she can write more)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I've worked there for three years, but then I left. → ✅ I worked there for three years, but then I left. (It's finished: past simple.)
  • ❌ Einstein has won the Nobel Prize. → ✅ Einstein won the Nobel Prize. (He's no longer alive: past simple.)

Today, this morning, this year: finished or not? B1

Speaking at 10 amSpeaking at 3 pm
I've had two coffees this morning. (still morning)I had two coffees this morning. (morning is over)
Have you seen Reza this morning?Did you see Reza this morning?

  • (at 11 am) I've sent ten emails this morning. (morning not over)
  • (at 6 pm) I sent ten emails this morning. (morning over)
  • I've seen her today. We had lunch together at one. (today + a specific time)
  • We've had a lot of rain this year. (year not over)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ (at 9 pm) Have you seen the doctor this morning? → ✅ (at 9 pm) Did you see the doctor this morning? (At 9 pm the morning is over.)
  • ❌ I've phoned him at 10 o'clock today. → ✅ I phoned him at 10 o'clock today. (A specific time: past simple.)

Practice: B1

7. I <span class="gap">___</span> in a bank for two years when I was younger. Now I'm a teacher.
It's finished: past simple.
8. She has taught here for twenty years. Does she still teach here?
Present perfect + for = still true now.
9. Leonardo da Vinci <span class="gap">___</span> (paint) the Mona Lisa.
He's no longer alive: past simple.
10. It's 4 pm. I'm hungry because I <span class="gap">___</span> breakfast this morning.
At 4 pm the morning is over: past simple.
11. It's 9 am. <span class="gap">___</span> the news this morning?
Still morning: present perfect.
12. I've talked to the manager today. I <span class="gap">___</span> (talk) to her at 11.
A specific time: past simple.

B2 · Upper-intermediate

American and British English B2

British English (usual)American English (also common)
I've just eaten.I just ate.
Have you finished yet?Did you finish yet?
I've already seen it.I already saw it.
Have you ever been to Mexico?Did you ever go to Mexico? / Have you ever been…?
I've lost my keys.I lost my keys.

  • (BrE) Have you had lunch yet? / (AmE) Did you have lunch yet? (both correct)
  • (BrE) She's just left. / (AmE) She just left. (both correct)
  • (both) She left ten minutes ago. (finished time: past simple everywhere)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ (in any variety) I've just finished it ten minutes ago. → ✅ I finished it ten minutes ago. (With ago, only the past simple.)
  • ❌ (in any variety) Did you see him yet yesterday? → ✅ Did you see him yesterday? (yet doesn't go with a finished time.)

Practice: B2

13. Which sentence is correct in both British and American English?
The present perfect with already is correct everywhere.
14. An American friend says I just got here. What is the British English equivalent?
BrE: present perfect I've just got here.
15. (British English) <span class="gap">___</span> (you / see) the new Batman film yet?
British English with yet: present perfect.

C1 · Advanced

When both are possible: the meaning changes C1

  • I've always wanted to visit Japan. (I still want to.) / I always wanted to visit Japan. (Maybe I've visited now, or I've given up.)
  • Have you heard? They're getting married! (news, now) / Did you hear the news this morning? (a specific occasion)
  • I've recently started running. / I recently started running. (Both are fine with recently.)
  • Who's eaten my sandwich? (I've just noticed; the result) / Who ate my sandwich? (also natural, especially in American English)

  • My father has always loved gardening. (still true)
  • My father always loved gardening. (he has probably died, or it's no longer true)
  • Have you seen my glasses? I can't find them. (present result)
  • Did you see my glasses when you were in the kitchen? (a specific occasion)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I always wanted to try sushi, and I still want to. → ✅ I've always wanted to try sushi, and I still want to. (Still true: present perfect.)
  • ❌ Have you heard the president's speech last night? → ✅ Did you hear the president's speech last night? (last night: past simple)

Practice: C1

16. I always wanted to be a pilot. What does this suggest?
Past simple: the situation is seen as finished.
17. Which sentence suggests that the speaker still loves the city?
Present perfect + always = still true.
18. I <span class="gap">___</span> (always / want) to learn the piano, and now I'm finally taking lessons.
Both are possible: have always wanted connects the wish to now; always wanted sees it as past.
