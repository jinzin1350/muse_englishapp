Present Perfect vs Past Simple

Level: B1 · Free grammar guide

Use the past simple for a finished time in the past: I went to Paris in 2019.

Use the present perfect when the time is not finished or not important, or when the past connects to now: I've been to Paris three times. She's lived here since 2015.

Form

Past simplePresent perfect
PositiveI worked / I wentI have worked / she has gone
NegativeI didn't workI haven't worked
QuestionDid you work?Have you worked?

Comparison

Past simplePresent perfect
finished time: yesterday, last week, in 2019, two years agounfinished time: today, this week, so far, since, for (until now)
a specific past moment: When did you arrive?life experience, no time given: Have you ever been to Japan?
finished period: He lived in Italy for ten years (he doesn't now)period up to now: He's lived in Canada for ten years (he still does)
people who are dead: Shakespeare wrote 37 plays.people who are alive, life so far: She's written three books.

Never use the present perfect with a finished time expression: I saw him yesterday (not I have seen him yesterday).

With just, already and yet, American English often uses the past simple (I just ate), while British English prefers the present perfect (I've just eaten). Both are correct. See Present Perfect with For, Since, Just, Yet, Already.

Examples

  1. I went to Paris in 2019. (finished time)
  2. I've been to Paris three times. (experience)
  3. We had dinner two hours ago. (ago)
  4. I've drunk three coffees so far today. (unfinished time)
  5. She's lived here since 2015. (since: still true)
  6. My grandfather lived in Italy for ten years, then moved to Canada. (finished period)
  7. When did you start learning English? (when question)
  8. Have you ever eaten sushi? (experience question)
  9. She broke her leg last year. (finished time)
  10. This is the best film I've ever seen. (superlative + present perfect)

Common mistakes

❌ I have seen him yesterday.

✅ I saw him yesterday. (Finished time: past simple.)

❌ When have you arrived?

✅ When did you arrive? (When questions: past simple.)

❌ I live here since 2015.

✅ I've lived here since 2015. (Since + unfinished period: present perfect.)

❌ I've been to Rome last summer.

✅ I went to Rome last summer. (Last summer is finished: past simple.)

❌ We know each other for ten years.

✅ We've known each other for ten years. (Up to now: present perfect.)

❌ Did you finished?

✅ Did you finish? / Have you finished? (After did, use the base form.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. I ___ to Paris in 2019.
Finished time (in 2019): past simple, went.
2. She ___ here since 2015. She loves it.
Since + still true: present perfect, has lived.
3. We ___ dinner two hours ago.
Ago = finished time: past simple, had.
4. ___ you ever eaten sushi?
Experience + past participle: Have you ever eaten…?
5. When ___ you start learning English?
When questions: past simple, did.
6. I ___ three cups of coffee so far today.
So far today = unfinished: have drunk.
7. My grandfather ___ in Italy for ten years before he moved to Canada.
A finished period: past simple, lived.
8. How long ___ you known each other? – Since we were at school.
Up to now: How long have you known…?
9. She ___ her leg last year, but it's fine now.
Last year = finished time: broke.
10. We ___ each other for five years. We're best friends.
Up to now, still true: have known.
11. Choose the correct sentence.
Finished time: past simple, I saw him yesterday.
12. This is the best film I ___.
Superlative + present perfect: have ever seen.
13. Shakespeare ___ 37 plays.
He's dead, so his life is finished: past simple, wrote.
14. I ___ in this company since January.
Since + up to now: present perfect, 've worked.
15. Did you enjoy the concert last night? – Yes, it ___ amazing.
Last night = finished: past simple, was.
16. How many countries ___ so far in your life?
Life so far, question order: have you visited.
