Present Perfect vs Past Simple

Level: B1 · Free grammar guide

H1: Present Perfect vs Past Simple

Direct answer

Use the Past Simple for actions that finished at a specific time in the past. Use the Present Perfect when the time is not given, when the result matters now, or when the time period isn't finished yet (today, this week). The key question is: Am I talking about WHEN it happened, or about the RESULT now?

The rule / form box

Past Simple

  • Form: verb + -ed (regular) / irregular past form
  • I visited Rome in 2019.
  • She finished her homework an hour ago.

Present Perfect

  • Form: have / has + past participle
  • I have visited Rome. (no time given — the experience matters)
  • She has finished her homework. (the result matters now)

British and American English: British English usually says "I've just finished." American English often uses the Past Simple with just, already and yet: "I just finished." "Did you eat yet?" Both are correct. The exercises on this page follow British English.

Examples (12)

  1. I bought a new bike yesterday. (Past Simple — specific time: yesterday)
  2. I have bought a new bike. (Present Perfect — the news matters now)
  3. She lived in Paris from 2018 to 2021. (Past Simple — finished period)
  4. My brother has lived in Rome since 2022. (Present Perfect — still lives there)
  5. We saw the new exhibition last weekend. (Past Simple — when: last weekend)
  6. We have seen the new exhibition. (Present Perfect — we know it, no time given)
  7. He lost his keys this morning. (Past Simple — specific time, finished)
  8. He has lost his keys. (Present Perfect — result now: he can't open the door)
  9. They have already eaten lunch. (Present Perfect — already, result: not hungry)
  10. Did you watch the match last night? (Past Simple — question about a specific time)
  11. I've drunk three coffees today. (Present Perfect — today isn't finished yet)
  12. I drank three coffees yesterday. (Past Simple — yesterday is finished)

    13. Common mistakes

    ❌ I have seen him yesterday.

    ✅ I saw him yesterday. (Present Perfect does not go with a finished past time word like "yesterday", "last week", "in 2019", "ago".)

    ❌ I have lived in Toronto for three years ago.

    ✅ I have lived in Toronto for three years. / I lived in Toronto three years ago. ("For" + Present Perfect, no "ago". "Ago" + Past Simple.)

    ❌ Have you ever went to Japan?

    ✅ Have you ever been to Japan? (After have / has, use the past participle, not the past simple (went). The past participle of go is gone, but for visits we use been: 'Have you ever been to Japan?' = Did you go there and come back?)

    ❌ She has finished school in 2020.

    ✅ She finished school in 2020. (A finished year → Past Simple.)

    ❌ I didn't have seen that film.

    ✅ I haven't seen that film. / I didn't see that film. (Negative Present Perfect: haven't + past participle. Negative Past Simple: didn't + base verb.)

    ❌ When have you arrived?

    ✅ When did you arrive? (Questions with When ask about a specific time → Past Simple.)

    Exercises

    Choose the correct answer. You will see the explanation after checking.

    1. I ___ my passport last week.
    "Last week" is a finished past time → Past Simple.
    2. She ___ at this company since 2020.
    "Since 2020" + still true now → Present Perfect. "She" takes "has".
    3. ___ you ever ___ sushi?
    "Ever" + experience question → Present Perfect: have + past participle.
    4. We ___ to the beach yesterday.
    "Yesterday" → Past Simple. The past of "go" is "went".
    5. He ___ just ___ his exam results.
    "Just" + recent result → Present Perfect. "He" takes "has".
    6. So far this year, we ___ three new teachers.
    "So far" means from the start of a period until now → Present Perfect.
    7. They ___ here for five years, but they moved away in 2023.
    They moved away — the period is finished → Past Simple. "Have lived" would mean they still live here.
    8. ___ she ___ the email yet?
    "Yet" in a question → Present Perfect. "She" takes "has".
    9. I ___ him at the conference in March.
    "In March" = specific finished time → Past Simple.
    10. The train ___ already ___.
    "Already" + result now → Present Perfect. "The train" takes "has".
    11. Choose the correct sentence:
    "In 2021" = finished time → Past Simple. Present Perfect does not go with a specific finished past time.
    12. How long ___ you ___ your car?
    "How long" + still true → Present Perfect. "Have you had" = from the past until now.
    13. She ___ never ___ to a concert.
    "Never" + experience → Present Perfect. "She" takes "has", past participle is "been".
    14. We ___ our grandparents last Sunday.
    "Last Sunday" = finished time → Past Simple.
    15. When ___ you ___ your new job?
    A question with "When" asks about a specific time → Past Simple. Don't say "When have you started?"
    16. Shakespeare ___ many famous plays.
    Shakespeare's life is finished → Past Simple. Compare: "My favourite author has written twelve books." (She is alive and still writing.)
    17. He ___ in London since he was a child.
    "Since he was a child" + still true → Present Perfect. "He" takes "has".
    18. They ___ the project last month, and the client is happy with the result.
    "Last month" = finished time → Past Simple, even though the result still matters. The time word decides the tense.
