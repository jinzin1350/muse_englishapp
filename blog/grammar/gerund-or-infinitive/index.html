Gerund or Infinitive

Level: B1 · Free grammar guide

A gerund is the -ing form of a verb used like a noun: Swimming is good for you. An infinitive is to + base verb: I want to swim.

After some verbs we use the gerund, and after others the infinitive. You need to learn which verbs go with which form.

Verbs followed by -ing

enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, suggest, keep, practise (UK) / practice (US), consider, miss, imagine, can't stand, don't mind, give up

  1. I enjoy cooking. (enjoy + -ing)
  2. She avoided answering the question. (avoid + -ing)
  3. Would you mind closing the door? (mind + -ing)

Verbs followed by to + infinitive

want, decide, hope, plan, promise, agree, refuse, offer, learn, manage, need, seem, afford, would like

  1. We decided to stay at home. (decide + to)
  2. He refused to help us. (refuse + to)
  3. I can't afford to buy a new car. (afford + to)

For a negative infinitive, put not before to: He promised not to be late.

Verb + person + to + infinitive

ask, tell, want, invite, encourage, allow, remind, advise + person + to: My parents want me to study law. She reminded me to call the bank.

Not want that…: I want you to come. (not I want that you come)

-ing after prepositions and as a subject

  • After a preposition, always use -ing: I'm interested in learning Japanese. Thanks for helping me. She left without saying goodbye.
  • As the subject of a sentence: Learning a language takes time.

In look forward to and be used to, to is a preposition, so it takes -ing: I'm looking forward to seeing you.

Verbs that take both forms

like, love, hate, prefer, start, begin, continue can take either form with little or no change in meaning: I like swimming / I like to swim. It started raining / It started to rain. After would like, would love, would prefer, use to: I'd like to go.

A few verbs change their meaning: I stopped smoking (I don't smoke now) but I stopped to smoke (I stopped what I was doing in order to smoke). Remember, forget and try work in a similar way.

Examples

  1. I enjoy reading before bed. (enjoy + -ing)
  2. We decided to take the train. (decide + to)
  3. Smoking is not allowed here. (-ing as the subject)
  4. She's good at drawing. (preposition + -ing)
  5. He promised to call me. (promise + to)
  6. I don't mind waiting. (mind + -ing)
  7. They asked us to leave. (ask + person + to)
  8. I'm thinking of changing my job. (preposition + -ing)
  9. Would you like to come with us? (would like + to)
  10. Have you finished cleaning the kitchen? (finish + -ing)

Common mistakes

❌ I enjoy to cook.

✅ I enjoy cooking. (Enjoy + -ing.)

❌ She decided going home.

✅ She decided to go home. (Decide + to + infinitive.)

❌ I'm interested in learn Spanish.

✅ I'm interested in learning Spanish. (After a preposition, use -ing.)

❌ I want that you come.

✅ I want you to come. (Want + person + to + infinitive.)

❌ I look forward to see you.

✅ I look forward to seeing you. (To is a preposition here, so use -ing.)

❌ He suggested to go to the cinema.

✅ He suggested going to the cinema. (Suggest + -ing (or suggest that we go).)

❌ Thank you for help me.

✅ Thank you for helping me. (After for, use -ing.)
