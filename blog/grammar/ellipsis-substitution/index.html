حذف و جایگزینیEllipsis and Substitution

Good English avoids repeating words. We can leave words out (ellipsis): I wanted to go, but I couldn't (go). Or we can replace them with a short word (substitution): I don't like this shirt. Can I see a different one?

For short answers and so do I, see So Do I, Neither Do I. For I think so, see the same page.

B1 · B2 · C1 · C2

B1 · متوسط

One and ones: the red one, these ones B1

Use one (singular) and ones (plural) instead of repeating a countable noun: I don't like this jacket. I prefer the black one. These shoes are nicer than the old ones.

PatternExample
the + adjective + oneWhich car is yours? ~ The blue one.
a + adjective + oneMy phone is broken. I need a new one.
the one(s) + phrase / clauseThe one on the left is mine. The ones I bought were cheap.
which one(s)?Which one do you want?

Don't use one for uncountable nouns: I like white wine more than red (not red one).

After this / that, one is often used: I'll take this one. After these / those, ones is optional in BrE: I prefer those (ones).

  • This cup is dirty. Can I have a clean one? (one)
  • The ones in the window are on sale. (ones + phrase)
  • I'd like a coffee. A large one, please. (a + adjective + one)
  • Which one is your house? (which one)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ My bike is old. I want a new. → ✅ My bike is old. I want a new one. (adjective + one)
  • ❌ I like green tea more than black one. → ✅ I like green tea more than black. (uncountable: no one)

تمرین سطح B1

1. My phone is very old. I need a new ___.
Singular: a new one.
2. These jeans are too small. Do you have any bigger ___?
Plural: bigger ones.
3. I don't like the red dress. I prefer the blue ___. (one word)
Singular: the blue one.

B2 · بالاتر از متوسط

I'd love to, I didn't want to: to instead of the whole verb B2

Use to alone instead of repeating the whole infinitive: Would you like to come? ~ I'd love to. I didn't want to go, but I had to.

FullWith ellipsis
Would you like to dance? ~ I'd love to dance.~ I'd love to.
I meant to call you, but I forgot to call you.…but I forgot to.
You can stay if you want to stay.…if you want to.
He didn't go, although he promised to go.…although he promised to.

Keep to: I'd love to. (not I'd love.) But after want and like in clauses with if, when, what, to is often left out: Do what you want. Come whenever you like.

With be and have, keep them after to: I'm not rich, and I don't want to be. I haven't finished, but I'd like to have by Friday.

  • Can you come tomorrow? ~ I'll try to. (to)
  • I didn't want to leave, but I had to. (had to)
  • You don't have to help, but you can if you'd like to. (if you'd like to)
  • She isn't a doctor, but she'd like to be. (to be)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ Would you like to come? ~ Yes, I'd love. → ✅ Would you like to come? ~ Yes, I'd love to. (keep to)
  • ❌ I didn't call her. I forgot it. → ✅ I didn't call her. I forgot to. (to, not it)

She went out and bought milk: leaving out repeated words B2

After and, but, or, we often leave out a repeated subject, auxiliary or verb: She went out and (she) bought some milk. I can sing but (I) can't dance. Ali ordered fish and Sara (ordered) chicken.

FullWith ellipsis
He opened the door and he went in.He opened the door and went in.
I've finished the report and I've sent it.I've finished the report and sent it.
She plays tennis and she plays golf.She plays tennis and golf.
Ali drinks tea and Sara drinks coffee.Ali drinks tea and Sara coffee. (formal)

You can only leave out words that are the same in both parts: I'll call you and (I'll) explain.

After a comparison, leave out the repeated verb: She's taller than her brother (is).

  • We went to the beach and swam in the sea. (subject left out)
  • I've washed the dishes and dried them. (auxiliary left out)
  • He can speak French but not Spanish. (verb left out)
  • My brother is older than me. (after a comparison)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ She's tired and she's tired of her job. (repeated) → ✅ She's tired of her job. (unnecessary repetition)
  • ❌ I've cleaned the kitchen and I've cleaned the bathroom. → ✅ I've cleaned the kitchen and the bathroom. (leave out the repeated words)

تمرین سطح B2

4. Do you want to come to the cinema with us? ~ I'd love ___!
Keep to: I'd love to.
5. I didn't want to work on Saturday, but I had ___.
Had to.
6. Did you call your mother? ~ No, I forgot ___. I'll call her now. (one word)
Forgot to.
7. She opened the letter and ___ it quickly.
Same subject: and read.
8. I've packed my bag and ___ my ticket.
Same auxiliary have: and printed.
9. I can play the guitar but ___ the piano. (one word)
Ellipsis: but (I can)not (play) the piano.

C1 · پیشرفته

Do so, do that, if so, if not, I'm afraid so C1

Substitute a whole verb phrase with do so (formal), do that / do it (informal). Replace a whole clause with so (If so…, I'm afraid so) or not (If not…, I hope not).

PatternExample
do so (formal)He was asked to leave, but he refused to do so.
do that / do it (informal)I'll clean the car. ~ No, I'll do it.
if so / if notAre you free? If so, come over. If not, call me.
so after verbsIs it true? ~ I believe so. I told you so!
not after verbsWill it rain? ~ I hope not. I'm afraid not.

Do so replaces a verb + its object or complement: Anyone who wants to leave early may do so (= may leave early).

Use so and not after think, hope, believe, suppose, expect, be afraid and in if so / if not.

  • Visitors may take photos, but they must not do so with flash. (do so)
  • Do you need help? If so, let me know. (if so)
  • Is the shop open? ~ I don't think so. (so)
  • Have you finished? If not, keep working. (if not)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ She promised to call, but she didn't do. → ✅ She promised to call, but she didn't do so. (do so)
  • ❌ Is it going to rain? ~ I hope no. → ✅ Is it going to rain? ~ I hope not. (I hope not)

Want a coffee? Sounds good. Seen Ali? C1

In informal speech, we often leave out words at the start of a sentence (subjects and auxiliaries) when the meaning is clear: (Do you) Want a coffee? (It) Sounds good. (Have you) Seen Ali? (I'm) Sorry I'm late.

FullSpoken ellipsis
Do you want a drink?Want a drink?
Have you seen my keys?Seen my keys?
It sounds great.Sounds great.
I don't know.Don't know. / Dunno. (very informal)
Are you ready?Ready?
I'm sorry I'm late.Sorry I'm late.

This is normal in conversation, text messages and emails to friends, but not in formal writing.

  • Looks like rain. ((It) looks like rain)
  • Got a minute? ((Have you) got a minute?)
  • See you later! ((I'll) see you later)
  • Anyone want a sandwich? ((Does) anyone want…?)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ Dear Sir, Hope you are well. Want to apply for the job. (formal letter) → ✅ Dear Sir, I hope you are well. I would like to apply for the job. (formal writing: no ellipsis)
  • ❌ Sounds like good. → ✅ Sounds good. (sound + adjective)

تمرین سطح C1

10. You may leave early if you wish to ___.
Formal: do so.
11. Are you coming to the party? If ___, please bring some drinks.
If so.
12. Is the museum open on Mondays? ~ I'm afraid ___. It's closed. (one word)
I'm afraid not.
13. In Want a lift?, which words are left out?
(Do you) want a lift?
14. In Seen the news?, which words are left out?
(Have you) seen the news?
15. Let's meet at 7. ~ ___ good! See you then. (one word: = It sounds)
(That) sounds good.

C2 · تسلط کامل

As did we, than do most people, so would I C2

Formal comparative and additive patterns use an auxiliary (often with inversion): She was tired, as were we. They earn more than do most people. He loved the film, as did I.

PatternExample
as + auxiliary + subjectThe prices rose, as did the costs.
than + auxiliary + subject (formal)Women live longer than do men.
so + auxiliary + subjectShe passed, and so did everyone else.
as + subject + auxiliary (neutral)She was nervous, as I was.

Inversion after as and than is formal; the normal order is also correct: …than men do. …as I did.

Make sure the auxiliary matches the tense of the first verb: He had finished, as had I. She will win, as will you.

  • Ali left early, as did Sara. (as did)
  • City dwellers spend more on rent than do people in villages. (than do)
  • My parents were born in Iran, as were my grandparents. (as were)
  • He has passed, and so have I. (so have)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ She loves jazz, as does I. → ✅ She loves jazz, as do I. (auxiliary agrees with the subject: do I)
  • ❌ He had already left, as did I. → ✅ He had already left, as had I. (same tense: had)

تمرین سطح C2

16. The company's profits rose sharply, ___ its share price.
Same tense (past simple): as did.
17. Older people tend to sleep less than ___ younger people.
Than do + plural subject.
18. My brother had already eaten, as ___ I. (one word)
Same tense: as had I.
