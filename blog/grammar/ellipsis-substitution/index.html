Ellipsis and Substitution

Level: C1 · Free grammar guide

Natural English avoids repeating words. We either replace them with a short word (substitution) or leave them out (ellipsis):

I don't like this jacket. Can I see the blue one? (substitution) I can't swim, but my sister can. (ellipsis: can swim)

Substitution

ReplacesWordExample
a countable nounone / onesThese shoes are too small. Do you have bigger ones?
a verb phrasedo / does / didShe speaks French better than I do.
a verb phrase (formal)do soHe was asked to leave, and he did so at once.
a positive clausesoWill it rain? – I think so.
a negative clausenotIs the shop open? – I'm afraid not.

So / not are common after think, hope, expect, suppose, guess, be afraid: I hope so. I hope not. I expect so.

With think, expect, suppose, the usual negative is I don't think so (more natural than I think not). With hope and be afraid, use not: I hope not. I'm afraid not.

Ellipsis

  • After an auxiliary or modal: I haven't finished, but Sara has. Will you help? – Yes, I will.
  • After to (keep the to): Would you like to come? – I'd love to. I didn't want to go, but I had to.
  • After and, but, or, when the subject or verb is the same: She washed the dishes and (she) dried them.
  • In short answers and replies: Who wants cake? – I do!

Examples

  1. I'd like a coffee. Would you like one? (one = a coffee)
  2. The red apples are sweeter than the green ones. (ones = apples)
  3. Nobody works harder than she does. (does = works)
  4. Are you coming tonight? – I hope so. (so = that I'm coming)
  5. Is it going to rain? – I hope not. (not = that it won't rain)
  6. I don't think so. (negative with think)
  7. I've never been to Asia, but my brother has. (ellipsis after has)
  8. Do you want to come? – I'd love to, but I'm busy. (ellipsis after to)
  9. Applicants who wish to apply should do so by Friday. (formal do so)
  10. She said she'd call, and she did. (ellipsis after did)

Common mistakes

❌ I don't think it.

✅ I don't think so. (Use so to replace a clause.)

❌ I hope no.

✅ I hope not. (Negative substitution: not.)

❌ I'd love.

✅ I'd love to. (Keep to when the verb is left out.)

❌ These are the old ones shoes.

✅ These are the old ones. (Ones replaces the noun; don't use both.)

❌ She can't come, but her husband can come it.

✅ She can't come, but her husband can. (After the auxiliary, leave out the repeated words.)

❌ I like this cheese. Do you like one?

✅ Do you like it? (One replaces a countable noun; for the same specific thing, use it.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. I don't like this jacket. Can I see the blue ___?
Replaces a singular countable noun: one.
2. Is it going to rain? – I hope ___.
Negative substitution after hope: not.
3. She speaks French better than I ___.
Replaces speak French: do.
4. Will you be at the meeting? – I don't think ___.
I don't think so.
5. Would you like to come with us? – I'd love ___!
Keep to: I'd love to.
6. These shoes are too small. Do you have any bigger ___?
Replaces a plural noun: ones.
7. He was asked to leave the room, and he did ___ immediately.
Formal substitution: did so.
8. I haven't finished my homework yet, but Sara ___.
Ellipsis after the auxiliary: Sara has (finished).
9. I can't play the piano, but my sister ___.
Ellipsis after the modal: my sister can (play).
10. Who wants more cake? – I ___!
Short reply: I do.
11. I didn't want to go, but I had ___.
Keep to: I had to (go).
12. Is the shop open on Sunday? – I'm afraid ___.
I'm afraid not.
13. You promised to clean your room. – I know, and I ___ tomorrow.
Future ellipsis: I will (clean it) tomorrow.
14. Choose the sentence with correct ellipsis.
After the auxiliary, leave out the rest: my brother has.
15. Do you think the train will be late? – I expect ___. It's always late.
Positive substitution: I expect so.
16. I asked him to apologise, but he refused ___.
Keep to: he refused to (apologise).
