Adverbs of Manner (slowly, well, hard)

Level: A2 · Free grammar guide

Adverbs of manner tell us how someone does something: She speaks quietly. He drives carefully. They play well.

An adjective describes a noun (a careful driver); an adverb describes a verb (he drives carefully).

Adjective + -ly

RuleExamples
Most adjectives: + -lyquick → quickly, bad → badly, careful → carefully
Consonant + y → -ilyhappy → happily, easy → easily, angry → angrily
-le → -lyterrible → terribly, gentle → gently, comfortable → comfortably
-ic → -icallyautomatic → automatically, dramatic → dramatically

Irregular adverbs

AdjectiveAdverbExample
goodwellShe sings well.
fastfastHe runs fast.
hardhardThey work hard.
latelateThe train arrived late.
earlyearlyI got up early.

Hardly and lately are different words: hardly = almost not (I can hardly hear you), lately = recently (I haven't seen him lately).

Word order

Put the adverb after the verb, or after the object if there is one: She speaks slowly. She speaks English slowly. (not She speaks slowly English)

Adjective or adverb?

After be, look, feel, seem, sound, taste, smell, use an adjective, because it describes the subject: You look happy. This soup tastes delicious. That sounds great.

Examples

  1. Please read the instructions carefully. (careful → carefully)
  2. The children played happily in the garden. (happy → happily)
  3. He speaks Spanish very well. (good → well)
  4. She works hard and never complains. (hard (not hardly))
  5. I passed the test easily. (easy → easily)
  6. We arrived late because of the traffic. (late (not lately))
  7. The doors open automatically. (-ic → -ically)
  8. He's a careful driver. (adjective + noun)
  9. You look tired. (look + adjective)
  10. I can hardly believe it! (hardly = almost not)

Common mistakes

❌ She speaks English very good.

✅ She speaks English very well. (The adverb of good is well.)

❌ He speaks very well English.

✅ He speaks English very well. (Put the adverb after the object.)

❌ She works very hardly.

✅ She works very hard. (Hard is the adverb. Hardly means almost not.)

❌ He runs very fastly.

✅ He runs very fast. (Fast doesn't change.)

❌ You look beautifully.

✅ You look beautiful. (After look, use an adjective.)

❌ The children played happyly.

✅ The children played happily. (Consonant + y → -ily.)
