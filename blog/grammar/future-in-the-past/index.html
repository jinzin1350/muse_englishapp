Future in the Past (was about to, was to, would)

Level: C1 · Free grammar guide

Sometimes we talk about the past and need to say what was in the future at that time: I knew she would call. I was about to leave when the phone rang.

You probably know was going to. This page covers the other forms: would, was about to, was on the point of, was to and was to have done.

The forms

FormMeaningExample
would + verbfuture seen from the past (prediction, promise, report)He promised he would be back by midnight.
was about to + verbvery near future at that timeI was about to leave when the phone rang.
was on the point of + -ingeven nearer; just about to (often interrupted)She was on the point of giving up when she found the answer.
was / were to + verbdestiny (what really happened later), or an official planThey met in 1990. They were to marry ten years later.
was / were to have + past participlea plan that didn't happenThe meeting was to have taken place at 10, but it was cancelled.

Would: the past of will

Would is common after reporting and thinking verbs in the past: I thought it would be a nice day. She said the package would arrive the next day. As a child, I had no idea that one day I would live in Canada.

See Reported Speech for will → would in reported statements.

Was to: destiny in stories

Was to is often used in biographies and stories to say what happened later, seen from an earlier moment: At the time, nobody knew that this small shop was to become a global brand. It sounds literary, so it's mostly used in writing.

Little did they know that… often comes before was to or would: Little did I know that the decision was to change my life.

Examples

  1. I knew he would never see her again. (would)
  2. We were about to have dinner when the guests arrived. (about to, interrupted)
  3. The train was just about to leave when we got to the platform. (about to)
  4. He was on the point of resigning when they offered him a promotion. (on the point of)
  5. She was to become one of the most famous writers of her time. (destiny)
  6. The new hospital was to have been opened in 2020, but the work was delayed. (failed plan, passive)
  7. I thought it would be easy, but it wasn't. (would after think)
  8. I couldn't believe what I was about to see. (about to)
  9. The president was to visit the factory the next day. (official plan)
  10. At that moment, I didn't know the decision was to change my life. (destiny)

Common mistakes

❌ I was about leaving when you called.

✅ I was about to leave when you called. (About to + base form.)

❌ She was on the point to give up.

✅ She was on the point of giving up. (On the point of + -ing.)

❌ He said he will call me.

✅ He said he would call me. (Future from a past point of view: would.)

❌ They were to married in 2000.

✅ They were to marry in 2000. (Was / were to + base form.)
