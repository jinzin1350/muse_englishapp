Verbs + Prepositions (depend on, belong to)

Level: B1 · Free grammar guide

Many verbs are followed by a fixed preposition before their object: It depends on the weather. This bag belongs to me. I'm waiting for the bus.

After the preposition, use a noun, a pronoun or verb-ing: She apologised for being late.

Common verbs + prepositions

PrepositionVerbsExample
ondepend on, rely on, insist on, concentrate onYou can rely on me.
tobelong to, listen to, talk to, apologise to, happen toWho does this belong to?
forwait for, look for, pay for, apologise for, ask forCan I pay for this by card?
aboutworry about, complain about, talk about, dream aboutDon't worry about it.
inbelieve in, succeed in, specialise inDo you believe in luck?
ofconsist of, think of, dream ofThe team consists of five people.
atlook at, laugh at, arrive at (a place)Why are you laughing at me?
withagree with, deal with, share withI agree with you.

Arrive in a city or country, arrive at a building or place: We arrived in Rome. We arrived at the hotel. Never arrive to.

Verbs with no preposition

Some verbs take a direct object with no preposition, even though your language may use one: discuss the plan (not discuss about), marry someone (not marry with), enter the room, call / phone someone, reach the top, answer the question.

Different preposition, different meaning

  • look at (with your eyes), look for (try to find), look after (take care of)
  • think about (consider), think of (have an idea or opinion): What do you think of my new hat?
  • agree with a person, agree to a plan or request: They agreed to our offer.

Examples

  1. The price depends on the season. (depend on)
  2. I've been waiting for you for an hour! (wait for)
  3. She apologised for the mistake. (apologise for)
  4. Who's going to look after the dog? (look after = take care of)
  5. I'm looking for my glasses. (look for = try to find)
  6. Do you believe in ghosts? (believe in)
  7. He finally succeeded in passing his driving test. (succeed in + -ing)
  8. My grandfather complains about everything. (complain about)
  9. We discussed the problem for hours. (no preposition)
  10. She married a doctor. (no preposition)

Common mistakes

❌ It depends of the price.

✅ It depends on the price. (Depend on.)

❌ We discussed about the plan.

✅ We discussed the plan. (Discuss has no preposition.)

❌ She married with an engineer.

✅ She married an engineer. (Marry has no preposition (but: get married to).)

❌ We arrived to the airport at 6.

✅ We arrived at the airport at 6. (Arrive at a place, arrive in a city. Never arrive to.)

❌ I'm waiting the bus.

✅ I'm waiting for the bus. (Wait for.)

❌ Listen me!

✅ Listen to me! (Listen to.)

❌ She apologised for be late.

✅ She apologised for being late. (After a preposition, use -ing.)
