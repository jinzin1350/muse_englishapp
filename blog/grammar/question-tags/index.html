Question Tags (…, isn't it? …, don't you?)

B1 · B2 · C1

A question tag is a short question at the end of a sentence: It's a nice day, isn't it? We use tags to check information or to ask the listener to agree.

Usually, a positive sentence has a negative tag, and a negative sentence has a positive tag. The tag uses the same auxiliary as the sentence.

B1 · Intermediate

Positive sentence, negative tag (and the other way) B1

SentenceTag
You're a student,aren't you?
She can swim,can't she?
They live here,don't they? (present simple → do)
He went home,didn't he? (past simple → did)
You haven't finished,have you?
It won't rain,will it?

  • It's hot today, isn't it? (be)
  • You like football, don't you? (present simple → don't)
  • Your sister lives in Rome, doesn't she? (he/she → doesn't)
  • They didn't call, did they? (negative → positive)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ You're tired, isn't it? → ✅ You're tired, aren't you? (same verb and subject: aren't you)
  • ❌ She went home, wasn't she? → ✅ She went home, didn't she? (past simple → did)
  • ❌ Tom is here, isn't Tom? → ✅ Tom is here, isn't he? (tag uses a pronoun)

Intonation: a real question or asking for agreement? B1

  • Rising (↗): you don't know the answer and really ask: You're coming tonight, aren't you? ↗ (I'm not sure.)
  • Falling (↘): you're fairly sure and want the listener to agree: It's a lovely day, isn't it? ↘ (I'm sure it is.)

  • It's really cold, isn't it? ↘ (asking for agreement)
  • You've got the tickets, haven't you? ↗ (checking: I'm not sure)
  • That was a fantastic film, wasn't it? ↘ (agreement)
  • The meeting's at three, isn't it? ↗ (checking)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ It's a lovely day, is it? (asking for agreement) → ✅ It's a lovely day, isn't it? (positive sentence → negative tag)

Practice: B1

1. You're from Canada, ___?
Positive sentence with are → aren't you?
2. She works in a bank, ___?
Present simple → doesn't she?
3. They haven't arrived yet, ___? (two words)
Negative sentence → positive tag: have they?
4. You're not sure if your friend has a car. How do you say You've got a car, haven't you?
A real question: rising.
5. What does falling intonation on a tag usually mean?
Falling = asking for agreement.
6. You and a friend are looking at a sunset. You say: It's beautiful, ___? (two words)
Asking for agreement (falling intonation).

B2 · Upper-intermediate

Special tags: aren't I, let's… shall we, imperatives, there is B2

SentenceTag
I'm late,aren't I? (not amn't I)
Let's go,shall we?
Open the window,will you? / can you? / could you?
Don't be late,will you?
There's a problem,isn't there?
Everybody's here,aren't they?
Nothing happened,did it?

  • I'm right, aren't I? (I am → aren't I)
  • Let's have a break, shall we? (let's → shall we)
  • Pass me the salt, would you? (imperative → would you)
  • There's no milk, is there? (there is + negative word → is there)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I'm late, amn't I? → ✅ I'm late, aren't I? (I am → aren't I)
  • ❌ Let's go, don't we? → ✅ Let's go, shall we? (let's → shall we)
  • ❌ Nobody came, did he? → ✅ Nobody came, did they? (nobody → they)

Never, hardly, no one: negative words take a positive tag B2

  • You never listen to me, do you? (never → positive tag)
  • He hardly ever smiles, does he? (hardly → positive tag)
  • No one was hurt, were they? (no one → they)
  • There's nothing we can do, is there? (nothing → positive tag)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ You never call me, don't you? → ✅ You never call me, do you? (never → positive tag)
  • ❌ Nobody saw it, didn't they? → ✅ Nobody saw it, did they? (nobody → positive tag)

Practice: B2

7. I'm in the right room, ___?
I am → aren't I?
8. Let's watch a film, ___?
Let's → shall we?
9. There's a bank near here, ___? (two words)
There is → isn't there?
10. You've never been to Spain, ___?
Never = negative → positive tag.
11. She hardly ever goes out, ___?
Hardly = negative → positive tag.
12. Nobody called while I was out, ___? (two words)
Nobody → positive tag with they.

C1 · Advanced

Same-way tags and tags in requests C1

  • So you've finally arrived, have you? (surprise / irony)
  • You're moving to Japan, are you? How exciting! (interest)
  • You couldn't open the window, could you? (polite request)
  • I don't suppose you've seen my keys, have you? (tentative question)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ You couldn't help me, couldn't you? → ✅ You couldn't help me, could you? (negative sentence → positive tag)

Practice: C1

13. Which sentence shows surprise or interest?
Positive + positive tag = surprise or interest.
14. Which is the most polite request?
Negative sentence + positive tag sounds tentative and polite.
15. I don't suppose you know the answer, ___? (two words)
I don't suppose…, do you?
