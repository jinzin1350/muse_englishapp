Be About To, Be Due To and Be To

B1 · B2 · C1

Apart from will and going to, English has other phrases for the future. They add meaning: be about to (very soon), be due to (scheduled), be to (official plans and orders).

Many of them are common in the news and in formal writing. For the main future forms, see Will vs Going To.

B1 · Intermediate

Be about to: very soon B1

  • Quick, get inside! It's about to rain. (very soon)
  • The shop is about to close. Please make your final purchases. (very soon)
  • I was about to leave when the phone rang. (interrupted plan)
  • We were about to give up when we found the key. (nearly happened)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The film is about starting. → ✅ The film is about to start. (about to + base verb)
  • ❌ The bus is about to leave tomorrow. → ✅ The bus leaves tomorrow. / The bus is about to leave. (about to = immediately (no future time phrase))

Practice: B1

1. Sit down! The concert <span class="gap">___</span> about to begin.
Be about to + base verb.
2. I was <span class="gap">___</span> to go to bed when my sister called.
Was about to = I was just going to.
3. Hurry! The train is about <span class="gap">___</span> (leave).
About to + base verb.

B2 · Upper-intermediate

Be due to: scheduled events B2

  • The President is due to arrive at noon. (scheduled)
  • Your essay is due next Monday. (deadline)
  • The film was due to start at 8, but it started late. (planned, didn't happen on time)
  • When is the baby due? (expected birth)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The train is due arriving at six. → ✅ The train is due to arrive at six. (due to + base verb)

Practice: B2

4. The plane is <span class="gap">___</span> to land in ten minutes.
Scheduled: due to.
5. What does The report is due on Friday mean?
Due = expected / the deadline.
6. The new metro line <span class="gap">___</span> (be / due / open) in 2027.
Scheduled: is due to open.

C1 · Advanced

On the point of, on the verge of, be set to C1

PhraseMeaningExample
be on the point of + -ingvery close toI was on the point of leaving when she arrived.
be on the verge of + -ing / nounvery close to (often emotional or major)She was on the verge of tears.
be set to + verbexpected to (news style)Fuel prices are set to rise.
be poised to + verbready to (news style)The team is poised to win the title.

  • The company is on the point of closing down. (very close)
  • Scientists are on the verge of a major discovery. (very close (major))
  • Unemployment is set to fall this year. (news: expected)
  • The government is poised to announce new rules. (news: ready to)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I was on the point to leave. → ✅ I was on the point of leaving. (on the point of + -ing)
  • ❌ Prices are set rising. → ✅ Prices are set to rise. (set to + base verb)

Be to: official plans and orders C1

  • The Queen is to open the new hospital on Monday. (official plan)
  • You are not to tell anyone about this. (order)
  • If the city is to grow, it needs better transport. (if it wants to)
  • The two leaders are to meet in Geneva. (news)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The President is to visiting Canada. → ✅ The President is to visit Canada. (be to + base verb)

Was to and was to have done: destiny and plans that failed C1

  • Little did he know that this was to be his last match. (destiny)
  • The hotel was to have opened in 2020, but the pandemic stopped it. (failed plan)
  • They were to marry the following spring. (planned)
  • It was to change her life forever. (destiny)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The meeting was to have take place on Monday. → ✅ The meeting was to have taken place on Monday. (was to have + past participle)

Practice: C1

7. He was on the point <span class="gap">___</span> when he suddenly changed his mind.
On the point of + -ing.
8. Which phrase is most typical of a news headline?
Set to is typical news style.
9. The two countries are on the <span class="gap">___</span> of war. (one word: very close to)
On the verge / brink of.
10. News: The government <span class="gap">___</span> to build 10,000 new homes.
Official plan: is to.
11. What does You are to stay in your rooms mean?
Be to = an order.
12. If we <span class="gap">___</span> (be) to win the match, we need to score early.
If … are to = if we want to.
13. In 1975 he wrote a small book that <span class="gap">___</span> make him famous.
Destiny (seen from the past): was to.
14. What does The bridge was to have been finished last year mean?
Was to have been = a plan that failed.
15. The concert <span class="gap">___</span> (be) to have taken place on Saturday, but the singer was ill.
Was to have + past participle.
