فعل‌های وجهی برای حدس و استنتاجModals of Deduction and Probability

We use modal verbs to show how sure we are about something, based on what we know: She must be at home (I'm sure). She might be at home (perhaps). She can't be at home (I'm sure she isn't).

The same verbs talk about the past with have + past participle: She must have left early. This page also covers stronger and more formal ways to talk about probability, like bound to and likely to. For might as perhaps in general, see May and Might.

B1 · B2 · C1

B1 · متوسط

Must and can't: I'm sure B1

must = I'm sure it's true. can't = I'm sure it isn't true. He's been working all day. He must be tired. That can't be Tom. He's in Paris.

How sure?ModalExample
I'm sure it's truemustThe lights are on. They must be at home.
I'm sure it isn't truecan'tShe can't be 50. She looks so young!

We use must when we have a good reason (evidence) to be sure: You've been travelling for 20 hours. You must be exhausted.

For the negative, use can't (or couldn't), not mustn't: That can't be right. Not That mustn't be right (in British English, mustn't means not allowed).

In American English, must not is also used for negative deductions: He must not be home. His car is gone. In British English, can't is the usual choice.

  • You must be Mina's brother. You look just like her. (I'm sure)
  • This can't be the right address. It's a car park! (I'm sure it isn't)
  • There must be a mistake on the bill. (evidence: the total is wrong)
  • He can't be hungry. He's just eaten a huge pizza. (I'm sure he isn't)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ He mustn't be at home. The lights are off. (British English) → ✅ He can't be at home. The lights are off. (negative deduction: can't)
  • ❌ She must to be tired. → ✅ She must be tired. (must + base verb)

Might, may, could: it's possible B1

might / may / could = it's possible (perhaps true). couldn't = I'm sure it isn't. He might be at work. It could be a virus.

How sure?ModalsExample
possiblemight, may, couldShe might / may / could be at the library.
possible notmight not, may notShe might not be at home.
I'm sure it isn'tcan't, couldn'tShe couldn't be at home. I saw her at the gym.

Use might, may or could when you think something is possible but you don't have enough evidence to be sure: Where's my phone? ~ It could be in the car.

Careful with the negatives: might not / may not = perhaps not. Couldn't = I'm sure it isn't (like can't). He might not be at home (perhaps he isn't) is different from He couldn't be at home (impossible).

  • The noise might be the wind. (possible)
  • Don't eat that. It could be poisonous. (possible)
  • She may not know the answer. (perhaps not)
  • It couldn't be John. He's in hospital. (impossible)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ She could not be at home, I'm not sure. → ✅ She might not be at home. I'm not sure. (perhaps not: might not)
  • ❌ It can be the postman at the door. → ✅ It might / could be the postman at the door. (a particular possibility: might / could)

تمرین سطح B1

1. You've been walking all day. Your feet ___ be really sore.
I'm sure, from the evidence: must.
2. That ___ be Sara's car. She sold it last year.
I'm sure it isn't: can't.
3. Ahmad has three jobs. He ___ (must / be) very busy.
I'm sure: must be.
4. I'm not sure where Ali is. He ___ be in the library.
Possible, not sure: might.
5. Which sentence means perhaps she isn't at home?
Might not = perhaps not.
6. Someone's at the door. It ___ (could / be) the delivery man.
Possible: could be.

B2 · بالاتر از متوسط

Must be doing, might be doing, can't be doing B2

For actions in progress now, use must / might / can't + be + -ing: She isn't answering. She must be driving.

To make a deduction about what is happening now, use modal + be + -ing: Listen! Someone must be playing the piano. He can't be sleeping. The light is on.

It also works for future arrangements: They might be coming tomorrow.

  • The phone's busy. She must be talking to her mother. (sure, now)
  • Why is he smiling? He might be thinking about his girlfriend. (possible, now)
  • You can't be working already! You only woke up five minutes ago. (sure not, now)
  • They could be waiting for us at the other entrance. (possible)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ She must sleeping. → ✅ She must be sleeping. (must + be + -ing)
  • ❌ He can't be work now. It's Sunday. → ✅ He can't be working now. It's Sunday. (be + -ing)

Must have done, can't have done: sure about the past B2

must have + past participle = I'm sure it happened. can't / couldn't have + past participle = I'm sure it didn't happen.

How sure?FormExample
sure it happenedmust have + past participleThe ground is wet. It must have rained.
sure it didn't happencan't have / couldn't have + past participleHe can't have seen me. He didn't say hello.

For deductions about the past, use modal + have + past participle: She must have left early. Her coat isn't here.

Can't have and couldn't have mean the same in deductions: You can't / couldn't have finished already!

Must have is often pronounced /ˈmʌstəv/. Don't write must of: She must have forgotten.

  • The window is broken. Someone must have thrown a ball. (sure it happened)
  • You must have been so happy when you heard the news. (sure)
  • She can't have driven here. She doesn't have a car. (sure it didn't)
  • They couldn't have heard us. We were very quiet. (sure it didn't)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ He must went home. → ✅ He must have gone home. (must have + past participle)
  • ❌ She can't have saw it. → ✅ She can't have seen it. (past participle: seen)
  • ❌ You must of been tired. → ✅ You must have been tired. (have, not of)

Might have done, could have done: possible in the past B2

might / may / could have + past participle = perhaps it happened. might not have = perhaps it didn't. She might have missed the bus.

When we think something possibly happened in the past, use might / may / could have + past participle: Where's Leila? ~ She might have gone home. She could have missed the train.

For perhaps it didn't happen, use might not have / may not have: He may not have received my email. (Remember: couldn't have = I'm sure it didn't.)

Could have also describes something that was possible but didn't happen: You were lucky. You could have been killed! (but you weren't)

  • I can't find my wallet. I might have left it in the taxi. (perhaps)
  • She may not have heard the doorbell. (perhaps not)
  • Be careful! You could have fallen! (possible but didn't happen)
  • They could have taken a different road. (possible explanation)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ He might not have came. → ✅ He might not have come. (past participle: come)
  • ❌ She couldn't have got my message, but I'm not sure. → ✅ She might not have got my message. I'm not sure. (perhaps not: might not have)

تمرین سطح B2

7. The kids are very quiet. They ___ something naughty!
Sure, now: must be doing.
8. Sara ___. I can hear her singing in the shower.
Sure it isn't happening: can't be sleeping.
9. Don't call Ali now. He ___ (might / have) dinner.
Possible, now: might be having.
10. The streets are wet. It ___ in the night.
Sure it happened: must have rained.
11. Tom didn't study at all, but he got 98%. He ___ the answers before the exam!
Sure it happened: must have seen.
12. I can't find my keys. I ___ (must / leave) them at the office.
Sure it happened: must have left.
13. Hassan isn't here yet. He ___ the earlier train.
Perhaps it happened: might have missed.
14. What does You could have been hurt! mean?
Could have = possible but didn't happen.
15. I'm not sure why she's angry. I ___ (might / say) something wrong.
Perhaps: might have said.

C1 · پیشرفته

That'll be the postman: will and should for assumptions C1

will / won't for confident assumptions (That'll be Ali at the door). should for what we expect (He should be home by now).

Will can make a confident assumption about the present, based on what we know about routines and habits: The doorbell's ringing. That'll be the postman. (He always comes at this time.)

Will have + past participle does the same for the past: You'll have heard the news, I suppose.

Should shows what we expect, often based on plans: The flight should have landed by now. (That's what's expected.) See Should and Ought To.

From most to least sure: must / will → should → may / might / could → can't / won't (sure it isn't).

  • Someone's at the door. That'll be my sister. (confident assumption)
  • Don't call him now. He'll be asleep. (I know his habits)
  • You'll have seen the photos on Instagram. (assumption about the past)
  • It's 6 pm. Dad should be leaving work about now. (expectation)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ That will to be John. → ✅ That will be John. (will + base verb)

Bound to, likely to, may well: other ways to show probability C1

be bound to / be sure to = certain. be likely to = probable. be unlikely to = not probable. may well / could well = quite possible.

ExpressionHow sure?Example
be bound to / be sure to / be certain tocertainIt's bound to rain at the weekend.
be likely to / there's a good chance (that)probablePrices are likely to rise.
may well / could well / might wellquite possibleShe may well be right.
be unlikely to / there's little chance (that)not probableHe's unlikely to win.

These phrases are common in news, reports and formal discussions about the future.

Word order: likely is an adjective (She is likely to win), but probably is an adverb (She will probably win). Don't say She will likely win in British English (it is common in American English).

Bound to can also suggest it always happens: If you leave food out, the dog's bound to eat it.

  • You've studied so hard. You're bound to pass. (certain)
  • The new law is unlikely to change much. (not probable)
  • There's a good chance we'll be late. (probable)
  • The final could well be the best match of the year. (quite possible)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ She is likely win. → ✅ She is likely to win. (likely to + verb)
  • ❌ It's bound rain. → ✅ It's bound to rain. (bound to + verb)

تمرین سطح C1

16. The phone's ringing. ___ be Mum. She always calls on Sundays.
Confident assumption: That'll be.
17. What does You'll have heard the news mean?
Will have + past participle = an assumption about the past.
18. Don't phone Sara now. She ___ (will / be) at the gym. She always goes at seven.
Confident assumption: will be.
19. It's a holiday weekend. The roads ___ be very busy.
Certain: be bound to.
20. Which sentence means it probably won't happen?
Unlikely to = not probable.
21. Experts say house prices are ___ to fall next year. (one word: the opposite of unlikely)
Likely to = probable.
