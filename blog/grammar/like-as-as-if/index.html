Like, As and As If

A2 · B1 · B2 · C1

Like means similar to: She sings like a professional. As means in the role of (He works as a waiter) or in the same way + clause (Do as I say). As if describes how something seems: It looks as if it's going to rain.

For unreal as if + past (He talks as if he knew everything), see Unreal Past.

A2 · Elementary

Like (similar to) or as (a job or role)? A2

like = similar toas = in the role of
He talks like a teacher. (He isn't a teacher.)He works as a teacher. (He is a teacher.)
She looks like her mother.I used a box as a table.
It tastes like chicken.She's famous as a singer.

  • My brother eats like a horse. (similar to)
  • I worked as a waiter when I was a student. (a real job)
  • She looks like a film star. (similar to)
  • We used the sofa as a bed. (a use)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ He works like a taxi driver. (it's his job) → ✅ He works as a taxi driver. (a real job: as)
  • ❌ My son is as his father. → ✅ My son is like his father. (similar to: like)

B1 · Intermediate

As I said, as usual, do as I say B1

PhraseExample
as + clause (in the same way)Do it as I showed you.
as I said / as you know / as we agreedAs I said, the meeting is at 3.
as usualHe was late, as usual.
as expected / as plannedEverything went as planned.
like + clause (informal)Like I said, it's not my problem.

  • As you can see, the room is very small. (as you can see)
  • She was wearing black, as usual. (as usual)
  • Please do as you're told! (in the same way)
  • The project was finished as planned. (as planned)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Like usual, he forgot his keys. → ✅ As usual, he forgot his keys. (as usual)
  • ❌ As I've told you before, but you don't listen. → ✅ As I've told you before, you need to listen. (as + clause, then the main clause)

Look like, look happy, what's it like? B1

PatternExample
look / sound / feel + adjectiveThat sounds great. You look happy.
look / sound / feel + like + nounThat sounds like a good idea. She looks like her sister.
What's … like? (description)What's your new boss like? ~ She's very friendly.
What does … look like? (appearance)What does he look like? ~ He's tall with dark hair.

  • You look worried. Is everything OK? (look + adjective)
  • This sounds like a great plan. (sound like + noun)
  • What's the weather like in Toronto? (description)
  • What does your brother look like? (appearance)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ You look like tired. → ✅ You look tired. (look + adjective (no like))
  • ❌ How is your new teacher like? → ✅ What is your new teacher like? (what … like?)

B2 · Upper-intermediate

Look as if, sound as though, feel like B2

PatternExample
It looks as if / as though + clauseIt looks as if they've gone out.
You sound as if + clauseYou sound as if you're tired.
It seems as if + clauseIt seems as though nobody cares.
I feel as if / like + clauseI feel like I've known you for years. (informal)
It looks like + clause (informal)It looks like it's going to snow.

  • It looks as if the shop is closed. (seems true)
  • You sound as though you need a holiday. (sound as though)
  • It feels like it's going to be a long day. (informal like)
  • She looked as if she'd seen a ghost. (past)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ It looks as it's going to snow. → ✅ It looks as if it's going to snow. (as if + clause)
  • ❌ You seem as you're worried. → ✅ You seem as if you're worried. (seem as if)

Practice: B2

12. There are no lights on. It looks as <span class="gap">___</span> they've gone away. (one word)
As if / as though.

C1 · Advanced

Such as, unlike, alike, likewise, as is often the case C1

WordExampleMeaning
such as / likeI love fruit such as mangoes and kiwis.for example
unlikeUnlike my brother, I hate sport.different from
alikeThe twins look alike. Students and teachers alike enjoyed it.similar / both
likewiseShe worked hard. Likewise, her team gave 100%.in the same way
as is often the caseAs is often the case, the cheapest option was the worst.as usually happens
as can be seenAs can be seen in Figure 2, sales rose.formal

  • Big cities, such as Tokyo and London, are expensive. (examples)
  • Unlike most of my friends, I don't have a car. (different from)
  • My sister and I think alike. (similar)
  • As was expected, the team won easily. (formal as clause)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The two brothers are very like. → ✅ The two brothers are very alike. (after be: alike)
  • ❌ Alike his father, he became a doctor. → ✅ Like his father, he became a doctor. (similar to: like)
