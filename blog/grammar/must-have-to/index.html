must و have to (اجبار)Must and Have To (Obligation)

We use must and have to to say that something is necessary: I have to get up early tomorrow. You must wear a helmet.

Their negatives are very different: You mustn't smoke here (it's not allowed) but You don't have to come (it isn't necessary). For advice, see Should and Ought To.

A2 · مقدماتی

Have to: it's necessary A2

have to / has to + base verb = it's necessary: I have to work on Saturday. She has to wear a uniform.

I / you / we / theyhe / she / it
PositiveI have to go.She has to go.
NegativeI don't have to go.She doesn't have to go.
QuestionDo you have to go?Does she have to go?

Have to works like a normal verb: it uses do / does for questions and negatives: Do you have to work tomorrow?

It is very common for rules, routines and things that other people decide: In my job I have to answer a lot of emails.

Pronunciation: in fast speech, have to sounds like /ˈhæftə/ and has to like /ˈhæstə/.

  • I have to take the bus to work. (it's necessary)
  • My brother has to wear glasses. (he / she / it: has to)
  • Do we have to pay for parking? (question)
  • You don't have to bring anything. (not necessary)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ She have to work today. → ✅ She has to work today. (he / she / it: has to)
  • ❌ Do you have go? → ✅ Do you have to go? (have + to)
  • ❌ I must to go now. → ✅ I have to go now. (or I must go now.) (must has no to)

Must: strong obligation and strong advice A2

must + base verb for strong obligation, often the speaker's own feeling, and for rules: I must call my mum. You must wear a seat belt.

Must is the same for every person and has no to: I must go, she must go. Not She musts go or I must to go.

We use must for:

  • things we feel are necessary: I must remember to call Lisa.
  • rules and signs, often in writing: Passengers must wear a seat belt.
  • strong recommendations: You must try this cake. It's amazing!

Must is only used for the present and future. For the past, use had to: Yesterday I had to work late. Not I musted. See Must or have to?

  • I must finish this essay tonight. (my own feeling)
  • Visitors must sign in at reception. (a written rule)
  • You must visit Isfahan. It's beautiful. (strong recommendation)
  • We must have dinner together soon. (friendly suggestion)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ She musts leave now. → ✅ She must leave now. (must: no -s)
  • ❌ You must to see this film. → ✅ You must see this film. (must + base verb (no to))
  • ❌ I must went to the bank yesterday. → ✅ I had to go to the bank yesterday. (past: had to)

Mustn't or don't have to? A2

mustn't = it's not allowed / don't do it. don't have to = it's not necessary (you can if you want).

mustn'tdon't have to
You mustn't smoke here. (It's forbidden.)You don't have to come. (It isn't necessary.)
You mustn't tell anyone. (Don't tell anyone!)You don't have to tell me. (You can if you want.)
Students mustn't use phones in the exam.Students don't have to wear a uniform.

Mustn't (must not) means you have an obligation not to do something: You mustn't park here.

Don't / doesn't have to means there is no obligation: You don't have to pay. It's free. You can do it, but it isn't necessary.

This is one of the most common mistakes. Ask yourself: Is it forbidden? → mustn't. Is it optional? → don't have to.

  • You mustn't touch that wire. It's dangerous. (forbidden)
  • You don't have to take your shoes off. It's fine. (not necessary)
  • We mustn't be late for the interview. (it's important not to)
  • She doesn't have to work on Fridays. (no obligation)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ You don't have to smoke in the hospital. (meaning it's forbidden) → ✅ You mustn't smoke in the hospital. (forbidden: mustn't)
  • ❌ It's free. You mustn't pay. → ✅ It's free. You don't have to pay. (not necessary: don't have to)

B1 · متوسط

Must or have to? B1

must often = the speaker thinks it's necessary. have to = a rule or situation makes it necessary. For the past and future, use had to / will have to.

musthave to
I must stop eating sweets. (I decide)I have to wear a uniform. (the school decides)
You must come and see us. (I want you to)You have to show your passport. (it's the law)

In many sentences both are possible, especially in British English. But there's often a difference:

  • must = the speaker's feeling or decision: I must call her. (I think it's important.)
  • have to = an outside rule or situation: I have to call her. It's my job.

In American English, have to (or have got to) is much more common than must for obligation.

Have got to means the same as have to, and it's common in informal speech: I've got to go. It's only used in the present.

  • I must go to bed earlier. I'm always tired. (my decision)
  • I have to be at work by eight. My boss is strict. (an outside rule)
  • This is a great book. You must read it. (speaker's recommendation)
  • In Canada you have to be 16 to drive. (a law)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I must wear a uniform at work. (the company decides) → ✅ I have to wear a uniform at work. (outside rule: have to (more natural))
  • ❌ I've got to went yesterday. → ✅ I had to go yesterday. (past: had to)

Had to, will have to, have had to B1

Must has no past or future form. Use had to for the past, will have to for the future, have had to for the present perfect.

TimeFormExample
Pasthad toI had to work late yesterday.
Past negativedidn't have toWe didn't have to pay.
Futurewill have toYou'll have to wait.
Present perfecthave / has had toI've had to cancel my trip.
After modalsmight / may have toWe might have to move.

Questions in the past: Did you have to wait long?

For the future, must and have to are possible when the decision is made now: I must / have to leave early tomorrow. Use will have to when the obligation will exist in the future: If you fail, you'll have to take the exam again.

  • When I was a child, I had to walk to school. (past obligation)
  • Did you have to show your passport? (past question)
  • You'll have to book a table. It's always busy. (future)
  • She's had to work every weekend this month. (present perfect)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I musted work yesterday. → ✅ I had to work yesterday. (past: had to)
  • ❌ Did you had to wait? → ✅ Did you have to wait? (did + have to)

Need to, don't need to, needn't B1

need to = it's necessary. don't need to / needn't = it isn't necessary: You needn't come. You don't need to come.

Need to works like a normal verb: I need to buy some milk. Do you need to leave?

For no necessity, use don't need to or needn't (more British, a bit formal): You don't need to shout. / You needn't shout. Both mean the same as don't have to.

Needn't is followed by the base verb with no to: You needn't worry. Not You needn't to worry.

For the past, didn't need to and needn't have have different meanings. See Should Have, Could Have, Needn't Have.

  • I need to charge my phone. (necessary)
  • You don't need to bring a towel. The hotel has them. (not necessary)
  • You needn't worry. Everything is fine. (not necessary (BrE))
  • Do I need to book in advance? (question)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ You needn't to come. → ✅ You needn't come. (needn't + base verb)
  • ❌ She doesn't needs to work. → ✅ She doesn't need to work. (doesn't + need)
  • ❌ Need I to bring anything? → ✅ Do I need to bring anything? (question: Do I need to…?)

C1 · پیشرفته

Formal obligation: be required to, be obliged to, be to C1

Formal ways to talk about obligation: be required to, be obliged to, be compelled to. Orders and rules: be to (You are to report at 9). Prohibition: be not permitted / be prohibited from.

In formal writing (rules, contracts, official letters) must and have to are often replaced by:

  • be required to + verb: All staff are required to complete the training.
  • be obliged to + verb: The company is obliged to inform its customers. (often legal)
  • be compelled / forced to + verb: something makes you do it against your wishes: He was compelled to resign.

Be to + verb gives an official order or instruction: You are to report to the manager at 9 am. Nobody is to leave the building.

For prohibition: be not permitted to, be prohibited from + -ing, be forbidden to / from: Smoking is not permitted. Visitors are prohibited from taking photos.

In everyday speech these sound very formal. Use them in writing, notices and official contexts.

  • Applicants are required to submit two references. (formal requirement)
  • The landlord is obliged to repair the heating. (legal duty)
  • Guests are not permitted to bring food into the rooms. (prohibition)
  • You are to stay here until the police arrive. (official order)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ Passengers are required wear seat belts. → ✅ Passengers are required to wear seat belts. (required to + verb)
  • ❌ Visitors are prohibited to take photos. → ✅ Visitors are prohibited from taking photos. (prohibited from + -ing)

تمرین سطح C1

19. All employees ___ to wear protective clothing.
Be required to + verb.
20. Visitors are prohibited ___ the animals.
Prohibited from + -ing.
21. You ___ (be) to report to the main office at 8 am tomorrow.
Be to = an official instruction.
