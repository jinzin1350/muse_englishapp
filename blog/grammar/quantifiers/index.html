Quantifiers: All, Both, Either, Neither, None, Few

Level: B1 · Free grammar guide

Quantifiers say how many or how much: All my friends came. Neither of us knew the answer. None of the shops were open.

Some quantifiers talk about two things (both, either, neither) and some about three or more (all, most, none).

Two things: both, either, neither

WordMeaningExample
boththe two togetherBoth my parents are doctors.
eitherone or the other (it doesn't matter which)You can sit at either end.
neithernot one and not the otherNeither answer is correct.

Either and neither + singular noun: neither answer. With of + plural: neither of the answers. After either of / neither of, the verb is often singular in formal English and plural in speech: Neither of them is / are ready. Both are used.

Three or more: all, most, some, none

WordExample
allAll (of) the students passed.
mostMost people like chocolate. Most of my friends live here.
someSome of the questions were hard.
noneNone of my friends was / were there.

None of + plural noun can take a singular or a plural verb: None of the shops was / were open. Both are correct.

Directly before a noun, use no, not none: There are no buses. None is used alone or with of: None of the buses… – How many buses? – None.

When do you need of?

Use of before the, my, these and other determiners, and before pronouns: Most of the people… All of them… Both of us… Without a determiner, don't use of: Most people like music. (not Most of people)

With all and both, of is optional before the / my: all (of) the students, both (of) my sisters. Before a pronoun you always need it: both of them, all of us, neither of you.

Each and every

Each = every one, seen separately (two or more): Each student has a book. Every = all the members of a group of three or more: Every room has a balcony. Both take a singular noun and verb.

Few or a few?

A few = some, a small number (positive). Few = not many, almost none (negative): I have a few friends here, so I'm happy. / I have few friends here, so I'm lonely. See also Much, Many, A Lot Of, A Few, A Little.

Examples

  1. Both my brothers live in Canada. (two people)
  2. Neither of my parents speaks English. (not one and not the other)
  3. You can pay by card or in cash. Either is fine. (one or the other)
  4. All the shops close at 9 p.m. (three or more)
  5. Most people prefer summer to winter. (no of without the)
  6. None of the questions were easy. (none of + plural)
  7. Each student gets a certificate. (singular noun)
  8. Every room has a balcony. (singular noun and verb)
  9. Very few people came to the meeting. (few = not many)
  10. Both of us were tired after the trip. (pronoun → of)

Common mistakes

❌ Most of people like music.

✅ Most people like music. (No of without the, my, these…)

❌ Both of my sister are teachers.

✅ Both of my sisters are teachers. (Both + plural noun.)

❌ Neither answers is correct.

✅ Neither answer is correct. (Neither + singular noun.)

❌ There are none buses after midnight.

✅ There are no buses after midnight. (Before a noun, use no.)

❌ Every students have a book.

✅ Every student has a book. (Every + singular noun and verb.)

❌ All of they came.

✅ All of them came. (After of, use an object pronoun: them.)

❌ I have few friends here, so I'm very happy.

✅ I have a few friends here, so I'm very happy. (A few = some (positive). Few = almost none.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. ___ my parents are teachers: my mum and my dad.
Two people together: Both.
2. ___ of the students passed the exam. It was too difficult.
Not one student passed: None of…
3. You can take ___ bus. They both go to the station.
One or the other, it doesn't matter: either + singular noun.
4. ___ people enjoy music.
In general, without the: Most people (no of).
5. There are ___ trains on Sundays.
Directly before a noun: no.
6. Choose the correct sentence.
All + of + object pronoun: all of them.
7. ___ student has a locker.
Singular noun: Every student.
8. I invited Sam and Lea, but ___ of them came.
Two people, and not one of them came: neither.
9. ___ answer is correct. They're both wrong.
Not one and not the other: Neither + singular noun.
10. Very ___ people came to the meeting, so it was cancelled.
Almost nobody (negative meaning): few.
11. ___ of my sisters live abroad.
Two sisters + plural verb live: Both.
12. I've got ___ friends in this city, so I'm never lonely.
Some friends (positive meaning): a few.
13. How many tickets are left? – ___. They're all sold.
Used alone, without a noun: None.
14. We can meet on Monday or Tuesday. ___ day is fine for me.
One of the two, it doesn't matter which: Either.
15. Each room ___ a TV.
Each + singular noun + singular verb: has.
16. Most ___ my friends have cars.
Before my: most of my friends.
