Quantifiers: All, Most, Each, Every, None

A2 · B1 · B2 · C1

Quantifiers tell us how many or how much: all, most, some, none, each, every, both, either, neither. Many can be used with or without of: most people / most of the people.

For much, many, a few and a little, see Much, Many, A Few, A Little. For both… and and either… or, see Both… And, Either… Or.

A2 · Elementary

All, most, some, no: with or without of A2

General (no of)Specific (of + the / my / these…)
All children love games.All (of) the children in my class are friendly.
Most people work hard.Most of the people I know work hard.
Some shops close on Sundays.Some of the shops in my street are closed.
No students passed.None of the students passed.

  • Most people in Canada speak English. (general)
  • Most of my friends are married. (specific)
  • All of us were tired. (pronoun)
  • None of the shops were open. (none of)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Most of people like chocolate. → ✅ Most people like chocolate. (general: no of)
  • ❌ Most the students passed. → ✅ Most of the students passed. (specific: most of the)
  • ❌ All of them is here. → ✅ All of them are here. (plural verb)

Practice: A2

1. <span class="gap">___</span> people like holidays.
In general: most + noun.
2. <span class="gap">___</span> my friends live in Toronto.
Specific (my friends): most of.
3. I invited ten people, but none <span class="gap">___</span> them came. (one word)
With a pronoun: none of them.

B1 · Intermediate

Each or every? B1

EachEvery
one by one: Each player has a number.all together: Every player wants to win.
also for two: She had a ring on each hand.not for two (not every hand)
each of the students, each of themevery one of the students (not every of)
alone: They cost $5 each.repeated time: every day, every three hours

  • Every child needs love. (all children)
  • The teacher spoke to each student individually. (one by one)
  • Each of my parents has a car. (each of)
  • I go to the gym every other day. (regular time)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Every of the students has a laptop. → ✅ Each of the students has a laptop. (with of: each)
  • ❌ Every students are here. → ✅ Every student is here. (singular noun + singular verb)
  • ❌ I visit my parents each two weeks. → ✅ I visit my parents every two weeks. (repeated time: every)

Both, either, neither (of) B1

Word+ noun+ of
bothboth children (plural)both of the children, both of them
eithereither day (singular)either of the days, either of them
neitherneither answer (singular)neither of the answers, neither of them

  • Both my brothers live in Toronto. (both)
  • You can park on either side of the street. (either)
  • Neither restaurant was open. (neither)
  • I don't like either of these shirts. (negative: either)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Both of them is teachers. → ✅ Both of them are teachers. (both: plural verb)
  • ❌ Neither of them didn't come. → ✅ Neither of them came. (neither: positive verb)
  • ❌ I don't want neither of them. → ✅ I don't want either of them. (negative verb: either)

Practice: B1

4. <span class="gap">___</span> of the children got a small gift.
With of: each of.
5. I check my email <span class="gap">___</span> few hours.
Repeated time: every few hours.
6. We bought three tickets. They cost $20 <span class="gap">___</span>, so $60 in total. (one word)
Alone at the end: each.
7. I have two sisters. <span class="gap">___</span> of them are doctors.
The one and the other: both (+ plural).
8. Tuesday or Wednesday? ~ <span class="gap">___</span> day is fine for me. I'm free all week.
One or the other: either + singular.
9. I called Ali and Reza, but <span class="gap">___</span> of them answered. (one word)
Not one and not the other: neither.

B2 · Upper-intermediate

All or whole? All, everything, no and none B2

PatternExample
the / my whole + singularI spent the whole day at home. She ate the whole pizza.
all (the) + uncountable / pluralall the money, all day, all my friends
everything aloneEverything is fine. (All is fine sounds old-fashioned)
all + relative clauseAll I want is some rest. (= the only thing)
no + noun / none aloneThere's no milk. ~ How much is there? None.
all after the subjectWe all agreed. They're all here.

  • I read the whole book in one night. (whole + singular)
  • All my friends came. (all + plural)
  • Everything went well. (everything)
  • All we need is a little more time. (all = the only thing)
  • How many tickets are left? ~ None. (none alone)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ She spent whole day shopping. → ✅ She spent the whole day shopping. (the whole)
  • ❌ There are none tickets. → ✅ There are no tickets. (+ noun: no)

Practice: B2

10. I was so hungry that I ate <span class="gap">___</span> pizza.
The whole + singular noun.
11. How much money is left? ~ <span class="gap">___</span>. We spent it all.
Alone: none.
12. I didn't sleep at all last night. I was awake <span class="gap">___</span> night. (one word)
All night = the whole night.

C1 · Advanced

None of them is or are? All of whom, each and every C1

PatternExample
none of + pluralNone of the candidates was / were suitable. (singular = formal)
quantifier + of whom / whichShe has three sons, all of whom are doctors. I bought two books, neither of which was cheap.
each and everyEach and every one of you has helped.
not all vs noneNot all students passed. (some failed) / None of the students passed.
every other / every fewevery other day (= every second day), every few weeks
all of + pronounall of us (not all us); us all after a verb: She thanked us all.

  • None of the reports has been published yet. (formal: singular)
  • I have two brothers, both of whom live abroad. (both of whom)
  • Not all doctors agree with this. (some don't)
  • I see my parents every other weekend. (every second)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I have three sisters, all of them are married. → ✅ I have three sisters, all of whom are married. (in one sentence: of whom)
  • ❌ All us were tired. → ✅ All of us were tired. (all of + pronoun)

Practice: C1

13. He has three daughters, <span class="gap">___</span> are lawyers.
In one sentence: all of whom.
14. Which sentence means that some students passed and some failed?
Not all = some did, some didn't.
15. I go to the gym every <span class="gap">___</span> day: Monday, Wednesday, Friday… (one word)
Every other day = every second day.
