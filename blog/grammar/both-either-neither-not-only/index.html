Both, Either, Neither and Not Only

Level: B1 · Free grammar guide

We use both, either and neither to talk about two people or things: Both my sisters live in Toronto. You can have either tea or coffee. Neither Tom nor Anna came.

Not only… but also adds a second, often surprising, point: She speaks not only English but also Chinese.

Both, either, neither

WordMeaningExample
both… andA and B (two)Both my brother and my sister are doctors.
either… orA or B (one of two)We can go either by bus or by train.
neither… nornot A and not BNeither my phone nor my laptop is working.
not only… but alsoA and also BThe hotel was not only cheap but also comfortable.

With neither… nor and either… or, the verb usually agrees with the noun closest to it: Neither my parents nor my sister is coming.

Both of, either of, neither of

Before the, my, these or a pronoun, use of: Both of my parents. Either of these days is fine. Neither of us likes coffee.

  • both + plural verb: Both of them are here.
  • either + singular noun: Either day is fine for me.
  • neither of + plural noun: the verb can be singular (more formal) or plural (informal): Neither of my friends is / are coming.

Neither already has a negative meaning, so don't add not: Neither of them came (not Neither of them didn't come).

Too, either and neither in short answers

StatementAgreeing
I love pizza. (positive)Me too. / So do I. / I love it too.
I don't like horror films. (negative)Me neither. / Neither do I. / I don't like them either.

Use too in positive sentences and either in negative sentences: I don't eat meat either (not too).

Examples

  1. Both my parents were born in Italy. (both + plural noun)
  2. Both of us love hiking. (both of + pronoun)
  3. You can pay either by card or in cash. (either… or)
  4. Which colour do you want? – Either is fine. (either = one or the other)
  5. Neither Sam nor Lisa knew the answer. (neither… nor)
  6. I invited two friends, but neither of them came. (neither of)
  7. He's not only a great teacher but also a talented musician. (not only… but also)
  8. I can't swim. – Neither can I. (short answer)
  9. I don't like coffee. – I don't like it either. (either in negatives)
  10. We visited both Rome and Florence. (both… and)

Common mistakes

❌ Neither Tom or Anna came.

✅ Neither Tom nor Anna came. (Neither goes with nor.)

❌ Both my sister and I am students.

✅ Both my sister and I are students. (Both + plural verb.)

❌ I don't like it too.

✅ I don't like it either. (In negative sentences: either.)

❌ Neither of them didn't come.

✅ Neither of them came. (Neither is already negative.)

❌ Either days are fine.

✅ Either day is fine. (Either + singular noun.)

❌ She speaks not only English but also speaks Chinese.

✅ She speaks not only English but also Chinese. (Use the same kind of word after not only and but also.)
