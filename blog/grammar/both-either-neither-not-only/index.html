Both… And, Either… Or, Neither… Nor, Not Only… But Also

A2 · B1 · B2 · C1

These paired conjunctions join two words, phrases or clauses: I speak both English and Persian. We can go either today or tomorrow.

For both, either and neither on their own (both of them, either day), see Quantifiers.

A2 · Elementary

Both… and A2

JoinsExample
two nounsBoth Tehran and Toronto are big cities.
two adjectivesThe hotel was both cheap and clean.
two verbsHe both writes and directs his films.

  • Both my brother and my sister live in Vancouver. (two subjects)
  • The film was both funny and sad. (two adjectives)
  • I like both tea and coffee. (two nouns)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Both Ali and Sara is coming. → ✅ Both Ali and Sara are coming. (plural verb)
  • ❌ She is both intelligent or kind. → ✅ She is both intelligent and kind. (both… and)

Practice: A2

1. ___ my parents and my grandparents live in Shiraz.
Both… and.
2. Both Reza and Mina ___ doctors.
Two subjects: plural verb.
3. She can play both the piano ___ the guitar. (one word)
Both… and.

B1 · Intermediate

Either… or: one or the other B1

UseExample
a choiceYou can have either fish or chicken.
two verbsWe can either stay here or go out.
negativeI don't speak either French or German. (= I speak neither)
verb agreementEither Ali or his sisters are coming. Either his sisters or Ali is coming.

  • You can either call me or send an email. (choice)
  • Either you leave now, or I call the police. (strong choice)
  • She doesn't eat either meat or fish. (negative)
  • Either Monday or Tuesday is fine. (two subjects)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ You can have either tea and coffee. → ✅ You can have either tea or coffee. (either… or)
  • ❌ Either Ali or Reza are coming. → ✅ Either Ali or Reza is coming. (nearest subject: singular)

Neither… nor: not one and not the other B1

UseExample
two subjectsNeither Ali nor Sara was at the party.
two objectsI like neither coffee nor tea.
two adjectivesThe film was neither funny nor interesting.
two verbsHe neither called nor wrote.

  • Neither my mum nor my dad speaks English. (two subjects)
  • The hotel was neither clean nor cheap. (two adjectives)
  • She neither smiled nor said hello. (two verbs)
  • Neither the bus nor the trains were running. (nearest subject)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Neither Ali nor Reza didn't come. → ✅ Neither Ali nor Reza came. (positive verb)
  • ❌ I like neither fish or meat. → ✅ I like neither fish nor meat. (neither… nor)

Practice: B1

4. You can pay either by cash ___ by card.
Either… or.
5. Either my parents or my brother ___ going to meet you at the airport.
Nearest subject: my brother (singular).
6. We can ___ go to the cinema or stay at home. (one word)
Either… or.
7. Neither my sister ___ my brother likes vegetables.
Neither… nor.
8. The weather was ___ hot nor cold. It was perfect.
Neither… nor.
9. Neither the teacher nor the students ___ (be) ready for the test yesterday.
Nearest subject: the students (plural).

B2 · Upper-intermediate

Not only… but also B2

PatternExample
before words or phrasesHe speaks not only English but also Arabic.
before the verbShe not only sings but also writes her own songs.
at the start + inversionNot only is it cheap, but it's also fast.
but… as well / tooNot only did we miss the train, but we lost our tickets as well.

  • The app is not only free but also easy to use. (adjectives)
  • She not only works full-time but also studies at night. (verbs)
  • Not only did he arrive late, but he also forgot the documents. (inversion)
  • Not only is this hotel cheap, it's also very central. (inversion with be)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Not only he forgot my birthday, but he also lied. → ✅ Not only did he forget my birthday, but he also lied. (at the start: inversion)
  • ❌ She is not only clever and also kind. → ✅ She is not only clever but also kind. (not only… but also)

Practice: B2

10. She speaks not only Persian ___ also Turkish.
Not only… but also.
11. Not only ___ late, but he also forgot the tickets.
At the start: inversion (did he arrive).
12. Not only ___ (be) the food delicious, but it was also cheap.
At the start: inversion (was the food).

C1 · Advanced

Parallel structure, nor after a negative, whether… or C1

PatternExample
parallel structureShe was praised both for her courage and for her honesty.
nor + inversionI have never been to Spain, nor do I want to go.
whether… or (not)Whether you like it or not, you have to go.
short answersI can't swim. ~ Neither / Nor can I.

  • You must either pay now or cancel the order. (parallel verbs)
  • The report was neither accurate nor complete. (parallel adjectives)
  • He didn't apologise, nor did he explain. (nor + inversion)
  • Whether we win or lose, we'll enjoy the game. (whether… or)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The job is both challenging and it pays well. → ✅ The job is both challenging and well paid. (parallel structure)
  • ❌ She didn't call, nor she wrote. → ✅ She didn't call, nor did she write. (nor + inversion)

Practice: C1

13. I don't eat meat, ___ do I eat fish.
After a negative: nor + inversion.
14. Which sentence has correct parallel structure?
Parallel items: swimming and cycling.
15. ___ you agree or not, the decision has been made. (one word)
Whether… or not = it doesn't matter which.
