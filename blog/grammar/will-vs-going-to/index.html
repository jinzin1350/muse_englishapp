Will vs Going To

A2 · B1 · C1

Both will and be going to talk about the future, but they're often used in different situations: The phone's ringing. ~ I'll get it. (deciding now) / I'm going to call my mum tonight. (already decided)

This page shows the main differences and when both are possible. For each form on its own, see Will and Shall and Be Going To.

A2 · Elementary

Decision now (will) or decided before (going to)? A2

will: deciding nowgoing to: decided before
A: The bin is full. B: OK, I'll take it out.I'm going to take the bin out after dinner. (I planned it)
A: I'm hungry. B: I'll make you a sandwich.I'm going to make lasagne tonight. (I bought everything)

  • A: Someone's at the door. B: I'll get it. (deciding now)
  • I'm going to sell my old bike. I've already put an ad online. (decided before)
  • A: We need bread. B: Oh, I'll go to the bakery. (deciding now)
  • We're going to have a barbecue on Saturday. We've invited everyone. (decided before)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ A: The phone's ringing. B: I'm going to answer it. → ✅ A: The phone's ringing. B: I'll answer it. (deciding now: will)
  • ❌ I'll visit my uncle next weekend. I bought the tickets last month. → ✅ I'm going to visit my uncle next weekend. I bought the tickets last month. (decided before: going to)

Practice: A2

1. A: It's cold in here. B: OK, <span class="gap">___</span> close the window.
Deciding now: I'll.
2. I <span class="gap">___</span> my grandparents this weekend. I told them last week.
Decided before: going to.
3. A: There's no coffee left. B: Really? I <span class="gap">___</span> (buy) some on my way home.
Deciding now: will buy.

B1 · Intermediate

Predictions: opinion (will) or evidence (going to)? B1

will: opinion / beliefgoing to: evidence now
I think it'll rain tomorrow.Look at those clouds. It's going to rain.
You'll love this film.The score is 5–0. We're going to win!

  • I'm sure she'll be a great teacher. (opinion)
  • She's studied so hard. She's going to pass. (evidence)
  • In 2050, cars will drive themselves. (belief about the distant future)
  • Mind the step! You're going to trip! (evidence now)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Look! That tree will fall on the car! (it's moving now) → ✅ Look! That tree's going to fall on the car! (evidence now: going to)

Will, going to or present continuous? B1

FormUseExample
present continuousarrangements: people, time, place fixedI'm flying to Rome on Friday.
be going tointentions, decided plans; evidenceI'm going to look for a new job.
willdecisions now, offers, promises, opinionsI'll call you later.
present simpletimetables and schedulesThe train leaves at 6.

  • I'm having lunch with my boss tomorrow. (arrangement)
  • I'm going to clean the car this afternoon. (intention)
  • Don't worry, I'll help you. (offer now)
  • The conference starts on Monday. (timetable)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I'll see the dentist at 3 tomorrow. (it's an appointment) → ✅ I'm seeing the dentist at 3 tomorrow. (arrangement: present continuous)
  • ❌ The bus will leave at 7.15 every morning. → ✅ The bus leaves at 7.15 every morning. (timetable: present simple)

Practice: B1

4. Look at the traffic! We <span class="gap">___</span> be late.
Evidence now: going to.
5. I think robots <span class="gap">___</span> most jobs in the future.
Opinion about the distant future: will.
6. Watch out! The ladder <span class="gap">___</span> (fall)!
Evidence now: is going to fall.
7. I've got an appointment. I <span class="gap">___</span> the doctor at 10 tomorrow.
A fixed arrangement: present continuous.
8. According to the timetable, the next train <span class="gap">___</span> at 9:45.
Timetable: present simple.
9. A: I haven't got any money. B: Don't worry. I <span class="gap">___</span> (lend) you some.
An offer now: will.

C1 · Advanced

When both are possible, and formal uses of will C1

  • Prices will rise / are going to rise next year. (both possible)
  • The ceremony will take place at noon. (formal announcement)
  • I will pass this exam, whatever it takes! (determination)
  • Flight BA 207 will board at gate 12. (announcement)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The president is gonna visit Canada next week. (in a news report) → ✅ The president will visit Canada next week. (formal: will)

Practice: C1

10. Which sentence is the most typical of a news report?
Formal style: will.
11. In which sentence are BOTH will and going to possible?
A general prediction allows both.
12. Announcement: The train to Montreal <span class="gap">___</span> (depart) from platform 3.
Formal announcement: will depart (the present continuous or simple are also used).
