Past Perfect and Narrative Tenses

New: Past Perfect guide — form, uses, and 24 exercises.

Level: B1 · Free grammar guide

We use the past perfect (had + past participle) for something that happened before another past action: When I arrived, the film had already started.

In stories we use it together with the past simple (the main events) and the past continuous (the background).

How to form the past perfect

FormExample
Positivehad + past participleShe had left.
Negativehadn't + past participleI hadn't eaten.
QuestionHad + subject + past participle?Had you met him before?

In speech, had is often 'd: I'd forgotten. They'd gone.

When do we use the past perfect?

  • For the earlier of two past actions: I couldn't get in because I had lost my keys.
  • With already, just, never, before and by the time: By the time we got there, the shop had closed.

When the order is already clear (for example with after or before), the past simple is often enough: After I finished work, I went home. / After I had finished work… Both are correct.

Narrative tenses: telling a story

TenseUse in a storyExample
past simplethe main events, in orderHe opened the door and saw a letter.
past continuousthe background; actions in progressIt was raining and the wind was blowing.
past perfectsomething that happened earlierSomeone had left it there the night before.

It was raining when I got home. I was tired because I had worked for twelve hours. While I was making dinner, the phone rang. It was my sister. She had just heard some amazing news.

Past simple or past perfect?

The tense changes the order of events: When I arrived, the film started. (I arrived, then the film started.) When I arrived, the film had started. (The film started before I arrived.)

Examples

  1. When we got to the station, the train had already left. (the earlier action)
  2. I hadn't seen the film before, so I didn't know the ending. (negative)
  3. Had you ever been to Asia before that trip? (question)
  4. She was nervous because she had never flown before. (never)
  5. By the time the police arrived, the thieves had escaped. (by the time)
  6. I was walking home when I realised I had left my phone at work. (three narrative tenses)
  7. He had just finished dinner when the doorbell rang. (just)
  8. They were hungry because they hadn't eaten all day. (reason)
  9. The streets were wet. It had rained in the night. (earlier event)
  10. When I opened the fridge, I saw that someone had eaten my cake. (main events + earlier event)

Common mistakes

❌ When I arrived, the film has started.

✅ When I arrived, the film had started. (Before another past action: past perfect, had + past participle.)

❌ I had went to bed early.

✅ I had gone to bed early. (Use the past participle: gone.)

❌ She was tired because she had work all day.

✅ She was tired because she had worked all day. (Had + past participle.)

❌ Had you ate before you came?

✅ Had you eaten before you came? (Use the past participle: eaten.)

❌ By the time I got there, everyone has left.

✅ By the time I got there, everyone had left. (By the time + past simple → past perfect for the earlier action.)

❌ I was sad because my dog has died.

✅ I was sad because my dog had died. (The earlier past event: past perfect.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. When we got to the cinema, the film ___ already started.
The film started before we arrived: had + past participle.
2. ___ you met Sarah before the party?
Past perfect question: Had + subject + past participle (met).
3. I couldn't pay because I ___ my wallet at home.
Leaving the wallet happened first: had left.
4. She ___ never seen snow before she moved to Canada.
Before another past event: had never seen.
5. The streets were wet because it ___ in the night.
It rained before the moment we're talking about: had rained.
6. By the time we arrived, the guests ___.
By the time + past simple → the earlier action is past perfect: had gone.
7. Choose the sentence that means the film started BEFORE I arrived.
The past perfect shows the earlier action: the film had started.
8. I ___ home when I realised I had forgotten my keys.
The background action in progress: I + was walking.
9. They were hungry because they ___ all day.
Negative past perfect: hadn't + past participle (eaten).
10. He was nervous because he ___ before.
Had never + past participle (flown).
11. She ___ just finished dinner when the phone rang.
Finished just before another past action: had just finished.
12. It was a beautiful day. The sun ___ and the birds were singing.
The background of a story: past continuous, was shining.
13. When I opened the fridge, I saw that someone ___ my cake.
Someone ate it earlier: had eaten.
14. I didn't recognise him because he ___ a beard.
He grew the beard before I saw him: had grown.
15. Which tense do we use for the background of a story?
The past continuous describes the background: It was raining…
16. Had you ever ___ to Asia before that trip?
Had + past participle: been.
