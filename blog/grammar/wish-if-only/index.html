wish و if only (آرزو و حسرت)Wish and If Only

We use wish and if only to say that we want a situation to be different from what it is or was: I wish I had more time. (I don't have enough time.)

The verb after wish moves one step back in time: present → past simple, past → past perfect. The same happens in Second Conditional and Third Conditional.

B1 · B2 · C1

B1 · متوسط

Wish + past simple: things you want to be different now B1

wish + past simple for a present situation you don't like: I wish I lived near the sea. (I don't.)

Real situationWish
I don't have a car.I wish I had a car.
I live in a small flat.I wish I didn't live in a small flat.
I'm not tall.I wish I were (was) taller.
I don't know the answer.I wish I knew the answer.

After wish, the past simple talks about the present. It shows the situation isn't real.

With be, both were and was are used after I / he / she / it. Were is more formal: I wish I were on holiday.

Don't use will or the present after wish for a present situation: I wish I had more money. Not I wish I have more money.

  • I wish I spoke French. (I don't speak French)
  • She wishes she didn't have to work on Saturdays. (she has to)
  • I wish it were summer. (it isn't summer)
  • Do you ever wish you lived somewhere else? (question)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I wish I have a bigger house. → ✅ I wish I had a bigger house. (wish + past simple)
  • ❌ I wish I am taller. → ✅ I wish I were (was) taller. (wish + were / was)
  • ❌ I wish I will know the answer. → ✅ I wish I knew the answer. (wish + past simple)

Wish + could: abilities and possibilities B1

wish + could for something you can't do now: I wish I could swim. I wish I could come to your party.

Use could after wish when you're talking about ability or possibility: I can't drive. → I wish I could drive.

It's also used to say politely that you can't do something: I wish I could help, but I'm busy.

  • I wish I could play the piano. (I can't play it)
  • We wish we could stay longer, but our flight is tonight. (not possible)
  • I wish I could remember where I put my keys. (I can't remember)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I wish I can speak Japanese. → ✅ I wish I could speak Japanese. (wish + could)
  • ❌ I wish I would swim. → ✅ I wish I could swim. (ability: could, not would)

Wish or hope? B1

hope = something that is possible: I hope you pass. wish = something that isn't true or likely: I wish I were younger.

hope (possible)wish (not real)
I hope it's sunny tomorrow.I wish it were sunny today. (it isn't)
I hope you can come.I wish you could come. (you can't)
I hope I passed. (I don't know yet)I wish I had passed. (I didn't)

After hope, use normal tenses, including the present and will: I hope she calls. I hope she'll call.

After wish, the verb moves back: past simple, past perfect, could, would.

Wish + person + noun is used for good wishes: We wish you a happy new year. I wish you good luck! Here wish means hope you have.

  • I hope you enjoy the film. (possible)
  • I wish you didn't live so far away. (not real now)
  • I hope they won. I haven't seen the score. (I don't know yet)
  • Good luck! I wish you all the best. (good wishes)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I wish you have a good trip. → ✅ I hope you have a good trip. (A possible future: hope.)
  • ❌ I hope I was taller. → ✅ I wish I were (was) taller. (Not real: wish.)

B2 · بالاتر از متوسط

Wish + past perfect: regrets about the past B2

wish + past perfect for something in the past you regret: I wish I hadn't eaten so much. (I ate too much.)

What happenedWish
I ate too much.I wish I hadn't eaten so much.
I didn't study.I wish I had studied.
You told her.I wish you hadn't told her.

Use the past perfect after wish to talk about the past. It expresses regret: we can't change it.

Don't use would have after wish: I wish I had known. Not I wish I would have known (heard in informal American English, but avoid it in writing and exams).

For the same regret with a modal, use should have: I should have studied. See Should Have, Could Have, Needn't Have.

  • I wish I had gone to the concert. Everyone says it was amazing. (I didn't go)
  • She wishes she hadn't sold her old car. (she sold it)
  • I wish you'd told me earlier. (you didn't tell me)
  • Do you wish you had studied something else? (question)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I wish I didn't say that yesterday. → ✅ I wish I hadn't said that yesterday. (past: wish + past perfect)
  • ❌ I wish I would have known. → ✅ I wish I had known. (wish + past perfect)

Wish + would: wanting someone or something to change B2

wish + would to complain about someone's behaviour or a situation you want to change: I wish you would stop interrupting!

Use wish + somebody + would when you want someone (or something) to change their behaviour. It often shows annoyance: I wish my neighbours would turn the music down.

It can also be about the weather or a situation: I wish it would stop raining.

Don't use I wish I would… or we wish we would… (you can't wish yourself to change like this). Use could or the past simple: I wish I could stop smoking. I wish I didn't smoke.

Compare: I wish you didn't smoke (I don't like the fact that you smoke) and I wish you would stop smoking (I want you to make a change).

  • I wish you would listen to me! (annoyance)
  • I wish the bus would come. We've been waiting for ages. (want a change)
  • She wishes her son would tidy his room. (his behaviour)
  • I wish you wouldn't leave your shoes in the hall. (negative)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I wish I would have more time. → ✅ I wish I had more time. (not I wish I would)
  • ❌ I wish you will stop. → ✅ I wish you would stop. (wish + would)

If only: a stronger wish B2

If only = a stronger, more emotional I wish: If only I had more time! If only I hadn't said that! If only he would call!

If only works like wish, with the same tenses, but it's more emotional and dramatic:

  • present: If only I knew the answer!
  • past: If only we had left earlier!
  • change: If only it would stop raining!

If only often stands alone as an exclamation. It can also start a conditional: If only you'd told me, I could have helped.

Don't confuse if only (a strong wish) with only if (the one condition): I'll go only if you come. See Unless, As Long As, Provided.

  • If only I had a car! (present)
  • If only I hadn't lost my phone! (past regret)
  • If only the baby would sleep! (wanting a change)
  • If only I could see her again. (could)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ If only I have more money! → ✅ If only I had more money! (if only + past simple)
  • ❌ If only I didn't miss the plane yesterday! → ✅ If only I hadn't missed the plane yesterday! (past regret: past perfect)

C1 · پیشرفته

Formal wish: I wish to…, wish for… C1

In formal English, wish + to-infinitive = want: I wish to complain. wish for + noun = want something you don't have.

Wish + to + verb means want or would like, and it's formal: I wish to speak to the manager. Passengers who wish to change their seats should contact the crew.

Wish for + noun = to want something (often something hard to get): She has everything she could wish for. What would you wish for if you had three wishes?

Wish + object + adjective/adverb in fixed phrases: We wish him well.

In everyday speech, I'd like to or I want to are more natural than I wish to: I'd like to speak to the manager.

  • I wish to make a complaint. (formal: want)
  • Customers who wish to return items must keep the receipt. (notice)
  • We couldn't wish for better neighbours. (want)
  • Everyone at the office wishes you well in your new job. (fixed phrase)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I wish speaking to the director. → ✅ I wish to speak to the director. (wish + to-infinitive)
  • ❌ She wished a new bike for her birthday. → ✅ She wished for a new bike for her birthday. (wish for + noun)

تمرین سطح C1

19. Passengers who ___ to upgrade their seats should visit the desk.
Formal wish to = want to.
20. The children closed their eyes and wished ___ snow at Christmas.
Wish for + noun.
21. I ___ to report a lost passport. (formal, one word)
Formal wish to = want to.
