Wish and If Only

Level: B2 · Free grammar guide

We use wish and if only to talk about situations we want to be different. The verb after wish moves one step back in time: I wish I had more time. (I don't have enough time now.)

If only means the same as I wish, but it's stronger and more emotional.

Three ways to use wish

To talk aboutFormExample
the present (you're not happy with it)wish + past simple / past continuousI wish I knew the answer. I wish it wasn't raining.
the past (a regret)wish + past perfectI wish I had studied harder.
something annoying that you want to changewish + would + base verbI wish you would stop talking.

After wish, were is often used for all persons, especially in formal English: I wish I were taller. I wish I was taller is also correct, especially in speech.

For ability, use could: I wish I could swim.

Wish + would

Use wish + would to complain about other people's behaviour or a situation, and to show impatience: I wish the neighbours would be quieter. I wish it would stop raining.

We don't normally say I wish I would…. Use I wish I could… instead: I wish I could come.

If only

If only uses the same tenses as wish: If only I had listened to you! If only I had more money.

Wish or hope?

Use hope for things that are possible: I hope you pass your exam. Use wish for things that are unreal or impossible: I wish I were on holiday. For good wishes, use wish + person + noun: I wish you a happy birthday.

Examples

  1. I wish I lived closer to the sea. (present: past simple)
  2. I wish I had a bigger flat. (present: past simple)
  3. She wishes she could speak Chinese. (ability: could)
  4. I wish I hadn't eaten so much. (regret: past perfect)
  5. If only we had left earlier! (strong regret)
  6. I wish you would call me more often. (complaint: would)
  7. Don't you wish you were on holiday right now? (present: were)
  8. He wishes he had taken the job in Toronto. (regret)
  9. I wish it would stop raining. (impatience)
  10. If only I knew what to say. (present)

Common mistakes

❌ I wish I have more time.

✅ I wish I had more time. (Present wish: past simple.)

❌ I wish I didn't eat so much last night.

✅ I wish I hadn't eaten so much last night. (A past regret: past perfect.)

❌ I wish I can swim.

✅ I wish I could swim. (Ability: could.)

❌ I wish you stop shouting.

✅ I wish you would stop shouting. (Complaints about someone's behaviour: wish + would.)

❌ I wish you pass your exam.

✅ I hope you pass your exam. (For something possible, use hope.)

❌ I wish I would be taller.

✅ I wish I were (or was) taller. (Don't use I wish I would…)

❌ If only I would know the answer.

✅ If only I knew the answer. (Present: past simple.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. I wish I ___ more free time.
A wish about the present: past simple, had.
2. I wish you ___ making that noise. It's driving me crazy!
A complaint about someone's behaviour: would + base verb.
3. She wishes she ___ play the piano.
Ability: could.
4. I wish I ___ so much last night. I feel terrible.
A regret about the past: past perfect, hadn't eaten.
5. I ___ you pass your driving test tomorrow.
Something possible in the future: hope.
6. If only I ___ to your advice!
A strong regret about the past: past perfect, had listened.
7. He wishes he ___ the job in London. Now he regrets saying no.
A past regret: past perfect, had accepted.
8. I wish it ___ so cold today.
A wish about the present: past simple, wasn't (or weren't).
9. I wish the bus ___. We've been waiting for 40 minutes!
Impatience about a situation: would + base verb.
10. I wish I ___ taller.
A present wish: were (or was).
11. If only I ___ his phone number. I could call him now.
A present wish: past simple, knew.
12. Choose the correct sentence.
Ability: I wish I could drive.
13. My sister wishes she ___ in a big city instead of a small village.
A present wish: past simple, lived.
14. We wish we ___ to the concert. Everyone says it was amazing.
A regret about the past: had gone.
15. I wish you ___ me earlier. Now it's too late.
A regret about the past: past perfect, had told.
16. Which sentence is correct?
Good wishes: wish + person + noun, I wish you a great holiday.
