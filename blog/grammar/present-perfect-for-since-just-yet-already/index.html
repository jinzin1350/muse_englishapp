Present Perfect with For, Since, Just, Yet, Already

Level: B1 · Free grammar guide

We often use the present perfect (have/has + past participle) with for and since to say how long something has continued up to now: I've lived here for ten years.

We use it with just, already and yet to talk about recent actions and things we expect to happen: She's just left. Have you finished yet?

For and since

Use it withExamples
fora period of time (how long)for ten minutes, for two weeks, for a long time, for ages
sincea point in time (when it started)since 9 o'clock, since Monday, since 2015, since I was a child
  1. I've known Maria for five years. (a period)
  2. I've known Maria since 2021. (the starting point)
  3. He's worked here since he left university. (since + a past clause)

Ask with How long…?: How long have you had your car? Don't use the present simple: I've lived here for years. (not I live here for years)

With for and since, the situation is still true now. If it has finished, use the past simple: I lived in Rome for two years. (I don't live there now.)

Just, already and yet

WordMeaningPositionExample
justa very short time agobetween have/has and the past participleI've just had lunch.
alreadysooner than expectedbetween have/has and the past participle, or at the endShe's already finished her homework.
yetuntil now (we expect it to happen)at the end of negatives and questionsI haven't finished yet. Have you eaten yet?

Still + negative means something hasn't happened, and we're surprised or impatient: He still hasn't replied.

American and British English: Americans often use the past simple with just, already and yet: I just had lunch. Did you eat yet? Both styles are correct in their variety.

Been or gone?

She's gone to Paris. = She is in Paris now. She's been to Paris. = She went to Paris and came back.

Examples

  1. We've been friends for twenty years. (for + a period)
  2. I've had this phone since January. (since + a starting point)
  3. How long have you lived in Canada? (how long question)
  4. The film has just started. (just: very recent)
  5. I've already seen this episode. (already: sooner than expected)
  6. Have you booked the hotel yet? (yet in a question)
  7. They haven't arrived yet. (yet in a negative)
  8. She's been a teacher since 2018. (since + a year)
  9. I haven't seen him for ages. (for ages = for a long time)
  10. He still hasn't replied to my message. (still + negative)

Common mistakes

❌ I live here since 2019.

✅ I've lived here since 2019. (From the past until now: present perfect.)

❌ I've worked here since three years.

✅ I've worked here for three years. (For + a period of time.)

❌ I've known her for 2015.

✅ I've known her since 2015. (Since + a point in time.)

❌ I've finished yet my homework.

✅ I've already finished my homework. (Yet goes at the end of negatives and questions. Use already in positive sentences.)

❌ She has just went out.

✅ She has just gone out. (Use the past participle: go → gone.)

❌ How long do you know him?

✅ How long have you known him? (How long + present perfect.)

❌ I didn't see her since Monday.

✅ I haven't seen her since Monday. (Since + until now: present perfect.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. I've known Sarah ___ ten years.
Ten years is a period of time: for.
2. We've lived in this flat ___ 2020.
2020 is the starting point: since.
3. How long ___ you had your car?
How long + present perfect: have you had…?
4. She ___ here since she was 18.
Since + from then until now: has worked.
5. I've ___ had lunch, so I'm not hungry.
A very short time ago: just, between have and the past participle.
6. Have you finished your homework ___?
Yet goes at the end of questions.
7. They haven't arrived ___.
Yet goes at the end of negatives.
8. I ___ seen this film. Let's watch something else.
've already + past participle. Yet isn't used in positive sentences.
9. He ___ to Paris. He'll be back next week.
He is in Paris now: has gone. (Has been = he went and came back.)
10. I haven't seen my cousins ___ ages.
Ages is a period of time: for ages.
11. Choose the correct sentence.
From 2019 until now: present perfect, I've lived.
12. It's 10 a.m. and he ___ hasn't replied to my email from yesterday.
Still + negative shows we're surprised or impatient.
13. Where's Tom? – He's ___ gone out. You missed him by a minute.
A very short time ago: just.
14. How long have you known each other? – ___ we were at school.
We were at school is the starting point: Since.
15. My parents have been married ___ 30 years.
30 years is a period: for.
16. Have you ___ been to Mexico?
Ever = at any time in your life, in present perfect questions.
