Future Forms: Going To, Will and Present Continuous

Level: A2 · Free grammar guide

English has several ways to talk about the future. The three most common are be going to (plans and predictions from evidence), will (decisions, offers, promises and opinions) and the present continuous (arranged plans).

Often more than one form is possible. The guide below shows the usual choice in each situation.

Be going to: plans and evidence

Form: am / is / are + going to + base verb. Negative: She isn't going to come. Question: Are you going to buy it?

  • A plan or intention you decided before now: I'm going to visit my aunt this weekend.
  • A prediction based on what you can see now: Look at those clouds. It's going to rain.

Will: decisions, offers, promises and opinions

Form: will / 'll + base verb. Negative: won't (will not).

  • A decision made at the moment of speaking: I'm thirsty. I'll get some water.
  • Offers: That bag looks heavy. I'll carry it.
  • Promises: I won't be late, I promise.
  • Predictions based on your opinion: I think you'll love this film.

For offers and suggestions with I and we, use shall in questions: Shall I help you? Shall we go? In American English, Should I…? is also common.

Present continuous: arrangements

Use the present continuous for future plans that are fixed, usually with a time and other people involved: I'm meeting Sam at 6 tonight. We're flying to Rome on Monday.

Be going to is also correct here: I'm going to meet Sam at 6. The present continuous just sounds more fixed. Timetables and schedules often use the present simple: The film starts at 8.

Don't use the present simple for decisions, offers or promises: I'll answer it. (not I answer it)

Examples

  1. We're going to paint the kitchen this weekend. (plan)
  2. Look out! That glass is going to fall. (evidence)
  3. I'm tired. I think I'll go to bed. (decision now)
  4. I'll call you tomorrow, I promise. (promise)
  5. Shall I make some tea? (offer)
  6. She's starting her new job on Monday. (arrangement)
  7. I don't think it'll rain tomorrow. (opinion)
  8. Are you going to watch the match tonight? (plan question)
  9. They aren't coming to the party. They're busy. (arrangement, negative)
  10. The concert starts at 8 p.m. (timetable)

Common mistakes

❌ I will to call you.

✅ I will call you. (Will + base verb, without to.)

❌ She going to study medicine.

✅ She's going to study medicine. (Don't forget am/is/are.)

❌ The phone's ringing. I answer it.

✅ The phone's ringing. I'll answer it. (A decision now: use will.)

❌ I promise I don't tell anyone.

✅ I promise I won't tell anyone. (Promises: will / won't.)

❌ Will I open the window?

✅ Shall I open the window? (Offers with I: Shall I…?)

❌ It's going to raining.

✅ It's going to rain. (Going to + base verb.)

❌ I think it is raining tomorrow.

✅ I think it will rain tomorrow. (A prediction from opinion: will.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. I ___ visit my grandparents this weekend. I bought the train tickets yesterday.
A plan made before now: am going to + base verb.
2. A: We've run out of milk. B: Oh, really? I ___ some on my way home.
B decides now: 'll buy (will + base verb).
3. It's cold in here. ___ I close the window?
An offer with I: Shall I…?
4. She ___ study medicine at university.
A plan: is going to + base verb.
5. Don't worry, I ___ tell anyone. I promise.
A promise: won't (will not) + base verb.
6. A: Tea or coffee? B: I ___ coffee, please.
A decision at the moment of speaking: 'll have.
7. Look at those black clouds! It ___ rain.
A prediction from evidence you can see: is going to.
8. We ___ dinner with Tom and Anna at 7 tonight. We booked the table last week.
A fixed arrangement: present continuous, are having.
9. ___ you going to watch the match tonight?
Going to questions: Are + you + going to…?
10. Which sentence is a promise?
Promises use will: I'll always love you. The others are a plan, a prediction and a timetable.
11. Be careful! You ___ fall!
A prediction from what we can see now: are going to.
12. Choose the correct sentence.
Will + base verb: I'll help you.
13. They ___ to the party. They have to work.
A fixed plan, negative: aren't coming.
14. I think you ___ this book. It's really good.
A prediction from opinion: will + base verb.
15. The film ___ at 8 p.m., so let's meet at 7:30.
A timetable: present simple. The film (it) → starts.
16. I don't think it ___ tomorrow.
A prediction from opinion: I don't think + will + base verb.
