Future Forms in English

English doesn't have just one future tense. We choose between several forms depending on how sure we are and when we decided: I'll help you (deciding now), I'm going to move (my plan), I'm meeting Ali at 6 (arranged), The train leaves at 8 (timetable).

This page shows the big picture and helps you choose. Each topic below has its own page with rules, examples and exercises at every level.

The future forms at a glance

FormExampleMain use
willI'll call you later.decisions now, offers, promises, predictions (opinion)
be going toI'm going to learn French.plans and intentions; predictions from evidence
present continuousWe're having a party on Saturday.fixed arrangements
present simpleThe film starts at 9.timetables and schedules
future continuousThis time tomorrow I'll be flying.in progress at a future time
future perfectBy June I'll have finished.complete before a future time
be about toHurry! The bus is about to leave.very soon

Which one do I need?

  • Are you deciding now, offering or promising? → will: I'll get it!
  • Have you already decided? → be going to: I'm going to paint my room.
  • Is it arranged with other people (time, place)? → present continuous: I'm seeing the dentist at 4.
  • Is it a timetable or programme? → present simple: The class starts at 9.
  • Is it a prediction? → will (opinion) or going to (you can see the evidence): I think it'll rain. / Look, it's going to rain!
  • Will it be in progress or finished at a future time? → will be doing / will have done.
  • After when, as soon as, before, until → present: I'll call you when I arrive.

Topics in this family

Will and Shall

A2 B1 B2 C1

The future with will: predictions, decisions, offers and promises. I'll help you. It will be cold tomorrow. Shall I open the window?

Be Going To (I'm going to…)

A1 A2 B1 C1

Plans and predictions with evidence: I'm going to visit my aunt. Look at those clouds. It's going to rain.

Will vs Going To

A2 B1 C1

Will for decisions now and opinions; going to for plans already made and evidence. I'll answer it. I'm going to call her later.

Future Time Clauses (When I get home, I'll…)

A2 B1 B2 C1

After when, as soon as, before, after and until, use a present tense for the future: I'll call you when I get home.

Future Continuous and Future Perfect (will be doing, will have done)

B1 B2 C1

Seeing the future from a point in time: This time tomorrow I'll be flying. By Friday I'll have finished.

Be About To, Be Due To and Be To

B1 B2 C1

More ways to talk about the future: The film is about to start. The train is due to arrive at 6. The President is to visit Canada.

Future in the Past (was going to, would…)

B2 C1

Talking about the future from a point in the past: I knew you would come. We were going to leave, but it rained.
