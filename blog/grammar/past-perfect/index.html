زمان گذشتهٔ کامل (ماضی بعید)Past Perfect (had done)

We use the past perfect (had + past participle) for something that happened before another moment in the past: When I arrived, the film had already started.

It takes you one step further back than the past simple. If you need to review the past simple first, see Past Simple.

B1 · B2 · C1

B1 · متوسط

Form and main use: something earlier in the past B1

had + past participle for an action before another past moment.

FormExample
Positivehad + past participleShe had left. (She'd left.)
Negativehadn't + past participleI hadn't eaten.
QuestionHad + subject + past participle?Had you met him before?
Short answersYes, I had. / No, I hadn't.Had she called? – No, she hadn't.

Had is the same for every person: I had, you had, she had, they had. Use the past participle: gone, eaten, seen, been (not went, ate, saw). See the list of irregular verbs on Past Simple.

Think of two past moments. The past perfect is the earlier one: I couldn't get in (past) because I had lost my keys (earlier).

In speech, had is often 'd: I'd forgotten. They'd gone. Be careful: 'd can also mean would (I'd like). If a past participle follows, it's had.

  • I couldn't get in because I had lost my keys. (earlier past)
  • She was tired because she had worked all night. (reason in the earlier past)
  • I hadn't seen the film, so I didn't know the ending. (negative)
  • Had you been to Asia before that trip? (question)
  • The streets were wet. It'd rained in the night. ('d = had)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I had went to bed early. → ✅ I had gone to bed early. (Use the past participle: gone.)
  • ❌ Had you ate before you came? → ✅ Had you eaten before you came? (Use the past participle: eaten.)
  • ❌ She was tired because she had work all day. → ✅ She was tired because she had worked all day. (had + past participle (worked).)

Past simple or past perfect? The order of events B1

Past simple for events in order; past perfect for an event that happened earlier than the one you're talking about.

The tense changes the order of events:

  • When I arrived, the film started. = I arrived, then the film started.
  • When I arrived, the film had started. = The film started first, then I arrived.

When you tell events in the order they happened, use the past simple. Don't use the past perfect for every past action: I woke up, had a shower and left the house.

  • When we got to the station, the train had left. (it left first)
  • When we got to the station, the train left. (we arrived, then it left)
  • When I got home, someone had eaten my cake. (earlier)
  • I got up, made breakfast and went for a run. (in order: past simple)
  • I didn't know the way because I hadn't been there before. (earlier experience)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I had woken up, had had breakfast and had left. → ✅ I woke up, had breakfast and left. (Events in order: past simple.)
  • ❌ When I arrived, the film has started. → ✅ When I arrived, the film had started. (Earlier than a past moment: past perfect.)

By the time, already, just, never … before B1

by the time + past simple, then past perfect; already, just, never … before with the past perfect.

WordsExample
by the time + past simpleBy the time we arrived, the concert had finished.
alreadyWhen I called, she had already left.
justHe had just sat down when the phone rang.
never … beforeWhen she moved to Canada, she had never seen snow.
everHad you ever tried sushi before that night?

Already, just, never and ever go between had and the past participle: had already left, had just sat, had never seen.

  • By the time we got there, everyone had gone home. (by the time)
  • She had already ordered when I arrived. (already)
  • I'd just got into bed when the alarm went off. (just)
  • He was nervous because he had never flown before. (never … before)
  • By the time I finished my degree, my parents had moved to Canada. (by the time)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ By the time we got there, the shop closed. → ✅ By the time we got there, the shop had closed. (By the time + earlier event: past perfect.)
  • ❌ She had left already when I called. → ✅ She had already left when I called. (Already goes between had and the participle.)

Present perfect or past perfect? B1

have done = before now; had done = before a moment in the past.

Present perfect (before now)Past perfect (before then)
I'm not hungry. I've just eaten.I wasn't hungry. I'd just eaten.
I've never seen snow.Until I moved to Canada, I'd never seen snow.
She looks great. She's lost weight.She looked great. She'd lost weight.

The present perfect connects the past to now. The past perfect connects an earlier past to a moment in the past.

  • I can't come out. I haven't finished my homework. (now)
  • I couldn't go out because I hadn't finished my homework. (then)
  • Last summer I visited Rome. I'd never been there before. (before then)
  • When I met him, he had been in the army for ten years. (up to then)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ Before I came to Canada, I have never seen snow. → ✅ Before I came to Canada, I had never seen snow. (Before a past moment: past perfect.)
  • ❌ I'm not hungry. I'd already eaten. → ✅ I'm not hungry. I've already eaten. (Connected to now: present perfect.)

تمرین سطح B1

1. When we got to the cinema, the film ___ already started.
Earlier than got: past perfect, had started.
2. ___ you met Sarah before the party?
Past perfect question: Had you met…?
3. I had ___ to bed early, so I didn't hear the phone.
had + past participle: gone.
4. Choose the sentence that means the film started BEFORE I arrived.
Had started = it started first.
5. I got up, ___ a shower and left the house.
Events in order: past simple, had a shower.
6. I couldn't pay because I ___ my wallet at home.
Earlier than couldn't pay: had left.
7. By the time we arrived, the guests ___.
By the time + earlier event: had gone.
8. She ___ never seen snow before she moved to Canada.
never … before a past moment: had never seen.
9. He ___ just finished dinner when the phone rang.
Had just finished = a moment before the phone rang.
10. I'm not hungry. I ___ lunch.
Connected to now: present perfect, I've just had.
11. I wasn't hungry because I ___ lunch.
Before a past moment: past perfect, I'd already had.
12. Which sentence is correct?
Before a past moment: had never lived.

B2 · بالاتر از متوسط

It was the first time I'd…; states up to a past moment B2

It was the first time / the best … I'd ever + past perfect; state verbs up to a past moment use the past perfect simple.

After It was the first / second / third time… and after superlatives (the best, the worst…) in the past, use the past perfect: It was the first time I'd been on a plane. It was the best meal we'd ever had.

To say how long a state continued up to a past moment, use the past perfect simple with state verbs: They had known each other for years when they got married. (not had been knowing). For actions, the past perfect continuous is common: She had been working there for ten years when she left.

  • It was the first time she'd been on a plane. (first time)
  • That was the third time he'd lost his phone that month. (third time)
  • It was the most beautiful place I'd ever seen. (superlative)
  • We had known each other for years before we started working together. (state up to then)
  • I'd had the car for only a month when it broke down. (state: had had)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ It was the first time I've been abroad. → ✅ It was the first time I'd been abroad. (It was the first time + past perfect.)
  • ❌ We had been knowing each other for ten years. → ✅ We had known each other for ten years. (Know is a state: past perfect simple.)

تمرین سطح B2

13. It was the first time I ___ a horse.
It was the first time + past perfect: I'd ridden.
14. It was the best meal we ___ in years.
A past superlative + past perfect: we'd had.
15. They ___ each other for ten years when they got married.
Know is a state: past perfect simple, had known.

C1 · پیشرفته

When the past perfect is optional C1

If the order is already clear (after, as soon as, before) the past simple is also fine; once the earlier time is set, you can continue in the past simple.

When a time word like after, as soon as or before already makes the order clear, the past simple is also correct and the meaning doesn't change: After she had finished / finished work, she went home.

When you have set up an earlier time with the past perfect, you don't have to keep using it. You can continue in the past simple: I sold my old guitar last week. I'd had it since I was fifteen, and I played it almost every day.

But keep the past perfect when the past simple would change the order: When I got home, my son had cooked dinner (it was ready) vs When I got home, my son cooked dinner (he started after I arrived).

  • After the guests had gone / went, we cleaned up. (both fine)
  • As soon as I'd sent / sent the message, I regretted it. (both fine)
  • She asked about the salary before she accepted the job. (before + past simple)
  • We'd lived in that flat for years. We loved it, but it was too small. (continue in past simple)
  • When the police arrived, the thief had escaped. (needed: changes the order)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ After we have eaten, we went for a walk. → ✅ After we had eaten (or After we ate), we went for a walk. (A past sequence: past perfect or past simple, not present perfect.)

Past perfect in reported speech and thoughts C1

A present perfect becomes past perfect when reported in the past; a past simple can become past perfect or stay the same.

When we report words or thoughts in the past, a present perfect usually becomes past perfect: 'I've met him before.' → I was sure I'd met him before.

A past simple can become past perfect, but it can also stay in the past simple, especially when the time is clear: 'I lost my keys.' → She said she had lost / lost her keys.

If the situation is still true now, you can also keep the present perfect: He told me he's never been to Asia. See Reported Speech.

  • 'I've finished.' → She said she had finished. (present perfect → past perfect)
  • 'We haven't decided yet.' → They said they hadn't decided yet. (negative)
  • 'I lost my phone.' → He said he had lost / lost his phone. (both possible)
  • I realised I'd left my umbrella on the bus. (a thought)
  • Police said twenty people had been / were injured. (both possible)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I was sure that I have met him before. → ✅ I was sure that I had met him before. (Reported in the past: present perfect → past perfect.)
  • ❌ She said she hasn't finish yet. → ✅ She said she hadn't finished yet. (Past perfect, with the past participle finished.)

Plans that didn't happen: I had hoped to… C1

had hoped / meant / intended / planned / wanted to = a plan or wish that didn't happen.

Use had + hoped, meant, intended, planned, wanted, expected + to-infinitive for a plan or wish that didn't happen: I had hoped to finish by Friday, but my computer broke.

The past simple and the continuous forms are also used with a similar meaning: I hoped / was hoping / had been hoping to see you. The past perfect makes it clearest that it didn't happen.

  • We'd hoped to visit the museum, but it was closed. (didn't happen)
  • I'd meant to call you, but I forgot. (meant to)
  • She had intended to stay a week, but she left after two days. (intended to)
  • They had planned to retire at 60, but they're still working. (planned to)
  • I was hoping to see you at the party. (similar meaning)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I had hope to finish today, but I couldn't. → ✅ I had hoped to finish today, but I couldn't. (had + past participle: hoped.)
  • ❌ We had wanted visit you. → ✅ We had wanted to visit you. (had wanted + to-infinitive.)

تمرین سطح C1

16. In which sentence can the past perfect change to the past simple without changing the meaning?
After already shows the order, so after she finished means the same.
17. I sold my old guitar. I'd had it since I was fifteen, and I played it every day. Is played correct here?
After the past perfect sets the earlier time, you can continue in the past simple.
18. Which sentence has the same meaning as As soon as she had sent the email, she noticed the mistake?
As soon as already shows the order: both forms mean the same.
19. 'I've met him before.' → I was sure that I ___ him before.
Present perfect reported in the past → past perfect, had met.
20. 'We haven't decided yet.' → They said they ___ yet.
Present perfect → past perfect: hadn't decided.
21. 'I lost my keys.' → She said she … her keys. Which verb forms are possible?
A reported past simple can stay or become past perfect.
22. I ___ to finish the report by Friday, but my computer broke.
A hope that didn't happen: had hoped.
23. What does We had meant to call you mean?
Had meant to = a plan that didn't happen.
24. She ___ to stay longer, but she had to leave early.
A wish that didn't happen: had wanted.
