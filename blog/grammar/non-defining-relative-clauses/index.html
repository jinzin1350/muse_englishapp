Non-Defining Relative Clauses

Level: B2 · Free grammar guide

A non-defining relative clause adds extra information about someone or something we have already identified. We put it between commas, and the sentence still makes sense without it: My sister, who lives in Dublin, is a nurse.

Defining or non-defining?

DefiningNon-defining
tells us which oneadds extra information
no commascommas
My sister who lives in Dublin is a nurse. (I have more than one sister.)My sister, who lives in Dublin, is a nurse. (I have one sister, and she lives in Dublin.)
that is possiblethat is not possible
an object pronoun can be left outthe pronoun can never be left out

For the basics, see Defining Relative Clauses.

Relative pronouns in non-defining clauses

PronounUse it forExample
whopeopleMr Lee, who teaches us maths, is retiring.
whichthingsThe Eiffel Tower, which was completed in 1889, is in Paris.
whosepossessionAnna, whose brother is a pilot, travels a lot.
whereplacesToronto, where I grew up, is a big city.
whentimesIn 2020, when the pandemic started, we worked from home.
whompeople as the object (formal)The director, whom I met last year, was very kind.

Don't use that in non-defining clauses: My car, which is old, still works. (not My car, that is old, …)

Which for a whole idea

Which can refer to the whole previous clause: He passed all his exams, which surprised everyone. (not what surprised everyone)

All of whom, some of which

After a quantifier, use of whom (people) or of which (things): I have three brothers, all of whom live abroad. She wrote two novels, one of which became a film.

Non-defining clauses are more common in writing than in conversation.

Examples

  1. My grandmother, who is 90, still swims every day. (extra information about a person)
  2. The film, which lasted three hours, was fantastic. (which for things)
  3. We stayed in Lisbon, where my cousin lives. (where for places)
  4. Mark, whose wife is a chef, loves cooking too. (whose for possession)
  5. I lost my phone, which was really annoying. (which = the whole idea)
  6. Our new teacher, who comes from Australia, is very funny. (commas on both sides)
  7. The museum, which opened last year, is free on Sundays. (which for things)
  8. In July, when most people go on holiday, the city is quiet. (when for times)
  9. I invited ten people, most of whom came. (quantifier + of whom)
  10. She got the job, which was great news. (which = the whole idea)

Common mistakes

❌ My brother, that lives in Rome, is a chef.

✅ My brother, who lives in Rome, is a chef. (No that in non-defining clauses.)

❌ Paris, I visited last year, is beautiful.

✅ Paris, which I visited last year, is beautiful. (You can't leave out the pronoun in a non-defining clause.)

❌ He was late, what made me angry.

✅ He was late, which made me angry. (Use which for a whole idea.)

❌ My mother who is a doctor works long hours.

✅ My mother, who is a doctor, works long hours. (You have one mother, so the information is extra: use commas.)

❌ The book, which I bought it yesterday, is great.

✅ The book, which I bought yesterday, is great. (Don't repeat the object.)

❌ Sam, who his car broke down, took the bus.

✅ Sam, whose car broke down, took the bus. (Possession: whose.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. My sister, ___ lives in Dublin, is a nurse.
A person, non-defining (commas): who. That isn't possible here.
2. Rome, ___ I spent my childhood, is my favourite city.
A place where something happened: where (= in which).
3. The Eiffel Tower, ___ was completed in 1889, is very popular.
A thing, non-defining: which.
4. He missed the bus, ___ made him late for work.
Referring to the whole idea (missing the bus): which.
5. Choose the correct sentence.
Extra information about one person: commas on both sides and who.
6. Our neighbour, ___ dog barks all night, has finally moved.
Possession (his/her dog): whose.
7. I have two brothers, both of ___ are engineers.
Quantifier + of whom for people.
8. Which sentence has a non-defining relative clause?
Commas + extra information about someone already identified (my mum).
9. The concert, ___ started at 8, finished at midnight.
A thing, non-defining: which.
10. She didn't call me back, ___ was strange.
Referring to the whole idea: which.
11. Mr Smith, ___ I met at the conference, is now my boss.
A person as the object of met (formal): whom.
12. In 2019, ___ I finished university, I moved to Canada.
A time: when.
13. Which word can you NOT use in a non-defining relative clause?
That is only used in defining clauses.
14. The students, ___ had all studied hard, passed the exam.
People, non-defining: who.
15. Lisbon, ___ is the capital of Portugal, has lovely old trams.
Which is the subject of is: which. (Where would need another subject.)
16. My laptop, ___ I bought only last month, has stopped working.
A thing, non-defining: which. The pronoun can't be left out.
