جمله‌واره‌های موصولی غیرمحدودکنندهNon-Defining Relative Clauses (My sister, who lives in Paris, …)

A non-defining relative clause adds extra information about a person or thing that is already clear: My mother, who is 70, still works. If you remove the clause, the sentence still makes sense.

These clauses always have commas, use who or which (never that), and you can't leave out the pronoun. For clauses that identify which person or thing, see Defining Relative Clauses.

B2 · C1

B2 · بالاتر از متوسط

Extra information: commas, who and which B2

Use commas and who / which (not that). The pronoun can't be left out: Tom, who I met at school, is a pilot.

RuleExample
commas around the clauseTehran, which is the capital of Iran, is very big.
who for people, which for thingsMy boss, who is very kind, … / My car, which is ten years old, …
never thatMy car, that is old, … ✗
you can't leave out the pronounTom, I met at school, … ✗ → Tom, who I met at school, …

Non-defining clauses are common with names, unique things and nouns with my / your / this: My father, who… (I have only one father), The Eiffel Tower, which…

In speech, these clauses are less common; they're typical of writing.

  • My sister, who lives in Rome, is visiting next week. (extra information)
  • The Nile, which flows through eleven countries, is very long. (unique thing)
  • Mr Ahmadi, who I've known for years, is retiring. (object: can't leave out)
  • Our new flat, which we bought last year, has a big balcony. (extra information)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ My brother, that is a doctor, lives in Calgary. → ✅ My brother, who is a doctor, lives in Calgary. (non-defining: who, not that)
  • ❌ Paris, I love, is beautiful. → ✅ Paris, which I love, is beautiful. (can't leave out the pronoun)
  • ❌ My mother who is 60 loves dancing. → ✅ My mother, who is 60, loves dancing. (commas)

Defining or non-defining? The commas change the meaning B2

Without commas = which one (My brother who lives in Rome = I have more than one brother). With commas = extra information (My brother, who lives in Rome, = I have one brother).

SentenceMeaning
My sister who lives in London is a nurse.I have more than one sister; this is the one in London.
My sister, who lives in London, is a nurse.I have one sister. By the way, she lives in London.
The students who passed got a certificate.Only some students passed.
The students, who passed, got a certificate.All the students passed.

Ask yourself: Does the clause tell us which one? → defining, no commas. Is it just extra information? → non-defining, commas.

  • The passengers who had tickets got on the train. (only some had tickets)
  • The passengers, who had tickets, got on the train. (all had tickets)
  • My daughter who is at university is coming home. (I have more than one daughter)
  • My daughter, who is at university, is coming home. (I have one daughter)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ My mother who is a teacher lives in Isfahan. (you have one mother) → ✅ My mother, who is a teacher, lives in Isfahan. (one person: non-defining, commas)

Which for a whole idea: He was late, which annoyed me B2

which can refer to the whole previous clause, not just a noun: She passed all her exams, which was a big surprise.

A non-defining clause with which can comment on everything said before: The train was cancelled, which meant we had to take a taxi. (which = the fact that the train was cancelled)

Only which works here. Not that or what: He didn't call, which was strange (not what was strange).

  • It rained all week, which was disappointing. (which = the whole idea)
  • He forgot my birthday, which really upset me. (which = the fact he forgot)
  • The hotel was full, which meant we had to find another one. (result)
  • She apologised, which was nice of her. (comment)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ He passed the exam, what surprised everyone. → ✅ He passed the exam, which surprised everyone. (whole idea: which)
  • ❌ The shop was closed, that was annoying. → ✅ The shop was closed, which was annoying. (whole idea: which)

Whose, where and when in non-defining clauses B2

whose, where, when also work in non-defining clauses: My neighbour, whose dog barks all night, … Rome, where I studied, … In 2020, when we moved, …

The rules are the same as for defining clauses, but with commas: Our teacher, whose husband is a writer, gave us his book.

Where and when after a place or time that is already clear: I went back to Shiraz, where I grew up. She was born in 1995, when her parents lived in Paris.

  • Mina, whose brother is a doctor, wants to study medicine. (whose)
  • We stayed in Kyoto, where we visited many temples. (where)
  • I'll call you on Monday, when I'm back at work. (when)
  • The company, whose CEO resigned last week, is in trouble. (whose for an organisation)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ My friend, who's mother is a teacher, helped me. → ✅ My friend, whose mother is a teacher, helped me. (whose (not who's))
  • ❌ We went to Lisbon, which we stayed for a week. → ✅ We went to Lisbon, where we stayed for a week. (place: where (or which … in))

تمرین سطح B2

1. My grandfather, ___ is 90, still drives.
Person, non-defining: who.
2. The Amazon, ___ is the largest rainforest in the world, is in danger.
Thing, non-defining: which (not that).
3. Shakespeare, ___ wrote Hamlet, was born in 1564. (one word)
Person, non-defining: who.
4. My brother, who lives in Spain, is a chef. How many brothers do I probably have?
Non-defining (commas): just extra information.
5. The players who were injured didn't play. What does this mean?
Defining: it tells us which players.
6. My father, ___ is a pilot, travels a lot. (one word)
One father: non-defining who.
7. My flight was delayed by six hours, ___ meant I missed my meeting.
Which refers to the whole clause.
8. In He sold his car, which surprised me, what does which refer to?
Which = the whole previous idea.
9. She won the competition, ___ meant she could study abroad for free. (one word)
Which refers to the whole clause.
10. My uncle, ___ farm is in the north, grows rice.
Possession: whose.
11. We spent a week in Barcelona, ___ we saw the Sagrada Família.
Place: where.
12. In 1998, ___ I was six, my parents moved to Canada. (one word)
Time: when.

C1 · پیشرفته

Most of whom, some of which, all of whom C1

After quantifiers (some, many, most, all, none, both, neither, a few) + of, use whom (people) or which (things): I have two brothers, both of whom live abroad.

This structure is common in formal writing to give information about part of a group: The company employs 500 people, most of whom work part-time.

Use whom for people and which for things after of. Not of who or of them: …three cars, all of which are electric (not all of them are electric in one sentence without and).

Also: the majority of whom, half of which, one of which, the best of which.

  • I invited twenty friends, most of whom came. (most of whom)
  • She wrote ten novels, two of which became films. (two of which)
  • The tourists, many of whom were from Japan, loved the city. (many of whom)
  • We tried three restaurants, none of which was open. (none of which)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I have two sisters, both of who are married. → ✅ I have two sisters, both of whom are married. (after of: whom)
  • ❌ He has three cars, all of them are red. → ✅ He has three cars, all of which are red. (one sentence: all of which)

Formal patterns: in which, at which point, by which time C1

In formal English, a preposition can come before which / whom: the house, in which he was born. Fixed phrases: at which point, by which time, in which case.

In formal writing, put the preposition before which or whom: The hall, in which the concert was held, was full. (Informal: The hall, which the concert was held in, …)

Useful fixed phrases that link to the previous idea:

  • at which point = at that moment: He started shouting, at which point I left.
  • by which time = by that time: We arrived at midnight, by which time everyone was asleep.
  • in which case = if that happens: It may rain, in which case the match will be cancelled.
  • The report, to which I referred earlier, is online. (formal)
  • The music got louder, at which point the neighbours called the police. (at that moment)
  • The film ended at 11, by which time I was very tired. (by that time)
  • You might miss the bus, in which case call me. (if that happens)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ The hotel, in that we stayed, was lovely. → ✅ The hotel, in which we stayed, was lovely. (not preposition + that)
  • ❌ She might be late, in what case we'll start without her. → ✅ She might be late, in which case we'll start without her. (in which case)
