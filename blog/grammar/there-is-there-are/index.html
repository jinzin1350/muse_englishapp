There Is and There Are

Level: A1 · Free grammar guide

We use there is and there are to say that something exists or is in a place: There's a bank near my house. There are two cafés on this street.

Use there is with one thing or an uncountable noun, and there are with plural nouns.

How to form there is and there are

One thing / uncountablePlural
PositiveThere is (there's) a park. There's some milk.There are three parks. There are some eggs.
NegativeThere isn't a park. There isn't any milk.There aren't any parks.
QuestionIs there a park? Is there any milk?Are there any parks?
Short answersYes, there is. / No, there isn't.Yes, there are. / No, there aren't.

There's is very common in speech. We don't usually write a short form of there are.

Use some in positive sentences and any in negatives and questions with plural and uncountable nouns: There are some apples. There aren't any apples. Are there any apples?

How many… are there?

How many + plural noun + are there: How many students are there in your class? – There are 25.

There is or it is?

Use there is to say something exists. Use it is to describe it: There's a new café in town. It's very nice.

Don't use it has to say something exists: There's a park near my house. (not It has a park near my house)

There was and there were

In the past, use there was (one thing / uncountable) and there were (plural): There was a party last night. There were lots of people.

Examples

  1. There's a supermarket at the end of the street. (one thing)
  2. There are twenty students in my class. (plural)
  3. There isn't a lift in this building. (negative)
  4. There aren't any eggs in the fridge. (negative + any)
  5. Is there a pharmacy near here? (question)
  6. Are there any good restaurants in your town? (plural question)
  7. There's some water in the bottle. (uncountable)
  8. How many people are there in your family? (how many)
  9. There's a new shop on Main Street. It's really cheap. (there is, then it is)
  10. There were a lot of people at the concert. (past, plural)

Common mistakes

❌ There are a book on the table.

✅ There is a book on the table. (One thing → there is.)

❌ There is two cats in the garden.

✅ There are two cats in the garden. (Plural → there are. (You may hear there's + plural in casual speech, but it isn't correct in writing.))

❌ Is there any shops near here?

✅ Are there any shops near here? (Plural question → are there.)

❌ There aren't some apples.

✅ There aren't any apples. (Negatives use any.)

❌ It has a park near my house.

✅ There's a park near my house. (To say something exists, use there is.)

❌ There's a new café. There's very nice.

✅ There's a new café. It's very nice. (To describe it, use it is.)
