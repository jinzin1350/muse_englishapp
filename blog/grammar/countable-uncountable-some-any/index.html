Countable and Uncountable Nouns: Some and Any

Level: A1 · Free grammar guide

Countable nouns are things we can count: one apple, two apples. Uncountable nouns are things we can't count one by one, like liquids, materials and ideas: water, rice, money, information.

Uncountable nouns have no plural and don't take a / an.

Countable or uncountable?

CountableUncountable
a / an + singular: an apple, a chairno a / an: water, music
plural: apples, chairsno plural: not waters, musics
How many apples?How much water?

Common uncountable nouns

water, milk, coffee, tea, juice, bread, rice, pasta, cheese, meat, sugar, salt, money, time, information, advice, news, homework, furniture, luggage, music, weather, traffic

Some nouns are uncountable in English but countable in other languages: information, advice, news, furniture, luggage, homework. Say some information (not informations) and a piece of advice (not an advice). News takes a singular verb: The news is good.

To count uncountable things, use a container or a unit: a bottle of water, a cup of coffee, a slice of bread, a piece of information, a kilo of rice.

Some nouns can be both: Coffee is expensive (uncountable), but Two coffees, please (= two cups of coffee).

Some and any

Countable pluralUncountable
PositiveThere are some eggs.There's some milk.
NegativeThere aren't any eggs.There isn't any milk.
QuestionAre there any eggs?Is there any milk?

Use some in questions when you offer or ask for something: Would you like some tea? Can I have some water?

How much or how many?

Use how many with countable plural nouns and how much with uncountable nouns: How many eggs? How much sugar? More practice: Much, Many, A Lot Of, A Few, A Little.

Examples

  1. Can I have an apple, please? (countable, one)
  2. Can I have some water? (asking for something: some)
  3. We don't have any bread. (negative: any)
  4. Are there any tomatoes in the fridge? (question: any)
  5. I need some information about the course. (information is uncountable)
  6. There's some rice in the cupboard. (uncountable)
  7. She gave me a piece of advice. (counting an uncountable noun)
  8. How much money do you have? (uncountable)
  9. How many brothers do you have? (countable)
  10. The news is good today. (news + singular verb)

Common mistakes

❌ I need an information.

✅ I need some information. (Information is uncountable: no a/an.)

❌ Can you give me some advices?

✅ Can you give me some advice? (Advice has no plural.)

❌ There aren't some eggs.

✅ There aren't any eggs. (Negatives use any.)

❌ How many money do you have?

✅ How much money do you have? (Money is uncountable: how much.)

❌ The news are bad.

✅ The news is bad. (News takes a singular verb.)

❌ I bought two breads.

✅ I bought two loaves of bread. (Bread is uncountable. Use a unit: a loaf, a slice.)

❌ We have a lot of homeworks.

✅ We have a lot of homework. (Homework has no plural.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. Can I have ___ apple?
Apple is countable and starts with a vowel sound: an apple.
2. There isn't ___ milk in the fridge.
Negative with an uncountable noun: any.
3. Choose the uncountable noun.
Information is uncountable: no a/an, no plural.
4. Can you give me ___ advice?
Advice is uncountable: some advice.
5. Are there ___ eggs?
Questions with plural nouns: any.
6. I bought ___ bread for breakfast.
Bread is uncountable: some bread.
7. How ___ money do you need?
Money is uncountable: how much.
8. How ___ sisters have you got?
Sisters is countable plural: how many.
9. Choose the correct sentence.
Homework is uncountable: no plural, no a, and use a lot of or much, not many.
10. The news ___ very good today.
News is uncountable and takes a singular verb: is.
11. Would you like ___ tea?
Offers use some: Would you like some tea?
12. There are ___ people in the park.
Positive sentence with a plural noun: some.
13. We need a ___ of water for the trip.
Water is a liquid: a bottle of water.
14. I don't have ___ time today.
Negative with an uncountable noun: any.
15. She has ___ beautiful furniture in her flat.
Furniture is uncountable: some furniture.
16. Choose the countable noun.
You can count potatoes: one potato, two potatoes.
