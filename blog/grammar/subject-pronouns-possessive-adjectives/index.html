Subject Pronouns and Possessive Adjectives

A1 · A2 · B1 · B2

Subject pronouns (I, you, he, she, it, we, they) come before the verb: She works in a bank. Possessive adjectives (my, your, his, her, its, our, their) come before a noun: Her bank is near my house.

For me, him, her, us, them, see Object Pronouns. For mine, yours, hers, see Possessive Pronouns.

A1 · Beginner

Subject pronouns: I, you, he, she, it, we, they A1

SingularPlural
Iwe
youyou
he (a man / boy), she (a woman / girl), it (a thing / animal)they (people or things)

  • I'm a student. (I)
  • She is my sister. (she)
  • They live in Toronto. (they)
  • It's very hot today. (it (weather))

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ My father he is a doctor. → ✅ My father is a doctor. (don't repeat the subject)
  • ❌ Is very expensive. → ✅ It is very expensive. (English needs a subject)
  • ❌ i am from Iran. → ✅ I am from Iran. (capital I)

Possessive adjectives: my, your, his, her, its, our, their A1

Subject pronounPossessive adjective
Imy
youyour
hehis
sheher
itits
weour
theytheir

  • This is my brother. (I → my)
  • Sara is in her room. (she → her)
  • Reza lives with his parents. (he → his)
  • The dog is eating its food. (it → its)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Maryam is visiting his grandmother. → ✅ Maryam is visiting her grandmother. (the owner is a woman: her)
  • ❌ They love they children. → ✅ They love their children. (they → their)
  • ❌ Is this you bag? → ✅ Is this your bag? (you → your)

Practice: A1

1. This is my mother. ___ is a teacher.
A woman: she.
2. Ali and I are friends. ___ go to the same school.
Ali and I = we.
3. Where's my phone? ___ was here a minute ago. (one word)
A thing: it.
4. Ali has a question about his homework, so he's talking to ___ teacher.
Ali = he → his.
5. Mina and Leila are with ___ mother. They're sisters.
Two people → their.
6. I love ___ new phone. I bought it yesterday, and it's great! (one word: a possessive)
I → my.

A2 · Elementary

Its or it's? Their, there or they're? Your or you're? A2

PossessiveSounds the sameMeaning
itsit'sit is / it has
theirthere, they'rea place / they are
youryou'reyou are
whosewho'swho is / who has

  • The tree lost its leaves. (possessive)
  • It's a beautiful day. (it is)
  • They're at their friend's house over there. (they are / possessive / place)
  • You're going to love your new room. (you are / possessive)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The dog hurt it's paw. → ✅ The dog hurt its paw. (possessive: its)
  • ❌ Their going to be late. → ✅ They're going to be late. (they are: they're)
  • ❌ Is this you're coat? → ✅ Is this your coat? (possessive: your)

Practice: A2

7. ___ very cold today. Wear a coat.
It is → it's.
8. My neighbours are on holiday. ___ house is empty.
Possessive + noun: their.
9. ___ (you / are) late again! (one word)
You are → you're.

B1 · Intermediate

It for weather, time and distance; it as an empty subject B1

UseExample
weatherIt's cold. It's snowing.
time and datesIt's half past six. It's Monday.
distanceIt's about 5 km from here to the beach.
it + adjective + to / thatIt's easy to learn. It's strange that he didn't call.
it takes + timeIt takes an hour to get there.

  • It's windy today. (weather)
  • It's ten past nine. (time)
  • How far is it to the airport? (distance)
  • It's nice to meet you. (it + adjective + to)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Is very hot today. → ✅ It's very hot today. (weather: it)
  • ❌ This is important to drink water. → ✅ It's important to drink water. (it + adjective + to)
  • ❌ It needs two hours to get there. → ✅ It takes two hours to get there. (it takes + time)

Practice: B1

10. ___ snowing! Let's go outside.
Weather: it.
11. ___ about 20 minutes to walk to the station.
Time needed: it takes.
12. What day is ___ today? ~ It's Friday. (one word)
Dates and days: it.

B2 · Upper-intermediate

Singular they, generic you and one B2

UseExample
unknown personSomeone called, but they didn't leave a message.
after everyone, anyone, eachEveryone should bring their own lunch.
a person who uses theyAlex is my colleague. They're from Ottawa.
generic youYou can't smoke here. You never know what will happen.
generic one (formal)One should always be polite.

  • If a student is late, they must sign in at reception. (unknown person)
  • Everybody did their best. (after everybody)
  • You have to be 18 to vote here. (generic you)
  • One must respect the law. (generic one (formal))

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ If a customer complains, they is always right. → ✅ If a customer complains, they are always right. (singular they + plural verb)
  • ❌ Somebody left there bag. → ✅ Somebody left their bag. (possessive: their)

Practice: B2

13. Someone left ___ umbrella on the bus.
Unknown person: their.
14. If anyone calls, tell ___ I'll be back at five.
Unknown person, object: them.
15. A good teacher always listens to ___ students. (one word)
Singular they: their (or his / her).
