Modifying Comparatives (much better, twice as big)

Level: B1 · Free grammar guide

We can say how much bigger, better or more expensive something is by putting a word before the comparative: much bigger, slightly cheaper, far better, even worse.

We can also modify as… as: just as good as, twice as expensive as, nowhere near as fast as.

Big and small differences

DifferenceWordsExample
bigmuch, far, a lot, considerablyThe train is much faster than the bus.
smalla bit, a little, slightlyThis hotel is slightly cheaper.
surprisingevenThe first film was good, but this one is even better.

Don't use very with comparatives: much better (not very better). And don't use more with -er: much faster (not more faster).

The same words work with more + adjective: a lot more expensive, slightly more comfortable, far less interesting.

Modifying as… as

  • the same: just as good as, exactly as big as
  • almost the same: nearly as fast as, almost as tall as
  • not quite the same: not quite as warm as
  • a big difference: not nearly as / nowhere near as expensive as
  • multiples: twice as big as, three times as expensive as

Changing and connected comparatives

Repeat the comparative for something that keeps changing: It's getting harder and harder. Prices are going up more and more quickly.

Use the + comparative, the + comparative when one change causes another: The more you practise, the better you get. The sooner, the better.

Examples

  1. My new job is much more interesting than my old one. (big difference)
  2. Could you speak a bit more slowly, please? (small difference)
  3. The test was far easier than I expected. (big difference)
  4. It's cold today, but tomorrow will be even colder. (even)
  5. Their house is twice as big as ours. (multiple)
  6. The sequel isn't nearly as good as the first film. (big difference, negative)
  7. Online tickets are just as expensive as tickets at the station. (the same)
  8. The weather isn't quite as warm as yesterday. (small difference, negative)
  9. English is becoming more and more important. (changing)
  10. The older I get, the less I worry. (connected)

Common mistakes

❌ This is very better.

✅ This is much better. (Use much, far or a lot with comparatives, not very.)

❌ He's more taller than me.

✅ He's much taller than me. (Don't use more with -er adjectives.)

❌ Their house is two times bigger as ours.

✅ Their house is twice as big as ours. (Twice as + adjective + as.)

❌ More you study, better you do.

✅ The more you study, the better you do. (The + comparative, the + comparative.)

❌ It's getting more hard and hard.

✅ It's getting harder and harder. (Repeat the comparative form: harder and harder.)

❌ She's a lot of more confident now.

✅ She's a lot more confident now. (A lot + comparative, without of.)
