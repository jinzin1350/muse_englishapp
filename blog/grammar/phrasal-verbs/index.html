فعل‌های عبارتیPhrasal Verbs

A phrasal verb is a verb + a small word called a particle (up, down, on, off, out, back, in, over): get up, turn on, look after. The particle often changes the meaning completely: look (see) / look for (try to find) / look after (take care of).

Phrasal verbs are very common in everyday English, so learn them in real sentences, not as lists. This page also explains word order: turn it on, not turn on it.

A2 · مقدماتی

Get up, turn on, put on, look for A2

Everyday phrasal verbs: get up, wake up, sit down, stand up, turn on / off, put on, take off, look for, come back, go out. The particle changes the meaning.

Phrasal verbMeaningExample
get upleave your bedI get up at 7.
wake upstop sleepingI woke up at 5.
sit down / stand upsit / standPlease sit down.
turn on / turn offstart / stop a machine or lightTurn off the TV.
put on / take offstart / stop wearing clothesPut on your coat. Take off your shoes.
look fortry to findI'm looking for my keys.
come backreturnWhen are you coming back?
go outleave home to have funLet's go out tonight.

In English, you turn on a light or a TV (not open the light), and you put on clothes before you wear them.

  • I usually get up at half past six. (get up)
  • Turn on the light, please. (turn on)
  • It's cold. Put on a jumper. (put on)
  • She's looking for a new job. (look for)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ Can you open the light? → ✅ Can you turn on the light? (lights and machines: turn on)
  • ❌ I'm looking my phone. → ✅ I'm looking for my phone. (look for = try to find)
  • ❌ Put off your shoes, please. → ✅ Take off your shoes, please. (remove clothes: take off)

تمرین سطح A2

1. It's cold. Put ___ your jacket.
Start wearing: put on.
2. What time do you usually get ___ in the morning?
Leave your bed: get up.
3. Please take ___ your shoes before you come in. (one word)
Remove clothes: take off.

B1 · متوسط

Turn it off, not turn off it: separable verbs B1

With many phrasal verbs, a noun can go before or after the particle (turn off the TV / turn the TV off), but a pronoun must go in the middle: turn it off (not turn off it).

With a nounWith a pronoun
Turn on the TV. / Turn the TV on.Turn it on. (not Turn on it)
Pick up your toys. / Pick your toys up.Pick them up.
Fill in the form. / Fill the form in.Fill it in.
Wake up the kids. / Wake the kids up.Wake them up.

Common separable phrasal verbs: turn on / off, put on, take off, pick up, throw away, give back, fill in, write down, try on, put away, wake up (someone), call back.

A pronoun (it, them, me, him, her, us) always goes between the verb and the particle.

  • Can you pick me up at six? (pronoun in the middle)
  • Write down the address. / Write the address down. (noun before or after)
  • Don't throw it away! (pronoun in the middle)
  • Can I try these jeans on? (noun in the middle)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I'll pick up you at six. → ✅ I'll pick you up at six. (pronoun in the middle)
  • ❌ Turn off it, please. → ✅ Turn it off, please. (pronoun in the middle)
  • ❌ Can you fill in it? → ✅ Can you fill it in? (pronoun in the middle)

Look after her, get on the bus: inseparable verbs B1

Some phrasal verbs are inseparable: the object always goes after the particle, even a pronoun (look after her, come across it). Some have no object at all: get up, grow up, break down.

Phrasal verbMeaningExample
look aftertake care ofShe looks after her grandma. She looks after her.
come acrossfind by chanceI came across an old photo.
run intomeet by chanceI ran into Ali at the mall.
get on / get offenter / leave a bus or trainGet off at the next stop.
deal withhandleI'll deal with it.
get overrecover fromShe got over the flu quickly.
break down (no object)stop workingMy car broke down.
grow up (no object)become an adultI grew up in Shiraz.
turn up (no object)arriveHe turned up late.

With inseparable verbs, never put the object in the middle: look after the baby (not look the baby after).

Grow up has no object. To raise children, use bring up: She brought up three children.

  • Who looks after your dog when you travel? (look after)
  • I came across this café by chance. (come across)
  • The plane took off on time. (no object)
  • I'll deal with the problem tomorrow. (deal with)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ Can you look your sister after? → ✅ Can you look after your sister? (inseparable)
  • ❌ I came an old letter across. → ✅ I came across an old letter. (inseparable)
  • ❌ She grew up three children. → ✅ She brought up three children. (raise children: bring up)

تمرین سطح B1

4. This music is too loud. Can you turn ___?
Pronoun in the middle: turn it down.
5. Which is correct?
Pronoun in the middle: give it back.
6. Your room is a mess. Pick your clothes up and put ___ away. (one word)
Pronoun in the middle: put them away.
7. My mother looks ___ my kids when I'm at work.
Take care of: look after.
8. Which is correct?
Inseparable: ran into him.
9. Where did you grow ___? ~ In a small village near Tabriz. (one word)
Grow up.

B2 · بالاتر از متوسط

Look forward to, run out of, put up with B2

Three-part phrasal verbs (verb + particle + preposition) can't be separated: look forward to, get on with, run out of, put up with, come up with, catch up with, look up to, get away with, cut down on, keep up with.

Phrasal verbMeaningExample
look forward tobe happy about something in the futureI'm looking forward to the holidays.
get on withhave a good relationship withI get on well with my boss.
run out ofhave no moreWe've run out of milk.
put up withaccept something badI can't put up with the noise.
come up withthink of (an idea)She came up with a great plan.
look up toadmireI look up to my father.
get away withnot be punishedHe got away with cheating.
cut down onuse or do lessI'm trying to cut down on sugar.
keep up withstay at the same levelIt's hard to keep up with the news.

The object always goes at the end: I can't put up with it.

In look forward to, to is a preposition, so use -ing: I'm looking forward to seeing you.

  • I'm looking forward to the weekend. (look forward to)
  • We ran out of petrol on the motorway. (run out of)
  • How do you put up with him? (put up with)
  • He came up with a clever solution. (come up with)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I'm looking forward to see you. → ✅ I'm looking forward to seeing you. (to + -ing)
  • ❌ We've run out petrol. → ✅ We've run out of petrol. (run out of)

One phrasal verb, many meanings B2

Many phrasal verbs have several meanings: take off (clothes / a plane / become successful), pick up (lift / collect / learn), make up (invent / become friends again), work out (exercise / solve), put off (postpone).

Phrasal verbMeaningsExamples
take offremove clothes; leave the ground; become successfulTake off your coat. The plane took off. Her career took off.
pick uplift; collect someone; learn without studyingPick up the box. I'll pick you up at 6. I picked up some Spanish.
make upinvent; become friends againHe made up an excuse. They argued, then made up.
turn uparrive; make louderShe turned up late. Turn up the radio.
work outexercise; find the answerI work out at the gym. I can't work out this puzzle.
put offpostpone; make someone not want somethingThey put off the meeting. The smell put me off.
give upstop a habit; stop tryingHe gave up smoking. Don't give up!

Use the context to understand the meaning.

In formal writing, a one-word verb often sounds better: find out → discover, put off → postpone, carry out → conduct, go up → increase, look into → investigate.

  • The plane took off at 6 am. (leave the ground)
  • I picked up some Italian when I lived in Rome. (learn without studying)
  • They had a fight but made up the next day. (become friends again)
  • The meeting has been put off until Friday. (postpone)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ She gave up to smoke last year. → ✅ She gave up smoking last year. (give up + -ing)
  • ❌ He made up with a story about a lost wallet. → ✅ He made up a story about a lost wallet. (invent: make up (no with))

تمرین سطح B2

10. We've run ___ of coffee. Can you buy some?
Have no more: run out of.
11. I'm looking forward to ___ you next week.
Look forward to + -ing.
12. I don't know how you put ___ with your noisy neighbours. (one word)
Accept something bad: put up with.
13. In Her career really took off after her first film, took off means:
Take off = become successful.
14. Which is the most formal?
Formal one-word verb: postpone.
15. My grandfather gave ___ smoking when he was 50. (one word)
Stop a habit: give up.

C1 · پیشرفته

Particle meanings, phrasal nouns, and passives C1

Particles often have a general meaning (up = completely, out = to the end or disappear, on = continue, off = stop or leave, down = decrease). Many phrasal verbs make nouns (a breakdown, an outbreak, a setback) and can be passive (It was called off).

ParticleCommon meaningExamples
upcompletely; increaseeat up, use up, tidy up; speed up, go up
outto the end; disappear; clearlysort out, run out, die out, wipe out, point out
oncontinuecarry on, keep on, go on, drag on
offleave or start; stop or cancelset off; call off, wear off, lay off
downdecrease; stop working; writeslow down, cut down; break down; write down
overthink or check carefullythink over, look over, talk over

Phrasal nouns are common in news and business English: a breakdown, a takeover, an outbreak, a setback, a turnout, a drawback, a cutback, the outcome, the input. They're usually written as one word (sometimes with a hyphen: a check-in).

Phrasal verbs with an object can be passive: The match was called off. The issue is being looked into.

  • The effect of the coffee soon wore off. (off = stop)
  • Many species have died out. (out = disappear)
  • There has been an outbreak of flu. (phrasal noun)
  • The concert was called off because of the storm. (passive)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ The company is laying out 200 workers. → ✅ The company is laying off 200 workers. (lay off = stop employing)
  • ❌ We had a break down on the motorway. → ✅ We had a breakdown on the motorway. (phrasal noun: one word)

تمرین سطح C1

16. The match was called ___ because of the heavy rain.
Cancel: call off.
17. There has been a serious ___ of measles in the city.
Phrasal noun: an outbreak.
18. The effect of the painkiller will wear ___ after a few hours. (one word)
Stop gradually: wear off.
