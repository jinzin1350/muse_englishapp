کلمه‌های پرسشی و ساختن سؤالQuestion Words and Question Forms

In English questions, the auxiliary verb (be, do, have, can, will…) usually comes before the subject: Are you hungry? Do you like tea? Can she swim?

With question words (what, where, when…), the word goes first: Where do you live? This page goes from basic questions to negative and echo questions. For Do you know where…?, see Indirect Questions.

A1 · A2 · B1 · B2 · C1

A1 · مبتدی

Yes/no questions: Are you…? Do you…? Can you…? A1

Put be or an auxiliary (do, does, did, can, will, have) before the subject: Are you ready? Does she work here? Can you swim?

VerbStatementQuestion
beShe is Canadian.Is she Canadian?
present simpleThey live here.Do they live here?
he / she / itHe works here.Does he work here?
past simpleYou saw it.Did you see it?
modalShe can drive.Can she drive?
present perfectYou've been there.Have you been there?

With be and modals, just change the order. With other verbs, add do / does / did and use the base verb: Does she like it? Not Does she likes it?

Short answers repeat the auxiliary: Yes, she does. / No, I can't. / Yes, they are.

  • Are you a student? ~ Yes, I am. (be)
  • Do you speak English? ~ Yes, I do. (do)
  • Did they win the match? ~ No, they didn't. (did)
  • Have you finished? ~ Not yet. (have)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ You are hungry? (in writing) → ✅ Are you hungry? (be before the subject)
  • ❌ Does she likes coffee? → ✅ Does she like coffee? (does + base verb)
  • ❌ Do you can swim? → ✅ Can you swim? (modal: no do)

Wh- question words: what, where, when, who, why, how A1

Put the question word first, then the auxiliary and the subject: Where do you live? What is your name? Why did she leave?

WordAsks aboutExample
whatthings, actionsWhat do you do?
whereplacesWhere is the station?
whentimeWhen did you arrive?
whopeopleWho is that man?
whyreasonsWhy are you late?
howway, conditionHow do you get to work?
whichchoiceWhich bus goes to the airport?
whoseownerWhose phone is this?

Order: question word + auxiliary / be + subject + main verb: Where do you work? What are you doing?

  • What is your phone number? (what)
  • Where did you go on holiday? (where + did)
  • Why is the shop closed? (why + be)
  • Whose keys are these? (whose)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ Where you live? → ✅ Where do you live? (add do)
  • ❌ What time the film starts? → ✅ What time does the film start? (add does + base verb)
  • ❌ Why you are sad? → ✅ Why are you sad? (be before the subject)

تمرین سطح A1

1. ___ you like pizza? ~ Yes, I love it.
Present simple with you: Do you…?
2. ___ your sister a doctor?
Be question: Is your sister…?
3. ___ (he / play) the guitar? ~ Yes, he does.
Does + subject + base verb.
4. ___ is your birthday? ~ In May.
Time: When.
5. ___ do you go to school? ~ By bus.
The way: How.
6. ___ do you live? ~ In Toronto. (one word)
Place: Where.

A2 · مقدماتی

How much, how many, how long, how often, how far A2

How + adjective / adverb asks about amount, time and frequency: How much is it? How long does it take? How often do you exercise?

QuestionAsks aboutExample answer
How much…?price, uncountable amountIt's $20. / A lot.
How many…?countable numberThree.
How long…?length of timeTwo hours.
How often…?frequencyTwice a week.
How far…?distanceAbout 5 km.
How old…?ageI'm 30.

How long does it take (to…)? asks about the time a journey or job needs: How long does it take to get to the airport? ~ It takes about 40 minutes.

How much goes with uncountable nouns (money, time, water); how many with plural nouns (people, books).

  • How much does this jacket cost? (price)
  • How many brothers and sisters do you have? (number)
  • How long does it take to walk there? (time)
  • How often do you visit your parents? (frequency)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ How many money do you have? → ✅ How much money do you have? (uncountable: how much)
  • ❌ How long it takes to get there? → ✅ How long does it take to get there? (add does)

Who saw you? or Who did you see? Subject and object questions A2

When who / what is the subject, don't use do / did: Who called you? When it's the object, use do / did: Who did you call?

Subject question (no do)Object question (with do)
Who called you? (Someone called you.)Who did you call? (You called someone.)
What happened?What did you do?
Who lives here?Who do you live with?

Ask yourself: is the question word the person or thing that does the action? If yes, it's the subject: use normal word order, no do: Who broke the window? ~ Tom did.

If the question word is the person or thing that receives the action, use do / does / did: Who did Tom see? ~ He saw Maria.

  • Who told you that? (subject: someone told you)
  • Who did you tell? (object: you told someone)
  • What happened at the meeting? (subject: no do)
  • Which team won the match? (subject: no do)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ What did happen? → ✅ What happened? (subject question: no did)
  • ❌ Who did break the window? (asking who did it) → ✅ Who broke the window? (subject question)
  • ❌ Who you saw at the party? → ✅ Who did you see at the party? (object question: did)

Who is she talking to? What is it like? Prepositions at the end A2

In questions, prepositions usually go at the end: Who are you talking to? Where are you from? What is it like? = describe it.

When a verb has a preposition (talk to, look at, come from, wait for, think about), the preposition goes at the end of the question: What are you looking at? Who is this present for?

What is … like? asks for a description: What's your new school like? ~ It's big and modern. It's different from Do you like…? (Do you enjoy it?)

In very formal English, the preposition can go first: To whom did you speak? In normal English, Who did you speak to? is much more common.

  • Where are you from? (preposition at the end)
  • What are you thinking about? (think about)
  • Who is the letter from? (from)
  • What's the weather like in Vancouver? (describe it)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ From where are you? → ✅ Where are you from? (preposition at the end)
  • ❌ How is your new boss like? → ✅ What is your new boss like? (What … like?)

B1 · متوسط

What or which? What kind of…? B1

which = a choice from a small, known group: Which colour do you want, red or blue? what = an open choice: What colour is your car?

Use which when there is a limited number of options, often mentioned or obvious: Which of these bags is yours? Which way is the station, left or right?

Use what for an open question with many possible answers: What music do you like? What time is it?

Which one(s)? and which of + plural: Which one do you want? Which of your friends is coming? (Not what of.)

What kind of / what sort of asks about a type: What kind of films do you like?

  • Which do you prefer, tea or coffee? (two options)
  • What do you want for your birthday? (open question)
  • Which of these photos do you like best? (which of)
  • What kind of job are you looking for? (type)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ What of these is yours? → ✅ Which of these is yours? (which of)
  • ❌ What one do you want? → ✅ Which one do you want? (which one / which of)

تمرین سطح B1

16. ___ one do you want, the red one or the blue one?
A choice from two: Which.
17. ___ of your parents do you look like?
Which of + group.
18. ___ kind of music do you listen to? (one word)
What kind of…?

B2 · بالاتر از متوسط

Negative questions: Don't you…? Isn't it…? B2

Negative questions (Don't you…? Isn't it…? Haven't you…?) show surprise, ask for agreement, or check something we expect to be true.

Negative questions are used:

  • for surprise: Don't you like chocolate? (I'm surprised.)
  • to ask for agreement: Isn't it a beautiful day? (I think it is.)
  • to check what we expect: Haven't we met before? (I think we have.)
  • in complaints: Can't you be quiet?

Answering: answer about the fact, not the question form: Don't you like coffee? ~ No, I don't. (= I don't like it) / Yes, I do. (= I like it)

In formal English, not can go after the subject: Do you not agree?

  • Don't you remember me? (surprise)
  • Isn't this view amazing? (asking for agreement)
  • Haven't you finished yet? (surprise / impatience)
  • Wouldn't it be better to wait? (suggestion)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ Don't you like it? ~ No, I like it. → ✅ Don't you like it? ~ Yes, I do. / Yes, I like it. (answer about the fact)
  • ❌ Do you don't want it? → ✅ Don't you want it? (negative question: don't + you)

تمرین سطح B2

19. ___ tired? You've been working for ten hours!
Surprise: Aren't you…?
20. Don't you eat meat? ~ ___ I'm a vegetarian.
Answer about the fact: No, I don't.
21. ___ (it / not / be) a lovely evening?
Asking for agreement: Isn't it…?

C1 · پیشرفته

Echo questions and statement questions: You did what? C1

Echo questions repeat part of what someone said to show surprise or check: She's moving to Peru. ~ She's moving where? Statement questions keep normal word order with rising intonation: You're leaving?

An echo question repeats the sentence and replaces the surprising part with a question word, with strong stress: I paid $500 for it. ~ You paid how much? He married his boss. ~ He married who?

A statement question uses normal word order with rising intonation. It checks or expresses surprise about something we think we heard or understood: You've finished already? So you're not coming?

Short reply questions show interest: I've just got engaged. ~ Have you? Congratulations! See So Do I, Neither Do I.

These are spoken forms. In writing, use normal questions, except in dialogue.

  • I've sold the car. ~ You've sold what? (echo question)
  • They're getting married in Bali. ~ Where? (short echo)
  • You're not hungry? I made a huge dinner! (statement question)
  • He's 90. ~ Is he? He looks much younger. (reply question)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ You paid how much? (in a formal essay) → ✅ How much did you pay? (echo questions are spoken forms)

تمرین سطح C1

Echo question: replace the number with how many.
