Inversion After Negative Adverbials

B2 · C1 · C2

In formal or dramatic English, when a negative or limiting word starts the sentence (never, rarely, not only, only when, under no circumstances), we invert the subject and the auxiliary, like in a question: I have never seen such a mess. → Never have I seen such a mess.

If there's no auxiliary, add do / does / did: We rarely go out. → Rarely do we go out. For inverted conditionals (Had I known…), see Inverted Conditionals.

B2 · Upper-intermediate

Never have I…, rarely do we…, little did I know B2

Normal orderInversion (formal / emphatic)
I have never felt so happy.Never have I felt so happy.
We rarely see snow here.Rarely do we see snow here.
She seldom complains.Seldom does she complain.
I little knew what would happen.Little did I know what would happen.

  • Never have I eaten such delicious food. (never + present perfect)
  • Rarely do tourists visit this village. (rarely + do)
  • Seldom has the team played so well. (seldom + has)
  • Little did we know that our lives were about to change. (little did)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Never I have seen such a beautiful view. → ✅ Never have I seen such a beautiful view. (auxiliary + subject)
  • ❌ Rarely we go to the cinema. → ✅ Rarely do we go to the cinema. (add do)

C1 · Advanced

Not only … but also, not until, not once C1

Normal orderInversion
It's cheap, and it's also fast.Not only is it cheap, but it's also fast.
She speaks French and Spanish too.Not only does she speak French, but she also speaks Spanish.
They didn't arrive until midnight.Not until midnight did they arrive.
He didn't thank me once.Not once did he thank me.

  • Not only did he forget my birthday, but he also forgot our anniversary. (not only)
  • Not until the end of the film did I understand the story. (not until)
  • Not once has she been late. (not once)
  • Not only is this phone light, but it's also waterproof. (not only + be)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Not only he forgot, but he also lied. → ✅ Not only did he forget, but he also lied. (not only + auxiliary + subject)
  • ❌ Not until did I see the photos I believed it. → ✅ Not until I saw the photos did I believe it. (inversion in the main clause)

Only when, only after, only then, only by C1

PatternExample
Only when + clauseOnly when you leave home do you appreciate your parents.
Only after + noun / clauseOnly after the exam did I relax.
Only thenI read the letter again. Only then did I understand.
Only by + -ingOnly by working hard can you succeed.
Only if + clauseOnly if you pay in advance will you get a discount.
Only in + nounOnly in Japan have I seen such clean streets.

  • Only when it started to rain did we go inside. (only when)
  • Only by practising every day can you improve. (only by)
  • Only then did I realise my mistake. (only then)
  • Only after he left did I find his note. (only after)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Only when I got home I realised. → ✅ Only when I got home did I realise. (inversion in the main clause)
  • ❌ Only by work hard can you succeed. → ✅ Only by working hard can you succeed. (only by + -ing)

Under no circumstances, on no account, in no way C1

PhraseExample
Under no circumstancesUnder no circumstances should you leave the building.
On no accountOn no account must you touch the wires.
At no timeAt no time was the public in danger.
In no wayIn no way is this your fault.
NowhereNowhere else will you find such good coffee.
No way (informal)No way am I doing that!

  • Under no circumstances should you share your password. (rule)
  • On no account must the children play near the river. (warning)
  • At no time did I promise you a pay rise. (strong denial)
  • In no way does this change our plans. (in no way)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Under no circumstances you should open the box. → ✅ Under no circumstances should you open the box. (inversion: should you)
  • ❌ At no time I said that. → ✅ At no time did I say that. (add did)

C2 · Proficiency

Hardly had I, no sooner, not a word did he say C2

PatternExample
Hardly / Scarcely had … whenHardly had we sat down when the phone rang.
No sooner had … thanNo sooner had she arrived than she started complaining.
Not a (single) + nounNot a single word did he say.
So + adjective + inversionSo strong was the wind that trees fell down.
Such + be + nounSuch was the demand that the tickets sold out.

  • Scarcely had the film started when the power went off. (scarcely … when)
  • No sooner had I paid than I saw it cheaper online. (no sooner … than)
  • Not a single person did I recognise at the party. (not a single)
  • So loud was the music that we couldn't talk. (so + adjective)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Hardly had I arrived than it started to rain. → ✅ Hardly had I arrived when it started to rain. (hardly … when)
  • ❌ Not a word he said. (formal inversion) → ✅ Not a word did he say. (not a word + did)
