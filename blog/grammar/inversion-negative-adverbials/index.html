Inversion After Negative Adverbials

Level: C1 · Free grammar guide

When a sentence starts with a negative or limiting word, such as never, rarely, hardly, not only, only when, we use question word order (auxiliary + subject): Never have I seen such a beautiful view.

This is called inversion. It's formal and emphatic, and it's common in writing, speeches and storytelling.

How inversion works

Normal orderInversion
I have never seen such a view.Never have I seen such a view.
We rarely see talent like this.Rarely do we see talent like this.
He was late and he forgot the tickets.Not only was he late, but he also forgot the tickets.
I realised only when I got home.Only when I got home did I realise.

If there is no auxiliary, add do / does / did: We rarely see… → Rarely do we see… With be, just put be first: Not only was he late…

Common negative adverbials

  • Never / Rarely / Seldom (+ present perfect or present simple): Seldom has he been so angry.
  • Hardly / Scarcely… when and No sooner… than (one thing immediately after another, usually past perfect): Hardly had we arrived when it started to rain. No sooner had I sat down than the phone rang.
  • Not only… but (also): Not only does she speak French, but she also speaks Arabic.
  • Only when / Only after / Only then / Not until: the inversion is in the main clause: Not until the end did we find out the truth.
  • Under no circumstances / On no account / At no time / In no way: Under no circumstances should you open this door.
  • Little (= not at all): Little did I know that my life was about to change.

No sooner goes with than; hardly / scarcely go with when. Don't mix them.

Examples

  1. Never have I felt so proud. (never + present perfect)
  2. Rarely do we get snow in April. (rarely + do)
  3. Seldom has the city looked so beautiful. (seldom)
  4. Hardly had the match started when it began to rain. (hardly… when)
  5. No sooner had she left than he arrived. (no sooner… than)
  6. Not only is the hotel cheap, but it's also very comfortable. (not only… but)
  7. Only after the meeting did I understand the problem. (only after)
  8. Not until I got home did I notice the missing wallet. (not until)
  9. On no account must you share your password. (on no account)
  10. Little did they know that we were watching. (little = not at all)

Common mistakes

❌ Never I have seen such a mess.

✅ Never have I seen such a mess. (After a negative adverbial at the start: auxiliary + subject.)

❌ Rarely we go to the cinema.

✅ Rarely do we go to the cinema. (No auxiliary in the normal sentence: add do / does / did.)

❌ No sooner had I arrived when it rained.

✅ No sooner had I arrived than it rained. (No sooner goes with than.)

❌ Only when I got home I realised.

✅ Only when I got home did I realise. (With only when, the inversion is in the main clause.)

❌ Not only he is clever, but he's also kind.

✅ Not only is he clever, but he's also kind. (Not only at the start: inversion.)

❌ Under no circumstances you should leave.

✅ Under no circumstances should you leave. (Modal + subject after a negative adverbial.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. Never ___ such a beautiful sunset.
Never at the start: have I seen.
2. No sooner had we sat down ___ the phone rang.
No sooner goes with than.
3. Hardly ___ arrived when it started to rain.
Hardly… when + past perfect inversion: had we arrived.
4. Not only ___ late, but he also forgot the tickets.
With be: Not only was he…
5. Rarely ___ such talent in a young player.
No auxiliary, so add do: Rarely do we see.
6. Only when I got home ___ that I had lost my wallet.
Inversion in the main clause: did I realise.
7. Under no circumstances ___ the door to strangers.
Modal + subject: should you open.
8. Little ___ that his life was about to change.
Little (= not at all) + inversion: did he know.
9. Not until the end of the film ___ who the killer was.
Not until… + inversion in the main clause: did we find out.
10. Seldom ___ he been so angry.
Present perfect inversion: Seldom has he been…
11. At no time ___ the passengers in danger.
With be: At no time were the passengers in danger.
12. Choose the correct sentence.
Only after… + did I understand.
13. Hardly had she left ___ he arrived.
Hardly goes with when.
14. No sooner ___ the house than I realised I'd forgotten my keys.
No sooner + past perfect inversion: had I left.
15. Not only does she speak French, ___ she also speaks Arabic.
Not only… but (also).
16. On no account ___ leave your bags unattended.
Modal + subject: must you. (On no account is already negative.)
