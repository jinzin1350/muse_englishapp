Modals of Deduction: Must, Might, Can't

Level: B1 · Free grammar guide

We use must, might, may, could and can't to make guesses (deductions) about what is true now, based on the information we have: She's not answering. She must be busy. He might be at the gym. That can't be Tom; he's in Paris.

How sure are you?

ModalHow sureExample
mustalmost sure it's trueYou've been travelling all day. You must be tired.
might / may / couldit's possibleShe might be at home. / She may be at home. / She could be at home.
might not / may notit's possible that it isn't trueHe might not be awake yet.
can'talmost sure it isn't trueThat can't be the right answer. It doesn't make sense.

For a negative deduction, British English uses can't: He can't be hungry; he's just eaten. In American English, must not is also common: He must not be hungry.

Form

  • modal + base verb: She must know him.
  • modal + be + adjective, noun or place: They might be at work.
  • modal + be + -ing for something happening now: He can't be sleeping; the light is on.

Don't use to after these modals, and don't add -s: She must be (not must to be), He might know (not mights).

Must for deduction or obligation?

You must wear a seatbelt. = a rule (obligation). You must be Anna's sister. You look just like her! = a deduction. For obligation, see Must, Have To and Should.

Can is for general possibility, not for guesses about one situation now: It might be true (not It can be true) when you're talking about one particular thing.

Examples

  1. You've been working for twelve hours. You must be exhausted. (almost sure)
  2. Someone's at the door. It might be the delivery driver. (possible)
  3. That can't be the right answer. It doesn't make sense. (almost sure it isn't true)
  4. They may be stuck in traffic. (possible)
  5. He must earn a lot of money. He's got three cars. (must + base verb)
  6. Where's my phone? It could be in the car. (possible)
  7. The lights are off. They might not be at home. (possible negative)
  8. That can't be Tom. He's in London this week. (impossible)
  9. Listen to that noise! The neighbours must be having a party. (must be + -ing)
  10. You must be Anna's brother. You look just like her. (deduction from evidence)

Common mistakes

❌ She must to be at work.

✅ She must be at work. (No to after must.)

❌ It can be true, but I'm not sure.

✅ It might be true, but I'm not sure. (For a guess about one situation, use might, may or could.)

❌ They might are at home.

✅ They might be at home. (Modal + base verb: be.)

❌ She can't knows the answer.

✅ She can't know the answer. (Modal + base verb, no -s.)

❌ It must raining. Everyone has umbrellas.

✅ It must be raining. Everyone has umbrellas. (For something happening now: must be + -ing.)

❌ He mights be late.

✅ He might be late. (Modal verbs never take -s.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. You've been travelling all day. You ___ be tired.
You're almost sure: must + base verb.
2. That ___ be Tom. He's in Australia this month.
You're sure it isn't Tom: can't.
3. I'm not sure where Lisa is. She ___ be in the library.
I'm not sure = it's possible: might.
4. He ___ know the answer. He's a maths teacher.
You're almost sure from the evidence: must know.
5. Listen! The neighbours ___ a party.
Something happening now: must be + -ing.
6. Choose the correct sentence.
Modal + base verb: might be. No to, no -s.
7. The lights are off and nobody's answering. They ___ at home.
You're almost sure they aren't at home: can't be.
8. It ___ rain later, so take an umbrella just in case.
Just in case = it's possible: might.
9. Where are my keys? – They ___ be in your coat pocket. Have you checked?
A possibility: could + base verb.
10. She's been crying. Something ___ be wrong.
The evidence makes you almost sure: must.
11. That ___ be the right price. A new laptop never costs only £5.
You're sure it isn't true: can't.
12. He ___ be asleep. I can hear him talking on the phone.
The evidence shows it isn't true: can't.
13. You ___ be Anna's sister. You look exactly like her!
Strong evidence: must.
14. They may ___ stuck in traffic.
Modal + base verb: be.
15. Which sentence means 'I'm sure it isn't true'?
Can't = almost sure something isn't true.
16. She ___ the answer. She wasn't in class when we discussed it.
She wasn't there, so you're sure she doesn't know: can't know.
