Causative: Have/Get Something Done

Level: B2 · Free grammar guide

We use have something done when we arrange for another person to do something for us, usually a service: I had my hair cut yesterday. (A hairdresser cut it.)

Get something done means the same and is more informal: I need to get my car fixed.

Form: have / get + object + past participle

TenseExample
present simpleI have my car serviced every year.
past simpleShe had her nails done.
present continuousWe're having the kitchen painted.
present perfectHe's just had his eyes tested.
willI'll get the photos printed tomorrow.
modalYou should get that tooth checked.

Word order matters: the object goes before the past participle. I had my hair cut = someone cut it for me. I had cut my hair = past perfect: I cut it myself.

Bad experiences

We also use have something done for unpleasant things that happen to us, often done by someone we don't know: She had her bag stolen on the train. They had their flight cancelled.

Have someone do, get someone to do

StructureMeaningExample
have + person + base verbarrange for someone to do something (often as part of their job)I'll have my assistant call you.
get + person + to + base verbpersuade someone to do somethingI got my brother to help me move.

Examples

  1. I had my hair cut last week. (a service)
  2. We're getting the house painted next month. (present continuous for a plan)
  3. You should have your eyes tested. (modal + have + object + past participle)
  4. She had her phone stolen at the station. (a bad experience)
  5. Where do you get your clothes cleaned? (question)
  6. They've had solar panels installed. (present perfect)
  7. I need to get my passport renewed. (need to get + object + past participle)
  8. He had his car repaired at the garage. (past simple)
  9. I got my friend to check my essay. (get + person + to)
  10. The manager had us wait outside. (have + person + base verb)

Common mistakes

❌ I had cut my hair at the hairdresser's.

✅ I had my hair cut at the hairdresser's. (The object goes before the past participle.)

❌ We're having painted the house.

✅ We're having the house painted. (Have + object + past participle.)

❌ She had stolen her bag on the train.

✅ She had her bag stolen on the train. (She had stolen her bag means she stole it herself.)

❌ I got my brother help me.

✅ I got my brother to help me. (Get + person + to + base verb.)

❌ I need to get my car fix.

✅ I need to get my car fixed. (Use the past participle.)

❌ I'll have my assistant to call you.

✅ I'll have my assistant call you. (Have + person + base verb, without to.)
