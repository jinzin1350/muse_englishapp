Although, Despite and In Spite Of

Level: B1 · Free grammar guide

We use although, despite and in spite of to show contrast: something is true, but it's surprising because of something else. We went out although it was raining. We went out despite the rain.

The difference is grammar: what comes after each word.

What comes after each word?

WordFollowed byExample
although / though / even thougha clause (subject + verb)Although it was cold, we swam.
despite / in spite ofa noun or verb-ingDespite the cold, we swam. In spite of feeling tired, she kept going.
despite / in spite of + the fact thata clauseDespite the fact that it was cold, we swam.

Never say despite of. It's despite or in spite of.

Though and even though

Though is a little more informal than although. Even though is stronger: it shows more surprise. Though can also go at the end of a sentence, meaning however: It was expensive. I bought it, though.

But and however

But joins two clauses in one sentence: It was raining, but we went out. However usually starts a new sentence: It was raining. However, we went out.

Don't use although and but in the same sentence: Although it was late, we stayed. (not Although it was late, but we stayed)

Examples

  1. Although she was tired, she finished her work. (although + clause)
  2. Despite the traffic, we arrived on time. (despite + noun)
  3. In spite of the bad weather, the match went ahead. (in spite of + noun)
  4. He went to work even though he was ill. (even though: stronger)
  5. Despite having little money, they were happy. (despite + -ing)
  6. I enjoyed the film, though it was a bit long. (though + clause)
  7. Despite the fact that he trained hard, he lost the race. (the fact that + clause)
  8. In spite of losing the first game, they won the tournament. (in spite of + -ing)
  9. The restaurant was expensive. The food was great, though. (though at the end)
  10. It was sunny. However, it was quite cold. (however: new sentence)

Common mistakes

❌ Despite it was raining, we went out.

✅ Although it was raining, we went out. (Despite isn't followed by a clause. (Or: Despite the rain, we went out.))

❌ Although the rain, we went out.

✅ Despite the rain, we went out. (Although needs a clause; use despite before a noun.)

❌ In spite the noise, I slept well.

✅ In spite of the noise, I slept well. (In spite of — don't forget of.)

❌ Despite of the cost, they bought it.

✅ Despite the cost, they bought it. (There is no despite of.)

❌ Although it was late, but we stayed.

✅ Although it was late, we stayed. (Don't use although and but together.)

❌ Despite he was tired, he drove home.

✅ Despite being tired, he drove home. (Despite + -ing (or although + clause).)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. ___ it was raining, we went for a walk.
A clause follows (it was raining): Although.
2. She passed the exam despite ___ very little.
Despite + -ing.
3. In spite ___ the noise, I slept well.
The phrase is in spite of.
4. ___ the rain, we went for a walk.
A noun follows (the rain): Despite.
5. He went to work ___ he had a cold.
A clause follows (he had a cold): even though.
6. ___ feeling ill, she went to the party.
An -ing form follows: In spite of.
7. Choose the correct sentence.
Although + clause, and no but.
8. ___ the fact that it was expensive, we bought it.
Despite the fact that + clause.
9. The hotel was nice. The service was slow, ___.
At the end of a sentence, meaning however: though.
10. It was cold. ___, we went swimming.
Starting a new sentence with a comma: However.
11. ___ he trained hard, he didn't win.
A clause follows: Although.
12. We enjoyed the trip ___ the bad weather.
A noun follows: despite.
13. They were happy ___ having very little money.
An -ing form follows: despite.
14. Choose the correct ending: Despite ___, I slept well.
Despite + a noun: the noise.
15. ___ of the delay, the plane landed on time.
In spite of. (Despite of doesn't exist.)
16. It was raining, ___ we went out.
Joining two clauses in one sentence: but.
