Make or Do?

A2 · B1 · B2

Make usually means create or produce something new: make a cake, make a plan. Do is for activities, work and tasks: do your homework, do the dishes.

Many expressions don't follow the rule, so learn them as pairs: make a mistake (not do a mistake), do someone a favour (not make a favour).

A2 · Elementary

Make a cake, do your homework A2

make (create, produce)do (activities, work)
make a cake / dinner / tea / coffeedo your homework / an exercise
make a mess / a noisedo the dishes / the shopping / the cleaning
make the beddo the housework / the laundry
make clothes / a film / a listdo sport / yoga / exercise
make a phone calldo something / nothing / anything

  • I'll make some coffee. (produce)
  • Have you done your homework? (task)
  • Don't make a mess in the kitchen! (produce)
  • Who does the shopping in your family? (housework)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I need to make my homework. → ✅ I need to do my homework. (do homework)
  • ❌ Can you do a cake for the party? → ✅ Can you make a cake for the party? (make = produce)
  • ❌ I always do my bed in the morning. → ✅ I always make my bed in the morning. (make the bed)

Practice: A2

1. Can you help me <span class="gap">___</span> dinner tonight?
Produce food: make dinner.
2. I <span class="gap">___</span> my homework every evening.
Task: do homework.
3. Shh! Don't <span class="gap">___</span> a noise. The baby's asleep. (one word)
Make a noise.

B1 · Intermediate

Make a mistake, do me a favour B1

makedo
make a mistakedo someone a favour
make a decision / a choicedo your best
make money / a profitdo business
make progress / an effortdo damage / harm / good
make friendsdo research
make a plan / an appointmentdo a course / a degree
make an excuse / a complaintdo an exam / a test (BrE; also take)

  • Everyone makes mistakes. (make a mistake)
  • Could you do me a favour? (do a favour)
  • She's making good progress with her English. (make progress)
  • The storm did a lot of damage. (do damage)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I did a big mistake. → ✅ I made a big mistake. (make a mistake)
  • ❌ Can you make me a favour? → ✅ Can you do me a favour? (do a favour)
  • ❌ He does a lot of money. → ✅ He makes a lot of money. (make money)

It made me laugh; she made me wait B1

PatternExample
make + person + base verbOnions make me cry. (not make me to cry)
make + person + adjectiveThe news made her angry.
be made to + verb (passive)We were made to wait for an hour.
make yourself + past participleI couldn't make myself understood.

  • That joke made me laugh. (make + base verb)
  • The long wait made everyone tired. (make + adjective)
  • The teacher made us rewrite the essay. (force)
  • He was made to apologise. (passive: to)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ My mum made me to clean my room. → ✅ My mum made me clean my room. (no to in the active)
  • ❌ The story made me sadly. → ✅ The story made me sad. (make + adjective)

B2 · Upper-intermediate

Make sense, make do with, could do with B2

ExpressionMeaningExample
make sensebe logicalThis doesn't make sense.
make up your minddecideHave you made up your mind?
make the most ofuse something wellMake the most of the sunshine!
make do withmanage with something not idealWe'll have to make do with a small flat.
make itarrive in time; succeedWe made it just in time. She made it as a singer.
could do withwant or needI could do with a coffee.
do withoutmanage withoutI can't do without my phone.
have (nothing) to do withbe (not) connected toIt has nothing to do with you.

  • Your story doesn't make sense. (make sense)
  • I could do with a holiday. (could do with)
  • We missed the bus, but we still made it to the concert. (make it)
  • His job has something to do with computers. (to do with)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ It doesn't do sense. → ✅ It doesn't make sense. (make sense)
  • ❌ I can't make without coffee in the morning. → ✅ I can't do without coffee in the morning. (do without)
