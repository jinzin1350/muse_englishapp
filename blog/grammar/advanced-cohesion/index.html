Advanced Cohesion and Discourse Markers

Level: C2 · Free grammar guide

Cohesion is what makes a text flow: the words that link sentences and show how ideas relate. Discourse markers like moreover, consequently and admittedly tell the reader what kind of idea comes next.

At C2, the challenge is choosing the exact marker for the relationship, and punctuating it correctly.

Markers by function

FunctionMarkers
addingmoreover, furthermore, in addition, what is more
contrasthowever, nevertheless, on the other hand, conversely, meanwhile; whereas, while (join two clauses)
resultconsequently, as a result, therefore, thus, hence
concessionadmittedly, granted, that said, even so
examplesfor instance, for example, such as (+ noun), namely (= specifically)
rephrasingin other words, that is to say, i.e.
sequencefirstly, subsequently, finally
summaryin conclusion, to sum up, all in all, in short

Punctuation

Adverbs like however, moreover, therefore, consequently link two sentences (or two clauses joined by a semicolon). They can't join clauses with only a comma:

  • The plan was good. However, it failed.
  • The plan was good; however, it failed.
  • The plan was good, but it failed. (conjunction)
  • not: The plan was good, however it failed.

Whereas and while are conjunctions, so they join clauses in one sentence: My brother loves the city, whereas I prefer the countryside.

Reference words

Cohesion also comes from referring back instead of repeating: the former (the first of two) / the latter (the second of two), this + noun (this problem, this approach), such + noun (such measures), the above. We considered coffee and tea. The former is free; the latter costs $2.

See Ellipsis and Substitution for other ways to avoid repetition.

Examples

  1. The hotel was in a beautiful location. Moreover, the service was excellent. (adding)
  2. Demand fell sharply. Consequently, the factory had to close. (result)
  3. Admittedly, the plan has weaknesses, but overall it's a good one. (concession)
  4. Only one candidate, namely Dr Patel, met all the criteria. (specifying)
  5. Electric cars are cleaner. On the other hand, they're often more expensive. (contrast)
  6. Prices have risen. Meanwhile, wages have stayed the same. (contrast in time)
  7. The meeting has been postponed; that is to say, it won't take place today. (rephrasing)
  8. The samples were tested. Subsequently, the results were published. (sequence)
  9. My sister loves maths, whereas I prefer languages. (whereas joins clauses)
  10. All in all, the evidence supports the new theory. (summary)

Common mistakes

❌ The plan was good, however it failed.

✅ The plan was good. However, it failed. / …good; however, it failed. (However can't join two clauses with a comma.)

❌ I like tea. Whereas my brother likes coffee.

✅ I like tea, whereas my brother likes coffee. (Whereas joins two clauses in one sentence.)

❌ Many cities, such as for example London, have congestion charges.

✅ Many cities, such as London, have congestion charges. (Use such as or for example, not both.)

❌ Demand fell. Moreover, the factory closed.

✅ Demand fell. Consequently, the factory closed. (The closure was a result, not an extra point: use a result marker.)

❌ We visited Rome and Paris. The latter was our first stop. (Rome was first.)

✅ The former was our first stop. (The former = the first one mentioned; the latter = the second.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. The hotel was in a beautiful location. ___, it had excellent service.
Adding another positive point: Moreover.
2. Demand fell sharply. ___, the factory had to close.
Result: Consequently.
3. My brother loves city life, ___ I prefer the countryside.
A conjunction joining two contrasting clauses: whereas.
4. ___, the plan has some weaknesses, but overall it's a good one.
Concession before the main point: Admittedly.
5. We offer two drinks: coffee and tea. ___ is free; the latter costs $2.
The first of two (coffee): The former.
6. Many big cities, ___ London and Paris, have introduced congestion charges.
Examples + noun: such as.
7. The project was late and over budget. ___, it was a failure.
Rephrasing the same idea: In other words.
8. The experiment was repeated three times. ___, the results were published.
Sequence (after that): Subsequently.
9. Electric cars are cleaner. ___, they are often more expensive.
Contrast: On the other hand.
10. Choose the correctly punctuated sentence.
Semicolon + however + comma.
11. Concluding an essay: '___, the evidence strongly supports the new theory.'
Summary: All in all.
12. Only one candidate, ___ Dr Patel, met all the criteria.
Specifying the only one: namely.
13. The rules were unclear. ___, nobody knew what to do.
Result: As a result.
14. Prices have risen. ___, wages have stayed the same.
Contrast at the same time: Meanwhile.
15. The meeting has been postponed; ___, it will not take place today.
Rephrasing: that is to say.
16. Many people believe the theory. ___, the evidence for it is weak.
Contrast: Nevertheless.
