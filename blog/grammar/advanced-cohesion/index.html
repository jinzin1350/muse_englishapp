Advanced Cohesion and Discourse Markers

B2 · C1 · C2

Cohesion means making a text hang together: each sentence connects clearly to the next. We do this with reference words (this, the former), discourse markers in speech (anyway, mind you) and linkers in writing (in other words, hence, that said).

For basic linkers like however and therefore, see And, But, Or, So, Because. For leaving words out and do so, see Ellipsis and Substitution.

B2 · Upper-intermediate

This, such, the former and the latter B2

WordExample
this + nounPrices rose by 20%. This increase surprised everyone.
this (a whole idea)He never says thank you. This really annoys me.
such + nounI'd never seen such a mess. / We need to avoid such problems.
the former / the latterI visited Rome and Paris. The former was hotter; the latter was more expensive.
the above / the followingThe following points are important: …

  • Some people work from home. This saves them time and money. (this = the idea)
  • The company cut 500 jobs. This decision was criticised. (this + summary noun)
  • Tea and coffee are both popular, but the latter has more caffeine. (the latter = coffee)
  • Such behaviour is not acceptable. (such + noun)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I like tea and coffee and juice, but the latter is my favourite. → ✅ I like tea, coffee and juice, but the last is my favourite. (the latter: only two things)
  • ❌ I've never seen a such beautiful place. → ✅ I've never seen such a beautiful place. (such a)

Anyway, by the way, actually, mind you B2

MarkerUseExample
anywayreturn to the main point; finishAnyway, I'd better go now.
by the waychange the topicBy the way, have you seen Sara?
actuallycorrect or surpriseI'm not Spanish, actually. I'm Mexican.
mind you (BrE)add a contrasting pointIt's expensive. Mind you, it's very good.
I meanexplain or correct yourselfShe's nice. I mean, she's always helpful.
as I was sayinggo back after an interruptionAs I was saying, the plan is ready.

  • By the way, your phone is ringing. (new topic)
  • Actually, I've changed my mind. (correction)
  • Anyway, let's get back to work. (back to the main point)
  • The hotel was old. Mind you, it was very cheap. (adding a contrast)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Actually I'm working in a bank. (= now) → ✅ Currently I'm working in a bank. (actually = in fact, not now)
  • ❌ By the way, as I was saying, the plan is ready. → ✅ As I was saying, the plan is ready. (returning to the topic: as I was saying)

Practice: B2

1. We can take the bus or the train. ___ is cheaper, but the train is faster.
The first of two: The former.
2. Unemployment has risen sharply. ___ is a serious problem for the government.
A whole idea: This.
3. Ali and Reza both applied, but only the ___ got the job. Reza was very disappointed. (one word)
The first of two: the former (Ali).
4. ___, did you remember to buy milk? I forgot to ask you earlier.
A new topic: By the way.
5. I thought she was American. ~ No, she's Canadian, ___.
A correction: actually.
6. Sorry about that phone call. ___ I was saying, the meeting is on Friday. (one word)
As I was saying.

C1 · Advanced

In other words, namely, to sum up, that is to say C1

UseLinkersExample
orderfirstly, secondly, to begin with, lastlyTo begin with, the method is cheap.
rephrasingin other words, that is to say, i.e.The test was optional; in other words, you didn't have to do it.
examplesfor instance, for example, e.g., such asSome fruits, e.g. lemons, are very sour.
exact detailsnamelyTwo students, namely Ali and Sara, got full marks.
summing upto sum up, in conclusion, overall, all in allTo sum up, the plan has more benefits than risks.

  • To begin with, let's look at the costs. (order)
  • He's economical with the truth; in other words, he lies. (rephrasing)
  • Three cities, namely Paris, Rome and Madrid, were chosen. (exact details)
  • In conclusion, both options have advantages. (summing up)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Many fruits, i.e. apples and bananas, are cheap. (examples) → ✅ Many fruits, e.g. apples and bananas, are cheap. (examples: e.g.)
  • ❌ To summarise up, the results were positive. → ✅ To sum up, the results were positive. (to sum up (or to summarise))

Hence, thus, consequently, accordingly C1

LinkerExample
consequently / as a consequenceSales fell. Consequently, 200 jobs were lost.
thus (formal)The road was closed, thus causing long delays.
hence + nounThe town is built on a hill, hence the name Hilltown.
hence + clauseThe data is old; hence, the results may not be accurate.
accordinglyThe rules have changed. Please act accordingly. (= in a suitable way)

  • The project ran out of money; consequently, it was cancelled. (result)
  • She's a doctor, hence her interest in health. (hence + noun)
  • The bridge was damaged, thus cutting off the village. (thus + -ing)
  • The guests are VIPs, so please treat them accordingly. (in a suitable way)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The town is by the sea, hence it's called Seaside. → ✅ The town is by the sea, hence its name, Seaside. (hence + noun)
  • ❌ Prices rose, thus made life harder. → ✅ Prices rose, thus making life harder. (thus + -ing)

Practice: C1

7. The plan is too expensive. ___, we can't afford it.
Rephrasing: In other words.
8. Only one student, ___ Mina, passed every exam.
The exact person: namely.
9. To ___ up, the new system is faster, cheaper and safer. (one word)
Summing up: To sum up.
10. The company lost its biggest client. ___, profits fell by 40%.
Result: Consequently.
11. The town is next to a large lake, ___ its name, Lakeside.
Hence + noun.
12. Interest rates went up, ___ making mortgages more expensive. (one word)
Thus + -ing.

C2 · Proficiency

That said, admittedly, let alone, if anything C2

MarkerUseExample
admittedly / grantedaccept a point before arguingAdmittedly, it's expensive, but it lasts for years.
that said / having said thatadd a contrastThe hotel was noisy. That said, the staff were lovely.
be that as it mayaccept a point but continue (formal)You may be right. Be that as it may, we can't change the plan now.
all the same / even sodespite thisIt was raining. All the same, we went out.
indeedconfirm and strengthenThe task was difficult; indeed, it was almost impossible.
if anythingthe opposite, if there's any differenceThe new model isn't cheaper. If anything, it's more expensive.
let aloneand certainly not (after a negative)I can't afford a bike, let alone a car.
not to mentionand also (adding something important)It's cheap, not to mention delicious.

  • Granted, the course is short, but it's very intensive. (concession)
  • The film was long. Having said that, I enjoyed it. (contrast)
  • She doesn't speak French, let alone Russian. (let alone)
  • The traffic wasn't better. If anything, it was worse. (if anything)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I can afford a car, let alone a bike. → ✅ I can't afford a car, let alone a house. (let alone: after a negative, something even less likely)
  • ❌ That saying, I still think we should try. → ✅ That said, I still think we should try. (that said)

Practice: C2

13. I don't have time to read a newspaper, ___ a novel.
After a negative: let alone.
14. ___, the plan has some weaknesses. But overall, it's a good one.
Accepting a point: Admittedly.
15. The new manager isn't stricter than the old one. If ___, she's more relaxed. (one word)
If anything = the opposite is more true.
