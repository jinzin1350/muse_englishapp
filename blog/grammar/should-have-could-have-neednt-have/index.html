Should Have, Could Have, Would Have, Needn't Have

B1 · B2 · C1

We use modal + have + past participle to talk about things in the past that didn't happen or that we regret: I should have left earlier. (I didn't, and now I'm late.)

For deductions about the past (must have, can't have, might have), see Modals of Deduction. For if sentences about the past, see Third Conditional.

B1 · Intermediate

Should have / shouldn't have: regrets and criticism B1

FormMeaningExample
should have + past participleit didn't happen (but it was a good idea)I should have studied harder.
shouldn't have + past participleit happened (but it was a bad idea)You shouldn't have said that.

  • I should have brought a jacket. I'm freezing. (I didn't bring one)
  • You shouldn't have eaten all that cake. (you ate it)
  • They should have checked the weather before they left. (criticism)
  • Should I have told her? (question)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I should have went to the doctor. → ✅ I should have gone to the doctor. (should have + past participle)
  • ❌ You should of called me. → ✅ You should have called me. (have, not of)
  • ❌ I should studied more. → ✅ I should have studied more. (should + have + past participle)

Could have: a chance you didn't take B1

  • She could have been a professional singer. (possible, didn't happen)
  • You could have asked for help! (criticism)
  • The accident could have been much worse. (nearly happened)
  • I couldn't have finished without you. (not possible)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I could have go to the party, but I was too tired. → ✅ I could have gone to the party, but I was too tired. (could have + past participle)
  • ❌ You could helped me. → ✅ You could have helped me. (could + have + past participle)

Practice: B1

1. I failed the exam. I <span class="gap">___</span> harder.
A regret: should have + past participle.
2. You <span class="gap">___</span> that email to everyone. It was private!
It happened, but it was a mistake: shouldn't have sent.
3. We missed the bus. We <span class="gap">___</span> (should / leave) earlier.
A regret: should have left.
4. I <span class="gap">___</span> a lot of money in that job, but I turned it down.
A possibility that didn't happen: could have earned.
5. What does You could have called me! express?
Could have = you didn't, and you should have.
6. The fire was very serious. Someone <span class="gap">___</span> (could / die).
Nearly happened: could have died.

B2 · Upper-intermediate

Would have: what you were willing to do B2

  • I would have bought it, but it was too expensive. (willing, didn't happen)
  • She would have loved this place. (imagined past)
  • We would have stayed, but the kids were tired. (willing, didn't happen)
  • He wouldn't have agreed, even if you'd asked. (not willing)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I would have came, but I was sick. → ✅ I would have come, but I was sick. (would have + past participle)

Needn't have or didn't need to? B2

FormDid it happen?Example
needn't have + past participleyes, but it was unnecessaryI needn't have cooked. They'd already eaten.
didn't need to + base verbusually noI didn't need to cook. We went to a restaurant.

  • You needn't have hurried. The meeting started late. (you hurried)
  • We didn't need to book. There were lots of free tables. (so we didn't book)
  • I needn't have worried. The exam was easy. (I worried)
  • She didn't need to wait long. The bus came quickly. (no need)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I needn't have worry about the test. → ✅ I needn't have worried about the test. (needn't have + past participle)
  • ❌ I didn't need to brought an umbrella. → ✅ I didn't need to bring an umbrella. (didn't need to + base verb)

Practice: B2

7. I <span class="gap">___</span> you to the airport, but my car was at the garage.
Willing, but it didn't happen: would have driven.
8. What does My father would have loved this restaurant suggest?
Would have loved = an imagined past.
9. We <span class="gap">___</span> (would / come) to the party, but we didn't know about it.
Willing, but it didn't happen.
10. I took a taxi to the station, but the train was late. I <span class="gap">___</span> a taxi. (British English)
I took it, but it wasn't necessary: needn't have taken.
11. The museum was free, so we <span class="gap">___</span> tickets. We just walked in.
It wasn't necessary, so we didn't: didn't need to buy.
12. You <span class="gap">___</span> (need / not / bring) a present, but thank you!
You brought one, but it wasn't necessary.

C1 · Advanced

You shouldn't have!, should have for expectations, might have for annoyance C1

  • You shouldn't have! is a polite, warm reaction when someone gives you a present or does something kind. It doesn't mean they did something wrong: Oh, flowers! You shouldn't have!
  • Should have + past participle can describe an expectation about the past: The parcel should have arrived yesterday. (It was expected, but it hasn't arrived.) / They should have landed by now.
  • Might have / could have + past participle can express annoyance: You might have told me you weren't coming!

  • A cake for me? Oh, you shouldn't have! (polite thanks)
  • The train should have left ten minutes ago. Why are we still here? (expected)
  • You might have asked before you took my car! (annoyance)
  • By now they should have finished the building. (expected)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ You shouldn't have bought me. (meaning thank you) → ✅ You shouldn't have! / You shouldn't have bought me anything! (the fixed phrase)

Practice: C1

13. Your friend gives you an expensive present. What's a polite, warm reaction?
You shouldn't have! = thank you (you were too kind).
14. What does The report should have been ready yesterday mean?
Should have = an expectation that wasn't met.
15. You <span class="gap">___</span> (might / tell) me you were vegetarian! I made beef stew.
Annoyance: might have told.
