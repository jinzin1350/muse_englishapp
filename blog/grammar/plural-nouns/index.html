Plural Nouns in English

A1 · A2 · B1 · C1

Most English nouns make the plural with -s: one book, two books. But there are spelling rules (box → boxes, city → cities) and some common irregular plurals (man → men, child → children).

Some nouns are always plural (jeans, scissors), and some never take a plural at all. For those, see Countable and Uncountable Nouns.

A1 · Beginner

Regular plurals: -s, -es, -ies, -ves A1

RuleExamples
most nouns: + -sbook → books, car → cars
-s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x: + -esbus → buses, class → classes, dish → dishes, watch → watches, box → boxes
consonant + -y: y → -iescity → cities, baby → babies
vowel + -y: + -sday → days, boy → boys, key → keys
some words in -o: + -estomato → tomatoes, potato → potatoes, hero → heroes
other words in -o: + -sphoto → photos, piano → pianos, radio → radios
many words in -f / -fe: → -vesknife → knives, leaf → leaves, wife → wives, half → halves

  • I have two brothers and three sisters. (+ s)
  • She washed the dishes. (+ es)
  • There are many big cities in Canada. (y → ies)
  • Put the knives in the drawer. (f → ves)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I bought three tomatos. → ✅ I bought three tomatoes. (tomato → tomatoes)
  • ❌ My two babys are asleep. → ✅ My two babies are asleep. (consonant + y → ies)
  • ❌ The leafs are falling. → ✅ The leaves are falling. (leaf → leaves)

Irregular plurals: men, children, feet, people A1

SingularPlural
man / womanmen / women
childchildren
personpeople
foot / tooth / goosefeet / teeth / geese
mousemice
sheep / fish / deersheep / fish / deer (no change)

  • Three men and two women got on the bus. (men, women)
  • How many children do you have? (children)
  • Brush your teeth before bed. (teeth)
  • We saw a lot of sheep on the farm. (no change)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ There are many childs in the park. → ✅ There are many children in the park. (child → children)
  • ❌ My foots hurt. → ✅ My feet hurt. (foot → feet)
  • ❌ Ten persons came to the party. → ✅ Ten people came to the party. (person → people)

A2 · Elementary

Always plural: jeans, scissors, clothes, police A2

Always pluralExamples
clothes for two legstrousers, jeans, shorts, pyjamas
things with two partsglasses, scissors, headphones
othersclothes, stairs, goods, thanks, congratulations
plural verb, no -spolice, people, cattle

  • Your new trousers look great. (plural verb)
  • I need a pair of scissors. (a pair of)
  • Where are my glasses? (plural verb)
  • The police have arrested two men. (police + plural verb)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ My jeans is dirty. → ✅ My jeans are dirty. (jeans: plural verb)
  • ❌ I bought a new trouser. → ✅ I bought a new pair of trousers. (a pair of)
  • ❌ The police is coming. → ✅ The police are coming. (police: plural verb)

B1 · Intermediate

Group nouns: the team is or the team are? B1

NounOne unit (singular)The people in it (plural, BrE)
teamThe team is top of the league.The team are celebrating.
familyMy family is large.My family are all doctors.
staffThe staff is small.The staff are very helpful.

  • The government has announced new taxes. (one unit)
  • My family are coming for dinner. (BrE) (the people)
  • Our class is the biggest in the school. (one unit)
  • The staff are on strike. (BrE) (the people)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The police is investigating. → ✅ The police are investigating. (police: always plural)
  • ❌ The people is very friendly. → ✅ The people are very friendly. (people: plural)
  • ❌ The team are proud of its coach. → ✅ The team are proud of their coach. (plural verb → their)

Practice: B1

Police always takes a plural verb.
11. Which sentence is correct in both British and American English?
Singular verb + its: correct everywhere.
12. The company has changed <span class="gap">___</span> logo. (one word)
Singular verb → its.

C1 · Advanced

Criteria, crises, passers-by and the news is C1

TypeExamples
-is → -esanalysis → analyses, crisis → crises, thesis → theses, hypothesis → hypotheses
-on → -acriterion → criteria, phenomenon → phenomena
-um → -amedium → media, bacterium → bacteria
-us → -istimulus → stimuli, nucleus → nuclei (but bonuses, viruses)
same in bothseries, species, means, aircraft
compound nounspassers-by, mothers-in-law, runners-up

  • Several criteria were used to choose the winner. (criterion → criteria)
  • There have been two economic crises this decade. (crisis → crises)
  • Physics is my favourite subject. (-s, singular verb)
  • Both of his brothers-in-law are teachers. (compound plural)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The news are bad. → ✅ The news is bad. (news: singular)
  • ❌ This phenomena is rare. → ✅ This phenomenon is rare. (singular: phenomenon)
  • ❌ Two passer-bys helped me. → ✅ Two passers-by helped me. (compound: passers-by)

Practice: C1
