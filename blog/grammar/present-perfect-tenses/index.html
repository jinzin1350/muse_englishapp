حال کامل: ساده و استمراریPresent Perfect: Simple and Continuous

The present perfect connects the past with now. English has two forms: the present perfect simple (I've worked) and the present perfect continuous (I've been working).

This page shows the big picture and helps you choose. Each topic below has its own page with rules, examples and exercises at every level.

The two forms at a glance

Present perfect simplePresent perfect continuous
FormI have worked / she has workedI have been working / she has been working
Focusthe result, how many, experiencesthe activity, how long
ExampleI've written three emails.I've been writing emails all morning.
With for / sincestates: I've known her for years.activities: I've been waiting since 9.

Which one do I need?

  • Are you talking about a finished time (yesterday, last year, ago)? → not the present perfect: use the past simple. I saw her yesterday.
  • Is it a life experience or a result now, with no finished time? → present perfect simple: I've been to Rome. I've lost my keys.
  • Do you say how many or how much? → present perfect simple: I've read five books this year.
  • Is it an activity that started in the past and is still going on, or has just stopped? → present perfect continuous: It's been raining all day.
  • Do you say how long with for or since? → continuous for activities, simple for states: I've been learning English for two years. I've known him since 2010.

موضوع‌های این بخش

Present Perfect (I have done)

زمان حال کامل (ماضی نقلی)

A2 B1 C1

The past connected to now: I've lost my keys. Have you ever been to Rome?

For, Since and Ago

for، since و ago

A2 B1 B2 C1

For + a period (for two years), since + a starting point (since 2022), ago + past simple (two years ago).

Present Perfect vs Past Simple

حال کامل یا گذشتهٔ ساده

A2 B1 B2 C1

I've lost my keys (connected to now) or I lost my keys yesterday (finished time).

Present Perfect Continuous (I have been doing)

حال کامل استمراری

B1 B2 C1

An activity that started in the past and continues now, or has just stopped: I've been waiting for an hour.
