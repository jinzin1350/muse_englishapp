Prepositions of Time: In, On, At

Level: A1 · Free grammar guide

Use at for clock times (at 7 o'clock), on for days and dates (on Monday, on 5 May) and in for longer periods like months, years and seasons (in July, in 2026, in winter).

An easy way to remember: at = a point in time, on = a day, in = a longer time.

At, on or in? The rules

PrepositionUse it withExamples
atclock timesat 6:30, at 9 o'clock, at noon, at midnight
atsome fixed phrasesat night, at lunchtime, at the moment, at Christmas
ondays of the weekon Monday, on Fridays, on Saturday morning
ondates and special dayson 4 July, on my birthday, on New Year's Day
inparts of the dayin the morning, in the afternoon, in the evening
inmonths, seasons and yearsin March, in summer, in the summer, in 2025
inlonger periodsin the 1990s, in the 21st century, in the past, in the future

A day + part of the day → on: in the morning, but on Monday morning. In the evening, but on Friday evening.

UK and US: at the weekend is British English and on the weekend is American English. Both are correct. In American English, people also often say See you Monday without on.

No preposition: next, last, this, every

Don't use in, on or at before next, last, this, every, and before today, tomorrow, yesterday:

  1. I'll see you next Friday. (not on next Friday)
  2. We met last year. (not in last year)
  3. She goes swimming every Monday. (not on every Monday)
  4. What are you doing this weekend? (not at this weekend)
  5. I called him yesterday. (no preposition)

Quick chart: from long to short

  • in – the longest times: in 2030, in the summer, in April, in the morning
  • on – one day: on Tuesday, on 10 June, on my birthday
  • at – a point or a short time: at 8:15, at noon, at night

Examples

  1. The film starts at 8 pm. (clock time)
  2. My birthday is on 12 October. (date)
  3. We go skiing in winter. (season)
  4. I was born in 1998. (year)
  5. I work on Mondays and Wednesdays. (days)
  6. She always reads in the evening. (part of the day)
  7. I can't sleep at night. (fixed phrase: at night)
  8. The shop opens at 9 o'clock on Saturday mornings. (clock time + day and part of the day)
  9. We're having a party on New Year's Eve. (special day)
  10. This castle was built in the 12th century. (century)

Common mistakes

❌ I get up on 7 o'clock.

✅ I get up at 7 o'clock. (Clock times → at.)

❌ See you in Monday.

✅ See you on Monday. (Days → on.)

❌ My birthday is in 5 June.

✅ My birthday is on 5 June. (Dates → on. (But in June: a month.))

❌ I was born on 2001.

✅ I was born in 2001. (Years → in.)

❌ We have English on next Tuesday.

✅ We have English next Tuesday. (No preposition before next, last, this or every.)

❌ In Monday morning I go to the gym.

✅ On Monday morning I go to the gym. (A day + part of the day → on.)

❌ I study in the night.

✅ I study at night. (For a general habit, say at night. (In the night means during one night: I woke up in the night.))
