Can, Could and Be Able To (Ability and Possibility)

A1 · A2 · B1 · C1

We use can for ability and possibility now: I can speak three languages. Could is the past: I could swim when I was five. For other tenses, we use be able to: I'll be able to help you tomorrow.

Can and could are also used for requests and permission (Can I…? Could you…?). Those uses have their own page: Requests, Offers and Permission.

A1 · Beginner

Can and can't: ability A1

FormExample
PositiveI / you / he / she / we / they can swim.
NegativeShe can't (cannot) drive.
QuestionCan you play the guitar?
Short answersYes, I can. / No, I can't.

  • I can speak Farsi and English. (ability)
  • My grandma can't use a computer. (negative)
  • Can you ride a bike? ~ Yes, I can. (question + short answer)
  • What languages can you speak? (wh-question)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ He cans swim. → ✅ He can swim. (can: no -s)
  • ❌ I can to dance. → ✅ I can dance. (can + base verb)
  • ❌ Do you can cook? → ✅ Can you cook? (question: Can you…?)

Practice: A1

1. My little brother <span class="gap">___</span> read yet. He's only three.
Ability, negative: can't.
2. <span class="gap">___</span> you speak Spanish? ~ Yes, a little.
Ability question: Can you…?
3. My sister <span class="gap">___</span> (can / play) the piano very well.
Can + base verb.

A2 · Elementary

Could and couldn't: ability in the past A2

  • My grandmother could speak four languages. (general ability)
  • I couldn't open the door. It was locked. (one situation (negative))
  • Could you read before you started school? (question)
  • We could hear the waves all night. (senses)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ When I was ten, I can swim. → ✅ When I was ten, I could swim. (past: could)
  • ❌ I couldn't to sleep. → ✅ I couldn't sleep. (could + base verb)

Practice: A2

4. When I was a child, I <span class="gap">___</span> climb trees really well.
Past general ability: could.
5. I <span class="gap">___</span> find a parking space, so I was late.
Past, negative: couldn't.
6. It was so foggy that we <span class="gap">___</span> (not / could / see) the road.
Couldn't + base verb.

B1 · Intermediate

Be able to: other tenses and after other verbs B1

StructureExample
futureI'll be able to see you next week.
present perfectShe hasn't been able to sleep well recently.
after modalsYou might be able to get a ticket.
infinitiveI'd love to be able to play the guitar.
-ing formI enjoy being able to work from home.

  • I'll be able to drive when I pass my test. (future)
  • I haven't been able to contact him. (present perfect)
  • You should be able to finish by five. (after a modal)
  • I'd like to be able to speak French. (infinitive)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I will can help you tomorrow. → ✅ I will be able to help you tomorrow. (future: will be able to)
  • ❌ I haven't could finish it. → ✅ I haven't been able to finish it. (present perfect: been able to)
  • ❌ She might can come. → ✅ She might be able to come. (after modals: be able to)

Could or was able to? Managed to B1

  • She could play chess when she was six. (general ability)
  • The exam was hard, but I managed to pass. (one success, with difficulty)
  • After two hours, firefighters were able to put out the fire. (one success)
  • I couldn't get a ticket. They were sold out. (negative: couldn't is fine)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The test was difficult, but I could pass it. → ✅ The test was difficult, but I managed to pass it. (one success: managed to / was able to)
  • ❌ We managed get there on time. → ✅ We managed to get there on time. (managed + to)

Could and can for possibility and suggestions B1

  • The flight could be delayed because of the snow. (possible)
  • We could have a picnic if it's sunny. (suggestion)
  • You can buy tickets online. (generally possible)
  • Be careful. That dog could bite. (possible danger)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ It can rain tomorrow, so take an umbrella. → ✅ It could rain tomorrow, so take an umbrella. (a particular future possibility: could / might)
  • ❌ We could to go to the beach. → ✅ We could go to the beach. (could + base verb)

Practice: B1

Infinitive: to be able to.

One successful action: were able to.

C1 · Advanced

Can for typical behaviour, could for criticism C1

  • Teenagers can be difficult. (typical, sometimes true)
  • The motorway can get very busy on Fridays. (sometimes)
  • You could at least knock before you come in! (criticism)
  • The project could well take longer than planned. (strong possibility)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ My boss can to be very strict. → ✅ My boss can be very strict. (can + base verb)

Expressions with can and could C1

ExpressionMeaningExample
couldn't + comparativevery / completelyI couldn't be happier. (I'm extremely happy.)
couldn't agree moreI agree completelyI couldn't agree more.
can't help + -ingcan't stop myselfI can't help laughing at him.
can't stand / bear + -ing / nounreally dislikeI can't stand waiting.
could do with + nounwould like / needI could do with a holiday.
can't wait (to)very excitedI can't wait to see you!

  • The timing couldn't have been worse. (the worst possible timing)
  • I can't help thinking about the accident. (can't stop myself)
  • My dad can't stand loud music. (really dislikes)
  • This room could do with a coat of paint. (needs)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I can't help to laugh. → ✅ I can't help laughing. (can't help + -ing)
  • ❌ I could do with to have a rest. → ✅ I could do with a rest. (could do with + noun)

Practice: C1

Could at least = criticism.

20. I <span class="gap">___</span> laughing when he fell into the pool.
Couldn't help + -ing = couldn't stop myself.
21. I'm so tired. I could do <span class="gap">___</span> a cup of tea. (one word)
Could do with = would like / need.
