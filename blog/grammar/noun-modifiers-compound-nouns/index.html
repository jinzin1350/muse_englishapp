اسم‌های مرکب و اسم در نقش صفتCompound Nouns and Noun Modifiers

In English, a noun can work like an adjective in front of another noun: a bus stop, a coffee cup, a city map. The second noun is the main one: a bus stop is a kind of stop.

These are called compound nouns or noun modifiers. They're very common in everyday English, and even more common in news and formal writing.

A2 · مقدماتی

Noun + noun: a bus stop, a shoe shop A2

Put a noun before another noun to describe it: a bus stop, a tennis ball, a shoe shop. The first noun is usually singular, and the second noun is the main one.

CompoundMeaning
a bus stopa stop for buses
a shoe shopa shop that sells shoes
a coffee cupa cup for coffee
a city centrethe centre of a city
a phone numbera number for a phone
a kitchen tablea table in the kitchen

The first noun works like an adjective, so it's usually singular: a shoe shop (not a shoes shop).

The second noun is the main noun and takes the plural: two bus stops, three shoe shops.

Order matters: a flower garden is a garden; a garden flower is a flower.

  • Let's meet at the bus station. (noun + noun)
  • I need a new tennis racket. (noun + noun)
  • There are three car parks near here. (plural on the second noun)
  • What's your email address? (noun + noun)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I work in a toys shop. → ✅ I work in a toy shop. (first noun: singular)
  • ❌ Where's the stop bus? → ✅ Where's the bus stop? (main noun second)
  • ❌ We have two bus stop near our house. → ✅ We have two bus stops near our house. (plural on the main noun)

تمرین سطح A2

1. I'm waiting at the ___.
Main noun second: bus stop.
2. She works in a ___.
First noun singular: a shoe shop.
3. There are two ___ (car park) in the town centre. (two words)
Plural on the second noun.

B1 · متوسط

One word, two words or a hyphen? B1

Compound nouns can be written as one word (toothbrush, bedroom), two words (bus stop, credit card) or with a hyphen (mother-in-law, check-in). There's no fixed rule, so check a dictionary.

FormExamples
one wordbedroom, toothbrush, haircut, sunglasses, website, weekend
two wordsbus stop, credit card, swimming pool, post office, phone call
hyphenmother-in-law, check-in, passer-by, T-shirt

Very common compounds often become one word over time: email, website, weekend.

The stress is usually on the first part: BUS stop, BEDroom, SWIMming pool.

-ing + noun compounds often describe a purpose: a swimming pool (a pool for swimming), a washing machine, a parking space.

  • I need a new toothbrush. (one word)
  • Can I pay by credit card? (two words)
  • My mother-in-law lives with us. (hyphen)
  • Is there a parking space near your house? (-ing + noun)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I need a new machine washing. → ✅ I need a new washing machine. (-ing + noun: purpose first)
  • ❌ Our hotel has a pool swimming. → ✅ Our hotel has a swimming pool. (-ing + noun: purpose first)

تمرین سطح B1

4. Our hotel has a big ___.
-ing + noun: swimming pool.
5. Which compound is written as one word?
Bedroom is one word.
6. Can I pay by ___ (card / credit)? (two words)
Credit card: two words.

B2 · بالاتر از متوسط

A ten-minute walk, a five-star hotel B2

Use number + noun with a hyphen before a noun to describe time, age, size or value: a ten-minute walk, a five-star hotel, a three-year-old girl. The noun is singular: a two-hour film (not two-hours).

PatternExample
timea ten-minute break, a two-hour meeting, a three-week course
agea six-year-old boy (but: The boy is six years old.)
money, sizea ten-dollar bill, a 500-page book, a two-bedroom flat
ratinga five-star hotel, a three-course meal

Before a noun, the measurement word is singular and joined with a hyphen: a five-minute walk. After the verb, it's plural with no hyphens: The walk is five minutes long.

Compare: a ten-minute walk = ten minutes' walk (possessive). Both are correct.

  • It's a ten-minute walk to the station. (time)
  • We stayed in a five-star hotel. (rating)
  • They have a two-year-old daughter. (age)
  • She wrote a 300-page novel. (size)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ It's a two-hours flight. → ✅ It's a two-hour flight. (before a noun: singular)
  • ❌ My son is ten-years-old. → ✅ My son is ten years old. (after be: no hyphens)
  • ❌ a three-weeks holiday → ✅ a three-week holiday (before a noun: singular)

A coffee cup or a cup of coffee? B2

Noun + noun describes the type or purpose (a coffee cup = a cup for coffee). Of describes the contents (a cup of coffee). 's is for things that belong to a person or are made for a group of people (Ali's cup, a children's book).

FormMeaningExample
noun + nountype / purposea wine glass, a shoebox, a teacup
noun + of + nouncontents / amounta glass of wine, a box of shoes, a cup of tea
noun + 's + nounbelongs to a person; made for peopleSara's glass, a women's magazine, a children's book
noun + noun (parts)parts of thingsthe car door, the table leg

Compare: a matchbox (a box for matches, maybe empty) / a box of matches (a box with matches in it).

For things made for a group of people, use 's: a children's hospital, a men's shirt, a girls' school.

  • Can you pass me a wine glass? (purpose)
  • She drank a glass of wine. (contents)
  • I bought a children's book for my nephew. (made for children)
  • The car door won't open. (part)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I'd like a coffee cup with milk, please. (to drink) → ✅ I'd like a cup of coffee with milk, please. (contents: of)
  • ❌ He reads a mens magazine. → ✅ He reads a men's magazine. (made for men: 's)

تمرین سطح B2

7. It's a ___ drive from here to the airport.
Before a noun: singular with a hyphen.
8. Which is correct?
A five-year-old son.
9. We booked a ___ (three / course) meal for the party.
Before a noun: three-course.
10. I'm thirsty. Can I have ___?
Contents: a glass of water.
11. We keep our old photos in a ___.
Purpose / type: a shoebox (it has photos in it, not shoes).
12. She works at a ___ (children) hospital. (one word)
Made for children: children's.

C1 · پیشرفته

Compound adjectives and long noun phrases C1

Compound adjectives with hyphens are common before nouns: a well-known writer, a long-term plan, a part-time job, a high-quality product. News and formal English also use long noun + noun phrases: a government housing policy.

PatternExamples
adverb / adjective + participlea well-known actor, a good-looking man, a badly-paid job
adjective + nouna long-term plan, a part-time job, a last-minute decision, a high-quality product
noun + participlea time-consuming task, a record-breaking year, a man-made lake
long noun stringsa government education reform plan = a plan by the government to reform education

Use a hyphen when the compound comes before the noun: a well-known writer. After the verb, there's often no hyphen: The writer is well known.

Adverbs ending in -ly usually don't take a hyphen: a highly paid job (you'll also see badly-paid in British writing).

Long noun strings are compact but can be hard to read. Read them from the end: the last noun is the main one. In clear writing, you can unpack them: a plan for reforming education.

  • She's a well-known journalist. (compound adjective)
  • I'm looking for a part-time job. (adjective + noun)
  • Writing reports is very time-consuming. (noun + participle)
  • The city council parking rules changed this year. (noun string)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ It's a long term plan. → ✅ It's a long-term plan. (before a noun: hyphen)
  • ❌ a high quality's product → ✅ a high-quality product (compound adjective: hyphen, no 's)

تمرین سطح C1

13. She has a ___ job. She works on Mondays and Fridays.
Compound adjective: part-time.
14. Which sentence is correct?
Well-known (adverb + past participle).
15. Learning a language is a ___ (long / term) project.
Before a noun: long-term.
