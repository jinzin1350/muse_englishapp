جمله‌واره‌های وصفی (Participle Clauses)Participle Clauses

A participle clause is a short clause with an -ing form (walking), a past participle (built) or having + past participle (having finished), and no subject or conjunction: Feeling tired, she went to bed. (= Because she felt tired…)

Participle clauses are common in writing and make sentences shorter and smoother. The most important rule: the clause must have the same subject as the main clause.

B2 · C1 · C2

B2 · بالاتر از متوسط

-ing clauses: at the same time, or because B2

An -ing clause can show an action at the same time (Walking home, I met Ali) or a reason (Feeling ill, she stayed in bed). The subject is the same as in the main clause.

Participle clauseMeaning
Walking home, I met an old friend.While I was walking home…
Feeling tired, she went to bed early.Because she felt tired…
She sat by the window, reading a book.…and she was reading a book.
Not knowing what to do, I called my dad.Because I didn't know…

For the negative, put not before the -ing: Not having a car, we took the bus.

The clause can go before or after the main clause. Use a comma to separate them.

  • Hearing a noise, he went downstairs. (at the same time / reason)
  • Being a doctor, she knew what to do. (reason)
  • He left the room, slamming the door. (at the same time)
  • Not wanting to wake the baby, we spoke quietly. (negative reason)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ Not knew what to say, I stayed quiet. → ✅ Not knowing what to say, I stayed quiet. (-ing form)
  • ❌ Knowing not the answer, she guessed. → ✅ Not knowing the answer, she guessed. (not before -ing)

Past participle clauses: built in 1900 B2

A past participle clause has a passive meaning: Built in 1900, the house is now a museum. (= The house was built in 1900.) Written in simple English, the book is easy to read.

Participle clauseMeaning
Built in 1900, the bridge is still in use.The bridge was built in 1900…
Shocked by the news, she couldn't speak.Because she was shocked…
Made in Italy, these shoes are expensive.These shoes are made in Italy…
Asked about the plan, he said nothing.When he was asked…

Compare: Seeing the dog, the boy ran. (the boy saw: active) / Seen from the air, the city looks small. (the city is seen: passive)

  • Painted by Leonardo da Vinci, the Mona Lisa is very famous. (passive meaning)
  • Surprised by the question, she didn't answer. (reason)
  • Located in the centre, the hotel is perfect for tourists. (description)
  • Given the chance, I'd travel the world. (condition)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ Building in 1850, the church is very old. → ✅ Built in 1850, the church is very old. (passive meaning: past participle)
  • ❌ Writing by a famous author, the book sold well. → ✅ Written by a famous author, the book sold well. (passive meaning: past participle)

The man sitting there, the car parked outside B2

Participle clauses can replace relative clauses: the man sitting there (= who is sitting), the car parked outside (= which is parked), people living in cities (= who live).

Relative clauseReduced (participle) clause
The woman who is talking to Ali is my boss.The woman talking to Ali is my boss.
Students who want to join should register.Students wanting to join should register.
The car which was stolen last week was found.The car stolen last week was found.
Goods which are made in China are cheap.Goods made in China are cheap.

Use -ing for an active meaning and the past participle for a passive meaning.

Don't use an -ing clause for a single finished action in the past: The man who broke the window ran away (not The man breaking the window ran away).

  • Who's the girl wearing the red dress? (active)
  • The houses built last year are already sold. (passive)
  • Anyone needing help should call this number. (active)
  • The money found in the street was given to the police. (passive)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ The people waited for the bus were cold. → ✅ The people waiting for the bus were cold. (active: -ing)
  • ❌ The book writing by her grandfather is in the library. → ✅ The book written by her grandfather is in the library. (passive: past participle)

تمرین سطح B2

1. ___ late for work, she ran to the bus stop.
Reason: Being late = because she was late.
2. ___ what to say, he just smiled.
Negative: Not knowing.
3. ___ (look) out of the window, I saw a strange light in the sky.
At the same time: Looking.
4. ___ in 1889, the Eiffel Tower is the symbol of Paris.
Passive meaning: Completed.
5. ___ from the top of the mountain, the village looks tiny.
Passive meaning: Seen.
6. ___ (make) from recycled plastic, these bags are good for the environment.
Passive meaning: Made.
7. The man ___ next to the window is my uncle.
Active: sitting (= who is sitting).
8. Most of the cars ___ in this factory are exported.
Passive: made (= which are made).
9. The children ___ (play) in the park are from the local school.
Active: playing.

C1 · پیشرفته

Having finished, having been told C1

Having + past participle shows that one action happened before another: Having finished my work, I went home. Passive: Having been told the news, she called her mother.

PatternExampleMeaning
having + past participleHaving finished lunch, we went for a walk.After we had finished…
not having + past participleNot having seen the film, I can't comment.Because I haven't seen…
having been + past participleHaving been warned about the storm, they stayed home.Because they had been warned…

Use having + past participle when the first action is finished before the second, or to show a reason based on an earlier action.

With conjunctions: After finishing / After having finished work, I went to the gym.

  • Having lived in Japan, she speaks Japanese well. (earlier experience)
  • Having been invited, we had to go. (passive)
  • Not having studied, he failed the test. (negative)
  • Having saved enough money, they bought a house. (earlier action)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ Having finish the report, she sent it. → ✅ Having finished the report, she sent it. (having + past participle)
  • ❌ Having been invite to the wedding, we bought a gift. → ✅ Having been invited to the wedding, we bought a gift. (having been + past participle)

Dangling participles, and after / while / when + -ing C1

The participle clause must have the same subject as the main clause. Walking down the street, the rain started is wrong (the rain wasn't walking). You can keep a conjunction: After leaving, while waiting, when using, once finished.

Wrong (dangling)Correct
Walking home, the rain started.Walking home, I got caught in the rain. / While I was walking home, the rain started.
Having finished dinner, the dishes were washed.Having finished dinner, we washed the dishes.
Driving to work, an accident happened.While I was driving to work, an accident happened.

Conjunction + participle is common and clear: After finishing the course, you'll get a certificate. When using this app, keep your phone charged. Once opened, keep in the fridge.

  • While waiting for the bus, I read a book. (conjunction + -ing)
  • Before signing the contract, read it carefully. (before + -ing)
  • Once opened, the bottle should be kept cold. (once + past participle)
  • If stored correctly, the medicine lasts for years. (if + past participle)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ Arriving at the station, the train had left. → ✅ Arriving at the station, we found that the train had left. (same subject: we arrived)
  • ❌ Having eaten lunch, the bill was paid. → ✅ Having eaten lunch, we paid the bill. (same subject: we ate)

تمرین سطح C1

10. ___ the report, she sent it to her manager.
Earlier action: Having finished.
11. ___ twice, he decided to be more careful.
Passive: Having been warned.
12. Having ___ (spend) all his money, he had to walk home.
Having + past participle.
13. Looking out of the window, ___.
Same subject: I was looking.
14. ___ for the interview, I practised my answers in front of a mirror.
Conjunction + -ing.
15. Once ___ (open), the jar must be kept in the fridge.
Once + past participle.

C2 · تسلط کامل

With prices rising, the work done, weather permitting C2

Absolute clauses have their own subject: The work done, we went home. With + noun + participle: With prices rising, people spend less. Fixed expressions: weather permitting, all things considered, generally speaking.

PatternExample
noun + participle (own subject)The meeting over, everyone left. / The work done, we relaxed.
with + noun + -ingWith prices rising, many families are struggling.
with + noun + past participleWith the exams finished, students can relax.
fixed expressionsweather permitting, all things considered, generally speaking, strictly speaking, judging by, considering…

Absolute clauses are formal or literary. With + noun + participle is common in both speech and writing.

Fixed expressions like judging by / from, considering, generally speaking are accepted even when the subjects are different: Judging by his face, he's angry.

  • The sun having set, we lit a fire. (absolute (own subject))
  • With the kids asleep, the house was quiet. (with + noun + adjective)
  • We'll have the party outside, weather permitting. (fixed expression)
  • All things considered, it was a successful year. (fixed expression)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ With prices rise, people are spending less. → ✅ With prices rising, people are spending less. (with + noun + -ing)
  • ❌ Weather permits, we'll eat outside. → ✅ Weather permitting, we'll eat outside. (fixed: weather permitting)

تمرین سطح C2

16. ___ so many people ill, the meeting was postponed.
With + noun + adjective / participle.
17. We'll go hiking on Saturday, weather ___.
Fixed: weather permitting.
18. All things ___ (consider), I think we made the right choice.
Fixed: All things considered.
