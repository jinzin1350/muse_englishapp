Participle Clauses (-ing, -ed, having done)

Level: C1 · Free grammar guide

A participle clause is a short way to give information without a full clause with a subject and conjunction: As she opened the door, she saw… → Opening the door, she saw…

They are common in writing and make sentences more concise.

Three types

ParticipleMeaningExample
-ing (present)active; at the same time, or a reasonFeeling tired, I went to bed early. (= Because I felt tired)
-ed (past)passive meaningBuilt in 1889, the tower is now a symbol of Paris. (= It was built)
having + -ed (perfect)active; one action finished before anotherHaving finished work, she went home.
having been + -edpassive; finished beforeHaving been warned about the traffic, we left early.

Negative: put not first: Not knowing what to say, I stayed silent.

Reduced relative clauses

A participle can replace a relative clause: The woman who is sitting next to me → The woman sitting next to me. The people who were invited → The people invited.

Use being + -ed for a passive action in progress: The bridge being built now will be the longest in the country.

After conjunctions

We can keep the conjunction for clarity: Before leaving, turn off the lights. While living in Japan, I learned a lot. After graduating, he moved abroad. When asked, she refused to comment.

The subject must be the same

The participle clause and the main clause normally share the same subject. Otherwise the sentence is wrong or funny (a dangling participle):

  1. Walking home, the rain started. (wrong: the rain wasn't walking)
  2. Walking home, I got caught in the rain. (correct: I was walking)

Examples

  1. Realising it was late, we took a taxi. (reason, active)
  2. Woken by the noise, the baby started crying. (passive)
  3. Having left his keys at home, he couldn't get in. (earlier action)
  4. Not knowing the area, we got lost. (negative)
  5. Anyone wishing to join should contact the secretary. (reduced relative, active)
  6. Most of the people invited to the party were old friends. (reduced relative, passive)
  7. The letter, written in pencil, was hard to read. (passive)
  8. Before leaving the room, please turn off the lights. (conjunction + -ing)
  9. Abandoned by his friends, he felt very lonely. (passive, reason)
  10. Having been told the news, she called her parents. (passive, earlier)

Common mistakes

❌ Walking along the beach, the sun was setting.

✅ Walking along the beach, we watched the sun set. (The participle and the main clause need the same subject.)

❌ Building in 1889, the tower is famous.

✅ Built in 1889, the tower is famous. (Passive meaning: past participle.)

❌ Knowing not the answer, I said nothing.

✅ Not knowing the answer, I said nothing. (Not goes before the participle.)

❌ Having finish the report, she left.

✅ Having finished the report, she left. (Having + past participle.)

❌ The man sits next to me was reading.

✅ The man sitting next to me was reading. (Reduced relative clause: -ing.)

❌ Before to leave, lock the door.

✅ Before leaving, lock the door. (Conjunction + -ing.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. ___ in 1889, the Eiffel Tower is now a symbol of France.
Passive meaning (it was built): Built.
2. The woman ___ next to me on the train was reading a fascinating book.
Reduced relative clause, active: sitting (= who was sitting).
3. ___ the door, she realised someone had been in the house.
Active, at the same time: Opening (= as she opened).
4. Most of the people ___ to the party were old friends.
Passive reduced relative: invited (= who were invited).
5. ___ his keys at home, he couldn't get into the flat.
Active, earlier action: Having left.
6. Not ___ what to say, I stayed silent.
Negative participle: Not knowing.
7. ___ by the noise, the baby started crying.
Passive (the noise woke the baby): Woken.
8. Anyone ___ to join the club should contact the secretary.
Reduced relative, active: wishing (= who wishes).
9. Before ___ the room, please turn off the lights.
Conjunction + -ing: before leaving.
10. The letter, ___ in pencil, was hard to read.
Passive (it was written): written.
11. Choose the sentence with a correct participle clause.
Same subject (I was walking, I got caught): Walking home, I got caught in the rain.
12. ___ all the exercises, she checked her answers.
Active, finished before: Having completed.
13. While ___ in Japan, I learned a lot about the culture.
Conjunction + -ing: while living.
14. The bridge ___ now is going to be the longest in the country.
Passive action in progress now: being built.
15. ___ that it was late, we decided to take a taxi.
Active, reason: Realising (= because we realised).
16. ___ by his friends, he felt very lonely.
Passive (his friends abandoned him): Abandoned.
