should، ought to و had better (توصیه)Should, Ought To and Had Better

We use should to give advice and to say what we think is right: You should drink more water. Ought to means almost the same, and had better is stronger, often a warning.

Should is weaker than must: it's advice, not a rule. For obligation, see Must and Have To. For past regrets (I should have…), see Should Have, Could Have, Needn't Have.

A2 · مقدماتی

Should and shouldn't for advice A2

should / shouldn't + base verb for advice and opinions: You should go to bed earlier. You shouldn't eat so much sugar.

FormExample
PositiveYou should see a doctor.
NegativeYou shouldn't (should not) worry.
QuestionShould I call her?
OpinionI think you should apologise. I don't think you should go.

Should is the same for every person and is followed by the base verb: She should rest. Not She shoulds rest or She should to rest.

To give a softer opinion, we often use I think … should or I don't think … should: I don't think you should buy it. (more natural than I think you shouldn't buy it)

  • You should try the fish. It's delicious. (advice)
  • You shouldn't drive when you're tired. (negative advice)
  • What should I wear to the wedding? (asking for advice)
  • I think we should leave now. (opinion)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ You should to see a doctor. → ✅ You should see a doctor. (should + base verb)
  • ❌ She shoulds study more. → ✅ She should study more. (no -s)
  • ❌ Do I should call him? → ✅ Should I call him? (question: Should I…?)

تمرین سطح A2

1. You look tired. You ___ go to bed.
Advice: should + base verb.
2. ___ I bring a present to the party?
Asking for advice: Should I…?
3. You ___ (should / not / eat) so much chocolate. It's bad for your teeth.
Negative advice: shouldn't + base verb.

B1 · متوسط

Ought to B1

ought to + base verb = should. It's a little more formal and is less common in questions and negatives: You ought to call your mother.

Ought to has the same meaning as should for advice and what's right: You ought to tell the truth. = You should tell the truth.

Unlike should, it has to: ought to go (not ought go). It is the same for every person: She ought to go.

The negative ought not to / oughtn't to exists but sounds formal. Questions with ought (Ought I to…?) are rare. Most people use should for both: You shouldn't go. Should I go?

Ought to often suggests a duty or what is morally right: We ought to help them. In American English, should is much more common.

  • You ought to apologise to her. (= should)
  • We ought to be more careful with money. (what's right)
  • You ought not to park there. (formal negative)
  • Ought I to tell him? → Should I tell him? (questions: should is more natural)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ You ought apologise. → ✅ You ought to apologise. (ought + to)
  • ❌ She oughts to know. → ✅ She ought to know. (no -s)

Had better: strong advice and warnings B1

had better ('d better) + base verb = strong advice for a particular situation, often with a warning: You'd better hurry or you'll miss the train.

Had better is stronger than should. It suggests that something bad will happen if you don't follow the advice: You'd better take an umbrella. It's going to rain.

It's about a specific situation now or soon, not general advice: You'd better go now (not You'd better eat vegetables in general).

Form: had better (usually 'd better) + base verb. Negative: had better not: You'd better not be late. Even though it uses had, it's about the present or future.

  • We'd better leave now. The traffic is terrible. (warning)
  • You'd better not tell Mum about this. (negative)
  • I'd better call my wife. She's waiting for me. (my own decision now)
  • You'd better be careful. That dog bites. (warning)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ You'd better to go now. → ✅ You'd better go now. (had better + base verb)
  • ❌ You hadn't better be late. → ✅ You'd better not be late. (negative: had better not)
  • ❌ You would better hurry. → ✅ You'd better hurry. ('d = had)

Should for expectations: It should arrive tomorrow B1

should also means probably will or is expected to: The package should arrive tomorrow. They should be here by now.

Should can say that something is likely or expected, because it's normal or planned: The meeting should finish at five. (That's the plan.) This test should be easy for you. (I expect it.)

Should be + -ing / should have + past participle for expectations now or in the past: They should be arriving soon. The parcel should have arrived yesterday. (but it didn't)

With by now, should often suggests that the expected thing hasn't happened: It's 9 o'clock. Jo should be here by now. (Where is she?)

Compare should (I expect, but I'm not sure) and must (I'm sure, from evidence): She should be home soon vs Her car is outside. She must be home. See Modals of Deduction.

  • The train should be here in five minutes. (probably will)
  • You've studied a lot. You should pass easily. (I expect so)
  • It's 7 pm. The shops should be open until 8. (normally)
  • The repair should have taken an hour, but it took all day. (expected, didn't happen)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ The bus should to arrive soon. → ✅ The bus should arrive soon. (should + base verb)

Ought to = should.

Ought to + base verb.

Negative: 'd better not.

B2 · بالاتر از متوسط

Be supposed to B2

be supposed to + verb = what is expected, arranged or the rule (often not what really happens): We're supposed to wear a tie. You were supposed to call me!

Be supposed to says what should happen because of a rule, an arrangement or what people expect: You're supposed to check in an hour before the flight.

It often suggests that the thing doesn't happen, or didn't happen: I'm supposed to be on a diet, but I love cake! You were supposed to be here at six! (You weren't.)

It can also mean people say: This film is supposed to be very good.

Not supposed to = not allowed: You're not supposed to park here.

Don't forget the -d: supposed to (not suppose to). In speech the d is almost silent, but it is always written.

  • We're supposed to finish this by Friday. (an arrangement)
  • You weren't supposed to tell anyone! (you did tell)
  • This restaurant is supposed to be the best in town. (people say)
  • What are we supposed to do now? (what is expected)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ We're suppose to arrive at 9. → ✅ We're supposed to arrive at 9. (supposed, with -d)
  • ❌ You supposed to call me. → ✅ You were supposed to call me. (be + supposed to)

تمرین سطح B2

13. You ___ supposed to use your phone in the cinema.
Be not supposed to = not allowed.
14. The train was supposed to leave at 8, but it ___.
Was supposed to often means it didn't happen.
15. That new pizza place ___ (be) supposed to be amazing. Shall we try it?
Is supposed to = people say.

Should in that-clauses and for surprise C1

After verbs like suggest, insist, recommend and adjectives like important, essential, British English often uses should: I suggest that you should apply. Should also shows surprise: It's strange that she should say that.

After verbs and adjectives of suggestion, demand and importance, we can use should + base verb in the that clause (especially in British English): They recommended that we should book early. It's essential that everyone should attend.

The subjunctive (base verb with no should) is also correct and is more common in American English and formal writing: They recommended that we book early. See Subjunctive.

Should after expressions of feeling (strange, odd, surprising, a pity) adds surprise or emotion: It's odd that you should mention her. I was just thinking about her!

Both should and the subjunctive are correct after suggest / insist / important: I insisted that he should come / he come. The ordinary present (he comes) is also common in informal British English.

  • The doctor suggested that I should rest for a week. (suggestion)
  • It's important that every child should have access to education. (importance)
  • It's a pity that you should feel that way. (emotion)
  • I find it surprising that he should say such a thing. (surprise)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I suggest that you should to apply. → ✅ I suggest that you should apply. (should + base verb)
  • ❌ It's essential that he should arrives on time. → ✅ It's essential that he should arrive on time. (should + base verb)

تمرین سطح C1

16. The committee insisted that the report ___ rewritten.
Should + base verb (be rewritten).
17. Which sentence expresses surprise?
It's strange that … should = surprise.
18. It's vital that the patient ___ (should / take) the medicine every day.
Should + base verb.
