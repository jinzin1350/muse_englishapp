unless، as long as، provided، in case و even ifUnless, As Long As, Provided, In Case and Even If

If isn't the only word for conditions. English uses unless, as long as, provided (that), in case and even if to add different meanings: an exception, a strict condition, a precaution, or it doesn't matter.

After all of these, we use a present tense for the future, just like after if: I'll come unless it rains. For the basic patterns, see Zero and First Conditional.

B1 · متوسط

Unless = if … not B1

unless = if … not. Use a positive verb after it: I'll go unless it rains (= if it doesn't rain).

Unless means except if or if … not: You can't come in unless you have a ticket. = You can't come in if you don't have a ticket.

The verb after unless is positive in form, but the meaning is negative. Don't add not: unless it rains (not unless it doesn't rain).

Like if, unless takes a present tense for the future: Unless you hurry, you'll miss the bus.

  • I'll be there at eight unless the train is late. (= if the train isn't late)
  • Unless you book now, there won't be any seats. (= if you don't book)
  • Don't call me unless it's an emergency. (only if it's an emergency)
  • He won't help you unless you ask him. (= if you don't ask him)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I'll come unless it doesn't rain. → ✅ I'll come unless it rains. (unless + positive verb)
  • ❌ Unless you will study, you'll fail. → ✅ Unless you study, you'll fail. (no will after unless)

As long as, provided (that), providing B1

as long as / provided (that) / providing = only if: You can borrow my car as long as you're careful.

These phrases mean only if or on condition that. They make a strict condition: You can go out as long as you're back by ten. = You can go out, but only if you're back by ten.

Provided (that) and providing (that) mean the same, but they're more formal. On condition that is very formal: The loan was approved on condition that it was repaid within a year.

Use a present tense for the future: As long as you work hard, you'll pass. Not as long as you will work.

  • You can stay as long as you don't make a mess. (only if)
  • Provided that the weather is good, the match will go ahead. (formal)
  • I'll lend you the money providing you pay me back next week. (only if)
  • You can use the photos on condition that you credit the photographer. (very formal)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ You can come as long as you will be quiet. → ✅ You can come as long as you're quiet. (present after as long as)
  • ❌ Provided you don't hurry, you'll miss the train. → ✅ Unless you hurry, you'll miss the train. (provided = only if, not if not)

In case: a precaution B1

in case = because something might happen: Take an umbrella in case it rains. It's different from if.

We do something in case something happens = as a precaution, because it might happen: I'll take an umbrella in case it rains. (I'll take it now, because it might rain.)

Compare: I'll take an umbrella if it rains = I'll only take it if it is raining when I leave.

Use a present tense for the future after in case: in case it rains (not in case it will rain). For the past, use the past simple: I took my coat in case it got cold.

In case of + noun is used on signs and in formal English: In case of fire, use the stairs. (= if there is a fire)

  • Write down the address in case you forget it. (precaution)
  • I'll give you my number in case you need help. (because you might)
  • We left early in case there was traffic. (past)
  • In case of emergency, break the glass. (formal: if there is an emergency)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ Take a jacket in case it will be cold. → ✅ Take a jacket in case it's cold. (no will after in case)
  • ❌ I'll call you in case I'm late. → ✅ I'll call you if I'm late. (Call only when you're late: if.)

تمرین سطح B1

1. You won't get fit ___ you exercise regularly.
Unless = if … not: if you don't exercise, you won't get fit.
2. I'll go to the beach unless it's cold. This means ___.
Unless = if … not.
3. We'll be late ___ we leave now. (one word)
Unless = if we don't leave now.
4. You can watch TV ___ you've finished your homework.
Only if you've finished: as long as.
5. Which expression is the most formal?
On condition that is the most formal.
6. You can borrow my bike as long as you ___ (bring) it back tomorrow.
Present simple after as long as.
7. Take some snacks with you ___ you get hungry on the trip.
A precaution: in case.
8. I'll buy some extra food in case Tom comes. What does this mean?
In case = as a precaution, now.
9. Save your work regularly in case the computer ___ (crash).
Present simple after in case.

B2 · بالاتر از متوسط

Even if, even though and whether or not B2

even if = it doesn't matter if: I'll go even if it rains. even though = although (a fact). whether or not = in both cases.

Even if says that the result will be the same whatever happens: I'll finish this report even if I have to work all night. (Maybe I'll have to, maybe not. It doesn't matter.)

Even though is different: it introduces a fact, like although: Even though it was raining, we went for a walk. (It really was raining.) See Although, Despite and In Spite Of.

Whether or not (or whether … or not) = in both cases: I'm going, whether or not you come. / …whether you come or not.

  • Even if you apologise, she won't forgive you. (it makes no difference)
  • Even though he's rich, he lives in a tiny flat. (a fact: he is rich)
  • We'll play the match whether or not it rains. (rain or no rain)
  • I wouldn't tell you even if I knew. (imaginary: even if + past)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ Even it rains, we'll go. → ✅ Even if it rains, we'll go. (even if, not even alone)
  • ❌ Even if I was tired, I finished the work. (I really was tired) → ✅ Even though I was tired, I finished the work. (A fact: even though.)

تمرین سطح B2

10. I'm going to the concert ___ I have to go alone.
It doesn't matter if I have to go alone: even if.
11. ___ she had a cold, she went to work. (She really had a cold.)
A fact: even though.
12. We're leaving at nine, whether you like it ___ not.
Whether … or not.

C1 · پیشرفته

When unless doesn't work, and only if C1

Unless = except if. Don't use it for feelings or results of not happening (I'd be upset if you didn't come). Only if makes the condition the one and only condition.

Unless means except if. It doesn't work when the sentence is about the effect of something not happening, especially feelings: I'll be very upset if you don't come. Not unless you come. She'd be surprised if he didn't call. Not unless he called.

Unless is also unusual in questions: What will you do if she doesn't agree? Not unless she agrees.

Unless is often added as an afterthought: Let's take the bus, unless you'd rather walk.

Only if = in this one case and no other: I'll lend you money only if you promise to pay it back. In formal English it can start the sentence with inversion: Only if you promise will I lend you the money.

  • I'll be really disappointed if you don't pass. (feeling: if … not)
  • What happens if the payment doesn't go through? (question: if … not)
  • We could eat out, unless you're too tired. (afterthought)
  • You'll get a refund only if you keep the receipt. (the one condition)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I'll be sad unless you come to my party. → ✅ I'll be sad if you don't come to my party. (feelings: if … not)
  • ❌ What will you do unless you get the visa? → ✅ What will you do if you don't get the visa? (questions: if … not)

تمرین سطح C1

13. I'd be amazed ___ the exam. He's studied so hard.
A feeling about something not happening: if … not.
14. Which sentence is NOT natural?
For feelings, use if … not, not unless.
15. The machine will start ___ if both buttons are pressed together. (one word)
Only if = in this one case.
