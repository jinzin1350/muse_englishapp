Nominalisation in Formal English

B2 · C1 · C2

Nominalisation means using a noun where we could use a verb or an adjective: They decided quickly. → Their decision was quick. It makes writing more formal and helps you pack more information into a sentence.

Nominalisation is common in academic essays, reports and the news. But too much of it makes a text heavy, so use it with care. For other formal features, see Hedging and Distancing.

B2 · Upper-intermediate

Decide → decision, important → importance B2

Verb or adjectiveNoun
decide, explain, reducedecision, explanation, reduction
develop, improve, paydevelopment, improvement, payment
arrive, refuse, approvearrival, refusal, approval
fail, close, pressfailure, closure, pressure
important, different, performimportance, difference, performance
able, possible, secureability, possibility, security
aware, happy, weakawareness, happiness, weakness
grow, rise, fall, changegrowth, rise, fall, change (some nouns = verbs)

  • The reduction in costs was welcome. (reduce → reduction)
  • We noticed a big improvement in his work. (improve → improvement)
  • The arrival of the train was delayed. (arrive → arrival)
  • Nobody understood the importance of the meeting. (important → importance)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The decide was made yesterday. → ✅ The decision was made yesterday. (noun: decision)
  • ❌ There was a big grow in sales. → ✅ There was a big growth in sales. (irregular noun: growth)

C1 · Advanced

Because prices rose → because of the rise in prices C1

Clause (spoken style)Noun phrase (formal style)
Because prices rose sharply, …Because of / Due to the sharp rise in prices, …
When the president arrived, …On the president's arrival, …
The government decided to close the school.The government's decision to close the school …
If you don't pay, …Failure to pay …
They introduced new rules. This caused problems.The introduction of new rules caused problems.

  • The closure of the factory led to many job losses. (the factory closed)
  • Failure to follow the rules will result in a fine. (if you fail to follow)
  • On completion of the course, you'll receive a certificate. (when you complete)
  • The rapid growth of the city has caused traffic problems. (the city grew rapidly)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Due to prices rose, sales fell. → ✅ Due to the rise in prices, sales fell. (due to + noun phrase)
  • ❌ The quickly growth of the city surprised everyone. → ✅ The rapid growth of the city surprised everyone. (adverb → adjective)

C2 · Proficiency

Dense noun phrases, and when to avoid them C2

Heavy (too nominal)Clearer
There was a significant increase in the level of unemployment.Unemployment rose significantly.
The implementation of the plan was carried out by the team.The team implemented the plan.
A decision was made to conduct a review.We decided to review…

  • The three-year research project was funded by the EU. (pre-modification)
  • Prices fell. This fall surprised analysts. (nominalisation to refer back)
  • Unemployment rose sharply. (clear verb)
  • Recent government policy changes have helped small businesses. (dense noun phrase)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ We did an analysis of the data and a comparison of the results. (heavy) → ✅ We analysed the data and compared the results. (verbs are clearer here)
  • ❌ A three-years study was carried out. → ✅ A three-year study was carried out. (pre-modifier: no -s)
