Nominalisation in Formal English

Level: C2 · Free grammar guide

Nominalisation means expressing an action or quality as a noun instead of a verb or adjective: Prices rose sharply. → There was a sharp rise in prices.

It's typical of academic, business and news writing, because it packs more information into fewer words and sounds more objective.

How it works

Verb / adjective styleNominalised style
The government decided to raise taxes.The government's decision to raise taxes…
The company expanded rapidly.The company's rapid expansion…
They analysed the data carefully.A careful analysis of the data…
It is important to sleep well.The importance of good sleep…
Because the train arrived late, …The late arrival of the train…

Notice two changes: the verb becomes a noun, and the adverb becomes an adjective (rapidly → rapid).

Common noun endings

  • -tion / -sion: decide → decision, reduce → reduction, expand → expansion
  • -ment: develop → development, improve → improvement
  • -ance / -ence: perform → performance, important → importance, depend → dependence
  • -ity: possible → possibility, available → availability
  • -ness: aware → awareness, effective → effectiveness
  • -ure / -al / -ysis: fail → failure, arrive → arrival, analyse → analysis
  • Same form: rise, fall, increase, decrease, change, demand, growth (grow)

Noun + preposition

NounPrepositionExample
increase, decrease, rise, fall, decline, growthina rise in prices
effect, impact, influenceonan effect on small businesses
demand, need, reasonfora demand for organic food
solution, approach, access, answertoa solution to the problem
cause, analysis, importanceofthe cause of the drop in sales

Use of for the amount of a change: an increase of 5%, but an increase in prices.

Don't overdo it. Too many nouns make a text heavy and hard to read. Use nominalisation where it makes a sentence clearer or more formal.

Examples

  1. There has been a sharp rise in unemployment. (rise in)
  2. The new law will have an effect on small businesses. (effect on)
  3. There is growing awareness of climate change. (aware → awareness)
  4. The failure of the talks surprised everyone. (fail → failure)
  5. The rapid growth of the population has put pressure on housing. (grow rapidly → rapid growth)
  6. There is increasing demand for online courses. (demand for)
  7. We need to find a solution to this problem. (solution to)
  8. Students have limited access to affordable housing. (access to)
  9. A careful analysis of the data revealed a pattern. (analyse → analysis)
  10. The late arrival of the train meant we missed the meeting. (a clause becomes a noun phrase)

Common mistakes

❌ There was a rapidly increase in prices.

✅ There was a rapid increase in prices. (Before a noun, use an adjective, not an adverb.)

❌ The new rule had a big effect to sales.

✅ The new rule had a big effect on sales. (Effect on.)

❌ There is a growing demand of organic food.

✅ There is a growing demand for organic food. (Demand for.)

❌ The analyse of the data took weeks.

✅ The analysis of the data took weeks. (The noun is analysis.)

❌ What was the reason of the delay?

✅ What was the reason for the delay? (Reason for.)

❌ We need a solution for this problem.

✅ We need a solution to this problem. (In formal English, solution to is the standard choice.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. The government decided to raise taxes. → The government's ___ to raise taxes was unpopular.
decide → decision.
2. Prices rose sharply. → There was a sharp ___ prices.
a rise in + what changed.
3. People are increasingly aware of climate change. → There is growing ___ of climate change.
aware → awareness.
4. The new law will affect small businesses. → The new law will have an ___ small businesses.
The noun is effect, with on.
5. They failed to agree. → Their ___ to agree surprised everyone.
fail → failure.
6. We need to solve this problem. → We need to find a ___ this problem.
a solution to a problem.
7. The company expanded rapidly. → The company's ___ expansion surprised investors.
Before a noun, the adverb becomes an adjective: rapid.
8. Customers are demanding more organic food. → There is a growing ___ organic food.
a demand for something.
9. Unemployment fell. → There was a ___ in unemployment.
The noun is fall (same form as the verb).
10. It is important to sleep well. → The ___ of good sleep cannot be overstated.
important → importance.
11. Which version uses nominalisation? (Original: 'Because the train arrived late, we missed the meeting.')
arrived late → the late arrival of the train (a noun phrase).
12. The population grew. → The ___ of the population has put pressure on housing.
grow → growth.
13. They analysed the data carefully. → A careful ___ of the data showed a clear pattern.
analyse → analysis.
14. It is difficult for students to get housing. → Students have limited ___ housing.
access to something.
15. The two countries are cooperating more. → There is increased ___ between the two countries.
cooperate → cooperation.
16. Why did sales drop? → What was the ___ the drop in sales?
the cause of something (or the reason for).
