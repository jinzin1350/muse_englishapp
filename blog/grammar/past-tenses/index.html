Past Tenses in English

English has several ways to talk about the past. The past simple (I worked) is the most common. The others add information: an action in progress (I was working), something earlier (I had worked), or a habit that has changed (I used to work).

This page shows the big picture and helps you choose. Each topic below has its own page with rules, examples and exercises at every level.

The past tenses at a glance

TenseFormMain useExample
Past simpleworked, wentfinished actions and situationsI went to Paris in 2020.
Past continuouswas / were workingan action in progress at a past momentAt 8 pm I was cooking.
Used toused to workpast habits and states, not true nowI used to live in Shiraz.
Past perfecthad workedsomething earlier than another past momentThe film had started when we arrived.
Past perfect continuoushad been workingan activity up to a past momentI'd been waiting for an hour.

Which one do I need?

  • Is it a finished action or situation, or the next event in a story? → past simple: She closed the door.
  • Was it in progress at a moment in the past, or the background of a story? → past continuous: It was raining.
  • Is it a past habit or state that isn't true now? → used to: We used to have a dog.
  • Did it happen before another past moment? → past perfect: When I got there, she had left.
  • Was an activity going on up to a past moment, often with how long? → past perfect continuous: He had been working all night.
  • Is it be? → was / were: I was tired. They were late.
