Future Time Clauses (When I get home, I'll…)

A2 · B1 · B2 · C1

In sentences about the future, words like when, as soon as, before, after, until introduce a time clause. In the time clause, we use a present tense, not will: I'll call you when I get home. Not when I will get home.

This is the same rule as for if in the first conditional. For the difference between if and when, see Zero and First Conditional.

A2 · Elementary

When, as soon as, before, after + present A2

Time clause (present)Main clause (future)
When I finish work,I'll go to the gym.
As soon as the film ends,we'll leave.
Before you go out,turn off the lights.
After we eat,we're going to watch a film.

  • I'll call you when I get to the hotel. (when + present)
  • As soon as the shop opens, I'm going to buy that dress. (as soon as + present)
  • Please lock the door before you leave. (before + present)
  • We'll have dinner after the kids go to bed. (after + present)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I'll call you when I will arrive. → ✅ I'll call you when I arrive. (no will after when)
  • ❌ As soon as she will finish, we'll go. → ✅ As soon as she finishes, we'll go. (present after as soon as)

Practice: A2

1. I'll phone you when I ___ home.
When + present simple.
2. ___ the rain stops, we'll go for a walk.
As soon as + present.
3. We'll start the meeting when everyone ___ (arrive).
When + present simple (or present perfect).

B1 · Intermediate

When I've finished…: present perfect in time clauses B1

  • I'll lend you the book when I've read it. (reading first, then lending)
  • As soon as I've saved enough money, I'll buy a car. (complete first)
  • Don't go until you've signed the contract. (complete first)
  • After she's had lunch, she'll call you back. (complete first)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I'll give it back when I will have read it. → ✅ I'll give it back when I've read it. (no will in the time clause)

Practice: B1

4. You can watch TV when you ___ your homework.
Completed first: present perfect.
5. Which sentence stresses that the first action will be complete?
When I've finished = completely done first.
6. As soon as I ___ (pack) my bags, we can leave.
Completed first: have packed (or pack).

B2 · Upper-intermediate

Until, once, by the time, the moment, next time B2

ConjunctionMeaningExample
until / tillup to the time whenStay here until I get back.
onceafter, as soon asOnce you start, you can't stop.
by the timenot later thanBy the time you arrive, I'll have left.
the moment / the minuteimmediately whenI'll call you the moment I land.
next time / every timeon the next occasionNext time you come, bring your guitar.

  • I won't stop until I find it. (up to the time)
  • Once you've tried it, you'll love it. (after)
  • By the time the guests arrive, dinner will be ready. (not later than)
  • Tell me the moment you hear anything. (immediately)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I'll wait until you will come back. → ✅ I'll wait until you come back. (present after until)
  • ❌ By the time we will get there, the film will have started. → ✅ By the time we get there, the film will have started. (present after by the time)

Practice: B2

7. By the time we ___ to the station, the train will have left.
By the time + present simple.
8. I'll wait here ___ you finish. Take your time.
Up to the time when: until.
9. ___ you understand the basics, the rest is easy. (one word: as soon as)
Once = as soon as.

C1 · Advanced

When will is correct after when and if C1

  • I wonder when she'll call. (noun clause: will)
  • Do you know when the shop will open? (noun clause: will)
  • I'll call you when the shop opens. (time clause: present)
  • If you'll just wait a moment, I'll find the manager. (willingness)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I don't know when he arrives tomorrow. (asking about the time) → ✅ I don't know when he'll arrive tomorrow. (noun clause: will (the present is possible for timetables))

Practice: C1

10. Do you know when the next bus ___?
A noun clause after know: will is fine (comes is also possible for a timetable).
11. Which sentence is correct?
A time clause: present simple.
12. I have no idea when they ___ (finish) the new road.
A noun clause: will finish.
