Reported Speech: Statements and Questions

Level: B1 · Free grammar guide

We use reported speech to say what someone said without repeating their exact words: “I'm tired.” → She said (that) she was tired.

After a past reporting verb (said, told, asked), the tense usually moves one step back into the past.

Tense changes in reported speech

Direct speechReported speech
present simple: “I work at home.”past simple: He said he worked at home.
present continuous: “I'm cooking.”past continuous: She said she was cooking.
past simple: “I saw him.”past perfect: She said she had seen him.
present perfect: “I've finished.”past perfect: He said he had finished.
will: “I'll call you.”would: She said she would call me.
can: “I can swim.”could: He said he could swim.
must: “I must go.”had to: She said she had to go.

The tense change is not always necessary. If something is still true, you can keep the tense: “I live in Toronto.” → He said he lives (or lived) in Toronto. In speech, people also often keep the past simple: She said she saw him.

If the reporting verb is in the present (says, tells), don't change the tense: She says she's tired.

Say or tell?

  • say + (that) + sentence: He said (that) he was hungry.
  • tell + person + (that) + sentence: He told me (that) he was hungry.

Don't use a person after say (not He said me…), and don't use tell without a person (not He told that…). Some fixed phrases are different: tell the truth, tell a lie, tell a story. That is optional after both verbs.

Pronouns, time and place words

Change pronouns to match the new speaker: “I love your idea,” Sam told me. → Sam told me he loved my idea. When the time or place is different, the time and place words change too:

Direct speechReported speech
nowthen / at that moment
todaythat day
tonightthat night
yesterdaythe day before / the previous day
tomorrowthe next day / the following day
last weekthe week before / the previous week
next weekthe following week
herethere

Only change these words when the time or place has changed. If you report something on the same day, today can stay today.

Reported questions

Reported questions use normal sentence word order (subject + verb), no do / does / did and no question mark. Use ask, not say.

QuestionReported question
“Where do you live?”She asked me where I lived.
“What time is it?”He asked what time it was.
“Are you hungry?”She asked if / whether I was hungry.
“Did you see the match?”He asked if I had seen the match.

For yes/no questions, use if or whether: He asked if I was ready.

Reported requests and orders

Use ask / tell + person + to + base verb. For negatives, use not to: “Please sit down.” → She asked me to sit down. “Don't touch it.” → He told us not to touch it.

Examples

  1. “I'm busy.” → She said she was busy. (am → was)
  2. “I work in a hotel.” → He told me he worked in a hotel. (tell + person)
  3. “We're leaving.” → They said they were leaving. (present continuous → past continuous)
  4. “I lost my wallet.” → She said she had lost her wallet. (past simple → past perfect)
  5. “I've never been to Japan.” → He said he had never been to Japan. (present perfect → past perfect)
  6. “I'll help you.” → She said she would help me. (will → would; you → me)
  7. “I can't come tomorrow.” → He said he couldn't come the next day. (can't → couldn't; tomorrow → the next day)
  8. “Where is the station?” → She asked me where the station was. (statement word order)
  9. “Do you like jazz?” → He asked me if I liked jazz. (yes/no question → if)
  10. “Please close the window.” → She asked me to close the window. (request → ask + to)

Common mistakes

❌ He said me he was tired.

✅ He told me he was tired. (Tell + person. Say has no person after it.)

❌ She told that she was busy.

✅ She said that she was busy. (Tell needs a person: She told me that…)

❌ He asked me where did I live.

✅ He asked me where I lived. (Reported questions use normal word order and no did.)

❌ She asked me if was I hungry.

✅ She asked me if I was hungry. (Subject before the verb: if I was.)

❌ She asked me do I like jazz.

✅ She asked me if I liked jazz. (Use if or whether for reported yes/no questions.)

❌ He asked me what time is it.

✅ He asked me what time it was. (Normal word order: it was.)

❌ She told me don't worry.

✅ She told me not to worry. (Reported orders: tell + person + not to + verb.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. “I'm tired.” → She said she ___ tired.
Am moves back to was. She takes was.
2. “I'll call you tomorrow.” → He said he ___ me the next day.
Will → would. The next day shows we are reporting later.
3. He ___ me that he was hungry.
Tell + person + that. Say has no person after it.
4. She ___ that she was busy.
No person after the verb, so use said.
5. “Where do you live?” → She asked me where ___.
Reported questions use normal word order and no did: I lived.
6. “Are you hungry?” → He asked me ___ hungry.
Yes/no question → if + subject + verb.
7. “I've finished my homework.” → She said she ___ her homework.
Present perfect → past perfect: had finished.
8. “I can swim.” → He said he ___ swim.
Can → could. Modal verbs never take -s.
9. “What time is it?” → She asked me what time ___.
Normal word order in reported questions: it was.
10. “Did you see the match?” → He asked me ___ the match.
Yes/no question → if + subject + verb. Past simple → past perfect.
11. “Please sit down.” → The teacher asked us ___ down.
Requests: ask + person + to + base verb.
12. “Don't be late.” → He told me ___ late.
Negative orders: tell + person + not to + base verb.
13. “I'm leaving today.” A week later you report: She said she was leaving ___.
The day has changed, so today → that day.
14. Choose the correct sentence.
Told + me + that. Said can't take a person directly, and told needs one.
15. “Is the museum open on Mondays?” → I asked ___ the museum was open on Mondays.
Yes/no question → whether (or if).
16. “We're watching a film.” → They said they ___ a film.
Present continuous → past continuous. They takes were watching.
