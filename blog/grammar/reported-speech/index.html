نقل قول غیرمستقیمReported Speech (She said that…)

Reported speech (or indirect speech) is how we tell someone what another person said, without using their exact words: “I'm tired.” → She said (that) she was tired.

The main changes are the tense, pronouns and time words. For other reporting verbs (promise, suggest, warn), see Reporting Verbs.

A2 · مقدماتی

Say or tell? She said / She told me A2

say + (that) + what: She said (that) she was hungry. tell + person + (that): She told me (that) she was hungry.

saytell
She said (that) she was tired.She told me (that) she was tired.
He said hello.He told us a story.
She said to me that… (possible, less common)She told that… ✗ (needs a person)

Tell needs a person after it: tell me, tell him, tell the class. Say doesn't: say something. If you want a person after say, use to: He said to me…

That is optional after both: She said (that) she was busy.

Fixed phrases with tell: tell the truth, tell a lie, tell a story, tell a joke, tell the time. With say: say hello, say goodbye, say sorry, say something / nothing.

  • Tom said he was hungry. (say + what)
  • Tom told me he was hungry. (tell + person)
  • My mum told us a funny story. (tell + story)
  • She said goodbye and left. (say + goodbye)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ He told that he was tired. → ✅ He said that he was tired. / He told me that he was tired. (tell needs a person)
  • ❌ She said me the answer. → ✅ She told me the answer. (say + person: use tell)
  • ❌ Can you say me the time? → ✅ Can you tell me the time? (tell the time)

تمرین سطح A2

Tell + person.

Say + (that): no person after it.

B1 · متوسط

Tense changes: one step back B1

When the reporting verb is past (said, told), the tense usually moves one step back: “I'm tired” → She said she was tired. “I'll call” → He said he would call.

Direct speechReported speech
present simple: “I work here.”past simple: He said he worked there.
present continuous: “I'm leaving.”past continuous: She said she was leaving.
present perfect: “I've finished.”past perfect: He said he had finished.
past simple: “I lost it.”past perfect: She said she had lost it. (or lost)
will: “I'll help.”would: He said he would help.
can: “I can swim.”could: She said she could swim.
am going to: “I'm going to move.”was going to: He said he was going to move.

Pronouns also change to fit the new speaker: “I love my job.” → She said she loved her job.

The past simple can stay the same in reported speech when the time is clear: She said she lost / had lost her keys yesterday.

  • “I live in Toronto.” → He said he lived in Toronto. (present → past)
  • “We're having dinner.” → They said they were having dinner. (continuous)
  • “I've seen that film.” → She said she had seen that film. (present perfect → past perfect)
  • “I'll be late.” → He told me he would be late. (will → would)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ She said she is tired. (she isn't tired now) → ✅ She said she was tired. (backshift: was)
  • ❌ He said he will come. (the time is past) → ✅ He said he would come. (will → would)

When the tense doesn't change B1

Don't change the tense when the reporting verb is present (She says she's tired) or, optionally, when the situation is still true: He said he lives in Paris.

If the reporting verb is in the present (says, tells, thinks), the tense doesn't change: My doctor says I need more rest.

If the statement is still true now, you can keep the original tense: “I'm a vegetarian.” → She said she is a vegetarian. (She still is.) Both is and was are correct here.

General truths usually keep the present: The teacher said that water boils at 100°C.

When in doubt, backshift. It's always correct after a past reporting verb if the statement was true at that time.

  • The forecast says it's going to rain. (present reporting verb)
  • She said she has two children. (still true)
  • He told me the Earth goes round the Sun. (general truth)
  • Mina says she'll be late. (present reporting verb)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ He says he was tired now. → ✅ He says he's tired now. (present reporting verb: no change)

Time and place words: tomorrow → the next day B1

When the time or place has changed, words like now, today, tomorrow, here, this change too: “I'll see you tomorrow.” → He said he'd see me the next day.

Only change these words if the time or place is different when you report. If you report on the same day, “I'll call you tomorrow” can stay He said he'd call me tomorrow.

  • “I'm busy today.” → She said she was busy that day. (today → that day)
  • “We arrived yesterday.” → They said they had arrived the day before. (yesterday → the day before)
  • “I'll do it tomorrow.” → He said he'd do it the next day. (tomorrow → the next day)
  • “I like it here.” → She said she liked it there. (here → there)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ Last year she told me she was moving tomorrow. → ✅ Last year she told me she was moving the next day. (reported later: the next day)

Reported questions: He asked me where I lived B1

Reported questions use statement word order and no do / did: “Where do you live?” → He asked me where I lived. Yes/no questions use if / whether.

Direct questionReported question
“Where do you live?”He asked me where I lived.
“What are you doing?”She asked what I was doing.
“Are you married?”He asked if / whether I was married.
“Can you help me?”She asked if I could help her.

In reported questions: no question word order, no do / does / did, no question mark: She asked where the station was. Not She asked where was the station?

The tense changes in the same way as statements (backshift).

  • “How old are you?” → She asked me how old I was. (statement order)
  • “Did you enjoy the film?” → He asked if I had enjoyed the film. (yes/no: if)
  • “Where is the bank?” → A tourist asked me where the bank was. (statement order)
  • “Will you come?” → She asked whether I would come. (whether)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ He asked me where did I live. → ✅ He asked me where I lived. (no did; statement order)
  • ❌ She asked me was I hungry. → ✅ She asked me if I was hungry. (yes/no: if / whether)
  • ❌ They asked what time was it. → ✅ They asked what time it was. (statement order)

Reported orders and requests: told me to, asked me not to B1

tell / ask + person + (not) to + verb: “Sit down.” → She told me to sit down. “Please don't shout.” → He asked us not to shout.

DirectReported
“Close the door.”She told me to close the door.
“Don't touch it!”He told me not to touch it.
“Could you help me, please?”She asked me to help her.
“Please don't be late.”They asked us not to be late.

Use tell for orders and instructions, and ask for requests. The negative is not to + verb (not to don't).

  • “Wait here.” → The guard told us to wait there. (order)
  • “Can you call me back?” → She asked me to call her back. (request)
  • “Don't forget your passport.” → Mum told me not to forget my passport. (negative order)
  • “Please turn down the music.” → My neighbour asked us to turn down the music. (request)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ She told me don't worry. → ✅ She told me not to worry. (not to + verb)
  • ❌ He asked me that I help him. → ✅ He asked me to help him. (ask + person + to)
  • ❌ The teacher told to us to be quiet. → ✅ The teacher told us to be quiet. (tell + person (no to))

C1 · پیشرفته

Modals in reported speech C1

can → could, will → would, may → might, must → had to / must. Could, would, should, might, ought to don't change.

DirectReported
“I can help.”He said he could help.
“I will call.”She said she would call.
“It may rain.”He said it might rain.
“You must leave.”She said I had to / must leave.
“I should go.”He said he should go. (no change)
“I could come.”She said she could come. (no change)

Must for obligation can become had to or stay must: He said I had to / must sign the form. For deduction, must doesn't change: She said it must be a mistake.

Shall I / we questions become should: “Shall I open the window?” → He asked if he should open the window. (offer or advice)

Needn't can stay or become didn't need to / didn't have to.

  • “You may be right.” → She said I might be right. (may → might)
  • “We must hurry.” → He said we had to hurry. (must → had to)
  • “You should rest.” → The doctor said I should rest. (no change)
  • “Shall I carry it?” → He asked if he should carry it. (shall → should)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ She said she can come. (the time is past) → ✅ She said she could come. (can → could)
  • ❌ He said I must have to go. → ✅ He said I had to go. / He said I must go. (must → had to (or must))

Choosing the tense: reporting news, facts and opinions C1

Backshift is not automatic. Choose the tense to show if something is still true, and use present reporting verbs for news, written sources and opinions: The report says…

Native speakers often don't backshift when the information is still true or relevant: She told me she has a new job. (She still has it.) Backshifting can suggest the information is no longer true or that you don't believe it: He said he was 25 (but I think he's older).

For written sources and news, we often use present reporting verbs: The article says that… The government claims that… Darwin argues that…

For past facts, the past simple often stays (no need for the past perfect) when the order is clear: She said she was born in 1990.

In academic writing, present reporting verbs are common for ideas in published work: Smith (2010) argues that… See Reporting Verbs.

  • She told me she is pregnant. (still true)
  • He said he was innocent, but nobody believed him. (doubt / past situation)
  • The report says that unemployment has fallen. (written source: present)
  • She said she was born in Tabriz. (past fact: no change needed)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ She said she had been born in 1990. (correct but unnecessary) → ✅ She said she was born in 1990. (past facts: the past simple can stay)
