Gradable and Extreme Adjectives

B1 · B2 · C1

Gradable adjectives can be more or less: cold, big, good. Use very, a bit and extremely with them: very cold. Extreme adjectives already mean “very”: freezing, huge, amazing. Use absolutely or really with them: absolutely freezing.

Using the right word before an adjective is one of the easiest ways to sound more natural.

B1 · Intermediate

Very cold, absolutely freezing B1

Gradable (+ very)Extreme (+ absolutely)
coldfreezing
hotboiling
bighuge, enormous
smalltiny
goodamazing, fantastic, excellent
badterrible, awful
tiredexhausted
hungrystarving
angryfurious
interestingfascinating
scaredterrified

  • It's very cold today. (gradable)
  • It's absolutely freezing! (extreme)
  • The hotel was really amazing. (really: both)
  • I'm extremely tired. (gradable)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The film was very fantastic. → ✅ The film was absolutely fantastic. (extreme: absolutely)
  • ❌ I'm absolutely hungry. → ✅ I'm absolutely starving. (absolutely + extreme adjective)
  • ❌ Their house is very enormous. → ✅ Their house is absolutely enormous. (extreme: absolutely)

Practice: B1

1. I haven't eaten all day. I'm ___.
Extreme adjective + absolutely.
2. Their new house is ___. It has eight bedrooms!
Extreme adjective + absolutely.
3. The view from the top was ___ amazing. (one word)
Extreme adjective: absolutely amazing.

B2 · Upper-intermediate

Absolute adjectives: completely different, almost empty B2

Absolute adjectiveNatural combinations
impossibleabsolutely / completely impossible
differentcompletely / totally different
wrong / rightcompletely wrong, absolutely right
empty / fullcompletely empty, almost full
unique / perfectabsolutely perfect (very unique is common but avoided in careful English)
finished / readyalmost / nearly finished

  • Her new job is completely different from her old one. (completely)
  • You're absolutely right. (absolutely)
  • The cinema was almost empty. (almost)
  • It's totally impossible to finish today. (totally)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ This painting is very unique. → ✅ This painting is unique. (unique: no very in careful English)
  • ❌ The task was very impossible. → ✅ The task was completely impossible. (impossible: completely / absolutely)

Practice: B2

4. The two brothers are ___ identical. Nobody can tell them apart.
Absolute adjective: completely identical.
5. The petrol tank is ___ empty. We need to stop soon.
Not quite: almost empty.
6. You're ___ right. I should apologise. (one word)
Absolute adjective: absolutely right.

C1 · Advanced

Highly unlikely, deeply concerned: adverb + adjective pairs C1

AdverbNatural adjectives
highlyunlikely, likely, successful, recommended, qualified, effective
deeplyconcerned, moved, shocked, offended, grateful
bitterlydisappointed, cold, opposed
fullyaware, booked, insured, qualified
perfectlyclear, normal, capable, safe
utterly / totallyridiculous, exhausted, convinced
seriously / badlyill, injured, damaged

  • It's highly unlikely that it will snow in June. (highly)
  • I was deeply moved by her speech. (deeply)
  • The fans were bitterly disappointed. (bitterly)
  • I'm fully aware of the risks. (fully)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ The hotel comes strongly recommended. → ✅ The hotel comes highly recommended. (highly recommended)
  • ❌ He was heavily injured in the accident. → ✅ He was seriously injured in the accident. (seriously / badly injured)

Practice: C1

7. It's ___ unlikely that he'll get the job. He has no experience.
Collocation: highly unlikely.
8. We were ___ disappointed when the concert was cancelled.
Collocation: bitterly disappointed.
9. I'm ___ aware of the risks, but I still want to try. (one word)
Collocation: fully aware.
