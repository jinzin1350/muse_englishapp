Narrative Tenses: Telling Stories in the Past

B1 · B2 · C1

When we tell a story or describe past events, we use four narrative tenses together. Each one has a different job.

This page shows how to combine them, how to move back in time and forward again, and how to make a story clear and lively. For each tense on its own, see Past Simple, Past Continuous, Past Perfect and Past Perfect Continuous.

B1 · Intermediate

The four narrative tenses and their jobs B1

TenseJob in the storyExample
Past simplethe main events, in orderShe opened the letter and read it.
Past continuousthe background, actions in progressIt was raining and the wind was blowing.
Past perfectevents before the main storySomeone had left the door open.
Past perfect continuousactivities that continued up to the main storyShe'd been waiting for that letter for weeks.

  • We were having lunch when the earthquake started. (background + event)
  • When we got to the cinema, the film had already started. (earlier event)
  • He was angry because he'd been waiting for two hours. (earlier activity)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I was waking up at 7, had a shower and left. → ✅ I woke up at 7, had a shower and left. (The main events in order: past simple.)
  • ❌ When I arrived, the train already left. → ✅ When I arrived, the train had already left. (Earlier than the main event: past perfect.)

Going back in time and coming back B1

  • When I saw him, I knew him at once. We'd worked together years before. (flashback)
  • She opened the box. Her grandmother had given it to her when she was a child. (jump back)
  • I phoned the hotel after I saw the advert. (order clear: past simple is fine)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I had met him at a party. I had liked him and we had talked all night and we had danced. → ✅ I had met him at a party. I liked him and we talked all night and danced. (After the first verb, return to the past simple.)
  • ❌ When I arrived at the station, my train left. (it left earlier) → ✅ When I arrived at the station, my train had left. (The earlier event needs the past perfect.)

Practice: B1

1. Which tense do we normally use for the main events of a story?
The past simple moves the story forward.
2. It <span class="gap">___</span> heavily when we left the house, so we took an umbrella.
Background in progress: past continuous.
3. When I got to the airport, I realised I <span class="gap">___</span> (forget) my passport.
Earlier than realised: past perfect.
4. I was nervous before the interview because I <span class="gap">___</span> an interview in English before.
Experience before a past moment: had never had.
5. Which is the most natural way to continue? I recognised the woman at once. She…
Jump back with the past perfect, then continue in the past simple.
6. When I saw the photo, I remembered the day. We <span class="gap">___</span> (go) to the lake with our grandfather.
The jump back: past perfect.

B2 · Upper-intermediate

Time linkers in stories B2

LinkerUseExample
when / as soon asone event right after anotherAs soon as I saw him, I knew.
after / beforeorder (past simple is often enough)After we (had) eaten, we went out.
by the timesomething was already completeBy the time we arrived, the shop had closed.
untilup to that timeI waited until the rain stopped.
while / astwo things at the same timeWhile I was cooking, the phone rang.
during + nounin the period of somethingDuring the flight, I slept.
eventually / in the end / at lastafter a long time or difficultiesWe eventually found the hotel.

  • By the time the ambulance arrived, the man had stopped breathing. (by the time + past perfect)
  • We didn't leave until the storm had passed. (until)
  • During the night, the temperature fell to −20. (during + noun)
  • Eventually, after three hours, we reached the top. (after a long time)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ During we were travelling, we met a lot of people. → ✅ While we were travelling, we met a lot of people. (during + noun; while + clause)
  • ❌ By the time we arrived, the film already started. → ✅ By the time we arrived, the film had already started. (by the time → past perfect)
  • ❌ At the end we found a taxi. → ✅ In the end we found a taxi. (final result: in the end)

Choosing the right tense in a story B2

  • Is it the next event in the story? → past simple: I ran to the door.
  • Was it in progress at that moment? → past continuous: Someone was knocking.
  • Did it happen before the moment we're talking about? → past perfect: I had never seen him before.
  • Was it an activity going on up to that moment? → past perfect continuous: He had been driving all night.

  • The phone rang. I was in the shower. (event + state)
  • I had been working for ten hours, so I was exhausted. (earlier activity + state)
  • She didn't recognise me because I'd changed so much. (earlier change)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I was getting up, I was having breakfast and I was leaving. → ✅ I got up, had breakfast and left. (Events in order: past simple.)
  • ❌ Everyone was knowing the answer. → ✅ Everyone knew the answer. (know is a state verb.)

Practice: B2

7. <span class="gap">___</span> the summer, we visited my cousins in Rasht.
During + noun.
8. By the time we got to the restaurant, the kitchen <span class="gap">___</span>.
By the time + past simple → past perfect.
9. We waited <span class="gap">___</span> the last guest had gone, then we started cleaning.
Until = up to that time.
10. It was midnight. I <span class="gap">___</span> for hours and my eyes were tired.
An activity up to that moment: had been studying.
11. I ran outside to see what <span class="gap">___</span>.
Earlier than ran outside: had happened.
12. When I opened my eyes, the nurse <span class="gap">___</span> (stand) next to my bed.
In progress at that moment: was standing (in written stories, stood is also possible).

C1 · Advanced

Making stories lively: other tenses in narratives C1

  • So she looks at me and says, Who are you? (present for drama)
  • Every summer we would spend weeks at the lake. (would: repeated)
  • I was about to give up when I saw the sign. (plan interrupted)
  • We were going to have a party, but it was cancelled. (plan that changed)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ So I walk into the room and everybody looked at me. → ✅ So I walk into the room and everybody looks at me. (Keep the same time frame.)
  • ❌ I was about leaving when she arrived. → ✅ I was about to leave when she arrived. (be about to + base verb)

Practice: C1

13. In a spoken anecdote: So I'm sitting on the train, and suddenly a man <span class="gap">___</span> next to me.
Stay in the present once the story is in the present.
14. I <span class="gap">___</span> call you, but then I saw your message.
A plan that changed: was going to.
15. We were about <span class="gap">___</span> (leave) when the guests arrived.
Be about to + base verb.
