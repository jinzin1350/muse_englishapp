Present Perfect Continuous

Level: B1 · Free grammar guide

We use the present perfect continuous (have/has been + verb-ing) for an activity that started in the past and is still continuing, or that has just stopped and we can see the result: I've been waiting for an hour. You're wet! Have you been running?

How to form the present perfect continuous

FormExample
Positivehave / has + been + verb-ingShe's been studying all morning.
Negativehaven't / hasn't + been + verb-ingI haven't been sleeping well.
QuestionHave / Has + subject + been + verb-ing?How long have you been learning English?

When do we use it?

  • An activity from the past until now, often with for, since or how long: They've been living here since 2020.
  • A recent activity with a result we can see now: Your eyes are red. Have you been crying?
  • Repeated actions over a period up to now: I've been going to the gym a lot recently.

Present perfect continuous or present perfect simple?

Present perfect continuousPresent perfect simple
focus on the activity and how longfocus on the result, or how many / how much
I've been reading this book for two hours.I've read 50 pages. I've finished the book.
She's been writing emails all morning.She's written ten emails.

With a number or an amount, use the simple form: I've drunk three coffees today. (not I've been drinking three coffees)

State verbs (know, like, have = own, believe, understand) use the simple form: I've known her for years. (not I've been knowing) With live, work, study and teach, both forms are often possible: I've lived / I've been living here for five years.

Examples

  1. I've been waiting for the bus for twenty minutes. (still waiting)
  2. It's been raining all day. (all day up to now)
  3. How long have you been learning to drive? (how long)
  4. She's tired because she's been working late every night. (repeated activity)
  5. Have you been crying? Your eyes are red. (visible result)
  6. We've been looking for a new flat since January. (since + a starting point)
  7. He hasn't been feeling well recently. (negative)
  8. I've been reading this book all week, and I've nearly finished it. (focus on the activity)
  9. They've been playing tennis for two hours. (for + a period)
  10. What have you been doing? You're covered in paint! (visible result)

Common mistakes

❌ I've been knowing him for years.

✅ I've known him for years. (Know is a state verb: use the simple form.)

❌ She has been work here since May.

✅ She has been working here since May. (Been + verb-ing.)

❌ I am waiting for an hour.

✅ I've been waiting for an hour. (From the past until now: present perfect continuous.)

❌ How long are you learning English?

✅ How long have you been learning English? (How long + present perfect (continuous).)

❌ I've been drinking three cups of coffee today.

✅ I've drunk three cups of coffee today. (With a number, use the simple form.)

❌ He have been playing football.

✅ He has been playing football. (He, she, it → has.)

Exercises (16)

Choose the correct answer.

1. I ___ for you for an hour!
From the past until now: have been + verb-ing.
2. How long ___ you been learning English?
How long + have you been + verb-ing?
3. Your hands are dirty. What ___ doing?
A visible result of a recent activity: have you been + verb-ing?
4. She ___ in this company since 2018.
She + has been working. Don't use the present continuous with since.
5. I ___ him since we were children.
Know is a state verb: present perfect simple, 've known.
6. It ___ all day. The streets are wet.
All day up to now: 's been raining.
7. I ___ three emails this morning, and I've sent them all.
A number and finished results: present perfect simple, 've written.
8. We ___ for a new flat for months, but we haven't found one yet.
An activity continuing up to now: 've been looking.
9. He ___ well recently.
He + hasn't been feeling.
10. Choose the correct sentence.
For five years up to now: 've been living.
11. Why are you out of breath? – I ___.
A recent activity with a visible result: 've been running.
12. I've ___ 100 pages of this book so far.
A number (100 pages): present perfect simple, I've read.
13. ___ you been sleeping well?
Questions with you: Have you been + verb-ing?
14. They ___ tennis since 3 o'clock.
They + have been playing.
15. I'm so tired. I ___ all day.
The activity explains why you're tired now: 've been studying.
16. Which sentence focuses on the result?
The present perfect simple focuses on the finished result: I've painted the kitchen.
