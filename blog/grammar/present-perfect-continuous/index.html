Present Perfect Continuous (I have been doing)

B1 · B2 · C1

We use the present perfect continuous (have / has been + -ing) for an activity that started in the past and is still going on, or has just stopped: I've been waiting for an hour. You're wet! Have you been swimming?

It focuses on the activity and how long it has lasted. The present perfect simple (I've done) focuses on the result or how much. This page covers both and the cases where the choice changes the meaning.

B1 · Intermediate

Form and use: still going on, how long B1

FormExample
PositiveI've been learning English for three years.
he / she / itShe's been working since 8 am.
NegativeWe haven't been waiting long.
QuestionHow long have you been living here?
Short answersYes, I have. / No, she hasn't.

  • I've been studying for this exam for weeks. (still going on)
  • How long has it been snowing? (how long)
  • They've been building that bridge since 2022. (since)
  • We haven't been sleeping well recently. (negative)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I'm waiting for an hour. → ✅ I've been waiting for an hour. (How long up to now: present perfect continuous.)
  • ❌ She has been work here since May. → ✅ She has been working here since May. (have been + -ing)
  • ❌ How long you have been learning English? → ✅ How long have you been learning English? (Question: have + subject + been + -ing.)

An activity that has just stopped: evidence now B1

  • Your eyes are red. Have you been crying?
  • I'm tired because I've been working in the garden.
  • The ground is wet. It's been raining.

  • Sorry I'm dirty. I've been fixing the car. (evidence: dirty)
  • The kitchen smells of onions. Have you been cooking? (evidence: smell)
  • Why are you so tired? ~ I've been running. (evidence: tired)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Your eyes are red. Have you been cry? → ✅ Your eyes are red. Have you been crying? (been + -ing)
  • ❌ My hands hurt because I've been dig in the garden. → ✅ My hands hurt because I've been digging in the garden. (been + -ing)

Have been doing or have done? B1

Present perfect continuous (activity)Present perfect simple (result)
I've been reading your book. (not finished)I've read your book. (finished)
She's been writing emails all morning. (how long)She's written ten emails. (how many)
They've been painting the kitchen. (that's why it smells)They've painted the kitchen. (it's done)

  • I've been cooking all afternoon. (how long, the activity)
  • I've cooked dinner. Come and eat! (completed result)
  • He's been saving money for a new car. (still going on)
  • He's saved $5,000 so far. (how much)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I've been reading three books this month. → ✅ I've read three books this month. (how many: present perfect simple)
  • ❌ Look! I've been finishing my homework. → ✅ Look! I've finished my homework. (A completed result: simple.)

B2 · Upper-intermediate

Repeated actions and temporary situations B2

  • I've been going to bed late recently. (repeated)
  • We've been using my brother's car while ours is being repaired. (temporary)
  • The phone's been ringing all morning. (repeated)
  • My grandparents have lived in this village all their lives. (permanent)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I've always been living in Toronto. → ✅ I've always lived in Toronto. (always → simple)
  • ❌ She's been phoning me twice today. → ✅ She's phoned me twice today. (a number of times: simple)

State verbs and verbs with two meanings B2

VerbState (simple)Action (continuous is OK)
haveI've had this car for years. (own)We've been having problems with it. (experience)
thinkI've always thought he was right. (opinion)I've been thinking about your offer. (considering)
seeI've seen that film.She's been seeing someone new. (dating)
beHe's been ill for a week.He's been being very difficult. (behaving; rare)

  • We've known each other for twenty years. (state: simple)
  • I've wanted a dog since I was a child. (state: simple)
  • I've been thinking of changing jobs. (action: considering)
  • They've been having a lot of fun on holiday. (action: experiencing)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I've been knowing her since 2015. → ✅ I've known her since 2015. (know is a state verb)
  • ❌ She's been owning that shop for years. → ✅ She's owned that shop for years. (own is a state verb)

C1 · Advanced

Subtle differences: temporary, unfinished, annoyed C1

  • Temporary vs permanent: She's been working in sales (maybe for now) / She's worked in sales for twenty years (her career).
  • Unfinished vs complete: I've been reading the report (I'm in the middle) / I've read the report (I can discuss it).
  • Complaint or surprise: Who's been using my laptop? Someone's been eating my chocolate! (I can see signs, and I'm annoyed.)
  • Repeated intention: I've been meaning to call you. (I intended to many times, but didn't.)

  • Someone's been reading my diary! (annoyed: evidence)
  • I've been meaning to tell you something. (repeated intention)
  • I haven't eaten since breakfast. (none at all)
  • I haven't been eating well lately. (a pattern)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I've been meaning tell you. → ✅ I've been meaning to tell you. (mean + to + base verb)
  • ❌ The company has been reducing costs by 15% so far. (a completed result) → ✅ The company has reduced costs by 15% so far. (A completed, measured result: simple.)
