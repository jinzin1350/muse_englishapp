حال کامل استمراریPresent Perfect Continuous (I have been doing)

We use the present perfect continuous (have / has been + -ing) for an activity that started in the past and is still going on, or has just stopped: I've been waiting for an hour. You're wet! Have you been swimming?

It focuses on the activity and how long it has lasted. The present perfect simple (I've done) focuses on the result or how much. This page covers both and the cases where the choice changes the meaning.

B1 · B2 · C1

B1 · متوسط

Form and use: still going on, how long B1

have / has been + -ing for an activity that started in the past and continues now, often with how long, for, since.

FormExample
PositiveI've been learning English for three years.
he / she / itShe's been working since 8 am.
NegativeWe haven't been waiting long.
QuestionHow long have you been living here?
Short answersYes, I have. / No, she hasn't.

The activity started in the past and is still happening: It's been raining all day (and it's still raining). How long have you been learning to drive?

Common time words: for, since, how long, all day, all morning, recently, lately.

She's been = she has been. Don't confuse she's been working (present perfect continuous) with she's working (present continuous, now).

  • I've been studying for this exam for weeks. (still going on)
  • How long has it been snowing? (how long)
  • They've been building that bridge since 2022. (since)
  • We haven't been sleeping well recently. (negative)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I'm waiting for an hour. → ✅ I've been waiting for an hour. (How long up to now: present perfect continuous.)
  • ❌ She has been work here since May. → ✅ She has been working here since May. (have been + -ing)
  • ❌ How long you have been learning English? → ✅ How long have you been learning English? (Question: have + subject + been + -ing.)

An activity that has just stopped: evidence now B1

We use it for an activity that has recently stopped when we can see the result now: You're out of breath. Have you been running?

The activity may have just finished, but we can see or feel its effects now:

  • Your eyes are red. Have you been crying?
  • I'm tired because I've been working in the garden.
  • The ground is wet. It's been raining.

The focus is on the activity (what you have been doing), not on a completed result.

For something that is finished and its completed result, use the simple: I've washed the car. (It's clean.) I've been washing the car. (That's why I'm wet.)

  • Sorry I'm dirty. I've been fixing the car. (evidence: dirty)
  • The kitchen smells of onions. Have you been cooking? (evidence: smell)
  • Why are you so tired? ~ I've been running. (evidence: tired)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ Your eyes are red. Have you been cry? → ✅ Your eyes are red. Have you been crying? (been + -ing)
  • ❌ My hands hurt because I've been dig in the garden. → ✅ My hands hurt because I've been digging in the garden. (been + -ing)

Have been doing or have done? B1

have been doing = the activity, how long, maybe not finished. have done = completed, a result, how many / how much.

Present perfect continuous (activity)Present perfect simple (result)
I've been reading your book. (not finished)I've read your book. (finished)
She's been writing emails all morning. (how long)She's written ten emails. (how many)
They've been painting the kitchen. (that's why it smells)They've painted the kitchen. (it's done)

Ask how long? → usually continuous: How long have you been playing tennis? Ask how many / how much? → simple: How many matches have you played?

With for / since, the continuous is common for activities: I've been learning Persian for two years. With live and work, both are possible: I've lived / 've been living here for ten years.

Don't use the continuous with a number of completed things: I've drunk three coffees. Not I've been drinking three coffees.

  • I've been cooking all afternoon. (how long, the activity)
  • I've cooked dinner. Come and eat! (completed result)
  • He's been saving money for a new car. (still going on)
  • He's saved $5,000 so far. (how much)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I've been reading three books this month. → ✅ I've read three books this month. (how many: present perfect simple)
  • ❌ Look! I've been finishing my homework. → ✅ Look! I've finished my homework. (A completed result: simple.)

تمرین سطح B1

1. I ___ for you since 3 o'clock! Where were you?
Activity up to now: have been waiting.
2. How long ___ Spanish?
How long up to now: present perfect continuous.
3. It ___ (rain) all day. I hope it stops soon.
Still going on: has been raining.
4. Your clothes are covered in paint! What ___?
Evidence now of a recent activity: have you been doing.
5. I'm exhausted. I ___ the house all morning.
The activity explains how I feel now.
6. He's out of breath because he ___ (run).
Evidence now: has been running.
7. I ___ four cups of coffee today.
How many: present perfect simple.
8. She's tired because she ___ all day.
The activity, how long: has been studying.
9. We ___ (paint) the living room. It looks great now!
Completed result: have painted.

B2 · بالاتر از متوسط

Repeated actions and temporary situations B2

The continuous can describe repeated actions over a period (I've been going to the gym a lot) and temporary situations (I've been staying with friends).

The activity doesn't have to be continuous. It can be repeated many times up to now: I've been playing a lot of tennis recently. She's been calling me every day.

It also suggests a temporary situation: I've been staying at my sister's since I came back. (not permanent) Compare: I've lived here all my life. (permanent)

With always, use the simple: I've always lived in Kerman. Not I've always been living.

A single short action in the continuous suggests repetition: He's been kicking the ball against the wall (again and again). He's kicked the ball over the fence (once).

  • I've been going to bed late recently. (repeated)
  • We've been using my brother's car while ours is being repaired. (temporary)
  • The phone's been ringing all morning. (repeated)
  • My grandparents have lived in this village all their lives. (permanent)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I've always been living in Toronto. → ✅ I've always lived in Toronto. (always → simple)
  • ❌ She's been phoning me twice today. → ✅ She's phoned me twice today. (a number of times: simple)

State verbs and verbs with two meanings B2

State verbs use the simple: I've known her for years. Verbs like have, think, see can be continuous only in their action meaning: I've been thinking about it.

Verbs for states (know, believe, like, love, want, own, belong, understand, be) are not normally used in the continuous, even with for or since: I've known him since school. Not I've been knowing him.

Some verbs have a state meaning and an action meaning:

VerbState (simple)Action (continuous is OK)
haveI've had this car for years. (own)We've been having problems with it. (experience)
thinkI've always thought he was right. (opinion)I've been thinking about your offer. (considering)
seeI've seen that film.She's been seeing someone new. (dating)
beHe's been ill for a week.He's been being very difficult. (behaving; rare)

For the full list of state verbs, see Stative Verbs.

  • We've known each other for twenty years. (state: simple)
  • I've wanted a dog since I was a child. (state: simple)
  • I've been thinking of changing jobs. (action: considering)
  • They've been having a lot of fun on holiday. (action: experiencing)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I've been knowing her since 2015. → ✅ I've known her since 2015. (know is a state verb)
  • ❌ She's been owning that shop for years. → ✅ She's owned that shop for years. (own is a state verb)

تمرین سطح B2

10. I ___ a lot of exercise lately. I feel great.
Repeated activity up to now: have been doing.
11. Which sentence suggests a temporary situation?
Continuous + this month = temporary.
12. My neighbour ___ (play) loud music every night this week.
Repeated up to now: continuous.
13. How long ___ your boyfriend?
Know is a state verb: simple.
14. I ___ about what you said, and I think you're right.
Think = considering (action): continuous is fine.
15. She ___ (have) that bike since she was twelve.
Have = own (state): simple.

C1 · پیشرفته

Subtle differences: temporary, unfinished, annoyed C1

The continuous can make a situation sound temporary, unfinished, or annoying: Who's been using my computer? The simple sounds more permanent or complete.

When both forms are grammatical, the continuous adds a nuance:

  • Temporary vs permanent: She's been working in sales (maybe for now) / She's worked in sales for twenty years (her career).
  • Unfinished vs complete: I've been reading the report (I'm in the middle) / I've read the report (I can discuss it).
  • Complaint or surprise: Who's been using my laptop? Someone's been eating my chocolate! (I can see signs, and I'm annoyed.)
  • Repeated intention: I've been meaning to call you. (I intended to many times, but didn't.)

In negative sentences, the simple often focuses on the gap since the last time: I haven't slept for two days (not at all). The continuous describes a pattern: I haven't been sleeping well (badly, recently).

In formal writing and reports, the simple is preferred for completed achievements: The project has reduced costs by 15%. The continuous suits ongoing processes: We have been monitoring the results.

  • Someone's been reading my diary! (annoyed: evidence)
  • I've been meaning to tell you something. (repeated intention)
  • I haven't eaten since breakfast. (none at all)
  • I haven't been eating well lately. (a pattern)

اشتباه‌های رایج:

  • ❌ I've been meaning tell you. → ✅ I've been meaning to tell you. (mean + to + base verb)
  • ❌ The company has been reducing costs by 15% so far. (a completed result) → ✅ The company has reduced costs by 15% so far. (A completed, measured result: simple.)

تمرین سطح C1

16. You find your desk in a mess. You say: Who ___ my things?
Annoyance at evidence: present perfect continuous.
17. Which sentence describes a career, not a temporary job?
The simple + a long period sounds permanent.
18. I ___ (mean) to reply to your email for days. Sorry!
A repeated intention: have been meaning to.
