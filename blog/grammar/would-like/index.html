Would Like, Would Rather and Prefer

A1 · A2 · B1 · B2 · C1

Would like is a polite way to say want: I'd like a glass of water, please. Would rather and prefer say which of two things you want more: I'd rather walk. I prefer tea to coffee.

These phrases are everywhere in daily English: ordering food, making plans and being polite. For would rather + another person (I'd rather you didn't), see Unreal Past.

A1 · Beginner

I'd like…, Would you like…? A1

FormExample
I'd like + nounI'd like a sandwich, please.
I'd like to + verbI'd like to book a table.
Would you like + noun?Would you like some water?
Would you like to + verb?Would you like to come with us?
short answersYes, please. / No, thank you. / Yes, I'd love to.

  • I'd like a coffee, please. (ordering)
  • We'd like to check in. (to + verb)
  • Would you like something to eat? ~ Yes, please. (offer)
  • Would you like to dance? ~ I'd love to! (invitation)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I'd like see the doctor. → ✅ I'd like to see the doctor. (would like + to + verb)
  • ❌ Would you like to a coffee? → ✅ Would you like a coffee? (would like + noun (no to))
  • ❌ I would like to coming. → ✅ I would like to come. (to + base verb)

Practice: A1

1. I'd ___ a cup of tea, please.
I'd like + noun.
2. ___ you like to join us for dinner?
An invitation: Would you like to…?
3. I'd like ___ (book) a room for two nights.
I'd like to + verb.

A2 · Elementary

Like or would like? A2

like (in general)would like (now / a specific wish)
I like coffee. (I enjoy it in general.)I'd like a coffee. (I want one now.)
Do you like dancing? (Is it a hobby?)Would you like to dance? (Now, with me?)
She likes travelling.She'd like to travel to Peru.

  • I like pizza. (in general)
  • I'd like a pizza tonight. (a specific wish)
  • Do you like Italian food? (your taste)
  • Would you like to try this? (an offer)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ Do you like a drink? (an offer) → ✅ Would you like a drink? (an offer: would you like)
  • ❌ I'd like going to the concert. → ✅ I'd like to go to the concert. (would like + to + verb)

I'd rather…: what you prefer A2

  • I'd rather go by train. (preference)
  • She'd rather stay in than go to the party. (rather … than)
  • I'd rather not say. (negative)
  • Would you rather live in the city or the countryside? (question)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I'd rather to stay here. → ✅ I'd rather stay here. (would rather + base verb)
  • ❌ I'd rather walk than taking a taxi. → ✅ I'd rather walk than take a taxi. (base verb after than)
  • ❌ I'd not rather go. → ✅ I'd rather not go. (would rather not)

Practice: A2

4. ___ you like horror films? ~ No, they scare me.
A question about taste in general: Do you like…?
5. It's hot. I'd like ___ a swim.
Would like to + verb.
6. My son ___ (like) playing video games. He plays every day.
In general: likes.
7. I'm tired. I'd rather ___ home.
Would rather + base verb.
8. Would you rather ___ pizza or pasta tonight?
Would rather + base verb.
9. I'd rather ___ (not / drive). It's snowing.
Would rather not + base verb.

B1 · Intermediate

Prefer and would prefer B1

PatternExample
prefer + noun + to + nounI prefer tea to coffee.
prefer + -ing + to + -ingI prefer walking to driving.
prefer to + verb + rather than + verbI prefer to walk rather than drive.
would prefer + nounI'd prefer water, please.
would prefer to + verb (+ rather than)I'd prefer to stay in tonight (rather than go out).

  • My father prefers tea to coffee. (general)
  • I prefer working at home to working in an office. (-ing to -ing)
  • I'd prefer to sit by the window, please. (particular situation)
  • She'd prefer to take a taxi rather than wait for the bus. (rather than)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I prefer coffee than tea. → ✅ I prefer coffee to tea. (prefer … to)
  • ❌ I'd prefer stay at home. → ✅ I'd prefer to stay at home. (would prefer + to + verb)
  • ❌ I prefer swimming to run. → ✅ I prefer swimming to running. (-ing after to)

Practice: B1

10. I prefer summer ___ winter.
Prefer … to.
11. I'd prefer ___ now, if that's OK.
Would prefer to + verb.
12. She prefers cycling ___ (to / drive) to work.
Prefer -ing to -ing.

B2 · Upper-intermediate

I'd like you to…, I'd prefer you to…, I'd prefer it if… B2

PatternExample
would like + person + to + verbI'd like you to meet my parents.
would prefer + person + to + verbI'd prefer you to stay here.
would prefer it if + person + past simpleI'd prefer it if you didn't smoke.
would rather + person + past simpleI'd rather you didn't smoke.

  • I'd like you to finish this by Friday. (what I want you to do)
  • My parents would like me to study medicine. (what they want)
  • I'd prefer you not to mention it. (negative)
  • I'd prefer it if you called before visiting. (polite request)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I'd like that you help me. → ✅ I'd like you to help me. (would like + person + to)
  • ❌ I'd prefer it if you don't tell anyone. → ✅ I'd prefer it if you didn't tell anyone. (prefer it if + past simple)

Practice: B2

13. I'd like ___ my new colleague, Leila.
Would like + person + to + verb.
14. I'd prefer it if you ___ me before you came.
Would prefer it if + past simple.
15. My boss would like me ___ (lead) the new project.
Would like + person + to + verb.

C1 · Advanced

I would have liked to…, I'd rather have… C1

  • would have liked to + base verb: I would have liked to see the museum, but there wasn't time.
  • would like to have + past participle (same meaning, a little more formal): I would like to have met her.
  • would have preferred to + base verb: She would have preferred to stay in a hotel.
  • would rather have + past participle: I'd rather have gone by train. (We didn't.)

  • I would have liked to say goodbye. (I didn't get the chance)
  • We'd have preferred to sit outside, but it was raining. (we didn't)
  • I'd rather have stayed at home. The party was boring. (I didn't)
  • She would like to have studied art. (formal)

Common mistakes:

  • ❌ I would have like to come. → ✅ I would have liked to come. (would have liked)
  • ❌ I'd rather had stayed. → ✅ I'd rather have stayed. (would rather + have + past participle)

Practice: C1

16. The tour was too short. I ___ more of the city.
A past wish that didn't happen: would have liked to see.
17. We went to a noisy club. I'd rather ___ to a quiet restaurant.
Would rather have + past participle.
18. She ___ (would / prefer / take) the train, but all the tickets were sold.
A past preference that didn't happen.
