Verb + -ing or To-Infinitive (want to, enjoy -ing)

Level: A2 · Free grammar guide

When one verb follows another, the second verb is either to + verb or verb-ing. It depends on the first verb: I want to go. I enjoy going.

There is no simple rule, so learn the most common verbs in each group.

Verbs + to-infinitive

want, need, would like, decide, hope, plan, learn, promise, agree, offer, refuse, expect, manage, can't afford

  1. I want to learn Italian. (want to)
  2. We decided to stay at home. (decide to)
  3. She promised to call me. (promise to)

Verbs + -ing

enjoy, finish, don't mind, can't stand, practise, miss, keep, avoid, suggest, imagine, feel like

  1. I enjoy cooking for my friends. (enjoy -ing)
  2. Have you finished eating? (finish -ing)
  3. He keeps asking the same question. (keep -ing)

Verbs with both

After like, love, hate, start, begin and continue, both forms are possible with the same meaning: It started to rain / It started raining. I love to swim / I love swimming.

But after would like, would love and would prefer, use to: I'd love to come. (not I'd love coming)

A few verbs, like remember, stop and try, change their meaning with -ing or to. See Gerund or Infinitive?.

Negatives and prepositions

  • Negative: put not before to or -ing: I decided not to go. I enjoy not having to get up early.
  • After a preposition, always use -ing: I'm good at cooking. Thanks for helping. She's interested in learning Spanish.

More examples

  1. I need to buy some new shoes. (need to)
  2. They're planning to move to Vancouver. (plan to)
  3. Do you mind waiting a few minutes? (mind -ing)
  4. I really miss seeing my friends every day. (miss -ing)
  5. She refused to answer my question. (refuse to)
  6. We can't stand waiting in long queues. (can't stand -ing)
  7. He offered to drive me home. (offer to)

Common mistakes

❌ I want going home.

✅ I want to go home. (Want + to-infinitive.)

❌ I enjoy to swim.

✅ I enjoy swimming. (Enjoy + -ing.)

❌ She decided go to bed.

✅ She decided to go to bed. (Decide + to-infinitive.)

❌ Have you finished to read the book?

✅ Have you finished reading the book? (Finish + -ing.)

❌ I'm good at cook.

✅ I'm good at cooking. (After a preposition, use -ing.)

❌ I decided to not go.

✅ I decided not to go. (Not goes before to.)

❌ We hope seeing you soon.

✅ We hope to see you soon. (Hope + to-infinitive.)
