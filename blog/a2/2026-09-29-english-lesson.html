📝 Grammar: 4A: Obligation and prohibition - have to, must, should

Use **have to** for a general rule or an outside rule. For example, you **have to** wear a hat when the wind is very cold at work. Use **must** for a strong personal rule or a personal decision. For example, I **must** bring water when I walk in the forest. Use **mustn't** for a prohibition, something that is not allowed. You **mustn't** pick the flowers in the park. Do not say **don't have to** for a prohibition. **You don't have to** means something is okay, not a rule. You **don't have to** run in the forest. You can walk.
