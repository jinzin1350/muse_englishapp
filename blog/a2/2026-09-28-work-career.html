Podcast: Work & Career

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Hello and welcome to Word Kitchen! I am Maya.

Lena

And I am Lena. Today we are talking about work and career, a big part of everyday life.

Maya

That is right. Let us start with the most important person at work, your boss. Your boss is the person in charge. My boss is kind and fair. What about your boss, Lena?

Lena

My boss is great too. A good boss listens and helps the team. A bad boss only gives orders. I think a kind boss makes the whole office happy.

Maya

So true. Now, the people you work with are your colleagues. My colleagues are friendly. I eat lunch with my colleagues every day.

Lena

My colleagues are wonderful. One of my colleagues taught me a new skill last week. Good colleagues make work fun.

Maya

Let us talk about money now, your salary. A salary is the money you get every month for your job. She gets a good salary. Lena, does a good salary make you happy at work?

Lena

Of course! A fair salary is important. But a high salary is not everything. My friend has a big salary but works too much.

Maya

Before you get the job, you have the interview. An interview is a meeting where people ask you questions for a job. I have a job interview tomorrow. Wish me luck, Lena!

Lena

Good luck! A job interview can be stressful. In the interview, they ask about your skills. Always smile in your interview.

Maya

Good tip! Then at work you have meetings. A meeting is a time when people come together to talk about work. The meeting starts at ten o'clock. We have a meeting every Monday morning.

Lena

I like short meetings. Long meetings are boring. Our team meeting today was only twenty minutes. That was a great meeting.

Maya

Where do you sit? At your desk, a table where you work. My desk is near the window. I keep my desk clean and tidy.

Lena

My desk is in the corner. Every morning I clean my desk and start the computer. A messy desk makes me nervous, so I like a tidy desk.

Maya

Sometimes work takes longer, and that is overtime. Overtime is work you do after your normal hours. I worked overtime last week. My boss asked for overtime this Friday too.

Lena

Overtime can be tiring. But overtime also means more pay. Do you like working overtime?

Maya

Not really! Now some good news, a promotion. A promotion is a better job with more pay. He got a promotion this year. Lena, do you want a promotion?

Lena

Yes, I do! I work hard for a promotion. A promotion means new skills and a higher salary. My dream is a promotion to team leader.

Maya

Then you meet customers. A customer is a person who buys things or services. The customer was happy. A happy customer comes back.

Lena

Always be kind to the customer. If a customer has a problem, help fast. Good customer service makes the company strong.

Maya

Last word, deadline. A deadline is the last day or time to finish something. The deadline is Friday. Our team finished before the deadline.

Lena

I hate a tight deadline. A clear deadline helps me plan. Let us meet the deadline and celebrate after!

Maya

You know, Lena, I remember my first job. My boss was strict, but my colleagues were so kind. They helped me learn everything at my new desk.

Lena

That is lovely. My first job was hard too. I had a small salary and lots of overtime. But every meeting taught me something new.

Maya

A first job is always a lesson. One day my boss called me to his office. I was nervous. Was it about a promotion, or about a missed deadline?

Lena

Oh no! What happened?

Maya

Good news! He said the customer loved my work, and I got a promotion. My salary grew, and I was so happy.

Lena

That is a wonderful story. Hard work brings a promotion, a better salary, and happy customers. Every interview, every meeting, every deadline is a step forward.

Maya

Wonderful! Let us quickly review: boss, colleague, salary, interview, meeting, desk, overtime, promotion, customer, deadline.

Lena

Ten useful work words. Use them every day and your English will grow. Thanks for listening!

Maya

📝Grammar: Articles - a / an, the, no article

Use **a** or **an** when you talk about one thing for the first time. We say **a boss** or **an interview** when we say them for the first time. Use **an** before a vowel sound. Say **an interview**, not a interview. Also use **a** or **an** to say what something is. For example: **a promotion** is a better job with more pay, and **a deadline** is the last day to finish work. Use **the** when you both know which one you mean. First you say **a desk**, then you say **the desk** because we know which desk. Use **the** with the only one, and with superlatives. For example: **the best salary**
