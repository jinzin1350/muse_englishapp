📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
disrupt /dis-RUPT/
verb
to completely change an industry or market by introducing something new
"Streaming services have disrupted the traditional television industry."
bandwidth /BAND-width/
noun
the mental or organizational capacity to handle tasks or problems
"I don't have the bandwidth to take on another project this week."
gamification /gay-muh-fuh-KAY-shun/
noun
using game-like elements such as points and badges in non-game activities
"The fitness app uses gamification to make daily workouts feel like a fun challenge."
granularity /gran-yuh-LA-ruh-tee/
noun
the level of detail in data or information
"We need more granularity in the sales report to see which products are underperforming."
obfuscate /OB-fuh-skayt/
verb
to deliberately make something unclear or difficult to understand
"The contract was written to obfuscate the real cost of the service."
synergy /SIN-er-jee/
noun
the combined effect of working together, greater than the sum of the parts
"The synergy between the two teams produced their best product ever."
deprecate /DEP-ruh-kayt/
verb
(in technology) to officially discourage the use of an old tool or system while it still works
"The old login system is deprecated; everyone should switch to the new one."
latency /LAY-tun-see/
noun
the delay before data or a response arrives in a system
"High latency makes video calls freeze and lag."
bottleneck /BOT-ul-nek/
noun
a point where progress slows down because of limited capacity
"The approval process is the bottleneck in our release schedule."
iterative /IT-uh-ray-tiv/
adjective
done in repeated small steps, with improvement each time
"The team took an iterative approach, releasing a better version every month."

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome to Word Kitchen, the show where we cook up the words you need for real life. I'm Maya.

Lena

And I'm Lena. Today we're talking tech, the language of modern work. Whether you write code or not, these ten words are everywhere.

Maya

They really are. Tech vocabulary has escaped the office and moved into everyday conversation. So let's master it.

Lena

First word: disrupt. To disrupt means to completely change an industry by introducing something new.

Maya

The classic example is how streaming services disrupted television. Nobody rents DVDs anymore. The whole business model just collapsed.

Lena

Or think about smartphones. One device disrupted cameras, paper maps, alarm clocks, even flashlights.

Maya

And here is the pattern. A small startup disrupts a comfortable market, the giants panic, and within a few years everyone is copying the newcomer.

Lena

That's why investors are always hunting for the next disruptor. They want to fund the company that will disrupt the disruptors.

Maya

There is a personal angle too. Sometimes a new habit disrupts your routine in a good way. My morning run disrupted my habit of scrolling news in bed, and I feel much better for it.

Lena

True. Not every disruption is destructive. The trick is to be the one doing the disrupting, not the one being disrupted.

Maya

Word two: bandwidth. In technology it means data capacity, but in office life it means your mental capacity.

Lena

"I don't have the bandwidth for that right now." It's the polite professional way to say I'm completely overwhelmed.

Maya

Managers love asking about your bandwidth. What they really mean is, can I give you one more project?

Lena

After our big product launch last spring, my bandwidth was gone for a full week. I just stared at my screen.

Maya

That's so real. And here is the lesson. If you don't protect your bandwidth, burnout is not a question of if, it's a question of when.

Lena

I started blocking focus time on my calendar. No meetings before ten. It gave me my bandwidth back.

Maya

Love that. Guard your bandwidth like you guard your money, because once it's spent, it's gone.

Lena

Word three: gamification. That means using game-like elements, points, badges, streaks, leaderboards, in activities that are not games.

Maya

My running app is pure gamification. I ran in the rain last Tuesday just to keep my three month streak alive. The app owns me.

Lena

That's the genius of gamification. It hijacks the part of your brain that loves rewards and progress bars.

Maya

Schools are deep into the gamification of learning now. Kids earn badges for finishing math drills and unlock levels for reading books.

Lena

And companies use gamification for training. Finish the safety course, earn a gold star, climb the leaderboard.

Maya

But there's a dark side. Cheap gamification feels manipulative. If the points are meaningless, people see through it fast.

Lena

Right. Good gamification makes genuine effort feel fun. Bad gamification is just a sticker on a boring task.

Maya

Word four: granularity. Simply put, it's the level of detail in your data or information.

Lena

"We need more granularity in this report." Translation: this summary is too vague. Show me the actual numbers.

Maya

The right granularity depends on the decision. A CEO needs the big picture. An engineer debugging a crash needs the finest detail.

Lena

Exactly. And too much granularity can paralyze a team. You can analyze forever and never decide.

Maya

I learned that the hard way. I once spent two weeks adding granularity to a dashboard nobody ever opened.

Lena

Ouch. So the rule is, zoom in for granularity, then zoom out before you act.

Maya

Word five: obfuscate. A beautiful word that means to deliberately make something unclear or hard to understand.

Lena

Legal contracts are designed to obfuscate the real price. Page forty-seven, paragraph nine, surprise fees.

Maya

My favorite example is corporate language. "We're rightsizing the team" is a fancy way to obfuscate a painful truth: people are losing their jobs.

Lena

Or when a product page says "pricing tailored to your needs." That usually obfuscates the fact that it's very expensive.

Maya

Jargon can obfuscate even a simple idea. If someone can't explain it in plain words, they might be hiding something.

Lena

My personal rule: never obfuscate bad news. Say it clearly, say it early, and people will respect you for it.

Maya

Word six: synergy. The idea that working together creates something bigger than the sum of the parts.

Lena

The synergy between design and engineering is where truly great products are born. One plus one equals three.

Maya

But let's be honest. Synergy is also the most abused buzzword in business. Every merger promises synergy, and very few deliver it.

Lena

Real synergy needs shared goals and honest communication. Without that, it's just a pretty word on a slide.

Maya

Remote work created surprising synergy during the pandemic. Teams collaborated across time zones in ways nobody expected.

Lena

I felt that on our team. The synergy between our writers and our developers made the launch so much stronger.

Maya

Word seven: deprecate. In tech, it means to officially retire an old tool or system while it still technically works.

Lena

"We're going to deprecate the old dashboard next quarter." Translation: stop using it, it's going away.

Maya

Here's the nuance. Deprecated doesn't mean dead. It means unsupported, unmaintained, living on borrowed time.

Lena

When a big platform deprecates a popular feature, developers everywhere panic. Migration is never as smooth as promised.

Maya

The respectful way to deprecate something is slowly. Warn loudly, give people months to adjust, then switch it off.

Lena

Companies that deprecate overnight earn a special kind of hatred from their users. Nobody forgets that.

Maya

Word eight: latency. The delay before data arrives. The silent enemy of every video call.

Lena

High latency is why your colleague freezes mid-sentence on a call. You nod politely at a frozen face. Painful.

Maya

Gamers obsess over latency. In competitive gaming, a delay of a few milliseconds decides who wins and who loses.

Lena

That's why companies spend millions fighting latency. Faster servers, data centers closer to users, cables across the ocean floor.

Maya

There's even social latency. That awkward pause before someone answers a difficult question in a meeting.

Lena

True. Low latency communication, fast clear answers, builds enormous trust in a team.

Maya

Word nine: bottleneck. The narrow point in a process where everything slows down.

Lena

On our team, approvals were the bottleneck. One manager, fifty requests, total gridlock for weeks.

Maya

The tricky thing about a bottleneck is that fixing one often reveals another. It's like squeezing a balloon.

Lena

Before you scale anything, find the bottleneck. Pushing more traffic through a narrow pipe just creates a bigger jam.

Maya

Smart teams remove the bottleneck instead of adding pressure. Automate the slow step, or give it more people.

Lena

I love that mindset. Don't yell at the team. Find the bottleneck and fix the system.

Maya

Final word: iterative. Doing things in small repeated steps, improving a little each time.

Lena

Take an iterative approach to everything. Launch, learn, improve, repeat. That's the whole philosophy.

Maya

Great software is iterative by nature. Nobody ships a perfect product on day one. Version one is always rough.

Lena

I'm iterative with my personal habits too. One small improvement a week beats a giant resolution every January.

Maya

Iterative feedback loops are the real secret. The faster you learn from real users, the faster you improve.

Lena

And being iterative takes the pressure off. You don't need perfection. You just need the next small step.

Maya

Let's do a quick recap, rapid fire. To disrupt is to shake up an entire industry with something new.

Lena

Bandwidth is your mental capacity to take on more work. Protect it.

Maya

Gamification turns ordinary tasks into games with points and badges.

Lena

Granularity is the level of detail in your information. Zoom in, then zoom out.

Maya

To obfuscate is to deliberately make something confusing. Don't do it.

Lena

Synergy is the magic that happens when a team truly works as one.

Maya

To deprecate is to politely retire old technology. Warn first.

Lena

Latency is the delay we all hate, from video calls to slow answers.

Maya

A bottleneck is the narrow point where all progress gets stuck.

Lena

And iterative means improving step by step, forever.

Maya

Here's your homework. This week, use these words in real life. Tell your manager about your bandwidth. Spot the bottleneck in your team's process.

Lena

And when a contract tries to obfuscate the price, or a meeting lacks synergy, you'll have the exact word for it.

Maya

Actually, let's practice right now. Quick role play. I'll be the manager, you be the engineer. Go.

Lena

Okay. So, real talk. I don't have the bandwidth to take on the new dashboard this sprint.

Maya

Hmm. What's the bottleneck? Is it the design work or the data?

Lena

The data. The analytics team won't give me the right granularity. I keep asking for detail and they send me summaries.

Maya

Classic. Tell you what, let's take an iterative approach. Ship a simple version first, then add detail later.

Lena

That works. And can we deprecate the old reporting tool while we're at it? The latency on it is terrible.

Maya

Done. See how natural that sounded? Ten advanced words, one normal conversation.

Lena

That's the goal. Use them until they feel like yours.

Maya

Language is leverage. These ten words will make you sound sharper in any tech conversation.

Lena

That's Word Kitchen for today. I'm Lena.

Maya

And I'm Maya. See you next time.

📝Grammar: First Conditional and Future Time Clauses (when, until, as soon as)

We use the **first conditional** to talk about real future possibilities. Its pattern is simple: **if + present simple, will + verb**. **If we disrupt** the market with this launch, we **will** attract serious investors. The condition is realistic, not imaginary. Future time clauses follow the same logic. After **when**, **until**, **as soon as**, **before**, and **after**, use the **present simple** — never **will**.
