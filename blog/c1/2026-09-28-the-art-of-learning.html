📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
pedagogy /peh-duh-GOH-jee/
noun
the method and practice of teaching
"Her pedagogy mixes short lectures with hands-on projects."
curriculum /kuh-RIK-yuh-lum/
noun
the subjects that make up a course of study
"The new curriculum adds coding classes in every grade."
syllabus /SIH-luh-bus/
noun
an outline of the topics in a course
"The syllabus lists every reading for the semester."
erudite /EH-ruh-dyte/
adjective
showing great knowledge
"The erudite professor could quote ancient texts from memory."
autodidact /aw-toh-dih-DAKT/
noun
a person who teaches themselves
"As an autodidact, she learned three languages online."
mnemonic /nih-MAH-nik/
noun
a trick that helps you remember something
"A simple mnemonic helped him remember the planets in order."
cram /KRAM/
verb
to study a huge amount in a short time
"He crammed all night before the final exam."
flunk /FLUNK/
verb
to fail an exam or course
"She flunked chemistry and had to retake it."
plagiarism /PLAY-juh-ri-zum/
noun
copying someone else's work as your own
"Plagiarism can get you expelled from university."
thesis /THEE-sis/
noun
a long research paper written for a degree
"His thesis on public health won a national prize."

Podcast: The Art of Learning

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Hello, and welcome back to Word Kitchen, the show where we cook up new English vocabulary and serve it with stories. I am Maya.

Lena

And I am Lena. Maya, today we have a very fitting theme for a vocabulary show. We are talking about education and learning itself.

Maya

Exactly. Ten advanced words about how we learn, how we teach, and what can go wrong in between. Our theme today is the art of learning.

Lena

I love that. So let us start with the word pedagogy. Pedagogy means the method and practice of teaching.

Maya

Right. A teacher's pedagogy is basically their whole approach in the classroom. Some teachers lecture nonstop, and that is one kind of pedagogy. Others let students discover things through projects, and that is a totally different pedagogy.

Lena

I had a history teacher whose pedagogy was brilliant. He never just gave us the answers. He would ask a question, let us argue about it, and then reveal the historical twist. His pedagogy turned a boring subject into the best class of the day.

Maya

That is a great example. And modern pedagogy keeps evolving. These days a lot of schools mix short lectures with group work, and researchers study which style of pedagogy actually helps students remember more.

Lena

Our next word is curriculum, which is the set of subjects that make up a course of study. So math, science, literature. All of that together is the curriculum.

Maya

Yes, the curriculum is the big picture of what a school teaches. In my country, the national curriculum changed when I was a teenager. Suddenly computer science became part of the curriculum, and everyone had to learn the basics of coding.

Lena

Same here. And a good curriculum is not just a list of topics. A well-designed curriculum builds skills year after year, so each year connects to the last. If the curriculum is a mess, students feel lost.

Maya

True. And designing a curriculum is genuinely hard work, because the curriculum has to serve every student, from the fastest learner to the one who needs more time.

Lena

Now, sitting under the curriculum is our next word, syllabus. The syllabus is the outline of topics for one specific course.

Maya

Right, so the curriculum is the whole school program, but the syllabus is just for, say, your chemistry class this semester. On the first day, the teacher hands out the syllabus, and you can see every test date and every reading.

Lena

I always read the syllabus like it was a treasure map. The syllabus told me exactly what was coming, so I could plan my semester. A clear syllabus is honestly a gift.

Maya

Totally. And if the syllabus changes halfway through, students get frustrated fast. Nobody likes a syllabus that keeps moving the goalposts. I once had a professor rewrite the syllabus three times, and the whole class complained.

Lena

Let us move to people now. Our next word is erudite, an adjective for someone who shows great knowledge.

Maya

An erudite person is deeply learned. My grandfather was erudite. He could talk about Roman history, astronomy, and classical music all in one dinner conversation.

Lena

I love people like that. An erudite professor can make the most complex idea sound simple. But here is the thing. An erudite person is not just someone with a big brain. The word erudite also suggests curiosity, a real hunger to keep learning.

Maya

Exactly. You can be erudite without being arrogant. The most erudite teacher I ever had was also the kindest person in the building.

Lena

Our next word is the opposite of the formal classroom. An autodidact is a person who teaches themselves.

Maya

Yes. An autodidact learns without a teacher. With the internet, being an autodidact is easier than ever. You can learn photography, programming, or even ancient languages as an autodidact.

Lena

My cousin is a total autodidact. He taught himself guitar, then video editing, then animation. He always says the autodidact life is about discipline, not talent. You have to show up every day.

Maya

That is so true. And many famous inventors were autodidacts. The autodidact path takes patience, but the reward is that you learn exactly what you love, at your own pace.

Lena

Speaking of learning tricks, our next word is mnemonic. A mnemonic is a trick that helps you remember something.

Maya

Like a rhyme or a silly sentence. My favorite mnemonic for the colors of the rainbow was a funny phrase my teacher gave us. A good mnemonic can save you in an exam.

Lena

Mnemonics are powerful. Studies show that a vivid mnemonic sticks in memory far better than plain repetition. If you want to remember a list, invent a mnemonic, make it weird and visual, and it will stay with you for years.

Maya

I use a mnemonic for every new language I learn. Honestly, a clever mnemonic is the difference between remembering and forgetting. My Spanish teacher gave us a mnemonic for irregular verbs, and I still use it today.

Lena

Now for the less glamorous side of student life. To cram means to study a huge amount in a very short time, usually the night before an exam.

Maya

Oh, I have crammed so many times. You drink coffee, you read fifty pages in three hours, and you hope your brain holds it together. To cram is stressful, but almost every student has done it.

Lena

Guilty. When I used to cram, I would tell myself it was a strategy. It was not. Research says that when you cram, you forget almost everything within a week. Still, sometimes you cram because life got busy, and you just do your best.

Maya

Right. A short cram session can work for a quiz, but it is a terrible way to actually learn anything deeply. I would rather study a little every day than cram at the last minute.

Lena

And what happens if the cramming fails? You might flunk. To flunk means to fail an exam or a course.

Maya

It is an informal word, very American. If you flunk a class, you usually have to retake it. I once thought I would flunk statistics, but I got a tutor and passed.

Lena

The fear of flunking can actually motivate people. Nobody wants to flunk, so they study harder. But honestly, to flunk one exam is not the end of the world. Many successful people flunked something along the way.

Maya

True. Failing, or flunking, can teach you how to study better next time. And some of the greatest scientists in history flunked at least one subject at school.

Lena

Our next word is a serious one. Plagiarism is copying someone else's work and presenting it as your own.

Maya

This is a big deal at universities. Plagiarism can get you expelled. I remember a classmate who committed plagiarism on a history paper, and the professor caught it in minutes.

Lena

Schools now use software to detect plagiarism automatically. The rules are strict because plagiarism steals someone's ideas. If you quote a source, cite it. That is how you avoid plagiarism completely.

Maya

And plagiarism is not just copying a whole essay. Even copying a few sentences without credit counts as plagiarism. Journalists have lost their jobs over plagiarism, so it is not just a student problem.

Lena

Finally, our last word is thesis. A thesis is a long research paper you write for a degree, usually at the end of a master's or a doctorate.

Maya

Yes. Writing a thesis is a huge project. My sister wrote her thesis on urban farming, and it took her almost a year. A good thesis makes an original argument and backs it up with evidence.

Lena

The hardest part of a thesis is choosing the topic. Once you pick your thesis question, everything follows. And when you finish your thesis, you defend it in front of professors, which is terrifying and thrilling at the same time.

Maya

A finished thesis is something to be proud of. It proves you can think independently. My friend cried happy tears the day her thesis was approved.

Lena

You know, hearing all these words together makes me think of my own school days. The whole education journey, from a child's first syllabus to a graduate's final thesis.

Maya

Mine too. I remember the exact moment I decided I loved learning. My fifth-grade teacher had this warm pedagogy where every lesson started with a riddle. The curriculum did not excite me, but her way of teaching did.

Lena

That is the magic of a great teacher. And I will admit something. I once tried to be an autodidact with the piano. I bought a keyboard, watched videos online, and lasted exactly two weeks. The autodidact dream died fast.

Maya

Some subjects need a guide. But I did teach myself to cook as an autodidact, one burnt dinner at a time. No syllabus, no curriculum, just trial and error.

Lena

And now you are erudite in the kitchen. See, it all connects. Whether you cram for finals or write a careful thesis, whether you invent a mnemonic or fear you will flunk, learning is one long adventure.

Maya

Beautifully said. Just promise me one thing. No plagiarism on your next piano attempt.

Lena

Let us recap all ten words from today's Word Kitchen on the art of learning. Pedagogy, the method and practice of teaching. Curriculum, the subjects that make up a course of study. Syllabus, the outline of topics for one course. Erudite, showing great knowledge. Autodidact, a self-taught person. Mnemonic, a trick that helps you remember. Cram, to study intensely at the last minute. Flunk, to fail an exam. Plagiarism, copying someone's work as your own. And thesis, a long research paper for a degree.

Maya

Use them this week. Read your syllabus with fresh eyes, invent a mnemonic for something tricky, and please, do not plagiarize, and do not cram the night before an exam. Thanks for joining us on Word Kitchen.

Lena

See you next time.

📝Grammar: Articles - a / an, the, no article

Use **a / an** with a singular countable noun when you say what something is or mention it for the first time. Words like *autodidact*, *thesis*, or *mnemonic* follow this pattern: first *a thesis*, then *the thesis*. Switch to **the** once the thing is known to both speakers, usually because you already mentioned it. So *a syllabus* on first mention becomes *the syllabus* afterwards. **The** also marks the only one of its kind and goes before superlatives.
