📚 Vocabulary

7 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
BMI /bee-em-EYE/
noun
Body Mass Index, a measure of body fat.
"Doctors use BMI to assess a person's healthy weight range."
brisk /BRISK/
adjective
Quick and energetic.
"We took a brisk walk to warm up."
burn calories /BURN KAL-uh-reez/
phrase
To use energy from food through physical activity.
"Running is an excellent way to burn calories quickly."
cardiovascular /kar-dee-oh-VAS-kyuh-ler/
adjective
Relating to the heart and blood vessels.
"Regular exercise improves cardiovascular health significantly."
cholesterol-rich /koh-LES-tuh-rol RICH/
adjective
Containing a high amount of cholesterol.
"Avoid cholesterol-rich foods for a healthier diet."
clog /KLOG/
verb
To block something, preventing movement or flow.
"Leaves can clog the drains during autumn storms."
diabetes /dy-uh-BEE-teez/
noun
A medical condition affecting blood sugar levels.
"Regular exercise helps manage diabetes effectively."

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I'm Maya, and this is the show where we build your English one great conversation at a time.

Lena

And I'm Lena. Maya, I started a new fitness routine this week, and I have to tell you all about it.

Maya

I love that. What did you do first?

Lena

I started simple. Every morning I take a brisk walk around the park before work. Brisk means quick and energetic, so I'm not strolling slowly, I'm really moving my legs.

Maya

A brisk walk is a perfect start. When you walk at a brisk pace, you burn calories without even noticing how hard you are working.

Lena

Exactly. I wanted to burn calories in a way that felt good, not punishing. A brisk walk for thirty minutes helps me burn calories, clear my head, and wake up properly.

Maya

That's smart. So many people try to burn calories with extreme workouts and give up after a week. A brisk daily walk is something you can actually keep doing.

Lena

That's what my doctor said too. She checked my BMI at my last appointment. BMI stands for Body Mass Index, a measure of body fat, and she uses BMI to see whether my weight is in a healthy range.

Maya

And what did she say about your BMI?

Lena

She said my BMI is fine, but she wants me to keep moving, because regular movement protects my cardiovascular health. Cardiovascular means relating to the heart and blood vessels, so cardiovascular exercise is anything that makes your heart work a little harder.

Maya

Before we go on, a quick word about the app that goes with this show.

Lena

Oh yes, if you are enjoying Muse English, you will love the Muse English app. It gives you daily words with photos and pronunciation, this podcast, shadowing stories, quizzes, grammar lessons, and a leaderboard where you earn points. It is free to install, so grab it today and keep learning with us every single day.

Maya

Like a brisk walk, a swim, or a bike ride. All of those are good for your cardiovascular system.

Lena

Right. She explained that good cardiovascular health lowers the risk of serious problems, including diabetes. Diabetes is a medical condition affecting blood sugar levels, and regular exercise helps manage diabetes effectively.

Maya

That's such an important point. People think diabetes is only about sugar, but movement matters just as much as diet.

Lena

She also talked about food. She said I should avoid cholesterol-rich foods most of the time. Cholesterol-rich means containing a high amount of cholesterol, and too much cholesterol can clog your arteries over time.

Maya

Clog is such a useful word. To clog something means to block it, preventing movement or flow. So fatty food can clog your blood vessels, just like leaves can clog a drain.

Lena

That's exactly the picture she painted. She said cholesterol-rich meals can slowly clog the arteries, and that makes the heart work harder. So now I read labels and I skip the most cholesterol-rich options.

Maya

Great tip. Now, Lena, tell me more about your routine. Do you only walk, or do you do other exercise too?

Lena

I walk every morning, and three evenings a week I go to the gym. At the gym I do a mix of cardio and light weights. The cardio part is great for my cardiovascular fitness, and the weights help me stay strong.

Maya

Do you track anything, like your BMI or how many calories you burn?

Lena

I check my BMI once a month, just to see the trend. I don't obsess over it, because BMI is only one measure, but it helps me notice changes. And my watch tells me roughly how many calories I burn in a workout, which is motivating.

Maya

I like that approach. When you burn calories through exercise you enjoy, it never feels like a punishment.

Lena

True. And I learned that to burn calories efficiently, short bursts of effort help. So sometimes during my brisk walk I add two minutes of faster walking, then slow down again.

Maya

Interval walking. That's a lovely way to make a brisk walk even better for your cardiovascular health.

Lena

It really is. My heart rate goes up, my breathing gets deeper, and afterwards I feel fantastic. My doctor says that kind of regular cardiovascular effort is one of the best defenses against diabetes.

Maya

And diet is the other half, right?

Lena

Absolutely. I still enjoy good food, but I cut back on cholesterol-rich dishes. Instead of fried food every day, I eat grilled fish, vegetables, and whole grains. Those foods don't clog your arteries the way heavy fried meals can.

Maya

That's a great image to remember. Anything that can clog a pipe can clog an artery, so choose food that keeps things flowing.

Lena

Exactly. And here's something interesting: my uncle has diabetes, and his doctor gave him the same advice. Regular exercise helps manage diabetes, and a brisk walk after meals is one of the simplest tools he has.

Maya

A brisk walk after meals is brilliant. It helps the body use sugar better, which is exactly what someone managing diabetes needs.

Lena

He told me his blood sugar readings improved within a month. He still takes his medicine, of course, but the walking made a real difference. He also watches his BMI, because carrying extra weight makes diabetes harder to control.

Maya

So BMI, brisk walking, burning calories, cardiovascular health, and diabetes all connect. It's one whole system.

Lena

It is. And the food side connects too. When you eat fewer cholesterol-rich foods, you are less likely to clog your arteries, and your cardiovascular system stays healthier for longer.

Lena

Exactly. Before we move on, let me remind you about something that will make all of this English stick.

Maya

Go on.

Lena

The Muse English app is free, and it is the perfect partner for this podcast. You get new words every day with photos and pronunciation audio, shadowing stories to practise speaking, quizzes to test yourself, grammar lessons, and points on the leaderboard as you improve. Download it free and make English part of your daily routine, just like a brisk walk.

Maya

I want to ask about motivation, because starting is easy and continuing is hard. How do you stay consistent?

Lena

I made it social. My neighbour joins me for the brisk morning walk, so we chat while we burn calories together. Having company makes it fun, and I never want to let her down.

Maya

That's clever. And do you ever check your progress?

Lena

Once a month I check my BMI, and I notice how much easier the brisk walk feels. At first I was out of breath; now I can talk the whole way. That tells me my cardiovascular fitness is improving.

Maya

That is real progress. Your heart is getting stronger, and your body is learning to burn calories more efficiently.

Lena

And my neighbour noticed the same thing. She said her doctor praised her cardiovascular health at her last checkup, and she no longer worries so much about diabetes because her whole family walks together now.

Maya

Families that walk together stay healthy together. I love that.

Lena

Me too. And we talk about food on our walks. We swap recipes that are not cholesterol-rich, and we remind each other that heavy, greasy meals can clog arteries over time, so we save those for special occasions.

Maya

Balance, not perfection. That's the secret.

Maya

One more thing I love about your routine is how simple it is. No fancy equipment, no expensive membership, just a brisk walk and a bit of common sense about food.

Lena

That's true. People think they need a personal trainer to improve their cardiovascular health, but a brisk walk costs nothing. And when you burn calories every day, even gently, the results add up fast.

Maya

And your story shows that small habits protect you from big problems. Regular walking helps manage diabetes, keeps your BMI steady, and stops fatty food from clogging your arteries.

Lena

Exactly. I tell everyone now: skip one cholesterol-rich takeaway a week, take a brisk walk instead, and watch what happens. Your heart, your weight, and your English all improve at the same time.

Maya

I love that. A brisk walk for your body and new words for your mind. That's a perfect daily routine.

Maya

Beautifully said. Now, Lena, give our listeners one final challenge for this week.

Lena

Here it is. Take a brisk walk every day this week, even just twenty minutes. Notice how you burn calories, how your heart feels, and how much better you sleep. If you know your BMI, check it once a month, not every day. Eat one less cholesterol-rich meal this week, and remember that small choices keep your arteries clear instead of letting them clog up.

Maya

And remember, regular movement protects your cardiovascular health and helps prevent and manage diabetes. Your heart will thank you.

Lena

It will. And your English will thank you too, because today you learned seven powerful words: BMI, brisk, burn calories, cardiovascular, cholesterol-rich, clog, and diabetes.

Maya

Use them this week. Tell a friend about your brisk walk, talk about how you burn calories, and explain what cardiovascular means. That's how words become yours.

Lena

And remember, every brisk walk is a small victory for your heart. Keep your BMI in check, enjoy food that does not clog your arteries, and stay active.

Lena

Thanks for listening, everyone. Keep moving, keep learning, and we'll see you tomorrow on Muse English.

Maya

Bye for now, and happy walking.

📝Grammar: Non-Defining Relative Clauses

A **non-defining relative clause** adds extra information about someone or something we have already identified. We put the clause between **commas**. The sentence still makes sense without it. Use **who** for people, **which** for things, **whose** for possession, and **where** for places. Never use **that** in a non-defining clause. You also cannot leave the pronoun out. **Which** can refer to a whole idea. After a quantifier, use **of whom** for people or **of which** for things. Writers use these clauses to add colour.
