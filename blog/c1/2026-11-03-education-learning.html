📚 Vocabulary

7 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
academic journal /ak-uh-DEM-ik JUR-nul/
noun phrase
A periodical containing scholarly articles on a specific subject.
"She published her research in a prestigious academic journal."
access online /AK-ses ON-line/
verb phrase
To get information or services using the internet.
"Students can access course materials online."
adjoining /uh-JOY-ning/
adjective
Next to or in contact with.
"The library is in the adjoining building."
aforementioned /uh-FOR-men-shund/
adjective
Referring to something previously mentioned.
"The aforementioned study highlights key findings."
articulate (adj.) /ar-TIK-yuh-lit/
adjective
Expressing ideas clearly and fluently.
"She is a highly articulate speaker, always clear and concise."
assess /uh-SES/
verb
To evaluate or estimate the nature, ability, or quality.
"Teachers assess students' progress through various assignments."
assignment /uh-SYN-munt/
noun
A piece of work or task given to students.
"Students must submit their history assignment by Friday."

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I am Maya, and with me as always is my friend Lena.

Lena

Hi everyone! Today we are talking about something every student knows very well, the world of university study, research, and big writing tasks.

Maya

That is right. Imagine you are a student, and your professor gives you a new assignment. An assignment is a piece of work your teacher asks you to complete, often an essay or a project. And this assignment is not a small one. It asks you to read real research and to write about it in your own words.

Lena

That sounds serious. Where do you even start with an assignment like that?

Maya

You start in the library. And here is our first useful word, academic journal. An academic journal is a magazine for researchers. It publishes serious articles written by experts, and students read it when they want trusted information. If your assignment is about climate, health, or history, an academic journal is one of the best places to look.

Lena

So an academic journal is not like a fashion magazine or a sports magazine. It is written by experts, for experts and students.

Maya

Exactly. And today, you do not need to sit in a dusty room to read one. You can access online versions of almost every academic journal. To access online means to reach information through the internet. So you can access online articles from your laptop, from your phone, or from a computer in the library.

Lena

I love that. I can access online articles while I drink my morning coffee, and I can save the best ones for later.

Maya

Yes, but there is a skill here too. When you access online sources, you must choose carefully. Not every website is trustworthy. That brings us to another important word, assess. To assess means to judge the quality of something. Teachers assess student work, and good students assess their sources before they use them.

Lena

So before I use an article in my assignment, I should assess it. I should ask who wrote it, when it was written, and whether the evidence is strong.

Maya

Perfect. That is exactly how you assess a source. And when you write, you need to sound clear. That is where our next word comes in, articulate. An articulate speaker expresses ideas clearly and smoothly. If you are articulate, people understand you easily, because your sentences are clean and your ideas follow a logical order.

Lena

I want to be articulate in my presentations. Sometimes I know the answer, but my words come out in a mess. Being articulate is a skill I can practise.

Maya

It is, and your assignment is a great place to practise it. An articulate essay does not use the fanciest words. It uses clear words in a clear order. Now, before we go further, let me tell you about something that can help you every single day.

Lena

If you are enjoying this episode, let me tell you about the Muse English app. It is completely free, and it gives you new words every day, each one with a photo and a clear pronunciation, plus this podcast, fun shadowing stories, quick quizzes, grammar lessons, and a leaderboard where you can collect points. Download Muse English today, and turn the minutes you spend on your phone into real progress in English.

Maya

Thanks, Lena. Now, back to our student and her assignment. She has found an academic journal, and she has started to assess the articles. But where is she working?

Lena

Good question. Is she at home, or in the library?

Maya

She is working in the library, and here is a lovely word for places, adjoining. Adjoining means next to, or joined to, something else. The library is in the adjoining building, right next to the main hall. So if someone asks where the library is, you can say it is in the adjoining building.

Lena

That is a useful word for giving directions. The cafe is in the adjoining room. My classroom is in the adjoining building. It is such a simple word, and it makes you sound so natural.

Maya

It does. And inside that adjoining building, our student finds a quiet reading room. She opens another academic journal, and she reads an article about ocean pollution. She wants to assess the study carefully, so she reads the introduction, the method, and the conclusion.

Lena

That sounds like a lot of reading. How does she keep track of everything?

Maya

She takes notes, and she uses a very formal word when she writes, aforementioned. Aforementioned means mentioned before, earlier in the same text. So she can write, the aforementioned study shows that plastic harms sea animals. The aforementioned study is the study she already talked about in the paragraph above.

Lena

Oh, I see. The aforementioned article, the aforementioned professor, the aforementioned result. It is a formal word, but it is very common in academic writing.

Maya

Exactly. And it appears in almost every academic journal. Writers use the aforementioned results to refer back to something without repeating the whole description. It keeps academic writing short and precise.

Lena

So far we have an assignment, an academic journal, a way to access online articles, a way to assess sources, a goal to be articulate, a library in an adjoining building, and the word aforementioned for things already mentioned. That is a full toolkit for a student.

Maya

It is. And here is the best part. When you learn words in a story like this, you remember them much longer than when you just read a list. Your brain connects the assignment with the library, the academic journal with the research, and the aforementioned study with the notes you took.

Lena

I agree. Let me give you an example from my own life. Last year, I had an assignment about healthy eating. I went to the library in the adjoining building, and I asked the librarian to help me access online journals. She showed me how to access online databases, and I found three articles in one academic journal. Then I had to assess each article, and that took time, because I wanted only strong evidence.

Maya

That is a perfect example. And how did you write it up?

Lena

I tried to be articulate. I wrote short paragraphs, and I used the aforementioned articles to support my points. My teacher said my writing was clear and articulate, and that was the best compliment I could have asked for.

Maya

Wonderful. And what happened to your assignment in the end?

Lena

I got a very good grade, and more importantly, I learned how to assess information for myself. Now, whenever I read news online, I assess the source before I believe it. That skill came straight from one assignment.

Maya

That is the power of learning by doing. Quick pause here, because I want to remind you about the Muse English app. Every day it brings you fresh words with photos and pronunciation audio, this podcast, shadowing stories to speak along with, quizzes, grammar lessons, and points and a leaderboard to keep you motivated. It is free and fun, and it is waiting for you. Install Muse English, and make English part of your day.

Lena

Thanks, Maya. Now, let me share a few practical tips for your next assignment. First, start early, so you have time to access online sources and to assess them properly. Second, read at least one academic journal article, not just blogs or short summaries. Third, write your draft in articulate sentences, and read it aloud to check the flow.

Maya

And fourth, use the aforementioned trick in your writing. When you mention a study once, you can call it the aforementioned study later, and your writing will sound much more academic.

Lena

Great tips. Let me add one more. If your university has a library in an adjoining building, go there. A quiet room, a good chair, and no distractions can change the way you study.

Maya

One more idea. When you assess an academic journal, look at the date first. A study from twenty years ago can still be useful, but newer research often matters more for your assignment.

Lena

That is true. And when you access online databases, save the best articles in folders, one folder for each assignment, so you never lose the aforementioned sources when you start writing.

Maya

Good system. And read your draft aloud. If a sentence sounds confusing, rewrite it until it sounds articulate. Clear sentences make a strong assignment, and they show the reader that you really understand the topic.

Lena

I do that too. I also ask a friend to read my assignment, because a second pair of eyes can assess the argument better than I can. If my friend cannot follow my ideas, I know my writing is not articulate enough yet.

Maya

Exactly. An articulate draft, solid sources from a trusted academic journal, and careful notes on the aforementioned studies. That is how a good assignment comes together.

Maya

Before we finish, let us quickly review our seven words together. An assignment is a task your teacher gives you. An academic journal publishes serious research articles. To access online means to reach information through the internet. To assess means to judge quality. Articulate means clear and fluent in speaking or writing. Adjoining means next to something. And aforementioned means mentioned before.

Lena

Beautiful recap. If you remember these seven words, your next assignment will feel much easier, and your academic reading will feel much more natural.

Maya

And if you want to practise them even more, listen to today's shadowing story, and try the quiz in the app.

Thanks for listening to Muse English. Keep learning, keep practising, and we will see you tomorrow with seven brand new words. Goodbye!

📝Grammar: Probability: Bound To, May Well, Likely To

English grades probability on a scale, from near certainty to impossibility. At the top sit **bound to**, **sure to**, and **certain to**: a well-prepared student is **bound to** pass, and a strong assignment is **sure to** impress its reader. One step down, for things that are probable, use **likely to**, **may well**, **there's a good chance**, **in all likelihood**, or **the chances are**. An articulate essay, for example, **may well** earn a higher mark than a rushed one. For mere possibility, use plain **may**, **might**, or **could**, or **there's a chance**.
