📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
itinerary /ih-TIN-uh-reh-ree/
noun
a detailed plan of a journey
"Their ambitious itinerary covered four countries in just one week."
sojourn /SOH-jurn/
noun
a temporary stay in a place
"His brief sojourn in Lisbon inspired a whole collection of poems."
detour /DEE-toor/
noun
a longer way around to avoid something
"A road closure forced us to take a lengthy detour through the countryside."
expedition /eks-puh-DIH-shun/
noun
an organized journey with a purpose
"The expedition set out to document wildlife in the remote rainforest."
caravan /KA-ruh-van/
noun
a group of travelers journeying together
"Centuries ago, a caravan of merchants carried silk across the desert."
wayfarer /WAY-feh-ruh/
noun
a traveler, usually on foot
"The weary wayfarer finally reached the mountain shelter at dusk."
odyssey /AH-duh-see/
noun
a long adventurous journey
"Her odyssey from a small village to the capital became a famous memoir."
excursion /eks-KUR-zhun/
noun
a short trip for pleasure
"The school arranged an excursion to the ancient coastal ruins."
traverse /truh-VERS/
verb
to travel across or through
"They traversed the entire glacier in less than six hours."
disembark /dis-em-BARK/
verb
to leave a ship, plane, or train
"Passengers began to disembark as soon as the ferry reached the dock."

Podcast: Travel & Transport

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome back to Word Kitchen, the show where we cook up English vocabulary one delicious conversation at a time. I am Maya.

Lena

And I am Lena. And Maya, today we are doing something a little different. We are going on a journey.

Maya

A journey! I love that. Not just a quick flight somewhere, but a real adventure. What do you call the biggest, most life changing kind of journey, Lena?

Lena

An odyssey. An odyssey is a long journey full of adventures, surprises, and stories. Every odyssey changes the person who takes it.

Maya

That is the perfect word to start with. I think everyone deserves one great odyssey in their life. My cousin's odyssey across South America lasted almost eleven months. She came back a completely different person.

Lena

An odyssey like that stays with you forever. Every letter she sent home was another chapter of that odyssey. I still get goosebumps thinking about it.

Maya

But here is the thing, Lena. Even the wildest odyssey needs some kind of plan. You cannot cross a continent with zero preparation.

Lena

True. You need an itinerary. An itinerary is a detailed plan of your journey. It tells you where you will sleep, what train you will take, and what you will see each day.

Maya

My last itinerary was a spreadsheet with twelve tabs. Honestly, writing that itinerary took longer than packing.

Lena

That might be too much itinerary. An itinerary should guide you, not control you. The best travelers write a loose itinerary and leave space for magic.

Maya

Exactly. My friend's itinerary fell apart on day two of her trip, and she says that broken itinerary gave her the best week of her life. So respect the itinerary, but do not worship it.

Lena

Good advice. Now, what happens when the plan breaks? That is when you meet our next word.

Maya

Let me guess. A detour?

Lena

Exactly. A detour is a longer way around something, usually because the direct road is blocked. And a detour is secretly a gift.

Maya

I agree. On my mountain road trip, a landslide forced us onto a detour that added three hours. That detour became the most beautiful drive of the entire trip.

Lena

See? The detour always delivers. A detour feels like a problem at first, but later it becomes your favorite story. Never be afraid of a detour.

Maya

I tell my students the same thing about life. When life gives you a detour, take it with curiosity.

Lena

Now let us go bigger. What about a journey with a real mission? That is an expedition.

Maya

An expedition is an organized journey with a purpose. Not a vacation, an expedition. Scientists, filmmakers, explorers.

Lena

Did you see that documentary about the expedition to Antarctica? That expedition spent two years preparing. Every person on that expedition had to learn survival skills.

Maya

Incredible. An expedition like that tests everything you have. I could never join a polar expedition, but a desert expedition? Maybe.

Lena

Speaking of deserts, long before airplanes, traders crossed deserts in a caravan.

Maya

A caravan! A group of travelers moving together. Picture a caravan of camels carrying silk and spices. That image is pure romance.

Lena

It was practical too. One traveler alone could get lost or robbed, but a caravan protected everyone. Safety in numbers.

Maya

And at night the whole caravan would stop and make camp. I can see the fires, smell the cooking, hear the stories. The caravan was a moving village.

Lena

The great silk road was crossed by caravan for centuries. Every caravan carried not just goods, but ideas, languages, and recipes.

Maya

But not everyone traveled in a group. Some walked alone. And the old word for a lonely traveler is wayfarer.

Lena

Wayfarer. Such a beautiful, old fashioned word. A wayfarer is a traveler, often on foot, walking from place to place.

Maya

Picture a tired wayfarer arriving at a small inn at sunset. That wayfarer has seen things no tourist ever sees.

Lena

I am a little jealous of the wayfarer. No schedule, no itinerary, just walking. The wayfarer's journey is the slowest kind of odyssey.

Maya

And the deepest. When you walk, you notice everything. A wayfarer might spend a whole month just crossing one valley.

Lena

Every wayfarer has a story worth hearing. If you ever meet a wayfarer, buy them dinner and listen.

Maya

Now, what if you stop walking for a while? That is a sojourn.

Lena

A sojourn is a temporary stay in one place during your travels. My friend's sojourn in Kyoto lasted two months.

Maya

And during that sojourn, she learned the tea ceremony, made local friends, and basically became a different person. A sojourn lets a place change you.

Lena

My dream is a sojourn in a small Italian village. One long, delicious sojourn learning to cook from a grandmother who speaks no English.

Maya

A sojourn is the opposite of rushing. If an odyssey is the whole book, a sojourn is your favorite chapter.

Lena

Now for something smaller. What about a short trip, just for fun? That is an excursion.

Maya

An excursion is a short trip, usually for pleasure. The hotel offered a free excursion to the coast, so we joined the morning excursion.

Lena

Excursions are perfect for tasting a place. I took an excursion to the old town last spring, and that little excursion convinced me to return for a full sojourn.

Maya

See the connection? An excursion can lead to a sojourn, and a sojourn can become part of a lifelong odyssey.

Lena

Let us talk about the hard part of travel. Sometimes you must traverse difficult land to reach your goal.

Maya

Traverse means to travel across or through something. Explorers had to traverse a mountain pass in a snowstorm.

Lena

To traverse that pass took four days. Every step to traverse the ice was dangerous. But once you traverse something hard, the pride lasts forever.

Maya

I once helped traverse a desert in an old jeep with friends. We did not walk it, but to traverse all that emptiness still felt like a real achievement.

Lena

And then, after every sea journey, comes the moment when you disembark.

Maya

Disembark means to leave a ship, an airplane, or a train. After three days at sea, we finally got to disembark.

Lena

The passengers began to disembark at dawn. When you disembark in a new country, even the air smells different. To disembark is to begin a new chapter.

Maya

I love that moment. You disembark, you step onto the dock, and your odyssey officially begins.

Lena

Or ends. We got a letter from a listener, Sara, whose grandmother's odyssey ended when she had to disembark in a port she had never heard of.

Maya

And that forced disembark became a sojourn that lasted forty years. She built a whole life there. What a story.

Lena

Sara says her grandmother always told her, the itinerary is just paper, but the detour is where life happens.

Maya

I want to try a little game with these words, Lena. I will describe a trip, and you tell me which words fit.

Lena

I love games. Go.

Maya

Okay. You plan a month in Portugal. You write down every hotel and train.

Lena

That is an itinerary, obviously. A very organized itinerary.

Maya

Halfway through, the railway workers go on strike, so you rent a car and drive along the coast instead.

Lena

A detour! The classic forced detour that becomes the highlight.

Maya

You fall in love with a fishing village and stay for three weeks, renting a room above a bakery.

Lena

That is a sojourn. A perfect, unplanned sojourn.

Maya

From there you join a group of hikers walking the old pilgrim road to the border.

Lena

Now you are part of a caravan. Or are you a wayfarer? Hmm.

Maya

Both, in a way. A wayfarer at heart, but walking with a caravan for safety. And the whole month together?

Lena

An odyssey. A small, personal odyssey. I win.

Maya

You do. And that is the point, really. These words are not just vocabulary. They are ten different ways to travel, from the careful itinerary to the brave traverse of unknown land.

Lena

And every traveler will disembark somewhere new one day, and take an excursion they will never forget.

Maya

That is beautiful. Let us do a quick recap of our ten words. Number one: itinerary.

Lena

An itinerary is a detailed plan of a journey. Write your itinerary, but keep it flexible.

Maya

Sojourn. A temporary stay in one place. Take a sojourn somewhere and let it change you.

Lena

Detour. A longer way around. Embrace the detour.

Maya

Expedition. An organized journey with a purpose. Maybe one day we will join an expedition.

Lena

Caravan. A group traveling together. The old trade routes were crossed by caravan.

Maya

Wayfarer. A traveler, often on foot and alone. Every wayfarer carries a story.

Lena

Odyssey. A long adventurous journey. Go find your own odyssey.

Maya

Excursion. A short trip for fun. Take an excursion this weekend.

Lena

Traverse. To travel across something difficult. Dare to traverse the hard road.

Maya

And disembark. To leave a ship, plane, or train. One day, disembark somewhere brand new.

Lena

Ten words, ten doors into the world of travel. Which one is your favorite, listeners? Tell us in the comments.

Maya

Thanks for wandering with us on Word Kitchen. I am Maya.

Lena

And I am Lena. Until next time, keep your itinerary loose, love your detours, and never stop moving.

📝Grammar: Choosing between gerunds and infinitives

A gerund is a verb ending in **-ing** that acts like a noun. An infinitive is **to** plus the base verb, as in **to disembark** or **to traverse**. Use the **gerund after prepositions** and after verbs like **enjoy, avoid, suggest, finish**. She suggested **extending** her sojourn; they finished **planning** the expedition. Use the **infinitive after adjectives** and to express **purpose**. It is also required after verbs like **want, decide, hope, learn**. He decided **to traverse** the valley on foot. Some verbs change meaning with each form. **Remember to disembark** means do not forget;
