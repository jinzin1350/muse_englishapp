📚 Vocabulary

7 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
appease /uh-PEEZ/
verb
To pacify someone by granting their demands.
"He tried to appease his angry boss with an apology."
astute /uh-STOOT/
adjective
Having an ability to accurately assess situations or people.
"An astute businessman, he quickly spotted the market opportunity."
averse /uh-VURS/
adjective
Having a strong dislike or opposition to something.
"He is not averse to taking risks."
aversion /uh-VUR-zhun/
noun
Strong dislike or feeling of repugnance towards something.
"She has a strong aversion to public speaking."
bite your nails /BYTE yor NAYLZ/
idiom
To chew on one's fingernails, usually from nervousness.
"She tends to bite her nails when she's stressed."
bliss /BLIS/
noun
Perfect happiness or joy.
"Lying on the beach was pure bliss."
blissful /BLIS-ful/
adjective
Extremely happy; full of joy.
"They spent a blissful honeymoon in the Maldives."

Lena

That is an astute move, honestly. An astute person reads a situation quickly and accurately, and Sara is astute. She notices that the angry employee is not really upset about money. He is upset because nobody listened to him.

Exactly. An astute leader listens first. Sara is astute enough to see that the whole team is anxious, so she holds a short meeting where everyone can speak honestly. Her astute reading of the room changes the mood completely.

Lena

And here is the interesting part. Sara herself is not averse to hard conversations. To be averse to something means to strongly dislike it or oppose it, but Sara is not averse to conflict when it is honest and respectful.

Maya

She is not averse to bad news either. Many managers are averse to criticism, but Sara welcomes it, because she knows that avoiding criticism only makes problems bigger. Being averse to feedback is a weakness, and Sara refuses to have it.

Lena

That reminds me of my own aversion to public speaking. An aversion is a strong dislike, almost a feeling of disgust, and my aversion to speaking in front of a crowd used to keep me quiet in meetings.

Maya

I understand that aversion completely. A lot of people share your aversion to public speaking, and the funny thing is, the more you avoid it, the stronger the aversion grows.

Lena

True. My aversion started to fade only when I forced myself to speak up in small meetings. Facing an aversion, instead of feeding it, is the only way to shrink it.

That is such a good lesson. Now, before our happier story, let me share one more work story, because it shows a different kind of character. My friend Daniel went for a big job interview last month, and he was terrified.

Lena

That is brave. Most people are averse to admitting any weakness in an interview. They are averse to honesty because they fear it will cost them the job.

But his honesty worked. The interviewer smiled and said his aversion to routine was actually a good sign for a creative role. Daniel walked out feeling something close to bliss, and when the offer came, it felt like a blissful ending to a stressful month.

I love that story. He faced his nerves, he did not bite his nails through the whole interview, and he turned an aversion into a strength. That is character.

Now, let us switch to a happier story, because our next words are all about joy. My cousin got married last summer, and the whole day was pure bliss.

Lena

Oh, tell me more. Bliss means perfect happiness, right?

Maya

Yes, bliss is perfect happiness, the kind that makes you forget everything else. The ceremony was outdoors, the sun was warm, and lying on the grass afterwards felt like bliss. For the bride and groom, the whole day was bliss from morning to night.

Lena

And the honeymoon? They spent a blissful honeymoon in the Maldives, I bet.

Maya

You guessed it. A blissful honeymoon, full of joy and calm. They described it as blissful, because every day felt easy and peaceful. A blissful holiday like that stays in your memory forever, and even now, when they talk about it, they call those two weeks the most blissful time of their lives.

Lena

I love that. But weddings also bring nerves. Did anyone bite their nails before the ceremony?

Oh yes, the groom! To bite your nails means to chew your fingernails when you are nervous, and he started to bite his nails the moment the music began. His best man whispered, do not bite your nails, you will need those hands for the ring.

That is so funny. I bite my nails too when I am stressed, and I always bite my nails during exams. My mother used to say, if you bite your nails, everyone will know you are nervous.

Maya

The groom laughed, stopped trying to bite his nails, and took a deep breath. By the end of the ceremony, there was no reason to bite your nails anymore, because everything had gone perfectly, and the rest of the day was pure bliss.

Maya

Now, let us put all seven words together, because character is really about how we handle all of these feelings. An astute person knows when to appease someone and when to stand firm. Sara, our manager, was astute, and she managed to appease her angry employee without giving away something unfair.

Lena

And she was not averse to the hard talk, which is rare. Most people are averse to conflict, but being averse to every disagreement just stores up trouble. Sara faced her own aversion to disappointing people, and that aversion lost its power over her.

Maya

Meanwhile, the wedding story shows the other side of character. The groom wanted to bite his nails from nerves, but he chose calm instead, and he found bliss. A blissful moment like that does not happen by accident. It comes from facing nerves and choosing joy.

Lena

I think that is the heart of it. Whether you feel an aversion to something scary, or you want to bite your nails before a big moment, or you are trying to appease someone who is upset, your character is what you do next.

Maya

And when you handle it well, you get those moments of bliss. A blissful life is not a life without problems. It is a life where an astute mind, honest conversations, and a little courage turn stress into something beautiful.

Lena

So here is our challenge for you this week. Notice one moment when you are averse to something, and ask yourself why. Notice when you want to bite your nails, and take a breath instead. And if someone around you is upset, try to appease them with real listening, not just quick promises.

That is a wonderful challenge. And remember, every one of these words, appease, astute, averse, aversion, bite your nails, bliss, and blissful, is waiting for you in the app, with photos, pronunciation, and a quiz to test yourself.

Thanks for learning with us today. Keep practising, keep noticing your emotions, and we will see you tomorrow on Muse English.

See you tomorrow, and may your week be full of blissful moments.

📝Grammar: Passive Reporting Structures (It is said that..., He is thought to...)

In news and formal writing, we often report what people in general say, think or believe without naming anyone. **It is said that** he is rich. He **is said to be** rich. There are two patterns. **It + passive + that** clause: **It is believed that** the thieves left the country. Subject + passive + to-infinitive: The thieves **are believed to have left** the country. Choose the infinitive by time. For the same time or a general fact, use **to + verb**: He **is said to be** very rich.
