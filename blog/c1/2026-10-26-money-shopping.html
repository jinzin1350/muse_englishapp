abject poverty /AB-jekt POV-er-tee/
phrase
The state of being extremely poor and without hope.
"Millions live in abject poverty worldwide."
achieve economic growth /uh-CHEEV ek-uh-NOM-ik GROHTH/
phrase
To increase a country's wealth and prosperity.
"Governments aim to achieve economic growth."
ailing /AY-ling/
adjective
In poor health or condition; failing.
"The ailing economy needs urgent intervention."
alleviate debt /uh-LEE-vee-ayt DET/
phrase
To make debt less severe or burdensome.
"The new policy aims to alleviate debt for students."
allocate funds /AL-uh-kayt FUNDZ/
phrase
To distribute money for a particular purpose.
"The government will allocate funds for education."
APR /AY-PEE-AR/
abbreviation
Annual Percentage Rate; the yearly cost of borrowing money.
"Always check the APR before taking out a loan."
boost the economy /BOOST thee ee-KON-uh-mee/
verb phrase
To improve the economic situation.
"Government spending aims to boost the economy."

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I am Maya, and this is Lena. Today we are talking about money, jobs, and the economy, because the news has been full of big economic words lately.

Lena

That is right, Maya. And some of those words sound difficult, but they are really about everyday life. When people talk about the economy, they are talking about jobs, prices, shops, and how families pay their bills.

Maya

Exactly. Let us start with a worrying phrase. In many parts of the world, millions of people still live in abject poverty. That means they are extremely poor, with almost no money, no safe home, and very little hope of a better life.

Lena

Abject poverty is a strong phrase, and it should make us stop and think. When a family lives in abject poverty, even buying food or medicine can be a daily struggle. It is not just being short of money for a week. It is a much deeper problem.

Maya

And here is where governments come in. One of their biggest jobs is to boost the economy, to make the economic situation better for everyone. When leaders try to boost the economy, they want more jobs, more spending in shops, and more confidence.

Maya

Thanks, Lena. Now, how does a country actually boost the economy? One answer is spending. When a government wants to boost the economy, it might build roads, schools, and hospitals. That creates jobs, and workers spend their pay in local shops.

Lena

Another way to boost the economy is to support small businesses. If a small bakery or a repair shop can hire one more worker, that is real progress. Many small steps together can really boost the economy over time.

Maya

A related goal is to achieve economic growth. To achieve economic growth means to increase a country's wealth and prosperity. It is not only about big numbers. It is about families having steadier incomes and better services.

Lena

Before we go further, let me tell you about something that helps me every single day. Lena

That is a good point. When a country manages to achieve economic growth, there is more money for schools and hospitals. But growth has to reach ordinary people. If only a few rich people benefit, most families will not feel that the country did achieve economic growth at all.

Maya

And sometimes the news describes an ailing economy. An ailing economy is one that is in poor condition, like a sick patient. An ailing economy might have rising prices, closing shops, and people losing jobs.

Lena

I like that image, Maya. Just as a sick person needs care, an ailing economy needs urgent attention. When reporters say an ailing economy needs help, they mean leaders must act quickly before more businesses close.

Lena

Maya

Yes. A new policy might aim to alleviate debt for students, for example. If a student loan is smaller, or the payments are spread over more years, that can alleviate debt and give young people a fairer start.

Lena

Maya

Lena

The APR is so important, Maya. Two loans can look the same, but a higher APR means you pay much more over time. A careful borrower always compares the APR before signing anything.

Maya

Exactly. If you borrow money to buy a car, ask about the APR. If you use a credit card, check the APR. Understanding the APR can save you hundreds, or even thousands, over the years.

Lena

Now, another key phrase is allocate funds. To allocate funds means to distribute money for a particular purpose. A government might allocate funds for education, for example, to build new schools and train teachers.

Maya

Lena

Maya

That is a lovely connection. Whether it is a country or a household, we all have to allocate funds. A budget is simply a plan for how to allocate funds across the things that matter most.

Maya

That is worth repeating, Lena. When a family must allocate funds for rent and food, every small saving matters, and a clear plan can really help.

Lena

And when a government wants to boost the economy, it should listen to those families first, because they know exactly where help is needed most.

Lena

That is the sad part, Maya. Even in a rich country, some people still live in abject poverty. A country can report growth while families in abject poverty still struggle to eat. That is why we must look beyond the headlines.

Maya

Absolutely. Real success means fewer people in abject poverty. If a plan to boost the economy leaves the poorest people behind, then it has not truly worked. A fair economy lifts everyone, not just the wealthy.

Lena

And remember the ailing economy we described earlier. An ailing economy often hits the poorest hardest. When an ailing economy loses jobs, families who were already struggling fall deeper into trouble.

Maya

So what can help? One idea is to allocate funds directly to the people who need them most. If a government decides to allocate funds for affordable housing and free school meals, that can change lives quickly.

Lena

Another idea is to alleviate debt for struggling families. Programs that alleviate debt can stop people from losing their homes. To alleviate debt is not about giving up. It is about giving people a chance to recover.

Maya

And we must not forget the APR. When families in difficulty borrow money, a high APR can trap them. Teaching people to check the APR is a small lesson that protects them from bigger problems.

Lena

That is so true. A low APR loan is very different from a high APR loan, even if the monthly payment looks similar at first. Always compare the APR, and ask questions before you borrow.

Lena

To do that, it decides to allocate funds for new jobs and training. It also creates a plan to alleviate debt for students and small businesses. And it reminds every borrower to check the APR before taking a loan.

Maya

Step by step, those choices can boost the economy. New jobs mean more spending, and more spending helps shops survive. Slowly, the country can begin to achieve economic growth again.

Lena

And the most important test is simple. Are fewer people living in abject poverty? Is the ailing economy getting stronger? If the answers are yes, then the plan is working.

When reporters describe an ailing economy, they mean an economy in poor condition that needs care.

Lena

A fair policy can alleviate debt for students and families, making their burden lighter.

Maya

Leaders must allocate funds wisely, choosing schools, hospitals, and transport.

Lena

Always check the APR before you borrow, because the APR shows the true yearly cost.

Maya

And when jobs return and shops fill again, we can say the country did boost the economy.

Lena

What a useful set of words, Maya. Money and the economy affect all of us, and now our listeners can talk about them with confidence.

📝Grammar: Cleft Sentences (It was... that, What I need is...)

A **cleft sentence** splits one idea into two parts to **emphasise** one of them. It tells the listener exactly which part matters most. **It-clefts** use **It is** or **It was**, then the focus, then **that** or **who**. Use **who** for people and **that** for things, places, or times. **Wh-clefts** start with a **what** clause, then **is** or **was**, then the focus. For an action, use **do**: **What I did was** (to) + verb. Keep the verb singular after **It** and after a **what** clause, even when the focus itself is plural. Cleft sentences are also a polite way to correct someone. You
