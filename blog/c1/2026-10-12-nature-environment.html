📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
ecosystem /EE-koh-sis-tuhm/
noun
all living things plus their environment working together as one system
"Wetlands are rich ecosystems that support countless species of birds and fish."
habitat /HA-bi-tat/
noun
the natural home where a plant or animal lives
"Melting sea ice is destroying the polar bear's natural habitat."
emission /ee-MISH-uhn/
noun
gas or other substances released into the air
"The city has cut its carbon emissions by a third in five years."
conservation /kon-sur-VAY-shuhn/
noun
protecting nature, animals, and natural resources
"Conservation programs have brought wolves back to the valley."
erosion /ee-ROH-zhuhn/
noun
soil or rock slowly worn away by wind or water
"Coastal erosion is eating away the cliffs near the fishing village."
permafrost /PUR-muh-frost/
noun
ground that stays frozen all year round
"Thawing permafrost is releasing methane into the atmosphere."
desertification /deh-zur-tuh-fuh-KAY-shuhn/
noun
rich farmland slowly turning into desert
"Overgrazing has accelerated desertification across the region."
sequestration /seh-kwes-TRAY-shuhn/
noun
capturing carbon and storing it away from the air
"Forests play a vital role in carbon sequestration."
anthropogenic /an-thruh-puh-JEN-ik/
adjective
caused by human activity
"Scientists agree that modern climate change is largely anthropogenic."
regenerative /ree-JEN-uh-ruh-tiv/
adjective
restoring or renewing nature rather than harming it
"Regenerative farming rebuilds the soil instead of wearing it out."

Maya

Lena

Maya

That reminds me of polar bears. Melting sea ice is destroying the polar bear's natural habitat, and habitat is our second word. A habitat is simply the natural home of an animal or a plant.

Lena

And habitats are shrinking everywhere. When forests are cut down, wild animals lose their habitats almost overnight. Protecting habitats is one of the simplest gifts conservation can give.

Maya

True. A shrinking habitat forces species into smaller areas, and then they compete for food. If we save the habitat, we often save everything that lives inside it.

Lena

That is a good rule to remember: protect the habitat, and you protect the whole community of life.

Maya

Our third word is emission. An emission is a gas released into the air, and we usually say carbon emissions when we talk about climate change.

Lena

Yes. Emissions come from cars, planes, factories, and even cows. Global emissions reached a record last year, which honestly frightened me.

Maya

But there is real progress too. Some cities have cut their emissions sharply by improving public transport and cycling lanes. Net-zero emissions is the goal many countries have promised to reach by the middle of the century.

Lena

So whenever you hear the word emissions in the news, picture invisible gases rising into the sky, and the race to bring emissions back down.

Maya

Exactly. Reducing emissions is probably the most urgent environmental task on Earth, and every fraction counts.

Lena

Our fourth word is conservation. Conservation means protecting nature, animals, and natural resources. My cousin works for a conservation group in Kenya.

Maya

That sounds fascinating. What do they do?

Lena

They run conservation projects to bring elephants back to an old migration route. Modern conservation is very scientific. They track herds with collars, dig water points, and work closely with local farmers.

Maya

That is the beautiful side of conservation. A conservation success story always gives me hope, because conservation proves that humans can repair damage, not only cause it.

Lena

And conservation needs all of us, not just experts. Even a small wild garden on a balcony is a tiny act of conservation.

Maya

Time for a reality check with word five: erosion. Erosion is soil or rock slowly worn away by wind or water.

Lena

Coastal erosion is the scary one for me. I have seen photographs where erosion ate away an entire beach in ten years. The cliffs just crumble into the sea.

Maya

Farmers fear soil erosion too. Heavy rain washes away the topsoil, and erosion steals the nutrients that crops need. To fight erosion, many farmers now plant hedges and rows of trees along their fields.

Lena

And after wildfires, erosion gets even worse, because no roots are left to hold the soil together. Erosion really shows how connected everything in nature is.

Maya

It is slow, silent, and powerful. Erosion reshapes coastlines while nobody is watching.

Lena

You know, Maya, all this talk makes me want to visit a national park this weekend. I want to stand inside a real ecosystem and just breathe it in.

Maya

Do it! There is a beautiful wetland reserve two hours from here. The rangers there do serious conservation work, and they manage the habitat carefully for migrating birds.

Lena

That sounds perfect. I will pack boots, because I hear the trails have serious erosion after the autumn rains.

Maya

And listen to the guides. They will explain how cleaner air and lower emissions nearby have helped the reserve recover, and how the old forests handle natural sequestration. You will hear half of today's words in a single afternoon.

Lena

I love that idea. Vocabulary sticks so much better when you can see the ecosystem, smell the habitat, and feel the story behind every single word.

Lena

Word six sounds like science fiction, but it is very real: permafrost. Permafrost is ground that stays frozen all year round, mostly in the far north of our planet.

Maya

And the permafrost is thawing, which terrifies climate scientists. Beneath the permafrost lie thousands of years of frozen plants and animals, and as the permafrost melts, it releases methane gas.

Lena

Methane traps far more heat than carbon dioxide. So permafrost thaw creates a vicious circle. Warming melts the permafrost regions, and melting permafrost causes even more warming.

Maya

Ancient permafrost even hides long-frozen microbes. It is a powerful reminder that the frozen north is not as permanent as we once believed.

Maya

Our seventh word is desertification. Desertification is rich farmland slowly turning into desert.

Lena

I read that desertification threatens farming across large parts of Africa and Asia. Slow desertification is almost invisible year to year, but after twenty years, the land is simply gone.

Maya

Overgrazing and careless irrigation speed it up. But scientists also know how to reverse desertification. Planting trees, resting the soil, and capturing rainwater can all push it back.

Lena

Communities that fight desertification often plant green belts of trees around their villages. It is slow, patient work, but desertification is not destiny.

Maya

I love that image: a wall of trees standing between a village and the advancing desert.

Maya

Lena

Word eight is a clever one: sequestration. Sequestration means capturing carbon and storing it away from the air.

Maya

Forests perform natural sequestration for free. Every growing tree is a sequestration machine, quietly pulling carbon out of the sky.

Lena

And engineers are building sequestration projects too, machines that suck carbon from the air and bury it deep underground. Sequestration by forests is cheap and beautiful, while industrial sequestration is still expensive and experimental.

Maya

The smartest strategy is probably both: protect forests for the sequestration they already provide, and develop the technology for the harder cases.

Lena

Either way, sequestration is one of the hopeful words in the whole climate story. It means we can clean up, not only slow down.

Maya

Word nine is my favorite adjective: anthropogenic. Anthropogenic means caused by human activity.

Lena

It is a word scientists love. When researchers say climate change is largely anthropogenic, they mean humans, not volcanoes and not the sun, are driving it.

Maya

Exactly. Separating natural change from anthropogenic change is the heart of climate science, and the evidence for anthropogenic warming is now overwhelming.

Lena

It is such a precise word. Instead of saying caused by people, you can say anthropogenic, and you instantly sound like a researcher. The anthropogenic impact on our oceans is visible in every bleached coral reef.

Maya

Using it correctly in conversation is a genuine C1 achievement.

Lena

And our tenth and final word: regenerative. Regenerative means restoring nature rather than harming it.

Maya

Regenerative farming is the best example I know. Instead of wearing out the soil, regenerative practices rebuild it. No deep ploughing, cover crops between seasons, and animals grazing in careful rotation.

Lena

I love the philosophy behind it. A regenerative approach asks a simple question: how can this farm give more than it takes? Regenerative agriculture is spreading fast, because farmers see healthier soil and stronger harvests with their own eyes.

Maya

There are even regenerative ocean projects now, where divers restore kelp forests and rebuild oyster reefs. When an activity is regenerative, it heals the world while it grows.

Lena

That is the perfect word to end on. From damage to healing, from taking to giving, that is what regenerative means to me.

📝Grammar: Quantifiers

When we describe amounts in English, we choose our words carefully. A healthy **ecosystem** needs **a lot of** rain, and scientists speak of **lots of** rainfall or **loads of** new species in positive sentences. We say **many** with countable nouns and **much** with uncountable nouns, mostly in negative sentences and questions. We do not see **many** polar bears in a shrinking **habitat**, and there is not **much** clean air left in cities with heavy **emissions**. **Plenty of** means more than enough, a comfortable surplus.
