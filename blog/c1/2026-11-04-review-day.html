📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
affluence /AF-loo-uhns/
noun
The state of having a great deal of money.
"The country's affluence led to increased consumption."
besiege /buh-SEEJ/
verb
To surround a place with armed forces; overwhelm.
"The city was besieged by enemy forces for months."
animal (rescue) shelter /AN-uh-muhl REH-skyoo SHEL-ter/
noun
A place providing temporary care for homeless animals.
"We adopted our dog from the local animal rescue shelter."
affinity /uh-FIN-uh-tee/
noun
A natural liking or understanding of something.
"He has a strong affinity for languages."
academic journal /ak-uh-DEM-ik JUR-nul/
noun phrase
A periodical containing scholarly articles on a specific subject.
"She published her research in a prestigious academic journal."
animal rights activist /AN-uh-muhl RYTS AK-tuh-vist/
noun
A person campaigning for the ethical treatment of animals.
"Animal rights activists protested against the fur industry."
adjoining /uh-JOY-ning/
adjective
Next to or in contact with.
"The library is in the adjoining building."
absolute poverty /AB-suh-loot POV-er-tee/
phrase
Lacking basic necessities like food, water, and shelter.
"Absolute poverty remains a global challenge."
annual bonus /AN-yoo-ul BOH-nus/
noun
An extra payment given to employees once a year.
"Employees received a generous annual bonus after a successful year."
articulate (adj.) /ar-TIK-yuh-lit/
adjective
Expressing ideas clearly and fluently.
"She is a highly articulate speaker, always clear and concise."

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I am Maya, and with me as always is my wonderful friend Lena.

Lena

Hi everyone! Today is a special day, because today is review day. That means no new words. Instead, we are going to look back at the words from the last three days and make them stick.

Maya

Exactly. Over the last three days we learned twenty one useful words, and today we will meet them again in real conversations. Let us start with a word about feelings. Lena, do you have a strong affinity for anything? Affinity means a natural liking or understanding of something.

Lena

Oh yes, I have a real affinity for languages. I have an affinity for the sound of new words, and I have an affinity for long conversations in English. When you have an affinity for something, learning it feels easy and fun.

Maya

That is a beautiful example. An affinity is not just a hobby. An affinity is a deep, natural connection. My brother has an affinity for music, and my mother has an affinity for cooking. What about work? Lena, imagine you work hard all year. What do you hope to get at the end of the year?

Lena

An annual bonus, of course! An annual bonus is an extra payment that employees receive once a year. A generous annual bonus feels like a thank you from your company. Some people save their annual bonus, and some people spend it on a holiday.

Maya

A good annual bonus can really change your plans. But before a company pays an annual bonus, managers do an assessment of risk. An assessment of risk means a careful judgement about what could go wrong. Every smart company makes an assessment of risk before it spends a lot of money.

Lena

That makes sense. A careful assessment of risk protects the company, and it also protects the workers. And speaking of work, here is a funny memory. Last year, my office held an auction for charity. An auction is a sale where people offer higher and higher prices, and the highest price wins. We had an auction of old computers, and everyone enjoyed it.

Maya

I love that idea. At the end of a project, a team must assemble all the results. To assemble means to put parts together into a whole. You assemble a report, you assemble a team, and you assemble evidence. The aforementioned report, the one we talked about earlier, was the result of weeks of work. Aforementioned simply means mentioned before.

Lena

So the aforementioned report was assembled from many smaller pieces. And that work had a real benefit for the whole office. A benefit is something good that comes from an action. The benefit of teamwork is clear: everyone learns, and the work gets better.

Maya

Now, before we move on, let me tell you about something that can help you every single day. The Muse English app is completely free, and it gives you fresh words every day with photos and clear pronunciation, plus this podcast, shadowing stories you can practise with, quick quizzes, grammar lessons, and a leaderboard where you earn points as you learn. Download Muse English today and turn a few minutes on your phone into real English progress.

Lena

Thanks, Maya. Now let us move to our second day of words, the world of nature and the environment. Maya, here is a big question. What is the difference between affluence and absolute poverty?

Maya

That is an important question. Affluence means the state of having a great deal of money. A country with affluence has wealthy people, big houses, and comfortable lives. Absolute poverty is the opposite. Absolute poverty means lacking basic necessities like food, water, and shelter. Absolute poverty remains a global challenge, and it is something we should all think about.

Lena

It is a strong contrast. Affluence can hide problems, because in a place full of affluence, people may forget that absolute poverty exists nearby. And affluence does not always bring happiness. Some people live with affluence and still feel empty, while others face absolute poverty with great courage.

Maya

Very true. Now, a harder word. Besiege means to surround a place with armed forces, or to overwhelm it. In history, armies would besiege a city for months. The city was besieged by enemy forces, and the people inside waited for help. Today we also use besiege in a softer way. Reporters can besiege a famous person with questions, and fans can besiege a star outside a theatre.

Lena

That softer meaning is useful. When problems besiege you, it feels like worries are surrounding you from every side. But let us talk about something kinder, an animal rescue shelter. An animal rescue shelter is a place that gives temporary care to homeless animals. We adopted our dog from the local animal rescue shelter, and it was the best decision we ever made.

Maya

An animal rescue shelter does wonderful work. The staff at an animal rescue shelter feed the animals, care for them, and help them find new homes. And often, an animal rights activist supports that work. An animal rights activist is a person who campaigns for the ethical treatment of animals. An animal rights activist might protest against the fur industry, or might raise money for an animal rescue shelter.

Lena

I admire that. A kind animal rights activist speaks for animals that cannot speak for themselves. And here is a wilder word from that day, ambush. An ambush is a surprise attack from a hidden place. In nature films, a lion will ambush its prey, waiting quietly and then moving fast. An ambush in a story always makes the scene exciting.

Maya

It does. And one more word from that day: anti-social. Anti-social describes behaviour that avoids people or harms social life. Staying home every single night can feel anti-social, but sometimes we all need quiet time. Even the most social person has an anti-social day now and then.

Lena

And do not forget the appliance. An appliance is a machine in your home, like a fridge or a washing machine. A broken appliance can besiege a family with trouble, because life without a fridge is hard. A good appliance saves time, and that is a real benefit at home.

Maya

Beautifully connected, Lena. Now our third day was all about education and learning. Let us revisit an academic journal. An academic journal is a magazine for researchers, full of serious articles written by experts. She published her research in a prestigious academic journal, and that is a proud moment for any scientist.

Lena

An academic journal is a trusted source. When students write an assignment, they often read an academic journal first. An assignment is a piece of work a teacher asks you to complete, and a good assignment uses strong sources. Today you can access online versions of almost every academic journal. To access online means to reach information through the internet, so you can access online articles from your laptop at home.

Maya

Exactly. You can access online articles while you drink your morning coffee, but you must assess them carefully. To assess means to judge the quality of something. Teachers assess student work, and good students assess their sources before they use them. If you assess a source well, your assignment becomes much stronger.

Lena

And when you present your assignment, you want to be articulate. An articulate speaker expresses ideas clearly and fluently. She is a highly articulate speaker, always clear and concise. If you are articulate, people understand you easily, because your sentences are clean and your ideas follow a logical order.

Maya

Being articulate is a skill you can practise every day. An articulate student does not use the fanciest words. An articulate student uses clear words in a clear order. And here is a useful word for places. Adjoining means next to, or in contact with. The library is in the adjoining building, right next to the main hall. So if someone asks where the library is, you can say it is in the adjoining building.

Lena

I love that word. The cafe is in the adjoining room, my classroom is in the adjoining building, and the reading room is adjoining the library. It is such a simple word, and it makes you sound so natural. And the aforementioned library, the one we mentioned a moment ago, has a quiet room where you can study in peace.

Lena

If you are enjoying this review, here is a friendly reminder. The Muse English app is free, and it is your daily companion for English. Every day you get new words with photos and pronunciation, this podcast, shadowing stories to speak along with, quizzes to test yourself, grammar lessons that explain the rules clearly, and a leaderboard where your points show your progress. Install Muse English today, and keep your learning streak alive.

Maya

Thanks, Lena. Now, before we finish, let us do a quick recap of our ten review words. Affinity, a natural liking. Annual bonus, an extra yearly payment. Affluence, having a great deal of money. Absolute poverty, lacking basic necessities. Besiege, to surround or overwhelm. Animal rescue shelter, a safe place for homeless animals. Animal rights activist, a person who defends animals. Academic journal, a magazine for researchers. Adjoining, next to something. And articulate, expressing ideas clearly.

Maya

Perfect recap, Lena. Those ten words, plus the other eleven from the last three days, are now part of your English. Keep using them, keep hearing them, and they will stay with you. Thank you for joining us on Muse English, and we will see you tomorrow with fresh words.

Lena

Goodbye everyone, and happy learning!

📝Grammar: Future in the Past (was about to, was to, would)

Sometimes we talk about the past and describe what was in the future at that time. For this we use **would**, **was about to**, **was on the point of**, **was to**, and **was to have done**. **Would** is the past form of will. We use it after past reporting verbs, for example when an articulate speaker said she **would** present her academic journal article the next day. **Was about to** means something was very near in the future at that time.
