📚 Vocabulary

umami /oo-MAH-mee/
noun
the savory 'fifth taste' found in mushrooms, aged cheese, and soy sauce
"The aged parmesan added a deep umami flavor to the soup."
deglaze /dee-GLAYZ/
verb
to pour liquid into a hot pan to loosen the browned bits stuck to it
"She deglazed the pan with red wine to make a rich sauce."
succulent /SUH-kyoo-lunt/
adjective
juicy, tender, and full of flavor
"The chef served succulent lamb that melted in our mouths."
piquant /pee-KAHNT/
adjective
pleasantly sharp or spicy in taste; lively and stimulating
"The piquant mustard gave the sandwich a pleasant kick."
insipid /in-SIP-id/
adjective
lacking flavor or interest; dull and bland
"The hospital cafeteria served insipid soup with no seasoning."
cloying /KLOY-ing/
adjective
excessively sweet or rich, to the point of being sickening
"The dessert was so cloying that I could only eat two bites."
braise /BRAYZ/
verb
to cook food slowly in a small amount of liquid with the lid on
"He braised the beef in red wine for three hours until it was tender."
emulsify /ee-MUHL-sih-fy/
verb
to blend oil and water-based liquids into a smooth, stable mixture
"Whisk the oil in slowly to emulsify the dressing."
connoisseur /kon-uh-SUR/
noun
an expert judge of taste, especially in food and drink
"As a connoisseur of fine chocolate, she could name the origin of each sample."
mouthfeel /MOWTH-feel/
noun
the texture and physical sensation of food in the mouth
"The creamy mouthfeel of the avocado made the salad luxurious."

📝 Grammar: Reported Speech: Sentences and Questions

Reported speech reports what someone said. **He said** and **She told me** are the classic reporting verbs, but advanced speakers use a much wider set. When the reporting verb is in the past, the tense usually **shifts back**. Present simple becomes past simple (I like → **she liked**), present continuous becomes past continuous, **will → would**, and **can → could**. The soup has umami → she said the soup **had** umami.
