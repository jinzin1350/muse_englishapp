Money & Shopping: the Art of Spending Smart — C1 English Lesson

📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
frugal /FROO-gul/
adjective
careful not to waste money
"Despite her high salary, she is frugal and packs her own lunch."
affluent /AF-loo-unt/
adjective
wealthy; having a lot of money
"The affluent district is full of designer shops and luxury cars."
squander /SKWON-dur/
verb
to waste money carelessly
"He squandered his inheritance on luxury cars and exotic holidays."
haggle /HAG-ul/
verb
to argue about the price before buying
"In the bazaar, you are expected to haggle before paying."
windfall /WIND-fawl/
noun
an unexpected gift or amount of money
"The tax rebate was a welcome windfall for the young couple."
lavish /LAV-ish/
adjective
very generous and expensive; showing a lot of money spent
"They threw a lavish wedding that cost a small fortune."
markup /MARK-up/
noun
the extra amount added to the cost price when selling
"The boutique adds a hefty markup to imported handbags."
exorbitant /ig-ZOR-bi-tunt/
adjective
unreasonably high; much too expensive
"Tourists complained about the exorbitant taxi fares."
recoup /ri-KOOP/
verb
to get back money you have spent or lost
"The company hopes to recoup its losses by raising prices."
thrifty /THRIF-tee/
adjective
careful with money; not wasteful
"Being thrifty with small expenses adds up to real savings."

Podcast: Money & Shopping: the Art of Spending Smart

Listen to today’s conversation.

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome back to Word Kitchen, everyone. I'm Maya.

Lena

And I'm Lena. Today's episode is all about money and shopping, two things everyone has strong feelings about.

Maya

Strong feelings, strong habits. Lena, how would you describe your relationship with money in one sentence?

Lena

Emotional and impulsive. I shop when I'm happy or stressed, and I almost never check my balance first.

Maya

Mine is the opposite. I'm the most frugal person in my friend group. I plan my spending like a military operation.

Lena

I believe it. You once spent three weeks researching a toaster.

Maya

And it's the best toaster I've ever owned. It's not about depriving yourself. It's about making sure every dollar works hard.

Lena

Meanwhile I have the opposite problem. I tend to squander money on things I barely use. Last spring I squandered a huge chunk of my savings on kitchen gadgets.

Maya

Kitchen gadgets? Like what?

Lena

A bread maker, a juicer, a fancy espresso machine. I used each of them twice. Now they just sit there, judging me.

Maya

Oh, I've been there. The trick is the forty-eight-hour rule. If you still want it after forty-eight hours, then consider it.

Lena

Forty-eight hours? I can't wait forty-eight minutes when there's a sale involved.

Maya

That's exactly when you need the rule most. Sale signs are designed to short-circuit your brain.

Lena

You're right. And you know what else I never do? I never try to haggle. I just pay whatever the tag says, even at flea markets.

Maya

That's leaving money on the table. Sellers at markets expect you to haggle. The first price is an opening offer, not a final answer.

Lena

My grandmother was a master of it. She could haggle a street vendor down to half price and they'd both walk away smiling.

Maya

That's a beautiful skill. It takes confidence and humor, and honestly it's respectful. You're engaging with the seller as a person.

Lena

I grew up in a pretty affluent suburb where nobody ever discussed prices. Talking about money was considered terribly rude.

Maya

Interesting. In affluent neighborhoods, the shops know customers won't question prices. So they charge whatever they like.

Lena

Exactly. The boutiques near my parents add an enormous markup to everything. A simple cotton shirt sells for two hundred dollars, and most of that is pure profit.

Maya

That markup pays for the fancy lighting, the perfume they spray in the store, the shopping bags with ribbon handles.

Lena

And the rent on that street is astronomical, so the markup has to cover that too.

Maya

It's a whole ecosystem. This is why I love outlet malls. Same brands, far smaller markup, and nobody judges you for hunting bargains.

Lena

I went to an outlet last month and felt like a genius. I bought a winter coat for a third of the downtown price.

Maya

That's smart shopping. A careful shopper compares prices, waits for sales, and never pays the first price they see.

Lena

I'm trying to retrain myself. My sister is incredibly thrifty. She furnishes her whole apartment secondhand and it looks like a design magazine.

Maya

Thrifty people are often the most creative. When you can't just throw money at a problem, you invent solutions.

Lena

But then there's my cousin's wedding last summer. The most lavish event I've ever attended. Three hundred guests, a string quartet, fireworks over the lake.

Maya

That sounds beautiful and terrifyingly expensive.

Lena

It was both. All that lavish spending, and half the guests left before dessert. They could have invited eighty people and saved a fortune.

Maya

There's nothing wrong with celebrating, but lavish for the sake of lavish rarely makes anyone happier.

Lena

True. Some of my favorite evenings have been the simplest. Takeout with friends costs almost nothing and I remember every one.

Maya

Exactly. Now, have you ever received an unexpected sum of money? A bonus, a refund, anything like that?

Lena

Oh yes. Two years ago my company gave everyone a surprise bonus. It felt like a total windfall.

Maya

And what did you do with this windfall? Be honest.

Lena

I booked a beach vacation within a week. I treated that windfall like free money.

Maya

The classic windfall trap. Money you didn't expect feels less real, so you spend it faster.

Lena

Then my car needed new brakes, and I wished I'd saved at least half of the windfall. Lesson learned the hard way.

Maya

Financial planners say the same thing every time. Save half of any unexpected money before you even think about spending it.

Lena

I follow that rule now. Speaking of spending, restaurant prices have become absolutely exorbitant lately, don't you think?

Maya

It's not just you. I paid thirty-eight dollars for a salad last week. Thirty-eight dollars. For leaves.

Lena

That is genuinely exorbitant. At some point expensive doesn't describe it anymore. You need a stronger word.

Maya

Exorbitant is exactly the word. When the bill arrives and you laugh out loud, that's exorbitant.

Lena

I've started cooking much more at home. When prices get that exorbitant, restaurants lose me as a customer.

Maya

Same here. My instincts kick in hard at those moments. I'd rather make a great meal at home than overpay for a mediocre one.

Maya

You know what quietly drains my account? Subscriptions. Streaming, apps, cloud storage. Each one is small, but together they're a flood.

Lena

Oh, I did an audit last month and found eleven active subscriptions. Eleven. I was paying for three music services at the same time.

Maya

That's so common. Companies count on you forgetting. I put every renewal date in my calendar now.

Lena

Smart. I canceled seven of them in one evening and instantly felt richer. It was the easiest money I've ever saved.

Maya

And online shopping is the other trap. One-click buying removes every moment of reflection. There's no walk to the register, no line, no pause.

Lena

Exactly. My phone is a casino. I see an ad, I tap, and two days later a box arrives that I barely remember ordering.

Maya

I deleted all the shopping apps from my phone. If I want something, I have to open the laptop and type it in deliberately. That tiny friction saves me a fortune.

Lena

Friction is underrated. My grandmother used to say that money should be a little difficult to spend, so you respect it.

Maya

Your grandmother sounds like she understood everything we're talking about today.

Lena

She really did. She kept every receipt in a shoebox and balanced her checkbook by hand.

Maya

That's beautiful. Seeing your spending written down is powerful. Numbers on a screen don't feel real.

Lena

They don't. One more confession: I once bought the same pair of boots twice because I forgot I'd ordered them. They arrived a week apart.

Maya

That's amazing. Did you keep both pairs?

Lena

I gave one pair to my sister. She still brings it up at every family dinner.

Maya

At least someone benefited. You know, most people don't lose money in one dramatic moment. They squander it slowly, a forgotten subscription at a time.

Lena

That's uncomfortably accurate. Now let me ask you something. What's the single best money decision you've ever made?

Maya

Honestly? Setting up automatic transfers to savings on payday. I never see the money, so I never miss it. It just quietly grows in the background.

Lena

The gold standard. Pay yourself first, before anyone else gets a cent.

Maya

Exactly. What about you? Best money decision?

Lena

Learning to cook properly in my twenties. It paid for itself a thousand times over. Every meal I make at home is money that stays in my pocket.

Maya

And it's healthier too. Double win.

Lena

So here's a question. All those kitchen gadgets I bought and never used. Is there any way to recoup that money?

Maya

Absolutely. Sell them online. You won't recoup the full amount, but you'll recoup more than you'd expect.

Lena

I listed the espresso machine yesterday. If it sells, I'll recoup about half of what I paid.

Maya

That's real money back in your pocket. And it clears physical space too, which has its own value.

Lena

It does feel good. Almost like a small bonus. Okay, be honest with me. Do you ever feel that being this careful makes life less fun?

Maya

Sometimes, briefly. But then I remember that money stress is the least fun thing of all. I'd rather be frugal and calm than flashy and anxious.

Lena

That's a good philosophy. You know what surprised me, though? Growing up around affluent families taught me that wealthy people negotiate constantly.

Maya

Oh, absolutely. The most affluent person I know argues over hotel bills. Wealthy people stay wealthy partly because they question every charge.

Lena

My old boss was a millionaire, and he'd spend twenty minutes disputing a forty-dollar fee. He refused to let anyone squander his money, including himself.

Maya

I love that. People assume the rich just squander money left and right, but the ones who keep it are usually disciplined.

Lena

So what's the difference between frugal and thrifty? They sound almost identical.

Maya

Great question. I'd say frugal describes your attitude, choosing to spend less. Thrifty describes your methods, the clever tactics you use.

Lena

So a frugal person skips the expensive coffee shop, and a thrifty person finds excellent beans on sale and brews at home.

Maya

Perfect summary. You can be one without the other, but they're a powerful combination.

Lena

My goal for next year: less lavish impulse buying, more careful planning. And the next time I get an unexpected bonus, I'm saving half before I even celebrate.

Maya

And if a boutique tries to charge you a crazy markup, you'll walk straight to the market and haggle like your grandmother.

Lena

I'll do my best. Maybe I'll even teach you to loosen up a little, and you can teach me to tighten up.

Maya

Before we wrap up, let's do a lightning round. One money tip each. Mine: wait forty-eight hours before any unplanned purchase.

Lena

Mine: audit your subscriptions twice a year. You'll be shocked at what you're still paying for.

Maya

And one more from me: learn the difference between price and value. Something cheap that breaks in a month is no bargain.

Lena

And from me: celebrate the small wins. Every time you resist an impulse buy, that's a victory worth noticing.

Maya

Deal. Thanks for joining us on Word Kitchen. Use these words this week, in real conversations, and notice how much richer your English becomes.

Lena

See you next time. Remember, your money should work as hard as you do.

📝Grammar: Choosing between comparatives and superlatives

Use **comparative adjective + than** when you compare two people, places, or things. Think of comparing a frugal budget with a lavish one. Spelling follows simple rules: **-er for short adjectives**, **more for long ones**. Double the final consonant after a short vowel (big → bigger); change y to i (thrifty → thriftier). Three adjectives are irregular: **good → better, bad → worse, far → further / farther**. Use further for abstract distance and figurative ideas. Use **(not) as + adjective + as** to say things are equal, or not equal. It compares a thrifty choice with an exorbitant one wit
