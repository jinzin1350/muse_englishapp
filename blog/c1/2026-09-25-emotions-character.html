📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
capricious /kuh-PRIH-shus/
adjective
changing suddenly and unpredictably, especially in mood or plans
"The capricious weather ruined our picnic twice this week."
stoic /STOH-ik/
adjective
calmly enduring pain or hardship without showing emotion
"She stayed stoic throughout the entire painful meeting."
melancholy /MEL-uhn-koh-lee/
noun
a deep, thoughtful sadness, often without a clear cause
"An autumn melancholy settled over him as the leaves began to fall."
petulant /PET-yuh-luhnt/
adjective
childishly bad-tempered or sulky, especially over small things
"His petulant tone made everyone uncomfortable at dinner."
gregarious /gruh-GAIR-ee-us/
adjective
enjoying the company of others; sociable and outgoing
"Her gregarious personality made her popular at every party."
reticent /RET-ih-suhnt/
adjective
quiet and unwilling to talk much about feelings or opinions
"He was reticent about his reasons for leaving the company."
sanguine /SANG-gwin/
adjective
cheerfully optimistic about the future, even in difficult times
"She felt sanguine about the exam results despite the hard questions."
disdain /dis-DAYN/
noun
a strong feeling of contempt or lack of respect
"He looked at the cheap imitation with disdain."
resentful /rih-ZENT-fuhl/
adjective
feeling angry and bitter because of unfair treatment
"She grew resentful after years of being overlooked for promotion."
nonchalant /non-shuh-LAHNT/
adjective
calm and relaxed in a way that can seem careless or unconcerned
"He gave a nonchalant shrug when asked about the delay."

Podcast: Emotions & Character

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome back to Word Kitchen. I am Maya.

Lena

And I am Lena. Today's theme is emotions and character. We have ten advanced words for describing how people feel and how they behave.

Maya

These are exactly the words that separate an intermediate speaker from an advanced one. When you can describe a personality precisely, people listen differently.

Lena

Let us get cooking. Our first word is capricious. It is an adjective for someone who changes suddenly and unpredictably, especially in mood or plans.

Maya

My old boss was capricious with deadlines. On Monday the report was due Friday, and by Wednesday morning it was due that afternoon.

Lena

That kind of capricious management destroys trust. People stop planning because the plan never survives the week.

Maya

Capricious weather does the same thing to picnics. I have cancelled three this year alone because the forecast changed overnight.

Lena

Her capricious moods were famous in the office. Nobody knew which version of her would walk through the door.

Maya

The advanced move is using capricious for markets, weather, and people alike. Anything that refuses to stay predictable earns this word.

Lena

Word two is stoic. A stoic person endures pain or hardship calmly, without complaining and without showing much emotion.

Maya

My grandfather was stoic about his illness. He never once complained, even on the hardest days.

Lena

There is real dignity in a stoic reaction, but it can also worry the people around you. They wonder what you are hiding.

Maya

He stayed stoic through the layoffs and spent his last weeks helping younger colleagues rewrite their resumes. That earned him enormous respect.

Lena

Being stoic is not the same as being cold. It is choosing to carry your feelings quietly instead of handing them to everyone.

Maya

You can also be too stoic. If you never show pain, people stop asking how you are.

Lena

Our third word is melancholy. As a noun, it means a deep, thoughtful sadness, often without any clear cause.

Maya

Autumn always brings a quiet melancholy for me. The short days, the falling leaves, it all feels beautifully sad.

Lena

There is a sweet side to melancholy. It slows you down and makes you notice things you would otherwise rush past.

Maya

After the concert ended, a wave of melancholy hit him. He sat in his car for twenty minutes, not wanting the night to end.

Lena

Her melancholy lifted the moment her sister arrived. Sometimes sadness just needs company, not solutions.

Maya

Melancholy is not depression. It is softer, more reflective, and honestly a little poetic.

Lena

Word four is petulant. It means childishly bad-tempered or sulky, especially over small things.

Maya

His petulant tone made the whole dinner awkward. He was upset because his football team had lost a match.

Lena

A petulant outburst in a meeting can undo years of a good reputation. People remember the tantrum long after they forget the apology.

Maya

She sounded petulant when the restaurant ran out of her favorite dessert. It was honestly funny, and also a little embarrassing for everyone.

Lena

Do not be petulant about the performance review. The feedback was fair, even if it stung at the time.

Maya

Petulant behavior is usually a sign of tiredness. Sleep first, complain later. That rule has saved me many times.

Lena

Our fifth word is gregarious. It describes someone who enjoys the company of others, someone sociable and outgoing.

Maya

Her gregarious personality lights up every party. Strangers become friends within minutes around her.

Lena

Gregarious people make the best hosts. They introduce guests to each other and keep the conversation moving all evening.

Maya

My most gregarious colleague knows everyone's coffee order. It sounds small, but it makes the whole team feel like family.

Lena

Even gregarious people need quiet time. Being social takes energy, even when you genuinely love it.

Maya

A gregarious nature is a real gift in a new city, where you arrive knowing no one and have to build a life from zero.

Lena

Word six is reticent. It means quiet and unwilling to talk much about your feelings or opinions.

Maya

He was reticent about the real reasons he left the company. We only learned the full story a year later.

Lena

My reticent neighbor rarely speaks, but when she does speak, every single word counts.

Maya

Being reticent is not the same as being rude. Some people simply process everything internally before they share it.

Lena

Her reticent smile told us she knew more than she was saying, but she was not going to say it.

Maya

A reticent style can be surprisingly powerful in negotiations. Silence makes the other side nervous and generous.

Lena

Our seventh word is sanguine. It means cheerfully optimistic about the future, even when the present looks difficult.

Maya

Despite the setbacks, she stayed sanguine about the launch. Her confidence calmed the entire team.

Lena

A sanguine outlook is a leadership superpower. People naturally follow optimism and run from despair.

Maya

His sanguine forecast for the economy sounded almost naive at first, but three of his four predictions actually came true.

Lena

I try to stay sanguine before exams. Panic has never once improved my score, so optimism is the better strategy.

Maya

It is hard to stay sanguine after a rejection letter, but the people who manage it are usually the ones who eventually succeed.

Lena

Word eight is disdain. As a noun, it means a strong feeling of contempt, a real lack of respect for someone or something.

Maya

He looked at the cheap imitation with open disdain and walked straight past it without slowing down.

Lena

Her disdain for small talk is legendary. She would rather discuss one big idea than ten updates about the weather.

Maya

The review was dripping with disdain. The critic clearly felt the movie was beneath serious discussion.

Lena

Masking disdain is an important social skill, especially when you are dealing with difficult clients.

Maya

You cannot fake respect forever. Disdain always leaks out eventually, usually in your tone of voice.

Lena

Word nine is resentful. It means feeling angry and bitter because you believe you have been treated unfairly.

Maya

She grew resentful after years of being passed over for promotion, and honestly, who could blame her.

Lena

Resentful employees do not complain loudly. They go quiet, and that quiet is always the warning sign.

Maya

He felt resentful when his idea was presented as someone else's work. That kind of theft stings for years.

Lena

A resentful tone crept into her voice every single time the new policy came up in conversation.

Maya

The only cure for feeling resentful is saying what you need before the bitterness has time to set in.

Lena

Our tenth and final word is nonchalant. It means calm and relaxed in a way that can seem careless or unconcerned.

Maya

He gave a nonchalant shrug when they announced the delay, as if two extra hours meant absolutely nothing to him.

Lena

Her nonchalant attitude toward the deadline worried me at first, but she delivered the project a full day early.

Maya

He tried to look nonchalant before the interview, but his tapping foot completely gave him away.

Lena

A nonchalant entrance can be genuinely powerful. Walking in calmly tells the room that you belong there.

Maya

Do not mistake her nonchalant style for laziness. She simply refuses to perform stress for an audience.

Maya

Before we recap, let us play with pairs, because these words get sharper in contrast.

Lena

Good idea. Take stoic and nonchalant. A stoic person is calm because they are strong. A nonchalant person is calm because they are simply not bothered. Same surface, completely different engine.

Maya

Exactly. Your surgeon should be stoic. Your friend who forgot your birthday can afford to be nonchalant, but you will not love it.

Lena

Next pair: petulant and resentful. Petulant is loud and short-lived, like a spark. Resentful is quiet and long-lived, like rust.

Maya

A petulant colleague slams a door today and apologizes tomorrow. A resentful colleague says nothing for a year and then quits without warning.

Lena

Both damage teams, but resentment is the one managers should fear. You cannot fix a feeling that nobody will name.

Maya

Third pair: melancholy and sanguine. Melancholy looks backward with tender sadness. Sanguine looks forward with bright confidence.

Lena

The same rainy Sunday can produce melancholy in one person and a sanguine plan for the week in another. The weather is neutral. The character is the lens.

Maya

And capricious deserves a partner too. Pair it with gregarious. A capricious friend cancels plans. A gregarious friend makes ten new plans to replace the one you lost.

Lena

That is the beauty of a gregarious circle. It absorbs the damage of capricious behavior, because there is always another dinner, another invitation, another evening.

Maya

Final pair: disdain and reticent. Disdain is loud inside and leaks outside. A reticent person holds everything inside, including admiration.

Lena

So a reticent colleague might respect you deeply and never show it, while a colleague full of disdain might smile politely and still leak contempt.

Maya

Which is why we read tone, not just words. The words say one thing. The character says another.

Lena

Ten words, five pairs, and every pair tells a small story about human nature.

Maya

Here is a challenge for our listeners. Pick three of today's words and describe someone you know.

Lena

I will go first. My uncle is gregarious at every wedding, completely stoic at every funeral, and quietly sanguine about everything in between.

Maya

Mine is my old landlord. Capricious with repairs, petulant about rent paid one day late, and full of disdain for anyone who questioned him.

Lena

That is a perfect character sketch in three words. That is exactly what this vocabulary is for. Not showing off, just seeing people clearly.

Maya

When you can name melancholy instead of just feeling blue, or spot resentful silence instead of just sensing tension, you understand the room faster.

Lena

And understanding the room is half of every negotiation, interview, and relationship.

Lena

Let us do a quick recap so these words stick. Capricious means unpredictable and changeable.

Maya

Stoic means calm and uncomplaining under pressure. Melancholy is a deep, thoughtful sadness.

Lena

Petulant means childishly sulky over small things. Gregarious means sociable and outgoing.

Maya

Reticent means quiet and reserved. Sanguine means cheerfully optimistic. Disdain means contempt.

Lena

Resentful means bitter about unfair treatment. And nonchalant means calmly, almost carelessly, unconcerned.

Maya

Use all ten this week in real conversation and they will stay with you forever.

Lena

That is it for today's Word Kitchen. We will see you next time.

Maya

Happy cooking, everyone.

📝Grammar: Present perfect and past simple

Use the **present perfect** — **have/has + past participle** — for past experiences when you do not say exactly when. "Have you ever worked for a capricious manager?" asks about your whole life, not a date. Pair it with **ever** and **never** for experiences. "She has never been petulant in a meeting" describes a lifetime habit, and "He has never lost his stoic calm" does the same. Use the **present perfect** for recent actions with a present result. "He has just sent a nonchalant reply" explains why everyone is calm right now. Use **already** in positive sentences and **yet** in negatives
