📝Grammar: Ellipsis and Substitution

Sometimes English leaves words out on purpose. This is called **ellipsis**. **Ellipsis** happens when the missing words are clear from the context. You will see it after auxiliary verbs like **do**, **have**, and **will**. Look at this. She took a sabbatical in spring, and he did too. The missing words are clear after **did**, so we still understand the sentence. Ellipsis is common at work. A manager says the stakeholders need an update, and another answers that so do we. **Substitution** works differently. It replaces a whole clause with a short word like **so**, **not**, **one**, or **do
