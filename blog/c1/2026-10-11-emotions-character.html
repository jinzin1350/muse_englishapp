📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
nostalgia /nos-TAL-juh/
noun
a deep longing for the past, often for a time or place that feels happier than now
"Walking through my childhood street filled me with an overwhelming nostalgia for the summers I thought would never end."
catharsis /kuh-THAR-sis/
noun
an emotional release that leaves you feeling cleansed or relieved
"Writing the letter I never sent became an unexpected catharsis, and I finally felt free of the old anger."
resilience /ri-ZIL-yuns/
noun
the ability to recover quickly and grow after difficulty or setbacks
"Her resilience after the company collapsed was remarkable; within a year she had rebuilt her career from scratch."
wistful /WIST-ful/
adjective
sad but thoughtful, quietly wishing for something you cannot quite have
"He gave a wistful smile as the train pulled away, already missing a city he had only just left."
disillusionment /dis-i-LOO-zhun-munt/
noun
the disappointment of realizing something is not as good as you once believed
"After a decade of idealism, her disillusionment with the political system was complete and irreversible."
equanimity /ee-kwuh-NIM-i-tee/
noun
calm and balance of mind, even in difficult situations
"Despite the chaos in the markets, the veteran diplomat answered every hostile question with perfect equanimity."
imperious /im-PEER-ee-us/
adjective
commanding and arrogant, acting as if you have authority over everyone
"The manager's imperious tone alienated the entire team, who resented being spoken to like schoolchildren."
recalcitrant /ri-KAL-si-trunt/
adjective
stubbornly refusing to obey rules or follow guidance
"The recalcitrant student ignored every warning, and by spring he had been suspended twice."
introspection /in-truh-SPEK-shun/
noun
the act of looking inward and examining your own thoughts and feelings
"A month of quiet introspection convinced her that the career change was not a whim but a necessity."
trepidation /trep-i-DAY-shun/
noun
a nervous fear or anxiety about something that is about to happen
"She approached the stage with trepidation, yet delivered the keynote without a single mistake."

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I'm Maya, and today we are mapping the landscape of emotion, those big, complicated feelings that simple words cannot quite hold.

Lena

And I'm Lena. Honestly, Maya, this is my favorite kind of episode, because everyone knows what it feels like to be overwhelmed by an emotion they cannot name. Today we give you ten names for those moments.

Maya

Exactly. And the first one is a feeling almost everyone knows: nostalgia. Nostalgia is that deep longing for the past, a time or place that feels happier in memory than it probably was.

Lena

Oh, nostalgia hits me every autumn. The smell of rain on dry pavement gives me this rush of nostalgia for my childhood neighborhood, even though I know the past was not actually perfect. I think nostalgia is beautiful, but it can also be a little dangerous.

Maya

Dangerous how?

Lena

Because nostalgia edits the past. We remember the warmth and forget the arguments. I caught myself in a wave of nostalgia last week, looking at old photographs, and I had to remind myself that the good old days had bad days too. Nostalgia lies a little, even when it comforts.

Maya

That is such a good point. And here is a perfect companion word for that mood: wistful. Wistful means sad but thoughtful, quietly wishing for something you cannot quite have.

Lena

I love the word wistful. There is something gentle about it. Yesterday I watched an old couple at the airport, and the woman gave her husband this wistful smile as their granddaughter waved from the gate. It was not grief, just a soft, wistful ache.

Maya

Beautiful. I felt wistful last month when a friend moved abroad. We stood on the platform, and everything was fine, we were happy for him, but there was that wistful edge to our laughter. Being wistful is not a failure of happiness, it is just its shadow.

Lena

I want to linger on that, because it connects to something deeper. A month of quiet introspection convinced me that a career change was not a whim but a necessity. Wait, that was your story, Maya! Tell it.

Maya

Ha, yes. Introspection is the act of looking inward, examining your own thoughts and feelings. For years I kept saying I was content, but one January I started a daily practice of introspection, twenty minutes each morning with a notebook, and the honesty of that introspection startled me. I was not content at all. I was restless.

Lena

That takes courage. Most people avoid introspection because they are afraid of what they will find. I did a weekend retreat once, total silence, and the introspection was brutal at first. But by the third day, the introspection felt like cleaning a window I had not known was dirty.

Maya

Exactly that. And speaking of cleaning, of clearing something heavy out, that brings us to catharsis. Catharsis is an emotional release that leaves you feeling cleansed or relieved.

Lena

Catharsis is one of the great experiences of being human. I remember watching a play where the final scene gave the whole audience this collective catharsis. People were crying and laughing at once, and you could feel the catharsis ripple through the room.

Maya

I had my own catharsis last year. Writing the letter I never sent became an unexpected catharsis, and I finally felt free of the old anger. I did not send it. I did not need to. The catharsis was in writing it, in finally saying the true thing, even if only to myself.

Lena

That is so powerful. There is research that says emotional expression, even private, gives the brain a kind of catharsis it cannot get from just thinking. So journaling really can be catharsis in action.

Lena

Before we continue, a quick word about something I genuinely use. If you are building a vocabulary for emotions like these, the Muse English app is worth a look. It gives you daily words with real photos and clear pronunciation, plus this podcast, shadowing stories, quizzes, grammar lessons, and a leaderboard with points to keep you going. It is completely free to install, so why not give it a try?

Maya

Glad you mentioned that. Now, where were we? Ah yes, the harder feelings. Disillusionment is the disappointment of realizing something is not as good as you once believed.

Lena

Disillusionment is brutal. After a decade of idealism, my friend's disillusionment with the political system was complete and irreversible. She had campaigned, donated, believed, and then the disillusionment set in when she saw how decisions were really made.

Maya

I felt a smaller version of disillusionment early in my career, when a mentor I idolized turned out to be quite ordinary up close. The disillusionment stung, but honestly it was maturing. Sometimes disillusionment is just growing up in disguise.

Lena

I like that framing. But what do you do when disillusionment meets a difficult person? Which brings me to imperious. Imperious means commanding and arrogant, acting as if you have authority over everyone.

Maya

Oh, we have all met the imperious type. The manager's imperious tone alienated the entire team, who resented being spoken to like schoolchildren. That imperious email, that imperious wave of the hand dismissing a question.

Lena

There is a difference between confident and imperious, though. A confident leader listens. An imperious one performs authority. I once worked with an imperious director who treated every meeting like a courtroom. Nobody dared to challenge the imperious atmosphere, and the work suffered for it.

Maya

And the mirror image, on the receiving end of all that authority, is recalcitrant. Recalcitrant means stubbornly refusing to obey rules or follow guidance.

Lena

The recalcitrant student ignored every warning, and by spring he had been suspended twice. I have to admit, there is a part of me that admires the recalcitrant spirit, the refusal to simply comply.

Maya

Admire it from a distance, maybe. I was a recalcitrant teenager myself, and my poor parents will confirm it. But a recalcitrant attitude in a team can be poison. The line between principled and merely recalcitrant is whether you listen before you refuse.

Lena

Well said. Now, the feeling that comes before a big difficult moment: trepidation. Trepidation is a nervous fear or anxiety about something that is about to happen.

Maya

She approached the stage with trepidation, yet delivered the keynote without a single mistake. I think trepidation gets a bad reputation. A little trepidation before something important means you care.

Lena

Absolutely. I felt pure trepidation before my first live broadcast. My hands were shaking, and the trepidation was honestly miserable. But the moment I started speaking, the trepidation turned into focus. Trepidation is just energy wearing a scary mask.

Maya

I love that. Now for the antidote to all this turbulence: equanimity. Equanimity is calm and balance of mind, even in difficult situations.

Lena

The veteran diplomat I interviewed last year had it. Despite the chaos in the markets, the veteran diplomat answered every hostile question with perfect equanimity. Not cold, not detached, just this deep equanimity that made everyone in the room settle down.

Maya

That is a rare skill. I think equanimity is trained, not born. Meditation helps, but so does perspective. The people I know with real equanimity have usually survived something hard, and the equanimity they show is the scar tissue of that survival.

Maya

If you are enjoying learning these words with us, the Muse English app puts them all in one place. Every day you get new words with photos and pronunciation audio, shadowing stories to speak along with, quizzes, grammar lessons, and points on a leaderboard as you learn. It is completely free, so install it today and keep the habit going.

Lena

And now our final word: resilience. Resilience is the ability to recover quickly and grow after difficulty or setbacks.

Maya

Her resilience after the company collapsed was remarkable; within a year she had rebuilt her career from scratch. I think resilience is the most hopeful word in today's list.

Lena

It really is. Resilience is not about never falling, it is about the speed and grace of getting up. I have seen resilience in friends who lost everything and still showed up for others. That kind of resilience cannot be taught, but it can be witnessed and borrowed.

Maya

And here is the thread that ties all ten words together. Nostalgia pulls us backward. Wistful colors the present. Introspection turns us inward. Catharsis clears the weight. Disillusionment strips the illusion. The imperious boss and the recalcitrant teenager test our patience. Trepidation shakes us before the leap. Equanimity steadies us in the storm. And resilience brings us home.

Lena

That is a beautiful summary. Let us do the quick recap for our listeners. Ten words for the inner life. Nostalgia, that deep longing for the past. Wistful, sad but thoughtful. Introspection, looking inward. Catharsis, emotional release. Disillusionment, the loss of an illusion. Imperious, arrogantly commanding. Recalcitrant, stubbornly disobedient. Trepidation, nervous fear before something big. Equanimity, calm balance of mind. And resilience, the strength to recover.

Lena

You know, I think what I love most about this list is that none of these feelings are villains. Even disillusionment, even trepidation, they are all information. They tell us what matters to us.

Maya

That is exactly it. Emotions are data, not defects. And when you can name them precisely, with words like equanimity or catharsis, you can work with them instead of just suffering through them.

Maya

Ten words, and every one of them gives you a finer brush for painting what you feel. Use them this week. Notice them in the books you read and the people you meet.

Lena

And we will be back tomorrow with ten brand new words on a brand new theme. Until then, keep feeling, keep naming, and keep learning.

Maya

This was Muse English. See you tomorrow.

📝Grammar: Third Conditional — and Mixed Conditionals

The third conditional is the grammar of regret and relief. It replays the past with a wistful "what if." The shape is fixed: **if + past perfect**, then **would have + past participle**. If I had known about the delay, I would have taken another route. Watch the contraction **'d**: in the **if**-clause it means **had**, in the result clause it means **would**. It is perfect for admitting past trepidation: if I hadn't frozen, I would have introduced myself. For less certain results, use **might have** or **could have** instead of **would have**.
