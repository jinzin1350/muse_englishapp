📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
altruistic /al-troo-ISS-tik/
adjective
Showing selfless concern for the well-being of others.
"His altruistic actions benefited the entire community."
brisk /BRISK/
adjective
Quick and energetic.
"We took a brisk walk to warm up."
cardiovascular /kar-dee-oh-VAS-kyuh-ler/
adjective
Relating to the heart and blood vessels.
"Regular exercise improves cardiovascular health significantly."
allocate funds /AL-uh-kayt FUNDZ/
phrase
To distribute money for a particular purpose.
"The government will allocate funds for education."
achieve economic growth /uh-CHEEV ek-uh-NOM-ik GROHTH/
phrase
To increase a country's wealth and prosperity.
"Governments aim to achieve economic growth."
abject poverty /AB-jekt POV-er-tee/
phrase
The state of being extremely poor and without hope.
"Millions live in abject poverty worldwide."
alleviate debt /uh-LEE-vee-ayt DET/
phrase
To make debt less severe or burdensome.
"The new policy aims to alleviate debt for students."
alarming /uh-LAR-ming/
adjective
Causing worry or fear.
"The rise in crime rates is alarming."
abhorrence /ab-HOR-unss/
noun
A feeling of intense dislike or hatred.
"His abhorrence for violence was evident."
clog /KLOG/
verb
To block something, preventing movement or flow.
"Leaves can clog the drains during autumn storms."

📝Grammar: Participle Clauses (-ing, -ed, having done)

A **participle clause** is a short way to give information without a full clause. Instead of saying **as she opened the door, she saw**, you can say **opening the door, she saw**. There are three main types. **-ing** is active and happens at the same time or gives a reason. **-ed** is passive, as in **built in 1889**. **Having + -ed** shows one action finished before another. A participle can also replace a relative clause. **The woman sitting next to me** means the woman who is sitting next to me. You can keep a conjunction for clarity, as in **before leaving** or **while living** in Jap
