📚 Vocabulary

7 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
phlegmatic /fleg-MAT-ik/
adjective
staying calm and unemotional, even in stressful situations
"Even when the flight was cancelled, he stayed phlegmatic and calmly rebooked his ticket."
imperturbable /im-pur-TUR-buh-bul/
adjective
so calm that nothing can disturb or upset you
"Her imperturbable manner made her an excellent negotiator under pressure."
schadenfreude /SHAA-dun-froy-duh/
noun
pleasure taken from someone else's misfortune
"He felt a flash of schadenfreude when his arrogant rival lost the competition."
languish /LANG-gwish/
verb
to lose energy or enthusiasm; to weaken from neglect
"Without sunlight and attention, the plants began to languish."
unflappable /un-FLAP-uh-bul/
adjective
staying calm and steady in a crisis or under pressure
"The unflappable nurse handled the emergency with steady hands."
vindictive /vin-DIK-tiv/
adjective
seeking revenge; wanting to hurt someone who hurt you
"She sent a vindictive email after being passed over for the promotion."
quixotic /kwik-SOT-ik/
adjective
extremely idealistic and unrealistic; pursuing noble but impractical goals
"His quixotic plan to build a floating city attracted only dreamers."

📝Grammar: Passive Reporting Structures (It is said that..., He is thought to...)

In news and formal writing, we often report what people in general say, think or believe without naming a source. Two passive reporting patterns do this job. Pattern one: **It is said that** + clause, as in *It is said that* the castle is haunted. It works with reporting verbs such as **say, think, believe, know, report, expect, consider, claim, allege, estimate** and **suspect**. Pattern two: subject + **is said to** + infinitive, as in *The castle **is said to be** haunted*.
