📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
anger /ANG-ger/
noun
A strong feeling of being annoyed or unhappy.
"He felt a lot of anger about the unfair decision."
cancel (a reservation) /KAN-sul uh rez-ur-VAY-shun/
verb
To say you will not use a booking.
"I need to cancel my dinner reservation."
arrangement /uh-RAYNJ-ment/
noun
A plan or agreement made for something.
"We made an arrangement to meet on Friday."
booking /BUK-ing/
noun
An arrangement to use something at a future time.
"Do you have a booking under your name?"
bunch /BUNCH/
noun
a group of things held together
"I bought a bunch of bananas."
absolutely /ab-suh-LOOT-lee/
adverb
completely, totally, or definitely
""Are you sure?" "Absolutely!""
ambitious /am-BISH-us/
adjective
Having a strong desire to succeed.
"She is an ambitious student who wants to go to university."
aggressive /uh-GRES-iv/
adjective
behaving in an angry or violent way
"His aggressive behavior scared everyone."
book (a table) /BUK uh TAY-buhl/
verb
Arrange to use something at a future time.
"We should book a table for dinner tonight."
able /AY-bul/
adjective
having the skill or power to do something
"I am able to speak three languages."

📝Grammar: The Passive: All Tenses and Passive Infinitive

The passive is **be** + past participle. To make any tense passive, put **be** in that tense. For example, we say **is being painted**, **has been finished**, and **will be announced**. With modals, use modal + **be** + past participle, like **must be signed** and **can be done**. For the past, use modal + **have been** + past participle, like **should have been sent**. The passive infinitive is **to be** + past participle, and the passive -ing form is **being** + past participle. With verbs like give and offer, either object can become the subject, so **I was given** a prize is common.
