📚 Vocabulary

7 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
amount /uh-MOWNT/
noun
A quantity of something.
"Please pay the full amount."
bank account /BANGK uh-KOWNT/
noun
Money you keep in a bank.
"I opened a new bank account last week."
cash machine /KASH muh-SHEEN/
noun
A machine where you can get money.
"I need to find a cash machine."
charge (interest) /CHARJ/
verb
To ask for money as a fee or price.
"The bank charges high interest on loans."
competitive /kum-PET-uh-tiv/
adjective
Wanting to win or be more successful than others.
"He is very competitive in sports."
consolidate /kun-SOL-uh-dayt/
verb
To combine things into one stronger whole.
"We need to consolidate our debts."
corporation tax /kor-puh-RAY-shun TAKS/
noun phrase
Tax that companies pay on their profits.
"The government plans to reduce corporation tax."

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I am Maya, and this is Lena. Today we are talking about money and shopping, and we have seven useful words for you.

Lena

Hi everyone! Money is part of daily life, so these words will help you every single day. Let us start with a story from Maya.

Maya

Last week I opened a new bank account. I walked into the bank with my passport, and the clerk was very friendly. She asked me what kind of bank account I wanted, and I said a simple one for daily spending.

Lena

A bank account is really the first step. When you have a bank account, your money is safe, and you can pay for things without carrying cash everywhere.

Maya

Exactly. But then I needed cash for the farmers market, because some stalls only take cash. So I went to find a cash machine near the park.

Lena

A cash machine is so handy. You put in your card, you type your code, and the cash machine gives you banknotes in seconds.

Maya

The cash machine asked me for the amount. I typed in fifty, and it gave me the exact amount. But I always check the amount on the screen before I take the money.

Lena

Good habit! The amount matters. If the amount is wrong, you should cancel and try again. I once typed the wrong amount at a cash machine, and I got much more cash than I wanted.

Maya

That is funny, and also a little scary. So, the amount, the bank account, and the cash machine are three words you will use all the time.

Lena

Before we go on, let me tell you about the Muse English app. If you like learning words like this, the app gives you fresh words every day with photos and pronunciation, plus this podcast, shadowing stories, quizzes, grammar lessons, and points on the leaderboard. It is free to install, and it makes ten minutes of English a daily habit you will actually enjoy.

Maya

Now let us talk about shopping. Lena, you went shopping for a jacket last weekend, right?

Lena

Yes! I compared prices in three shops. One shop had very competitive prices, and that is where I bought my jacket.

Maya

Competitive is a great word. When shops are competitive, they try to offer better prices than each other, and the customer wins.

Lena

Exactly. The first shop was expensive, but the second shop was much more competitive. I love competitive shops because they work hard to win your money.

Maya

And competitive does not only describe shops. People can be competitive too. My brother is very competitive in sports, and he hates losing a game.

Lena

My sister is the same. She is competitive about everything, even board games at home. But back to shopping: I finally bought the jacket at the most competitive shop in town.

Maya

Now, a story about my friend Sara. She had three small loans, and she was paying a different amount to each bank every month. It was stressful, so she decided to consolidate her debts.

Lena

To consolidate means to combine things into one stronger whole. So she went to her bank and asked them to consolidate all three loans into one loan.

Maya

Exactly. Now she pays one amount each month instead of three. When you consolidate your debts, life gets simpler, and you can see the full amount you owe in one place.

Lena

That is smart. But she must watch the interest, right? Because the bank can charge interest on the loan.

Maya

Yes. To charge means to ask for money as a fee or price. Her bank will charge interest every month, so she checks how much they charge before she signs anything.

Lena

Banks charge interest on loans, and they also charge fees for some services. If a bank wants to charge you too much, you can move your bank account to a more competitive bank.

Maya

That is the trick. A competitive bank will charge less interest, and that saves you a lot of money over a year. Always ask: how much will you charge me?

Lena

Let us talk about a bigger word now: corporation tax. A corporation tax is a tax that companies pay on their profits.

Maya

Right. When a company earns money, the government takes a part of it as corporation tax. The government plans to reduce corporation tax this year to help small businesses grow.

Lena

Small businesses care a lot about corporation tax. If corporation tax is high, a company keeps less profit. If corporation tax is low, it can hire more people.

Maya

And a competitive country often has low corporation tax, because it wants companies to move there. So corporation tax and competitive belong together in business news.

Lena

Maya

We got some listener questions this week, and they are perfect for our money topic. Lena, read the first one.

Lena

Tom asks: I want to save money, but I never know the amount I spend each month. What should I do?

Maya

Great question, Tom. First, look at your bank account statement. It shows every amount you spent, and you can see where your money goes. Then set a weekly amount as your limit, and try not to pass it.

Lena

And use cash sometimes. When you take a fixed amount from a cash machine, you can see the money disappear, and that helps you stop spending.

Maya

Exactly. The second question is from Ana. She asks: what is the difference between a competitive price and a cheap price?

Lena

A cheap price is just low, but a competitive price means the shop offers good value compared with other shops. A competitive shop may not be the cheapest, but it gives you quality, service, and a fair price together.

Maya

And Ana also asks about consolidate. She writes: my brother told me to consolidate my loans, but I do not understand the word.

Lena

Tell your brother he is right, Ana. To consolidate means to bring things together into one. When you consolidate your loans, you pay one amount to one bank, and you can see exactly what they charge each month.

Maya

One more question, from Ken. He asks: I read about corporation tax in the news. Does it affect ordinary people?

Lena

Yes, Ken, it does. When corporation tax is high, companies have less profit to spend on new jobs. When it is low, they may open new shops, and that creates more competitive prices for shoppers like us.

Maya

Thanks for the questions, everyone. Keep sending them, and we will answer more next time.

Maya

Now let us plan a shopping trip together, Lena, and use all seven words again. Imagine we have a Saturday budget.

Lena

Good idea. First, I check my bank account on my phone, so I know the amount I can spend. If I need cash, I stop at a cash machine on the way to the shops.

Maya

And we look for competitive prices. I always check two shops before I buy, because the more competitive shop usually saves me ten or twenty dollars.

Lena

Right. And if I buy something big, I check what the shop will charge for delivery. Some shops charge a lot for delivery, and that changes the full amount I pay.

Maya

That is true. The amount on the price tag is not always the final amount. Delivery, tax, and fees can change the amount a lot.

Lena

Speaking of tax, do you remember corporation tax? It is different from the tax we pay in a shop, because corporation tax is the tax companies pay on their profits.

Maya

Yes, and when the government cuts corporation tax, companies have more money to invest. Some use it to open new shops, and that makes the market more competitive for everyone.

Lena

That is a nice circle: lower corporation tax, more shops, more competitive prices, and happier shoppers like us.

Maya

And one more money tip. If you ever have several small debts, talk to your bank about how to consolidate them. When you consolidate, you pay one amount each month, and you always know what the bank will charge.

Lena

Great advice. Consolidate is one of those words that sounds formal, but it is really practical. Consolidate your debts, consolidate your shopping list, consolidate your plans.

Maya

Let us do a quick practice round now. Lena, give me a sentence with amount.

Lena

Please pay the full amount before Friday.

Maya

Bank account?

Lena

Maya

Cash machine?

Lena

Maya

Charge?

Lena

The bank charges high interest on loans, so read the contract first.

Maya

Competitive?

Lena

This shop is more competitive than the one across the street.

Maya

Consolidate?

Lena

We need to consolidate our debts into one monthly payment.

Maya

Corporation tax?

Lena

The government plans to reduce corporation tax for small companies.

Maya

Perfect. One more round, faster. Amount, bank account, cash machine, charge, competitive, consolidate, corporation tax. Say them with us at home!

Lena

Now a final story. Imagine you move to a new city. First, you open a bank account. Then you find a cash machine near your home. You check the amount you spend each week.

Maya

You compare shops and pick the most competitive one. If you have old debts, you consolidate them into one payment, and you always check what the bank will charge in interest.

Lena

And when you read the news, you will understand corporation tax, because you will know it is the tax companies pay on their profits. That is real English for real life.

📝Grammar: Future Continuous and Future Perfect

The **future continuous** (**will be** + -ing) describes an action in progress at a future time. For example, this time tomorrow you **will be shopping** for a jacket. The **future perfect** (**will have** + past participle) describes an action finished before a future time. By Friday, you **will have paid** the full amount. Use **will have been** + -ing (the **future perfect continuous**) to say how long something will have continued. By June, she **will have been working** here for five years.
