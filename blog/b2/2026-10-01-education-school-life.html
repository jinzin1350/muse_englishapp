Lena

The syllabus is really the student's survival guide. Read it in week one, and you won't get any bad surprises later.

Maya

And each semester — that's one of the two main periods of study in a school year, like autumn and spring — starts with a fresh syllabus and fresh courses.

Lena

Right. So in this semester, he's taking five courses. On the first day of each course, he sat in a huge hall for a lecture — that's a long talk on a subject given to students at a university.

Maya

Lena

Yes. His favorite professor explains everything with simple, funny examples. But those lectures also come with assignments — a piece of work that a student must do for school. That's our word, assignment.

Maya

Lena

Lena

Maya

That's great for his speaking skills too. Now, let me ask about something more serious: how is he paying for all this? Tuition — the money you pay to study at a school or university — is not cheap these days.

Lena

That's true. Tuition fees go up almost every year. But he was lucky — he won a scholarship. That's money given to a student to help pay for their education.

Maya

Wow, a scholarship! That's fantastic. So the scholarship covers his tuition, and he doesn't have to work a night job to pay the bills.

Lena

Exactly. He applied for it last spring, wrote a great essay, and they chose him. He's very proud.

Maya

He should be. Now, what about his studies — what's his major? Our word major is the main subject that a student studies at university.

Lena

His major is biology. He wants to become a doctor one day. Which means he'll also have to write a thesis — a long piece of writing about a research topic for a degree.

Maya

Lena

A whole year! That's dedication. Does your nephew have a mentor? A mentor is an experienced person who guides and advises someone less experienced.

Maya

Actually, the university pairs every new student with a mentor — usually an older student or a young professor. His mentor helped him choose the right courses in the first week.

Lena

A mentor can really change your path. My mentor in college was my English professor, and she pushed me to study harder than I thought I could.

Maya

Lena

Literacy matters everywhere in the world. Some charities work to improve literacy in rural areas, and without literacy, none of this education would even be possible.

Maya

Well said. Now, let me ask: what's the curriculum like at his university? The curriculum is all the subjects and lessons that a school teaches — the full program.

Lena

Maya

That sounds smart. And here's a funny story from his first weeks: on his second day, he was so confused about the schedule that he joked about leaving. His mother said, "Don't be a dropout!" — a dropout is a person who leaves school or university before finishing.

Lena

Maya

Definitely the safer choice. Now, exams are coming up in a few weeks, and he's starting to cram — that's our word, to cram, to study very hard in a short time before an exam.

Lena

Maya

Cramming can work for one night, but studying a little every day is much better. Your brain needs sleep to remember things!

Lena

True. Now let me take you back to high school for a second — did you ever get detention?

Maya

Detention — a punishment where a student must stay at school after classes. Oh yes. I got detention once for talking during a test. The teacher was not amused.

Lena

I got detention for being late to class three times in a row! Sitting alone in an empty classroom for an hour was so boring.

Maya

Lena

📝Grammar: Obligation and prohibition - have to, must, should

We use **have to** and **must** to talk about obligation — things that are necessary to do. Students **have to** enroll before the semester starts. **Have to** is for general or external rules: I **have to** submit my assignment on time. **Must** is more personal: I **must** finish my thesis this month. **Have to** is a normal verb, so it works in all tenses: I **had to** cram all night. She **has to** attend the seminar. **Must** only exists in the present. Do not confuse the negatives. **Don't have to** means no obligation: you **don't have to** read every page of the syllabus. **Mustn't*
