📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
cloud /KLAWD/
noun
online storage and computing services on the internet
"She saved all her photos to the cloud."
drone /DROHN/
noun
a small aircraft that flies without a pilot
"A drone flew over the beach and took amazing videos."
wearable /WEH-ruh-buhl/
noun
a small electronic device you wear on your body
"His new wearable tracks his heart rate during workouts."
glitch /GLICH/
noun
a small, temporary technical problem
"The video froze because of a small glitch in the app."
prototype /PROH-tuh-type/
noun
an early test model of a product
"The company built a prototype of the new phone before production."
phishing /FISH-ing/
noun
fake messages that try to steal your personal information
"He almost clicked a phishing email that looked like it came from his bank."
malware /MAL-wair/
noun
harmful software that can damage your device
"Antivirus software protects your computer from malware."
troubleshoot /TRUH-buhl-shoot/
verb
to find and fix problems with a machine or system
"She troubleshot the broken Wi-Fi and fixed it in ten minutes."
biometric /by-oh-MEH-trik/
adjective
using body features like fingerprints for identification
"Many phones use biometric locks such as face recognition."
autonomous /aw-TAH-nuh-mus/
adjective
able to work without human control
"The autonomous car drove itself through the city."

Podcast: Technology

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome to Word Kitchen, everyone. I am Maya.

Lena

And I am Lena. So glad you are here with us today.

Maya

Today we are talking about something that touches all of our lives, whether we love it or not. Technology.

Lena

Oh, definitely. Tech is everywhere now. Honestly, sometimes I feel like I spend half my day talking to devices instead of people.

Maya

Right? This morning my phone would not unlock. The biometric login just refused to recognize my face.

Lena

That is so annoying. Biometric security is usually great, but when your face unlock fails at seven in the morning, it feels personal.

Maya

Exactly. I was standing there making faces at my phone like a fool. Eventually I typed in my password like it was twenty ten.

Lena

Ha. But you know, biometric locks are one of my favorite modern inventions. My old laptop had no fingerprint reader at all.

Maya

Same. Now I unlock everything with my thumb. It is fast, and honestly, a strong biometric password is harder for thieves to steal than a short written password.

Lena

True. Though thieves are getting clever. Did I tell you about the phishing email I almost fell for last week?

Maya

No, what happened?

Lena

I got an email that looked exactly like it came from my bank. Same logo, same colors, same polite tone. It said my account was locked and I had to click a link.

Maya

Classic phishing. That is exactly how phishing attacks work. They create panic and rush you into clicking.

Lena

I was this close to clicking it. But something felt off. The sender address had a strange extra word in it. So I stopped and called the bank directly.

Maya

Smart move. The bank told me once that they never ask for passwords by email. A real phishing scam can look incredibly professional these days.

Lena

That is the scary part. Phishing used to be easy to spot, with bad spelling and weird grammar. Now the messages are smooth and convincing.

Maya

So how do you protect yourself? I try to slow down and look for small details, like the real sender address.

Maya

Good advice. Speaking of digital dangers, my cousin's laptop got infected with malware last month.

Lena

Oh no. How did it happen?

Maya

She downloaded what she thought was a free video editor. It looked normal, but it secretly installed malware that slowed her whole computer down.

Lena

That is awful. Malware is such a nightmare. Once it is inside your system, it can steal passwords or show fake ads all day.

Maya

She had to take it to a repair shop. They cleaned the malware completely, but it took two days and cost her real money.

Lena

That is why I always tell people to install proper antivirus software. Malware protection is not optional anymore.

Maya

Agreed. And only download programs from official websites. One careless click and you have malware running quietly in the background.

Lena

Right. So we have talked about staying safe. Now let us talk about the fun side of tech. Maya, you recently got a new wearable, right?

Maya

Yes! I finally bought a smartwatch. It is my first real wearable, and I am obsessed with it.

Lena

What do you use it for?

Maya

Everything. The wearable tracks my steps, my sleep, my workouts. It even reminds me to stand up if I sit too long.

Lena

I love wearable tech for fitness. My sister's wearable noticed her heart rate was unusual during a run and suggested she slow down.

Maya

Now let me tell you about my weekend disaster. My brother-in-law brought over his new drone.

Maya

Oh, a drone? Those things look like so much fun.

Lena

They are, until they are not. He was flying the drone over the park, filming the autumn trees, and it crashed right into a big tree.

Maya

No! Was the drone okay?

Lena

Miraculously yes. The drone bounced off a branch and landed in soft grass. But we learned that flying a drone takes real practice.

Maya

I have heard that. Modern drones have cameras that can film in incredible detail. Some farmers even use drones to check their crops.

Lena

Really? That is clever. And delivery companies are testing drones to drop off small packages faster.

Maya

I would love to see a drone deliver my lunch. Though I would worry about it dropping my sandwich on a roof somewhere.

Lena

Ha! Technology always has a funny side. Speaking of which, Maya, have you ever used an autonomous car?

Maya

Not yet, but I am fascinated by them. The idea that an autonomous vehicle can drive itself through a busy city still sounds like science fiction to me.

Lena

Me too. But they are real. In some cities, you can already order an autonomous taxi. You sit in the back and nobody is driving.

Maya

That would feel so strange. Still, autonomous driving could make roads much safer one day, since computers never get tired or distracted.

Lena

But let us be honest, technology does not always work perfectly. Remember my video call disaster yesterday?

Maya

Oh yes. Tell the story.

Lena

I was in an important video meeting, and suddenly my camera froze. My face was stuck in the funniest expression. Then the sound cut out completely.

Maya

A classic glitch. Video call glitches always happen at the worst possible moment.

Lena

It was such a strange glitch. Everyone could see my frozen face but nobody could hear me. I kept talking like an idiot for a full minute.

Maya

A software glitch during a live meeting is the worst. I restarted the app, and the glitch disappeared as mysteriously as it had appeared.

Lena

On the topic of imperfect tech, I saw something exciting last week. A friend who works at a tech company showed me a prototype of their new phone.

Maya

A real prototype? Like an early test version?

Lena

Yes. The prototype was bulkier than the final product will be, and the screen had a few rough edges. But it worked.

Maya

That is so cool. Seeing a prototype makes you realize how much work goes into a finished product.

Lena

Totally. She said this was the third prototype they had built. The first two had serious battery problems.

Maya

Testing prototypes must be fascinating. You get to touch the future before anyone else.

Lena

She told me that every prototype teaches the team something new. Sometimes a prototype reveals a problem they never expected.

Maya

It reminds me that even the smoothest devices started as rough prototypes. Perfection takes time.

Lena

So true. Now, a less glamorous question. Maya, do you keep your files in the cloud?

Maya

Absolutely. I moved everything to the cloud two years ago. All my photos, documents, work files.

Lena

Me too. Cloud storage has saved me so many times. When my old phone died, I lost nothing because the cloud had a full backup.

Maya

The cloud is wonderful until you forget your password. Then you are locked out of your own digital life.

Lena

Ha, yes. I pay a small monthly fee for extra cloud space. Honestly, cloud storage is one of the cheapest safety nets in modern life.

Maya

Agreed. My office uses a private cloud for company files, so everyone can reach the same documents from home or the office.

Lena

It is amazing how the cloud changed the way we work. No more carrying USB sticks everywhere.

Maya

Right? Now, here is a question for you. My home Wi-Fi has been terrible this week. Any advice on how to troubleshoot it?

Lena

Oh, I love a good troubleshooting session. First, turn the router off, wait thirty seconds, and turn it back on.

Maya

The classic move. I tried that, but the connection is still slow.

Lena

Then the next step to troubleshoot is to check if too many devices are connected at once.

Maya

Ha! Well, troubleshooting tech problems is mostly about patience. You test one thing at a time until you find the cause.

Maya

That is good life advice too. When you troubleshoot a problem step by step, you usually find the answer faster than guessing.

Lena

Exactly. Okay, lightning round time. Maya, what is your favorite piece of tech right now?

Maya

My wearable, hands down. What about you?

Lena

Honestly, biometric locks. I will never go back to typing passwords fifty times a day.

Maya

Fair. And the tech trend you are most excited about?

Lena

Autonomous vehicles. I cannot wait for a world where traffic jams drive themselves away. You?

Maya

Drones delivering everything. A drone bringing me coffee in the park would make my year.

Lena

And the tech problem you wish would disappear forever?

Maya

Phishing emails and malware, no question. If I never saw another phishing scam or cleaned another malware infection, I would be thrilled.

Lena

Same here. Technology should help us, not trick us.

Maya

Before we finish, let us do a quick recap of our ten words today. Lena, you start.

Lena

Okay. Cloud, a noun, online storage on the internet. My photos live in the cloud.

Maya

Drone, a noun, a small pilotless aircraft. Flying a drone takes practice.

Lena

Wearable, a noun, a device you wear like a smartwatch. My wearable tracks my sleep.

Maya

Glitch, a noun, a small temporary technical problem. A video call glitch froze my face.

Lena

Prototype, a noun, an early test model of a product. The third prototype finally worked.

Maya

Phishing, a noun, fake messages that steal your information. Never click a phishing link.

Lena

Malware, a noun, harmful software. Antivirus stops malware before it spreads.

Maya

Troubleshoot, a verb, to find and fix problems. Troubleshooting needs patience.

Lena

Biometric, an adjective, using body features for identification. Biometric locks use your face.

Maya

Autonomous, an adjective, working without human control. An autonomous car drives itself.

Lena

Beautiful. Thanks for joining us on Word Kitchen today, everyone.

Maya

We will see you tomorrow with a brand new set of words. Bye for now.

Lena

Bye!

📝Grammar: Reported speech — sentences and questions

We use reported speech to tell someone what another person **said**. It is how we share news, stories, and messages in everyday conversation. When the reporting verb (**said**, **told**) is in the past, tenses usually shift one step back. Present simple becomes **past simple**, **will** becomes **would**, present perfect becomes **past perfect**, **can** becomes **could**, and **must** becomes **had to**.
