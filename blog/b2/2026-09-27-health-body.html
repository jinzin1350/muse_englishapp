noun

noun

"You need a prescription to buy this medicine."

noun
the amount of medicine you should take at one time
"The correct dosage is written on the bottle."
strain /STRAYN/
verb

"She strained her back lifting a heavy box."
sprain /SPRAYN/
verb
to twist a joint painfully
"He sprained his ankle playing soccer."
chronic /KRON-ik/
adjective
continuing for a long time, usually about a sickness
"He suffers from chronic back pain."
hygiene /HY-jeen/
noun

"Good hygiene stops the spread of germs."

noun

"Fruit has nutrients like vitamins."

noun
the way you sit or stand

noun

endurance /en-DOOR-uhns/
noun

"Running builds endurance."

verb

📝Grammar: Present perfect + for / since, present perfect continuous

Use the present perfect with 'for' and 'since' for things that started in the past and are still true now. Use 'for' with a period of time: 'for ten years.' Use 'since' with a starting point: 'since 2009.' Use the present perfect continuous (have / has been + verb-ing) for actions that started in the past and continue now: 'How long have you been learning English?' We also use it for continuous actions that just finished when we can see the result: 'Your eyes are red - have you been crying?' Do not use the continuous with state verbs like 'know': say 'I've known him for years.'
