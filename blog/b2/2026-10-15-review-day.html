📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
bonus /BOH-nuhs/
noun
extra money paid to workers in addition to their salary
"The company gave every worker a Christmas bonus."
networking /NET-wurk-ing/
noun
meeting people who can help your career
"Networking at conferences helped her find a new job."
mute /MYOOT/
verb
to silence a microphone, speaker, or chat notifications
"Please mute your microphone when you are not speaking in the meeting."
probation /proh-BAY-shun/
noun
a trial period at the start of a new job
"During the six-month probation, the manager checks your work closely."
resign /ree-ZINE/
verb
to officially leave your job
"He decided to resign after ten years at the bank."
internship /IN-tuhn-ship/
noun
a short period of work experience to learn a job
"My sister got a summer internship at a design company."
spam /SPAM/
noun
unwanted messages or emails sent to many people at once
"I get so much spam in my inbox that I miss important emails."
influencer /IN-floo-uhn-ser/
noun
a person with a large online audience who can change what people buy or think
"The influencer recommended a new skincare brand to her two million followers."
accessory /ak-SES-uh-ree/
noun
a small item, like a scarf or a necklace, that completes an outfit
"A simple necklace is the perfect accessory for that dress."
chewy /CHOO-ee/
adjective
food that needs a lot of chewing
"These cookies are soft in the middle and slightly chewy."

📝Grammar: Non-Defining Relative Clauses

We use **non-defining relative clauses** to add extra information about a noun. The sentence still makes sense without the extra part. My sister, who lives in Toronto, works as a tailor. Remove **who lives in Toronto**, and the sentence is still complete. Use **who** for people, **which** for things, and **whose** for possession. An influencer, who has two million followers, visited our office. Always put **commas** around a non-defining clause. Without commas, the clause is necessary information, not extra. Never use **that** in a non-defining clause. Say *the bonus, which was generous* —
