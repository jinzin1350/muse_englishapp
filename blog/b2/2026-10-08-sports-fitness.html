📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
stamina /STAM-uh-nuh/
noun
the ability to keep doing something tiring for a long time
"She has amazing stamina. She ran the whole game without getting tired."
tournament /TOOR-nuh-ment/
noun
a sports competition with many matches, ending with one winner
"Our school team reached the final of the national tournament."
spectator /spek-TAY-ter/
noun
a person who watches a game or event but doesn't play
"Over forty thousand spectators filled the stadium last night."
comeback /KUM-bak/
noun
a return to success after a bad period or defeat
"After two injuries, his comeback was the story of the season."
rivalry /RY-vul-ree/
noun
a strong competition between two people or teams
"The rivalry between the two clubs goes back thirty years."
foul /FOWL/
noun
an action against the rules in a game
"The referee gave a foul when he pushed the other player."
disqualify /dis-KWOL-uh-fy/
verb
to stop someone from a competition for breaking the rules
"They disqualified him from the race for a false start."
marathon /MAR-uh-thon/
noun
a very long running race, about forty-two kilometers
"She trained for six months before her first marathon."
penalty /PEN-ul-tee/
noun
a punishment in a game for breaking a rule
"He scored the winning goal from a penalty in the last minute."
hydration /hy-DRAY-shun/
noun
keeping enough water in your body
"Good hydration is essential for runners on hot days."

That's good advice for any goal. You don't need superhuman stamina on day one. You build stamina one workout at a time.

He also learned about hydration the hard way. On one long training run, he didn't drink enough water, and he felt dizzy by mile fifteen. Proper hydration changed everything for him.

Maya

Hydration is so underrated. A lot of beginners ignore hydration completely. They buy expensive shoes, but they forget about hydration, which costs nothing and matters more.

Lena

Right. Daniel started carrying a water bottle on every run. He planned his hydration like a professional. He even set a timer on his watch as a hydration reminder.

Maya

Smart. In a marathon, hydration can be the difference between finishing strong and collapsing near the end. Coaches say hydration is just as important as training.

Lena

And what about the crowd? Daniel said the spectators carried him through the last five kilometers. Thousands of spectators lined the streets, cheering, ringing bells, holding signs.

Maya

That's my favorite part of a big race. A stadium full of spectators creates an energy you can feel. When forty thousand spectators scream your name, you find a second wind.

Lena

The fans were singing about the comeback for weeks. After a comeback like that, the whole town felt invincible. A comeback lifts everyone, not just the players.

Maya

There is another word hiding in that story. Your town's club and their neighbors, do they have a rivalry?

A penalty is all about nerves. The ball is right there, the goal is huge, but a penalty feels like the hardest kick in the world.

Maya

But wait, there's a twist in this story, isn't there? Something about the other team?

Yes. After the match, the tournament organizers reviewed the footage and found that the other team had fielded an ineligible player in the semifinal. They decided to disqualify them from the tournament.

Maya

Wow. To disqualify a team is a big decision. When officials disqualify someone, it means they broke the rules and they're out. Did the team appeal?

Lena

They tried, but the evidence was clear. The organizers said that if you cheat, they will disqualify you, no exceptions. It's a harsh rule, but if they never disqualified cheaters, nobody would trust the tournament.

Maya

So the lesson is simple. Train your stamina, respect your hydration, love the spectators, enjoy the rivalry, and never commit a foul that costs your team a penalty. And definitely don't do anything that gets you disqualified.

Lena

That was a nice summary. Let's go through our words once more in real sentences, so our listeners really lock them in.

Good idea. Stamina first. If you want to run a marathon, start by building your stamina with short runs. Without stamina, even a talented athlete fades early.

Lena

Hydration next. Good hydration keeps your body cool and your muscles working. I check my hydration before every workout now, and it makes a real difference.

Maya

Spectators. A true spectator cheers for effort, not just for winners. When the last spectator leaves, the stadium feels empty and quiet.

📝Grammar: Second conditional, choosing between conditionals

We use the **second conditional** to talk about imaginary or hypothetical situations. It describes a different reality, like a dream season with endless stamina. The form is **if + past simple**, **would / wouldn't + infinitive**. The past simple here does not mean past time. After **if**, we can use **was** or **were** with I, he, she, and it. Many speakers prefer **were**, especially when giving advice. We say **If I were you** to give advice. It is a fixed phrase, so we don't normally say *If I was you*.
