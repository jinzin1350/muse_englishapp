📚 Vocabulary

stamina /STAM-uh-nuh/
noun
the ability to keep doing something tiring for a long time
"She has amazing stamina. She ran the whole game without getting tired."
tournament /TOOR-nuh-ment/
noun
a sports competition with many matches, ending with one winner
"Our school team reached the final of the national tournament."
spectator /spek-TAY-ter/
noun
a person who watches a game or event but doesn't play
"Over forty thousand spectators filled the stadium last night."
comeback /KUM-bak/
noun
a return to success after a bad period or defeat
"After two injuries, his comeback was the story of the season."
rivalry /RY-vul-ree/
noun
a strong competition between two people or teams
"The rivalry between the two clubs goes back thirty years."
foul /FOWL/
noun
an action against the rules in a game
"The referee gave a foul when he pushed the other player."
disqualify /dis-KWOL-uh-fy/
verb
to stop someone from a competition for breaking the rules
"They disqualified him from the race for a false start."
marathon /MAR-uh-thon/
noun
a very long running race, about forty-two kilometers
"She trained for six months before her first marathon."
penalty /PEN-ul-tee/
noun
a punishment in a game for breaking a rule
"He scored the winning goal from a penalty in the last minute."
hydration /hy-DRAY-shun/
noun
keeping enough water in your body
"Good hydration is essential for runners on hot days."

📝 Grammar: 7B: Second conditional, choosing between conditionals

We use the **second conditional** to talk about imaginary or hypothetical situations. It describes a different reality, like a dream season with endless stamina. The form is **if + past simple**, **would / wouldn't + infinitive**. The past simple here does not mean past time. After **if**, we can use **was** or **were** with I, he, she, and it. Many speakers prefer **were**, especially when giving advice. We say **If I were you** to give advice. It is a fixed phrase, so we don't normally say *If I was you*.
