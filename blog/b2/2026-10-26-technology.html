📚 Vocabulary

7 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
(the) Web /WEB/
noun
the internet, a system of linked computer documents
"I found the information I needed on the Web."
account /uh-KOWNT/
noun
a record of your money or online profile
"I need to create an account to use this app."
address /uh-DRES/
noun
The number and street where you live.
"What is your home address?"
alter /AWL-tur/
verb
To change something, usually slightly.
"We need to alter the plan slightly."
analyse /AN-uh-lyz/
verb
To examine something carefully to understand it.
"We need to analyse the data to find a solution."
app /AP/
noun
A small computer program for a phone.
"I downloaded a new app for learning English."
application /ap-li-KAY-shun/
noun
A formal request, often for a course or job.
"Her application for university was successful."

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I am Maya, and this is Lena. Today we are talking about technology, and how it changes the way we live, work, and learn every single day.

Lena

Hi everyone! Technology is everywhere now. We wake up, we check our phones, and we start the day on the Web before we even finish our breakfast. It is a big part of modern life.

Maya

It really is. Think about a normal morning. First, I check the weather on my phone. Then I read the news on the Web. Then I open my calendar to see my plans for the day. All of that happens in five minutes, and I have not even left my kitchen.

Lena

Same here. And I always start by checking my messages. If a friend sent me a photo, I want to see it right away. If my bank sent me a message about my account, I want to read it carefully. Technology keeps us connected, but it also keeps us busy from the very first minute of the day.

Maya

That is so true. And before we dive in, here is a quick word about the Muse English app. If you love learning English, the app gives you new words every day with photos and clear pronunciation, plus this podcast, shadowing stories, fun quizzes, grammar lessons, and a leaderboard where you can collect points. It is free to install, so give it a try and keep your English growing every day.

Lena

Great tip, Maya. Now, let me tell you about my morning. I tried to log in to my email, but I had forgotten my password. I could not open my account at all, and I felt completely stuck.

Maya

Oh no! What did you do? Did you search the Web for help, or did you call someone?

Lena

I searched the Web first. I typed my question into the search box, and I found a simple guide. It told me to click on the button that says forgot password, and then check my email for a reset link.

Maya

That is the usual way. When you create an account on a website, you always need an email address. Your email address is like your home address on the internet. It tells the website where to send you messages.

Lena

Exactly. My home address is in Toronto, but my email address is what the website uses. I typed in my email address, and within one minute, the reset link arrived. Then I could open my account again, and I felt so relieved.

Maya

I had a similar problem last week. I wanted to alter the settings on my phone because the screen was too bright at night. I did not know where to start, so I asked a friend.

Lena

Did you manage to alter the settings in the end?

Maya

Yes, I did. I opened the settings menu, and I found the brightness control. I decided to alter the brightness, alter the sound, and even alter the wallpaper. Small changes can alter the whole feeling of your phone.

Lena

That is a good point. Sometimes we need to alter one small thing, and suddenly everything works better. I also like to analyse how I use my phone. Last month, I decided to analyse my screen time, and the results surprised me.

Maya

What did you find when you started to analyse your screen time?

Lena

I found that I spent three hours a day on social media. So I decided to analyse the numbers more carefully. I wanted to analyse which apps took the most time, and then I made a plan to use them less.

Maya

That is smart. When you analyse data about your own habits, you can make better choices. I should analyse my own screen time too. Maybe we can analyse our habits together next week and compare the results.

Lena

I would love that. Speaking of apps, I downloaded a new app yesterday for learning English. It is a small app, but it is very useful. The app gives me five new words every morning, and the app sends me a reminder at night.

Maya

That sounds like a great app. I use an app for cooking, an app for maps, and an app for music. Every app has a different job, but every app makes life a little easier.

Lena

True. But do you know the difference between an app and an application? People often mix them up. An app is a small program for your phone, while an application can also mean a formal request, like an application for a job or a university course.

Maya

That is an important difference. Last year, I wrote an application for a summer course. My application was long, and I had to check every part of the application before I sent it. In the end, my application was successful, and I got a place on the course.

Lena

Congratulations! Writing an application is hard work. You need to give your name, your address, and your reasons for applying. I remember my first application. I wrote my home address three times because I was so nervous.

Maya

I understand that feeling. Let me ask you something else. Do you ever shop online? I ordered a book last week, and I had to type my home address very carefully. One wrong number in the address, and the book goes to the wrong house.

Lena

Oh, that has happened to me. I once typed the wrong address, and my parcel went to another street. Now I always check my address twice before I press the order button. It is a small habit, but it saves a lot of trouble.

Maya

And sometimes I change my mind after I order something. I want to alter the delivery date, or alter the size, or even alter the colour. Good websites let you alter your order easily, and that makes shopping online much less stressful.

Lena

That is true. I also like to analyse prices before I buy. I compare three shops, I analyse the delivery costs, and then I choose the best one. When you analyse the options, you usually save money.

Maya

Exactly. And now, a quick break for another word about the Muse English app. The app is your daily English companion. You get fresh words with photos, clear pronunciation practice, this podcast, shadowing stories to speak along with, quizzes to test yourself, grammar lessons, and points on the leaderboard as you learn. Download it free today and make English part of your daily routine.

Lena

Thanks, Maya. Now back to our topic. Technology is not only about phones. Think about the Web itself. The Web connects people all over the world. On the Web, you can read news from another country, watch a lesson from a great teacher, or talk to a friend far away.

Maya

The Web is amazing, but we must use it wisely. When you visit a website, you should check that it is safe before you type your account details. Never share your password, and never share your home address with strangers on the Web.

Lena

Very good advice. I always tell my students to protect their account. If someone asks for your account password, do not give it. Your account belongs to you, and you should keep it safe.

Maya

And what about work? Many people now work from home, and they use the Web for meetings. They need a strong internet connection, a quiet room, and a good app for video calls. The app must be simple, or people get confused.

Lena

Yes, and companies also analyse a lot of information now. They analyse sales, they analyse customer feedback, and they analyse trends. When a company can analyse data well, it can make better products for its customers.

Maya

I agree. But technology also changes language learning. In the past, learners used big books. Now, learners open an app, listen to a podcast, and practise every day. The app can correct you, the app can remind you, and the app can keep track of your progress.

Lena

And teachers can alter their lessons more easily now. A teacher can alter a lesson plan in one minute, alter the examples, and alter the homework to fit the students. That kind of flexibility was impossible before the Web.

Maya

Exactly. Let me give you another example. My cousin wanted to change her career, so she wrote an application for a design course. She worked on her application for two weeks. She asked me to read her application, and I helped her improve it. Her application was accepted, and now she studies design online.

Lena

That is wonderful. Technology opens doors. But we should also remember to put our phones down sometimes. Go for a walk, meet a friend, and enjoy life away from the screen. Even a short break can alter your mood for the better.

Maya

You are right. So, to sum up today: protect your account, remember your email address, do not be afraid to alter your settings, take time to analyse your habits, enjoy a good app, and be careful with every application you send.

Lena

And explore the Web with curiosity, but also with care. The Web is a tool, and like any tool, it works best when we use it wisely.

Maya

One last thought before we go. Technology changes fast, and new words appear all the time. The best way to keep up is to learn a little every day. Read one article on the Web, listen to one podcast, and try one new app. Small steps lead to big progress.

Lena

I love that advice. Small steps, every day. That is how you build a strong habit, and that is how your English grows.

Maya

Thank you for listening to Muse English. Keep learning, keep practising, and we will see you tomorrow with a brand new lesson.

Lena

Goodbye, everyone, and happy learning!

📝Grammar: Verbs That Change Meaning: -ing or To-Infinitive

Some verbs can take both **-ing** and **to** + verb, but the meaning changes. **Remember** + **-ing** means a memory of something you did before. **Remember to** means something you still need to do. **Stop** + **-ing** means you do not do something any more. **Stop to** means you stop one thing in order to do another. **Try to** means you make an effort, and it may be difficult. **Try** + **-ing** means you do something as an experiment, to see if it works. **Regret** + **-ing** means you are sorry about something you did. **Regret to** is formal, and it introduces bad news. **Go on** +
