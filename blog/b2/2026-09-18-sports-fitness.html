📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
workout /WURK-out/
noun
a planned session of physical exercise
"My morning workout includes running and push-ups."
cardio /KAR-dee-oh/
noun
exercise that raises your heart rate
"Swimming is great cardio for burning fat."
sprint /SPRIHNT/
verb
to run very fast for a short distance
"She sprinted the last hundred meters of the race."
stretch /STRETCH/
verb
to extend your muscles to become more flexible
"Stretch your legs before you lift weights."
agility /uh-JIH-luh-tee/
noun
the ability to move quickly and change direction easily
"Tennis players need agility as well as power."
strength /STRENGTH/
noun
physical power and muscle force
"He built strength with heavy squats."
routine /roo-TEEN/
noun
a regular plan of exercises done in order
"She follows a strict gym routine."
technique /tek-NEEK/
noun
the correct way of performing a movement
"Good technique matters more than heavy weight."
compete /kum-PEET/
verb
to try to win against others
"He will compete in the national championship."
interval /IHN-ter-vul/
noun
a short burst of hard effort followed by rest
"Interval training burns calories fast."

Podcast: Sports & Fitness

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome back to Word Kitchen, the show where we learn English through real conversation. I'm Maya.

Lena

And I'm Lena. Today's theme is all about sports and fitness, something both of us love.

Maya

Absolutely. Lena, I've been thinking about how I never really had a fitness routine before this year. Now I can't imagine my life without one. A good routine changed everything for me.

Lena

I know the feeling. My workout used to be a total mess, honestly. I would show up at the gym with no plan, do whatever machine was free, and leave after twenty minutes. Now every workout has a clear purpose.

Maya

That is the key. A workout is so much better when you plan it. I start with a proper stretch, then my main sets, then I finish with another gentle stretch. My body thanks me every time.

Lena

Yes, the stretch is the part everyone skips, and it's the part you need most. I learned that the hard way. Last year I pulled a muscle because I forgot to stretch after my run, and I couldn't train for a month.

Maya

Ouch. That is painful and frustrating. Now I stretch before and after every single workout, even the short ones. It only takes five minutes, but it protects you so well.

Lena

Exactly. Stretch after cardio, stretch after strength, stretch after everything. Actually, let's talk about cardio, because people have strong opinions about it. Do you enjoy your cardio sessions?

Maya

Honestly, I used to hate cardio. Running felt boring. But then I tried interval training on the bike, and everything changed. One interval of hard effort, one interval of easy spinning, and time flies.

Lena

Interval training is brilliant. An interval workout burns more calories than a slow steady jog, and it takes half the time. I do a short interval session twice a week and my fitness improved fast.

Maya

And intervals keep your brain engaged. You count down the seconds of each interval, so you're never bored. I also mix in a sprint here and there. A good sprint wakes up your whole body.

Lena

I love a sprint finish at the end of a run. One final sprint, everything you've got. Sprint training builds serious speed, and honestly it just feels powerful.

Maya

It does. I remember my first sprint session on the track. I could barely sprint forty meters without gasping. Now I can sprint the full straight, and my legs feel like they belong to a real athlete.

Lena

That is progress. Speed is fun, but what about strength? A lot of people focus only on cardio and forget about strength training. Strength protects your joints and your bones.

Maya

So true. My strength work is simple: squats, deadlifts, and push-ups. Building strength takes patience, but every month I feel the strength difference in daily life, carrying groceries, climbing stairs.

Lena

Strength really is a life skill. But here's a question: which matters more, lifting heavy or doing the movement correctly? For me, technique wins every time.

Maya

Technique, no contest. Good technique prevents injuries. Bad technique with heavy weights is a recipe for pain. I always tell beginners to master the technique first, then add weight slowly.

Lena

And technique matters for every lift. Your squat technique, your bench press technique, your deadlift technique. One small fix in your technique can change everything about how an exercise feels.

Maya

Agreed. You know, a friend of mine used to train without any guidance, and his technique was all over the place. He hired a coach, fixed his technique in three sessions, and his progress doubled.

Lena

A coach is worth every penny. My routine really leveled up when I started following a structured routine instead of improvising. I mean, a fixed routine removes all the guesswork.

Maya

Can I ask about your rest days? I used to think rest was lazy. Now I treat rest as part of my routine. Without rest, every workout feels harder and your technique falls apart.

Lena

I learned the same lesson. My coach told me that strength grows while you rest, not while you train. So now I plan my rest days as carefully as my workout days.

Maya

That is mature thinking. People forget that recovery is training. I sleep eight hours, I eat enough protein, and my strength numbers keep climbing without extra gym time.

Lena

Nutrition is half the battle. I wasn't eating enough when I started, and my cardio sessions felt awful. Now I fuel properly and every interval feels sharper.

Maya

Same here. Let me ask you, what motivates you on the days you want to skip your workout? We all have those days.

Lena

I tell myself to just start. Five minutes, that's it. And once I start my stretch and my warm-up, I always want to finish the whole workout. Motivation follows action, not the other way around.

Maya

That is exactly right. And I keep a training diary. When I look back and see how far my strength and my agility have come, I get excited to train again.

Lena

A diary is a great idea. It also helps you spot patterns. If your sprint times drop for three weeks in a row, maybe you need more rest or a different routine.

Maya

Or better technique. When my technique slipped on squats last spring, my knees told me about it. I filmed myself, fixed one small thing, and the pain disappeared.

Lena

Filming yourself is such a smart move. I record my deadlift technique now and compare it month to month. Slow, steady progress is the most satisfying kind.

Maya

It really is. Okay, before we move on, one more question. If someone listening has never done any fitness training, where should they start?

Lena

Start with walking. Seriously. A thirty minute walk is wonderful cardio, and you can turn it into an interval walk by alternating fast and slow minutes. Add a basic stretch after, and you have a complete workout.

Maya

I love that advice. Walking is underrated. And once walking feels easy, they can try a beginner strength routine at home, bodyweight squats, push-ups against a wall, and gentle stretch sessions. No gym needed.

Lena

Exactly. The best workout is the one you'll actually do. The best routine is the one that fits your life. And the best technique is the one you can repeat safely, week after week.

Maya

Beautifully said. Now, I have to circle back to something. We keep talking about racing, but what if someone doesn't want to compete? Is fitness still worth it without ever competing?

Lena

A hundred percent. You never have to compete to enjoy fitness. Competing is just one flavor. Most of my training has nothing to do with any race. I train because it makes me feel strong and capable.

Maya

I agree completely. Whether you compete or not, the benefits are the same. Your heart gets stronger, your body gets more flexible, and your mind gets quieter.

Lena

And here's the thing: even if you never compete, training like an athlete changes how you see yourself. You start to trust your body again.

Maya

Let me describe my weekly routine, actually. Monday is strength, Tuesday is cardio, Wednesday I rest, Thursday is another strength workout, and Friday I do a mixed session. That routine works beautifully for my schedule.

Lena

That sounds like a dream routine. My workout week is similar. My morning workout on Mondays is strength, and my evening workout on Thursdays is mostly cardio and agility drills.

Maya

Agility drills? Tell me more. I never train agility, and I suspect I'm missing out.

Lena

Agility is your ability to move quickly and change direction, like a tennis player chasing a drop shot. My agility improved a lot from ladder drills. Agility training makes you feel light on your feet.

Maya

I need that. My agility is honestly terrible. I tripped over a cone at boot camp once, so embarrassing. Better agility would save me from moments like that.

Lena

It builds confidence too. Now, let me ask you something bigger. Have you ever thought about actually competing? Like signing up for a race?

Maya

Funny you ask. I am going to compete in a local five kilometer race next month. My first time. I never thought I would compete in anything, but here we are.

Lena

That is exciting. When you compete, the adrenaline changes everything. I compete in amateur tennis tournaments, and even a small match feels electric. You'll love it.

Maya

I hope so. My goal isn't to win, it's just to finish strong and enjoy the day. But there's something about saying, I am going to compete, that makes training feel more serious.

Lena

It absolutely does. So what's your race plan? How are you preparing?

Maya

My race plan is simple. Three easy runs a week, one interval session, and a long stretch session on Sundays. My coach says the stretch session matters most for staying injury free.

Lena

Smart. And during the race, remember to pace yourself. Don't sprint the first kilometer. Save your sprint for the final stretch of the course, and you will finish feeling strong.

Maya

Great advice. You know, Lena, when I started this journey, my whole routine felt impossible. Now that same routine feels like the best part of my day.

Lena

That's the magic of consistency. Speaking of training, let's recap our ten words for today. Number one: workout. Every workout needs a plan.

Maya

Number two: cardio. A little cardio keeps your heart happy. Number three: sprint. Add one sprint to your next run and feel the difference.

Lena

Number four: stretch. Never skip your stretch. Number five: agility. Train your agility and move like an athlete. Number six: strength. Build your strength patiently.

Maya

Number seven: routine. Follow a routine you actually enjoy. Number eight: technique. Perfect your technique before you add weight. Number nine: compete. Sign up, compete, and surprise yourself.

Lena

And number ten: interval. One more interval is all it takes to make progress. Maya, if our listeners remember just one thing from this episode, what should it be?

Maya

Start small, stay consistent, and enjoy the process. Find a workout you love, build your routine, and the rest will follow. See you next time on Word Kitchen.

Lena

See you next time. Go move your body.

📝Grammar: Future forms - present continuous, be going to, will / won't

English has three main ways to talk about the future. Each one fits a different situation. Use **be going to + infinitive** for plans you have already decided. For example, you are going to add cardio to your workout routine next month. Also use **be going to** for predictions with clear evidence. If your friend looks exhausted after one interval, he is going to rest soon. Use the **present continuous** for future arrangements you have already fixed. If you signed up for a race, say you are competing next Saturday. Use **will / won't** for instant decisions, promises, and offers. You will
