📚 Vocabulary

7 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
be promoted /bee pruh-MOH-ted/
verb phrase
Get a higher position in your job.
"She was promoted to manager last month."
break down /BRAYK DOWN/
phrasal verb
To stop working (for a machine or vehicle).
"My car broke down on the way to work."
brick /BRIK/
noun
A hard block used for building walls.
"The house is made of red bricks."
build /BILD/
verb
make something by putting parts together
"They will build a new house."
business plan /BIZ-nis PLAN/
noun
A detailed plan for a new business.
"We need a solid business plan to get funding."
carpenter /KAR-pin-ter/
noun
A person who builds and repairs things made of wood.
"My father is a carpenter and makes furniture."
CEO /SEE-ee-oh/
noun
Chief Executive Officer; the highest manager in a company.
"The CEO made an important decision for the company."

Let us start at the beginning. Sara started her job five years ago, carrying boxes and answering the phone. Last month, everything changed. She was about to be promoted, and she could feel it.

Maya

Wait, to be promoted means to get a higher position in your job, right? More responsibility, and usually a better salary too.

Lena

Exactly. When you be promoted, your company is saying, we trust you with more. Sara told me, I never dreamed I would be promoted so soon. She worked hard, she learned fast, and finally she did be promoted. Well, she was promoted to office manager.

Maya

I love that. And Tom, the carpenter in our story, what does a carpenter actually do all day?

Lena

A carpenter builds and repairs things made of wood. Tom the carpenter makes beautiful furniture, fixes doors, and builds wooden stairs. Every carpenter needs patience, because wood does not forgive a quick, careless hand.

Maya

My grandfather was a carpenter too. He always said a good carpenter measures twice and cuts once. I still remember the smell of fresh wood in his little workshop.

Lena

Beautiful. Now, Sara's company does not only repair wooden things. The company uses brick and stone as well. A brick is a hard block used for building walls, and their houses are famous for their strong red brick walls.

Maya

So the company can build a house from the ground up. They build wooden floors with a carpenter, brick walls with builders, and a warm kitchen where a family can cook together.

Lena

Yes, and to build well, you need more than strong hands. You need a plan. In fact, Sara's first big job as manager was to write a business plan for the whole company.

Maya

A business plan is a detailed plan for a business, is that right? It explains what you will sell, who will buy it, and how you will earn money.

Lena

Perfect. A good business plan also shows the money you need to start. Without a business plan, a bank will not give you funding. Sara spent three weeks on that business plan, checking every number twice.

Maya

Lena

Lena

Maya

Lena

Exactly. A car can break down, a washing machine can break down, and even an old computer can break down at the worst possible moment. Tom laughed and said, well, at least our tools cannot break down, because a hammer has no engine.

The CEO walked from room to room, touched the smooth brick wall, opened the wooden kitchen doors that the carpenter had made, and said, this house is proof that our business plan works. The CEO was so pleased that she decided to build ten more houses next year.

Ten more houses. That means more work for every carpenter, more brick walls to build, and maybe a bigger office for Sara.

Exactly. And at the small party that evening, the CEO stood up and announced two pieces of news. First, Sara would officially be promoted to operations manager. The room cheered, because everyone knew she deserved to be promoted.

Tom would be promoted as well. The head carpenter job was his. The CEO said, a company grows when good people grow. Tom smiled and said, tomorrow we build again, and we build even better.

📝Grammar: Present Perfect vs Past Simple

Use the **past simple** for a time that is finished. The action ended, and we often say when it happened: yesterday, last week, or in 2019. Use the **present perfect** when the past connects to now. The time is not finished, or the exact time is not important. The **past simple** form is the second form of the verb: **went**, **had**, **broke**. For questions and negatives, use **did**: **Did** you **work**? The **present perfect** form is **have** or **has** plus the past participle: I **have worked**, she **has gone**. Use the **present perfect** for life experience with no time given. *
