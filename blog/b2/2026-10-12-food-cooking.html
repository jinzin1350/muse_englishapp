📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
marinate /MAIR-uh-nayt/
verb
to leave meat or fish in a sauce before cooking so it tastes better
"Marinate the chicken for two hours before you grill it."
braise /brayz/
verb
to cook food slowly in a little liquid
"She braised the beef in red wine for three hours."
caramelize /KAIR-uh-muh-lyze/
verb
to cook sugar or vegetables until they turn sweet and brown
"Caramelize the onions slowly until they turn golden brown."
whisk /wisk/
verb
to beat eggs or cream very quickly with a whisk or fork
"Whisk the eggs and sugar together until smooth."
drizzle /DRIZ-uhl/
verb
to pour a thin stream of liquid over food
"Drizzle some olive oil over the salad before serving."
poach /pohch/
verb
to cook food gently in hot water
"Poach the eggs in simmering water for three minutes."
julienne /zhoo-lee-EN/
verb
to cut vegetables into very thin strips
"Julienne the carrots into thin strips for the stir-fry."
leftovers /LEFT-oh-vurz/
noun (plural)
food that is left after a meal
"We had pasta leftovers for lunch the next day."
glaze /glayz/
noun
a shiny sweet coating on food
"The chef brushed a honey glaze over the ham."
chewy /CHOO-ee/
adjective
food that needs a lot of chewing
"These cookies are soft in the middle and slightly chewy."

Maya

Lena

I tried that last winter. I braised beef in red wine with carrots and onions, and my whole apartment smelled amazing for hours.

Maya

It does, but it is worth it. Once you caramelize onions, they become sweet and soft, and you can put them on pizza, in sandwiches, or on a burger. You can caramelize sugar for desserts too, like on top of a creme brulee, or you can caramelize carrots in a hot pan to bring out their natural sweetness.

Maya

Exactly. The heat slowly changes the sugar, and that is what gives caramelized food that deep, rich color and sweet taste.

That happens to everyone at first. To poach an egg, you bring the water to a gentle simmer, not a wild boil, then you slide the egg in carefully and let it cook for about three minutes. The result is a poached egg with a firm white and a soft, runny yolk.

Lena

That sounds perfect on toast. So when you poach food, you always cook it gently in hot water?

Maya

Usually in water, but you can also poach fish in milk, or poach pears in sweet wine for dessert. The idea is the same: gentle heat, no rushing. Poached food stays delicate and light.

Lena

I like that word. Now here is one I only learned recently: julienne. My cooking class teacher told us to julienne the carrots.

Maya

It sounds fancy, but it just means cutting vegetables into very thin strips, like little matchsticks. When you julienne carrots or peppers, they cook faster and they look beautiful in a salad.

Lena

I tried it and my strips were all different sizes. The teacher said that when you julienne vegetables evenly, they cook at the same speed, which makes the dish taste better.

Maya

That is true. And julienne cuts are perfect for stir-fries, because thin strips cook in just a few minutes. Once you can julienne an onion quickly, you will feel like a real chef.

Lena

Slow progress, but I will get there. Now let us talk about real life. Maya, what do you do with leftovers?

Maya

I love leftovers! Honestly, some dishes taste even better the next day. I always cook extra rice and chicken so I have leftovers for lunch.

Lena

Maya

That is smart. And here is a great rule: if you marinate extra meat on Sunday, you can cook it on Monday, and then you have leftovers for Tuesday. One cooking session, three easy meals.

Lena

I love that system. Let us also talk about making food pretty. What about a glaze? I see shiny donuts and shiny ham, and I never know how they get that shine.

Maya

A glaze is a shiny coating, usually sweet, that you brush over food. You can make a simple glaze from honey or sugar and water. You glaze a ham before it goes in the oven, or you glaze a cake with a thin layer of apricot jam, and suddenly it looks professional.

Lena

So to glaze means to add that shiny layer, and the glaze itself is the coating?

Maya

Exactly. A chocolate glaze on a cake, a honey glaze on carrots, a sweet glaze on cinnamon rolls. It adds shine, sweetness, and flavor all at once.

Lena

Beautiful. Last word for today: chewy. I love chewy cookies, the ones that are soft in the middle.

Maya

Me too. Chewy means the food needs a lot of chewing, like a chewy caramel, chewy bread with a thick crust, or chewy dried mango. It is a great texture word. Some people love soft food, but I love that chewy bite.

Lena

Yes! A brownie should be chewy in the center, and pizza crust should be a little chewy too. It is one of my favorite food textures.

📝Grammar: Quantifiers

We use **a lot of**, **lots of**, and **plenty of** for large quantities. You can use them in positive, negative, and question sentences with both countable and uncountable nouns — for example, a lot of chicken to marinate, or plenty of vegetables to julienne. In more formal English, use **many** with countable nouns and **much** with uncountable nouns in negative sentences and questions. **A lot of** works fine in negatives and questions too, and it is much more common in speaking. For small quantities, use **a little** with uncountable nouns and **a few** with plural countable nouns. Both
