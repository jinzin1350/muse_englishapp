📝 Grammar: 3A: Choosing between comparatives and superlatives

Use a comparative adjective plus than to compare two people, places, or things, for example older than or more expensive than. Short adjectives add er: old becomes older, big becomes bigger, easy becomes easier. Longer adjectives use more: modern becomes more modern, difficult becomes more difficult. Some common adjectives are irregular: good becomes better, bad becomes worse, far becomes further or farther. Use not as plus adjective plus as to say two things are equal: as comfortable as, not as busy as. Use the plus a superlative to compare one person or thing with all of their group: the tal
