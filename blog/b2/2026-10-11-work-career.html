📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
internship /IN-tuhn-ship/
noun
a short period of work experience to learn a job
"My sister got a summer internship at a design company."
resign /ree-ZINE/
verb
to officially leave your job
"He decided to resign after ten years at the bank."
bonus /BOH-nuhs/
noun
extra money paid to workers in addition to their salary
"The company gave every worker a Christmas bonus."
networking /NET-wurk-ing/
noun
meeting people who can help your career
"Networking at conferences helped her find a new job."
probation /proh-BAY-shun/
noun
a trial period at the start of a new job
"During the six-month probation, the manager checks your work closely."
hiring /HY-uh-ring/
noun
the process of finding and employing new workers
"The hiring process took almost three months."
entrepreneur /ahn-truh-pruh-NUR/
noun
a person who starts their own business
"As an entrepreneur, she works long hours but loves her freedom."
career /kuh-REER/
noun
the series of jobs a person does in their working life
"After university, he began a career in marketing."
layoff /LAY-off/
noun
when a company ends workers' jobs to save money
"The factory announced fifty layoffs last week."
applicant /AP-lih-kuhnt/
noun
a person who applies for a job
"More than two hundred applicants applied for the position."

Tom worked at the same factory for eight years. Then one morning the manager called everyone together and announced fifty layoffs. A layoff is when a company ends workers' jobs because it has less work or less money. It is not the worker's fault.

That must have been terrible. A layoff is one of the scariest things that can happen in a career. One day you have a steady job, and the next day you do not.

📝Grammar: Third conditional

The third conditional talks about an imaginary past. We use it when we think about how things could have been different, like a career choice we did not make. The form is simple: **if + past perfect** in the condition, and **would have + past participle** in the result. For example: *If I had done my internship well, I would have passed my probation.* The short form 'd means two different words. In the if-part it means **had**. In the result part it means **would**. So "I'd" can mean "I had" or "I would" depending on where it stands. Use **might have** or **could have** instead of **would h
