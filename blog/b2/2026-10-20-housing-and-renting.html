📚 Vocabulary

7 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
attic /AT-tik/
noun
A space or room directly under the roof.
"We store old boxes in the attic."
bungalow /BUNG-uh-loh/
noun
a house with only one floor
"My grandparents live in a bungalow."
cellar /SEL-lur/
noun
An underground room, often used for storage.
"We keep old boxes in the cellar under the house."
choice /CHOISS/
noun
The act of choosing between things.
"You have a choice of three colours."
chopping board /CHOP-ping board/
noun
A board used for cutting food on.
"Always use a chopping board to protect the counter."
cloth /KLOTH/
noun
Material used for making clothes or cleaning.
"Use a clean cloth to wipe the table."
coat hanger /KOHT hang-ur/
noun
A shaped piece of wood or plastic to hang clothes.
"Please put your jacket on the coat hanger."

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I'm Maya, and today we're talking about one of the most exciting and stressful adventures in adult life: finding a place to live.

Lena

And I'm Lena. Whether you're renting your first apartment or moving across town, looking at new homes is full of big decisions. So today we're walking through the whole process together, from house hunting to signing the lease.

Maya

That's right. My cousin Sara just moved to a new city last month, and she had to make a choice between three very different places. A small bungalow, a top-floor apartment, and an old house with a cellar. She learned so much that I thought we should share it all with you.

Perfect topic. And we'll practice seven useful words about housing and renting along the way. Let's start with the bungalow, because that's what Sara looked at first. Maya, for anyone who doesn't know, what exactly is a bungalow?

A bungalow is a house with only one floor. No stairs at all. Sara loved the idea of a bungalow because she was tired of climbing stairs with heavy shopping bags. The bungalow she visited had a big garden, a bright living room, and even a small porch where she could drink her morning coffee.

Lena

That sounds lovely. But every home has trade-offs. What was the catch with this bungalow?

Maya

The rent was higher than she expected, and the bungalow was a little far from the city center. So the choice was really between comfort and location. And that word, choice, is one of our seven words today. A choice is simply the act of deciding between two or more things. When you rent a home, you are constantly making a choice: bigger kitchen or shorter commute, lower rent or more space.

Lena

Nice promo, Maya. Okay, back to Sara's house hunt. The bungalow was option one. Option two was a top-floor apartment, and the big selling point was the attic.

Maya

Yes. An attic is a space or room directly under the roof. This apartment had a converted attic that the landlord used as an office. It had a slanted ceiling, a round window, and the coziest reading corner. Sara immediately imagined herself working there.

Lena

Attics are charming, but they can be tricky in summer because heat rises. Did she think about that?

Maya

She did. The landlord showed her that the attic had good insulation and a small fan. But Sara also checked the practical things: water pressure in the shower, the age of the washing machine, and the kitchen. And the kitchen led to one of my favorite words today: the chopping board.

Lena

A chopping board is such a small thing, but it tells you a lot about a kitchen. A chopping board is a thick wooden board you use for cutting food on. If the apartment comes with a good chopping board and sharp knives, the landlord probably takes care of the place.

Maya

Exactly. Sara opened the kitchen drawers and found a chopping board, but it was old and cracked. The landlord promised to replace the chopping board before she moved in. Little details like a fresh chopping board show that the landlord respects the next tenant.

Lena

While she was inspecting, did she look at the bathroom and the storage? Closets are where I always find the truth about an apartment.

Maya

Oh, she opened every door. And in the hallway closet she found something that made her laugh: a single coat hanger. A coat hanger is a shaped piece of wood or plastic that you hang your clothes on. One lonely coat hanger in an empty closet, and suddenly she could picture her jackets hanging there.

Lena

I love that image. A coat hanger is such a simple object, but closets full of coat hangers mean the previous tenant actually lived there comfortably. Did the apartment have enough closet space for all her clothes?

Maya

That was the problem. There was only one small closet, and Sara has a lot of winter coats. She counted the space and realized she would need to buy extra coat hangers and a freestanding wardrobe. The landlord said the building had a storage room downstairs, which helped, but the choice was getting harder.

Maya

Thanks, Lena. So option two, the attic apartment, was charming but short on storage. Option three was completely different: an older house with a cellar.

Lena

A cellar is an underground room, often used for storage. Tell me about this one, because old houses with cellars can be wonderful or terrible.

Maya

This cellar was wonderful. It was dry, clean, and the previous owner had turned half of it into a laundry room and half into storage shelves. Sara keeps her winter sports equipment there in her imagination already. The cellar gave her something neither the bungalow nor the attic apartment had: real storage space for the things you don't use every day.

Lena

But old houses have old problems. What did she check?

Maya

Dampness. The number one enemy of any cellar is moisture. She looked for water stains on the cellar walls, sniffed for that musty smell, and asked the landlord directly about flooding. The landlord showed her a dehumidifier that runs in the cellar all year. That honest answer built a lot of trust.

Lena

Smart. And the kitchen in the old house?

Maya

Big and a little worn, but with character. The drawers were full of mismatched cloths. A cloth is a piece of fabric you use for cleaning or drying. She found a soft cloth for drying dishes, a thick cloth for wiping the counters, and the landlord even left a fresh cloth in every bathroom. Small things, but they told her the house was cared for.

Lena

You know, a clean cloth in the kitchen says more about a landlord than a fresh coat of paint. Anyone can paint. Leaving a good cloth for the next tenant means someone actually thought about daily life there.

Maya

Sara thought the same. And then came the hardest part: making the final choice. She had three real options. The bungalow was comfortable but expensive and far away. The attic apartment was charming but small. The old house with the cellar was spacious but needed some love.

Lena

How did she decide? When every option has a downside, the choice can feel impossible.

Maya

She made a list, which I think is the best advice for anyone renting. She wrote down her top five needs: budget, commute, storage, natural light, and a kitchen she could actually cook in. Then she scored each place. The old house with the cellar won, because storage and budget mattered most to her, and the cellar solved the storage problem completely.

Lena

That is such a practical method. And it shows that the right choice is personal. My choice would be different from your choice, and both can be smart. The important thing is to know what you actually need before you fall in love with a pretty attic or a sunny bungalow.

Maya

Exactly. She signed the lease last week. And on moving day, guess what she bought first? A new chopping board for the kitchen, a pack of fresh cloths for cleaning, and a whole set of matching coat hangers for the bedroom closet.

Lena

That is the perfect ending. From one lonely coat hanger to a full set. She made the place her own.

Maya

She also learned one more lesson about the cellar. When she visited in October, the cellar was dry, but the landlord told her to check it again after the first heavy rain, just in case. That advice applies to any cellar, any bungalow, any attic. Visit twice if you can, and always read the lease carefully before you make your choice.

Maya

Let's quickly recap our seven words. An attic is the room under the roof. A bungalow is a one-floor house. A cellar is an underground storage room. A choice is a decision between options. A chopping board is for cutting food. A cloth is fabric for cleaning. And a coat hanger holds your clothes in the closet.

Lena

Try using each word in your own sentence today. Tell a friend about your dream bungalow, or describe the attic in your grandparents' house. The more you use a word, the faster it becomes yours.

Maya

And remember, all seven words are in the Muse English app with photos, pronunciation, and quizzes. Practice a little every day, and your English will grow faster than you think.

Lena

Thanks for listening, everyone. Good luck with your own housing adventures, and we'll see you tomorrow.

Maya

Bye for now!

📝Grammar: Modals of Deduction: Must, Might, Can't

We use **must**, **might**, **may**, **could** and **can't** to make guesses about the present from the clues we have. The closet is empty and a coat hanger lies on the floor. Your guest **can't be** home. **Must** means you are almost sure something is true. The cellar lights are on and boxes are everywhere. She **must be** unpacking. **Might**, **may** and **could** mean it is only possible. The attic window is open. Sara **might be** up there reading. **Can't** means you are almost sure something is not true. That **can't be** the landlord's bungalow. He lives in another city. Form: mod
