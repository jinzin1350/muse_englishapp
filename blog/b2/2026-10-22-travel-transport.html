7 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
adventure holiday /ud-VEN-chur HOL-uh-day/
noun
A holiday involving exciting and dangerous activities.
"We went on an adventure holiday last year."
airport /AIR-port/
noun
a place where planes take off and land
"We arrived at the airport early."
beach /BEECH/
noun
Sandy or stony land next to the sea.
"We walked on the beach yesterday."
bicycle /BY-sih-kul/
noun
A vehicle with two wheels, you pedal it.
"I ride my bicycle to school."
breeze /BREEZ/
noun
A gentle, light wind.
"A cool breeze blew through the trees."
bumpy /BUM-pee/
adjective
not smooth, with many rough parts
"The road was very bumpy."
exhilarating /ig-ZIL-uh-RAY-ting/
adjective
Making you feel very happy, excited, and full of energy.
"The roller coaster ride was exhilarating."

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I'm Maya, and this is Lena.

Lena

And today we're talking about travel — the kind of travel that gives you stories for years.

Maya

Exactly. From quiet days on the beach to a full adventure holiday, we'll share our own travel tales and teach you seven wonderful words along the way.

Lena

Let's start with mine. Last summer I finally took the beach holiday I'd been dreaming about for years. Three whole weeks with nothing but sand, sea, and sunshine.

Maya

That sounds perfect. Which beach did you go to?

Lena

A small beach on the west coast. No crowds, no loud music, just a long stretch of golden sand. The beach was so quiet in the morning that I almost had it to myself.

Maya

Oh, I love that feeling. What did you do all day on the beach?

Lena

Honestly? Not much! I'd read a book in the shade, swim a little, walk along the water. And in the late afternoon, a soft breeze would come off the sea. That cool breeze was my favorite part of every day.

Maya

There's nothing like an ocean breeze. It just makes everything feel calm and slow.

Lena

Exactly. One evening the breeze picked up a little, and I sat on the warm sand and watched the waves until the sun went down. No breeze anywhere else has ever felt that good.

Maya

Getting to the beach must have been easy, right? Just a short flight?

Lena

Mostly. The airport was packed, of course — airports in summer always are — but my flight landed on time. An hour after leaving the airport, I was in a taxi, heading for the coast.

Maya

Did you stay on the beach the whole time, or did you explore?

Lena

I explored a little! One day I rented a bicycle and rode to a tiny fishing town along the coast. My bicycle was old and a bit rusty, but it was fun. Riding that bicycle along the cliffs, with the sea below and the breeze in my face — it was lovely.

Maya

A bicycle ride by the sea? That's the dream. And the beach was waiting for you when you got back.

Lena

It was! Though the road back to my hotel was incredibly bumpy — a rough, bumpy dirt track through the dunes. My poor bicycle shook the whole way.

Maya

A bumpy ride back to a calm beach. The perfect contrast.

Lena

Exactly. And that evening, with the breeze coming through my window, I thought: this is what holidays are for. I swear I could have stayed on that beach forever. Okay, quick pause, friends. Maya

Okay, my turn — and my summer was the complete opposite of quiet. I went on an adventure holiday in the mountains. A real adventure holiday: mountain biking, kayaking, the works.

Lena

An adventure holiday? That sounds amazing. And exhausting!

Maya

It was both! The centerpiece of the whole adventure holiday was the bicycle. Every morning we'd take a bicycle out on the mountain trails. My bicycle was a proper mountain bike — thick tires, strong brakes, the works.

Lena

I've never been brave enough to ride a bicycle on a real mountain trail. Was it scary?

Maya

The first trail was smooth and easy. But the second one was really bumpy. One long stretch was so bumpy that my hands went numb. Bumpy mountain trails are no joke, believe me.

Lena

Ha! I'd probably get off my bicycle and walk it down a bumpy hill.

Maya

Honestly, on the bumpiest part, I almost did exactly that. But then came the downhill ride — and it was exhilarating. Truly exhilarating. The wind, the speed, the green valleys below. I have never felt anything so exhilarating.

Lena

Okay, that does sound exhilarating. So the whole adventure holiday was worth the sore legs?

Maya

Completely. By day three, riding my bicycle felt natural, even on bumpy forest paths. And every evening, after an exhilarating day in the mountains, we'd sit by the lake and laugh about it all. It was the most exhilarating week of my entire year.

Lena

And what about the kayaking part of your adventure holiday?

Maya

Oh, that was on day four! The lake looked calm in the morning, but by noon the water got bumpy — little waves everywhere. Paddling through bumpy water is harder than it looks.

Lena

A bumpy lake! I never thought of water as bumpy.

Maya

It really was. But crossing that bumpy lake was exhilarating in its own way. And when I reached the far shore, a cool breeze came through the pine trees, and I just sat there, happy and tired. What an exhilarating morning.

Lena

Did you feel safe doing all this? An adventure holiday sounds wonderful, but also a little wild.

Maya

That's the key question. We had a fantastic guide, and the company checked every bicycle before we rode. A good adventure holiday always puts safety first — helmets, life jackets, the works. I'd never book an adventure holiday without that.

Lena

Good to know. So the only scary part was the bumpy trails?

Maya

Pretty much! And honestly, even the bumpy parts became fun by the end. On the last night, we had dinner on the mountain, and a warm breeze moved through the valley below us. I looked around at everyone laughing, and I thought: this is what travel should feel like. Exhilarating, a little bumpy, and completely unforgettable.

Lena

I'm almost convinced. Maybe next year I'll trade my beach for an adventure holiday. What should I know before I book a bicycle trip?

Maya

First, expect bumpy roads — pack gloves, your hands will thank you. And pick a bicycle that actually fits your height. A bad bicycle will ruin an adventure holiday faster than bad weather.

Lena

Noted. And do the mountains get a nice breeze, like the coast does?

Maya

Oh, absolutely. Up high, the breeze is cool and fresh, and after an exhilarating climb, that breeze feels like a reward.

Lena

I'm adding mountains to my list. But tell me — how do you even pack for an adventure holiday? My beach packing is easy: swimsuit, book, done.

Maya

Ha! Packing for an adventure holiday is an adventure in itself. Sturdy shoes for the bumpy trails, a rain jacket, and layers. The weather in the mountains changes fast — sunshine one minute, cold breeze the next.

Lena

And the bicycle? Do you bring your own?

Maya

No, the company provides the bicycle — a proper mountain bicycle, already tuned. You just bring yourself. Honestly, the hardest part of the whole adventure holiday was the flight home.

Lena

Speaking of flights — airport wisdom time. Download it free and start today. Now, back to travel.

Lena

Okay, travel wisdom time. Airports. I have a love-hate relationship with the airport.

Maya

Same here. The airport is where every adventure begins — and where things go wrong. My golden rule: get to the airport two hours early for international flights.

Lena

Wise. My worst airport memory: a delayed flight, then a cancelled connection, and I spent the whole night sleeping on an airport bench. The airport at three in the morning is a strange, sad place.

Maya

Oh no! You should have complained. Actually — that's good practice for our listeners too: complaining politely at an airport is a real-life English skill.

Lena

Very true. But here's a nicer airport thought: some airlines let you check a bicycle as luggage. You can fly into a new city, pick up your bicycle at the airport, and ride straight out into the sunshine.

Maya

That's brilliant. Airport to beach on two wheels, with the breeze in your hair. Now that is an exhilarating arrival.

Lena

Or skip the airport entirely and take the train! For shorter trips, I love trains. No long airport lines, no bumpy bus ride to the airport — you just sit back and watch the countryside.

Maya

True. But trains can't take you across an ocean. For a real adventure holiday far away, the airport is unavoidable. My rule: enjoy the journey, whatever it is. A bumpy road, a long flight, a quiet beach — it's all part of the story.

Lena

So where next, Maya? Another adventure holiday, or a calm beach this time?

Maya

Honestly? Maybe both. A week on the beach, then an adventure holiday in the hills nearby. Beach mornings, bicycle afternoons. Best of both worlds.

Lena

It really is. So whether your style is a calm beach, a wild adventure holiday, or a bit of both — travel gives you the best stories and the best English practice.

Maya

Let's do a quick recap of our seven words. An adventure holiday — a holiday with exciting, sometimes dangerous activities.

Lena

An airport — the place where planes take off and land.

Maya

A beach — sandy land next to the sea.

Lena

A bicycle — a vehicle with two wheels that you pedal.

Maya

A breeze — a gentle, light wind.

Lena

Bumpy — not smooth, with many rough parts.

Maya

And exhilarating — making you feel very happy, excited, and full of energy.

Lena

Try to use all seven today. Tell a friend about your most exhilarating trip, or your bumpiest bicycle ride!

Practice them in the app, keep listening, and we'll see you tomorrow. Thanks for listening to Muse English!

📝Grammar: Should Have, Could Have, Needn't Have

We use **modal + have + past participle** to talk about the past in three ways: regrets, missed chances, and unnecessary actions. **should have + past participle** means it was the right thing to do, but it didn't happen: "We **should have left** the beach earlier." **shouldn't have + past participle** means it happened, but it was a mistake. **could have + past participle** means it was possible, but it didn't happen: "We **could have taken** the train to the airport, but we missed it."
