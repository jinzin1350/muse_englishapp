📚 Vocabulary

7 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
access /AK-ses/
noun
the right or opportunity to use something
"Employees have access to the company's private network."
achievement /uh-CHEEV-munt/
noun
something good that you have done
"Winning the race was a great achievement."
administrator /ud-MIN-i-stray-tuh/
noun
A person who manages an organization or system.
"The system administrator fixed the problem."
aim /aym/
noun
a goal or purpose
"My aim is to learn English well."
ambassador /am-BAS-uh-duh/
noun
A high-ranking diplomat representing their country.
"The ambassador met with the president."
apply for /uh-PLY for/
phrasal verb
To make a formal request for something, like a job.
"You should apply for that manager position."
architect /AR-ki-tekt/
noun
A person who designs buildings.
"My uncle is an architect; he designs houses."

Welcome to Muse English! I'm Maya, and today we're talking about the long road of building a career, and the lessons it teaches us.

Maya

Our story is about my friend Daniel. Three years ago, Daniel was stuck. He had a good education but no clear direction. His aim was simple: find work that felt meaningful, with a team he respected. An aim is a goal or purpose, and that aim shaped every decision he made next.

Lena

Great promo, Maya. Now back to Daniel. He applied for twelve places in three weeks. His rule was simple: never apply for a job without researching the company first. When you apply for something thoughtfully, your application shows it.

Maya

And the architecture firm caught his eye because of one person: Priya, the lead architect. An architect is a person who designs buildings, and Priya was a brilliant one. Daniel had read an interview with the architect, and her approach to sustainable design amazed him. He wanted to learn from this architect more than anything.

Lena

The firm's office felt like the right place, too. And the administrator who greeted Daniel at the reception made a warm first impression.

Maya

An administrator is a person who manages an organization or system. In an office, the administrator often keeps everything running: schedules, documents, meeting rooms. This administrator, a man named Tom, noticed Daniel's nervousness and offered him coffee. The administrator even gave him a quick tour. Daniel told me later that the administrator's kindness was the reason he stayed calm in the interview. A good administrator can change the whole mood of a workplace.

Lena

That is so true. Daniel got the junior position, and his first week was a blur of new faces and new software. On day one, he was given access to the company's private network.

Maya

Access means the right or opportunity to use something. With access to the network came access to training videos, project files, and the company's design library. Having access to the right tools made his learning ten times faster.

Lena

Access really is a turning point in any job. It means they trust you. What did Daniel do with all that access?

Maya

He used it well. He watched the training videos, studied old projects, and asked Priya, the lead architect, for advice. The architect became his mentor, and Daniel says the architect taught him that design is not just about buildings, it is about the people who will live in them.

Lena

A great mentor is gold. And by the end of his first year, Daniel had a real achievement to celebrate.

Maya

An achievement is something good that you have done. Daniel's achievement was winning an internal design competition for a community library. It was a huge achievement for a junior employee, and the whole team applauded him. His manager called the achievement a sign of his future, and Daniel still keeps the award on his desk as a reminder of that achievement.

Winning a competition in your first year is impressive. But careers are rarely a straight line. Daniel's second year was harder. He was given more responsibility, and with it, more pressure.

His aim shifted, too. His new aim was to lead a small project team. That aim forced him to learn skills he had never needed before. Keeping a clear aim helped him through the difficult months.

I love how his aim kept evolving. And speaking of evolving, let me take a moment to talk about the tool that keeps your English evolving.

Thank you. The Muse English app is your daily coach. Could not have said it better myself. Now, there was one more twist in Daniel's story: the company started a program to visit universities, and they needed an ambassador.

Lena

An ambassador is a person who represents an organization or a country. Daniel became the firm's ambassador for young designers. As an ambassador, he gave talks at three universities about the world of design. Being an ambassador taught him public speaking, and students loved hearing from a real ambassador.

Maya

From nervous interviewee to ambassador in two years. That is quite a journey. And it led to the biggest achievement of his career so far.

Lena

At the end of year two, Daniel was promoted to project designer. The achievement felt even bigger because it came after the hard year. His manager said the promotion was an achievement earned through consistency, not luck. And Daniel told me the achievement mattered less than the person he had become while earning it.

Maya

That is a beautiful way to look at it. You know, I asked Daniel what advice he would give to someone starting out, and his answer was basically a masterclass in our seven words.

Lena

I love that. Let's hear Daniel's advice.

Maya

Lesson one: set your aim early, but let it change. Daniel said his first aim was just to get hired, and his later aim was to lead. An aim is not a prison; it is a direction. If your aim never changes, you may be aiming too low.

Lena

That is wise. And lesson two?

Maya

Apply for the jobs that scare you a little. Daniel told me that every time he applied for a role he felt unqualified for, he grew. When you apply for something big, you prepare harder and you learn faster. He still applies for new challenges inside the company every year.

Lena

Lesson three has to be about people, right? The architect and the administrator.

Maya

Exactly. Find an architect for your career, a mentor who designs your growth the way an architect designs a building. And be grateful for every administrator who keeps the system running. Daniel still thanks Tom, the administrator from his first day, every time he sees him.

Lena

People remember kindness. What about access?

Maya

Lesson four: when you get access, use it to lift others. Daniel now gives new hires access to his own notes and templates. He says access to knowledge should flow, not sit in a folder. The access he received became the access he shares.

Lena

And achievement?

Maya

Lesson five: let each achievement fuel the next one, but don't chase achievement for its own sake. Daniel keeps his competition award on his desk, but he says the real achievement was the discipline he built while earning it. An achievement on a shelf fades; the habits behind the achievement stay.

Lena

And the ambassador?

Maya

Lesson six: when you succeed, become an ambassador for others. Daniel visits universities because someone once believed in him. An ambassador gives back, and giving back teaches you more than any course. His final lesson: every career should end with you becoming an ambassador for the next person.

Lena

That is genuinely moving. Let's recap his journey with our seven words. Daniel set an aim, a goal or purpose, and that aim guided every step. He decided to apply for a job, to make a formal request, and he applied for twelve roles before one clicked.

Maya

He met an architect, a person who designs buildings, and the architect became his mentor. The office administrator, the person who manages the system, made his first day warm and welcoming.

Lena

He was given access, the right or opportunity to use the network, and that access unlocked his learning. He earned an achievement, something good he had done, when he won the design competition. And he became an ambassador, a person representing his firm at universities.

Maya

His story shows that a career is built one step at a time: a clear aim, the courage to apply for what you want, mentors like a good architect, support from people like an administrator, the access to learn, the pride of an achievement, and finally the honor of becoming an ambassador for your work.

Lena

And if any of these words still feel new, that is exactly what the Muse English app is for. You'll meet them again tomorrow in your shadowing story and quiz. Until then, keep building, keep learning, and remember: your aim today shapes your achievement tomorrow.

See you next time on Muse English. Bye for now.

Bye.

📝Grammar: Modals of Deduction in the Past (must have, can't have)

When we guess about the past, we use a fixed pattern: **modal + have + past participle**. It turns a clue into a conclusion. **must have** + past participle means you are almost sure it happened. *The ground is wet. It **must have rained** last night.* **might have**, **may have**, and **could have** + past participle mean it was possible. *He **might have missed** the train.* Your guess is open, not certain. **can't have** and **couldn't have** + past participle mean you are almost sure it did NOT happen. *She **can't have forgotten** the meeting.
