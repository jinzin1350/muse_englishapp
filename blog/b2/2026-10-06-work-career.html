📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
burnout /BURN-out/
noun
extreme tiredness caused by working too hard for too long
"After six months of seventy-hour weeks, she hit total burnout and took a month off."
dedication /de-duh-KAY-shun/
noun
the quality of working hard and giving your time and energy because you care
"His dedication to the project impressed the whole team."
multitask /MUL-tee-task/
verb
to do two or more things at the same time
"Most managers have to multitask between meetings, emails, and calls all day."
workload /WURK-lohd/
noun
the amount of work a person has to do
"The new system helped us share the workload more fairly."
perk /PURK/
noun
an extra benefit you get from your job, besides your pay (informal)
"One nice perk of the job is free lunch every Friday."
network /NET-wurk/
verb
to meet and connect with people who can help you in your career
"She networks at industry events to find new clients."
pivot /PIH-vut/
verb
to make a big change in direction, especially in your career or business
"After ten years in banking, he pivoted to teaching."
thrive /THRYVE/
verb
to grow, develop, and become very successful
"She really thrives in a busy, creative office."
delegate /DEH-luh-gayt/
verb
to give part of your work or responsibility to someone else
"A good manager knows how to delegate tasks to the right people."
milestone /MYLE-stohn/
noun
an important event or achievement in your work or life
"Launching the app was a huge milestone for our small team."

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome back to Word Kitchen, everyone. I'm Maya.

Lena

And I'm Lena. Today's episode is all about work and career, and we have ten really useful words that describe modern working life.

Maya

Ten words, one big theme, and a story that might sound very familiar to anyone with a job. Let's start with the first word, burnout.

Lena

Burnout. It's a noun, and it means extreme tiredness caused by working too hard for too long. Not just being tired after a long day. Burnout is deeper than that.

Maya

Exactly. You can sleep all weekend and still feel empty inside. That's burnout. A friend of mine works in advertising, and last year she hit real burnout. She was answering emails at midnight, skipping lunch, and never saying no to new projects.

Lena

That sounds like a classic recipe for burnout. When did she realize it was burnout and not just normal tiredness?

Maya

She said she stopped caring about work she used to love. She told me, I don't even recognize myself anymore. So her manager sent her to see a coach who works with people dealing with burnout, and the first thing the coach said was, you have been ignoring your limits for two years.

Lena

That is such an important lesson. Burnout doesn't arrive overnight. It builds up slowly, and by the time you notice it, you are already exhausted. I think the fear of burnout actually changed how a lot of people think about work.

Maya

It really did. After the pandemic, everyone started talking about burnout openly. Companies started offering mental health days because they finally understood that burnout costs them good employees.

Lena

Okay, word number two, dedication. This is a positive one. Dedication is a noun, and it means the quality of working hard and giving your time and energy because you care deeply about something.

Maya

Dedication is what makes great teams. My brother is a nurse, and his dedication is incredible. He stays late when a patient needs him, not because anyone asks him to, but because his dedication to his patients is personal.

Lena

That's a beautiful example. Dedication like that can't be forced. You either feel it or you don't. I also think dedication is what separates people who just do a job from people who truly love their work.

Maya

Yes. And dedication gets noticed. When I was at my first job, there was a woman whose dedication stood out immediately. She learned every new system, helped new people without being asked, and her dedication earned her a promotion within a year.

Lena

So dedication is basically the opposite of burnout in some ways. Burnout comes from giving too much without rest. Dedication is giving a lot because you want to. But even dedication needs boundaries.

Maya

That's so true. Dedication without rest can turn into burnout. So the secret is dedication plus balance. Speaking of balance, our third word is multitask. It's a verb, and it means to do two or more things at the same time.

Lena

We all multitask every day. I multitask every morning. I answer messages while drinking coffee and listening to the news. The question is whether multitasking actually works.

Maya

Studies say our brains don't really multitask. We just switch between tasks very quickly, which causes more mistakes. But some jobs demand it, like receptionists who multitask all day.

Lena

I try not to multitask when the work needs real focus. If I'm writing, I put my phone in another room. But during boring tasks, I don't mind. I listen to podcasts while I do routine admin work. That's my favorite way to multitask.

Maya

Ha, same here. Actually, our listeners are probably multitasking right now. Maybe you're listening while cooking or walking. That's fine. Just don't multitask during the quiz at the end.

Lena

Good advice. Word four is workload. It's a noun, and it means the amount of work a person has to do. A heavy workload means too much work. A light workload means an easy week.

Maya

Workload is a word managers love to use in meetings. We need to discuss the team workload. Translation: someone is about to get more work. My cousin's workload doubled when two colleagues left her department, and she had to absorb all their projects.

Lena

That's rough. An unfair workload is one of the biggest causes of stress at work. I once had a workload of three people's jobs during a busy season, and I barely slept for a month.

Maya

That's awful. A fair workload is so important. Smart companies actually track workload across the team, so one person doesn't drown while another has nothing to do. Sharing the workload evenly is a sign of a healthy workplace.

Lena

Absolutely. And when your workload is reasonable, you can actually enjoy the fifth word, which is perk. Perk is a noun, an informal one, and it means an extra benefit you get from your job, besides your pay.

Maya

Everyone loves a good perk. Free lunch is a classic. My friend's company has an amazing perk. Every employee gets a monthly budget for books and courses. That's a perk that actually helps your career.

Lena

That's a great perk. Some companies offer a gym membership as a perk. Others give extra vacation days as a perk. The best perk I ever had was working from home two days a week. That perk saved me hours of commuting.

Maya

Perks matter more than people think. When two jobs pay the same, the perks decide. A generous perk package can make an ordinary job feel special. But here's the thing. No perk is worth a terrible boss.

Lena

Words to live by. Word six is network, and we're using it as a verb today. To network means to meet and connect with people who can help you in your career.

Maya

Networking feels awkward to some people, but it's just building relationships. When I network, I try to be genuinely curious about people, not just collect contacts. I network at industry events, but I also network online.

Lena

Online is easier for shy people. You can network on professional sites by commenting on posts and sharing useful articles. I try to network with at least one new person every month. You never know where a conversation will lead.

Maya

That's the magic of it. My last job came from someone I met at a conference two years earlier. We stayed in touch, and when a position opened, she called me. That's why people say it's not what you know, it's who you know. And you only know them if you network.

Lena

True. Word seven is pivot. As a verb, it means to make a big change in direction, especially in your career or business.

Maya

Pivot is the word of the decade. So many people pivoted during the last few years. My neighbor was a travel agent for fifteen years, and when travel stopped, she pivoted completely. She learned web design and now she pivots between projects for different clients.

Lena

That's a brave pivot. It takes courage to pivot when you're comfortable. I have a friend who worked in finance for a decade, and he decided to pivot to teaching. People thought he was crazy, but he says it's the best decision he ever made.

Maya

Sometimes you have to pivot because the world changes around you. And sometimes you pivot because you change. Either way, a successful pivot starts with learning new skills.

Lena

Which brings us to word eight, thrive. Thrive is a verb, and it means to grow, develop, and become very successful.

Maya

I love this word. To thrive is the goal. Some people thrive under pressure. Others thrive in calm, quiet environments. The trick is finding where you personally thrive.

Lena

My sister thrives in chaos. She runs events, and the busier it gets, the calmer she becomes. She absolutely thrives when everything is happening at once. I'm the opposite. I thrive when I have long, quiet mornings to think.

Maya

That's such a good point. There's no single way to thrive. A great manager helps each person thrive in their own way. And companies where people thrive have less burnout, by the way.

Lena

Full circle. Word nine is delegate. It's a verb, and it means to give part of your work or responsibility to someone else.

Maya

Learning to delegate is one of the hardest skills for new managers. My first boss could not delegate at all. She did everything herself, worked until nine every night, and then complained about her workload. If she had learned to delegate, everyone would have been happier.

Lena

That's so common. People think if they delegate, the work won't be done well. But when you delegate clearly, with good instructions, people usually surprise you. To delegate well, you have to trust your team.

Maya

And delegating helps people grow. When a manager delegates an important project to you, it's a sign of respect. It says, I trust you. I always tell new leaders, delegate early, delegate often, and watch your team thrive.

Lena

Great advice. Our tenth and final word is milestone. It's a noun, and it means an important event or achievement in your work or life.

Maya

Milestones give us something to celebrate. Getting your first job is a milestone. Finishing a big project is a milestone. My team just hit a huge milestone. We launched our new website after eight months of work.

Lena

Congratulations. That's a real milestone. I remember my first promotion felt like a major milestone. I called my parents immediately. Milestones remind us how far we've come, especially on the hard days.

Maya

They really do. Celebrate small milestones too. Finishing a tough week without burnout, that's a milestone. Learning to delegate instead of doing everything yourself, that's a milestone.

Lena

Okay, let's do a quick review. Maya, give me the ten words.

Maya

Burnout, dedication, multitask, workload, perk, network, pivot, thrive, delegate, and milestone.

Lena

Perfect. And remember, you can find all ten words with their meanings and example sentences in today's lesson in the app. There's also a quiz waiting for you.

Maya

Plus the shadowing story, which uses all ten words again. Listen, repeat, and make these words yours.

Lena

That's it for today's Word Kitchen. Take care of yourselves, manage that workload, enjoy the perks, and keep thriving.

Maya

See you tomorrow, everyone.

📝Grammar: Modals of deduction - might, can't, must

Use **might**, **can't**, and **must** to guess about the present. You base your guess on what you see or know. Use **might** when you think something is possibly true. Your new colleague **might** be tired; her workload looks very heavy. Use **can't** when you are sure something is impossible. He **can't** be on holiday; his dedication keeps him at his desk every day. Use **must** when you are sure something is true.
