📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
automate /AW-tuh-mayt/
verb
to make a process run by machines or software without people doing it
"The company automated its billing system last year."
innovate /IN-uh-vayt/
verb
to introduce new ideas or methods
"Small startups often innovate faster than big corporations."
obsolete /ob-suh-LEET/
adjective
no longer useful because something newer exists
"Fax machines are almost obsolete now."
seamless /SEEM-liss/
adjective
smooth and continuous, with no problems or breaks
"The app offers a seamless connection between your phone and laptop."
breakthrough /BRAYK-throo/
noun
an important discovery or development
"The vaccine was a major breakthrough in medicine."
malfunction /mal-FUHNGK-shuhn/
verb
to stop working correctly
"The printer malfunctioned during the meeting."
accessible /ak-SESS-uh-buhl/
adjective
easy for everyone to use or reach
"Good design makes technology accessible to everyone."
intuitive /in-TOO-uh-tiv/
adjective
easy to understand without explanation
"The new interface is so intuitive that no manual is needed."
compatible /kuhm-PAT-uh-buhl/
adjective
able to work well together
"This charger is compatible with most phones."
streamline /STREEM-lyne/
verb
to make a process simpler and faster
"We streamlined the checkout process to save time."

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome back to Word Kitchen, everyone! I'm Maya.

Lena

And I'm Lena. Today we're talking about something that touches every single part of our lives — technology.

Maya

That's right. We've got ten delicious words today, all about tech. Words that describe how machines and software change the way we work, shop, and relax.

Lena

And I have to confess something. Ten years ago, technology scared me a little. Now I can't even make coffee without asking my smart speaker for the weather first.

Maya

Ha! Same here. I got a new phone last week, and I spent the whole evening just exploring it. So let's start with our first word: automate. When a company decides to automate a process, it means machines or software take over work that people used to do by hand.

Lena

Oh, that's everywhere now. My local grocery store decided to automate the checkout. Now there are six self-checkout machines and only one human cashier, and she mostly just helps confused customers.

Maya

Right. And car factories automate their assembly lines. Banks automate fraud detection. Hospitals automate appointment reminders. The idea is to let people focus on creative, human work while software handles the repetitive stuff.

Lena

But here's the big question everyone asks: when we automate jobs, do we create better jobs or just destroy old ones?

Maya

It's the debate of our time. When companies automate boring, repetitive tasks, workers can move into more interesting roles. But it also means some jobs simply disappear, and not everyone can retrain easily.

Lena

Okay, word two: innovate. To innovate means to introduce new ideas or methods. And honestly, this is my favorite kind of company — the ones that innovate instead of copying.

Maya

Mine too. The companies that innovate are the ones that surprise us. Think about how mobile phones looked twenty years ago — they were basically bricks with buttons.

Lena

They were! Then someone decided to innovate and put a full touchscreen on a phone, and suddenly everything changed.

Maya

Exactly. Small startups often innovate faster than big corporations because they can take risks. They don't have ten layers of managers saying no to every crazy idea.

Lena

And when you truly innovate, you don't just improve things a little — you change the game completely. You make people rethink what's possible.

Maya

But here's the sad truth: companies that fail to innovate get left behind. Remember video rental stores? They refused to innovate, and streaming services replaced them in just a few years.

Lena

Ouch. So the lesson is: innovate or disappear.

Maya

Pretty much. Which brings us to a slightly sad word for old gadgets: obsolete. Something obsolete is no longer useful because something newer and better exists.

Lena

Like my old DVD player. It's completely obsolete now. It just sits in a closet collecting dust.

Maya

Or fax machines. They're almost obsolete. I used to send faxes at my first job, and now young people don't even know what a fax machine is.

Maya

The funny thing is, today's amazing invention becomes tomorrow's obsolete gadget. Everything in tech moves so fast that nothing stays new for long.

Maya

Okay, word four: seamless. Seamless means smooth and continuous, with no problems, breaks, or interruptions.

Lena

Yes! The best technology feels seamless. You start watching a movie on your phone on the train, and it continues seamlessly on your TV when you get home.

Maya

I love that feeling. When my earbuds connect seamlessly to my laptop, I feel like I'm living in the future.

Lena

Companies work incredibly hard to create a seamless experience, because the moment something feels clunky or confusing, users leave and never come back.

Maya

Right. A seamless connection between all your devices is the dream. Nobody wants to restart their router five times a day or type in a password seventeen times.

Lena

But seamless can break, too. Last month my phone did a software update overnight, and in the morning nothing was seamless anymore. My watch wouldn't connect, my earbuds were silent — chaos.

Maya

Okay, word five: breakthrough. A breakthrough is an important discovery or development — one of those big moments that changes everything.

Lena

Like when scientists announced the breakthrough in battery technology. Suddenly electric cars could drive twice as far on one charge.

Maya

Or the big breakthrough in voice recognition. Ten years ago, talking to your phone was a joke — it never understood you. Now it actually works, most of the time.

Lena

Every breakthrough starts with someone refusing to accept the limits everyone else accepts. That's what I love about this word.

Maya

And behind every breakthrough, there are a thousand failures nobody talks about. For every success story, there are years of experiments that went nowhere.

Lena

True. But even small breakthroughs count. Last week I finally figured out how to automate my monthly reports, and it felt like a personal breakthrough.

Maya

I love that. Okay, word six: malfunction. To malfunction means to stop working correctly. And oh, do I have stories.

Lena

Don't we all. My printer decided to malfunction right before an important meeting last month. It just started printing blank pages, one after another.

Maya

Classic. My smart speaker malfunctioned during a dinner party and started playing heavy metal at full volume while we were eating dessert.

Lena

That's the thing about technology — when it works, it's pure magic. When it malfunctions, it's either comedy or tragedy, sometimes both.

Maya

I think every new gadget will malfunction at the worst possible moment. It's practically a law of nature. New phone on the day of a job interview? It will malfunction.

Lena

My advice is the classic one: turn it off and turn it on again. It fixes a malfunction about half the time, and the other half you just buy a new one.

Maya

Words of wisdom. Word seven: accessible. Accessible means easy for everyone to use or reach. This one matters a lot to me.

Lena

Same here. Good design makes technology accessible to everyone — including older people, kids, and people with disabilities.

Maya

Exactly. When apps have big buttons and clear menus, they become accessible to my grandmother. When they don't, she gives up and calls me for help.

Lena

And accessible doesn't just mean easy to use. It also means affordable. A phone isn't truly accessible if only rich people can buy it.

Maya

Great point. The best companies think about making their products accessible from day one, not as an afterthought.

Lena

Voice control is a perfect example. It made smartphones accessible to people who can't see the screen well. That's a real breakthrough in accessibility.

Maya

Beautifully said. Word eight: intuitive. Intuitive means easy to understand without any explanation. This is the gold standard for design.

Lena

Yes! The new interface is so intuitive that no manual is needed. You just pick it up, tap around, and it makes sense.

Maya

My favorite apps are intuitive. I never read the instructions — I just explore, and everything is where I expect it to be.

Lena

When designers innovate well, the result feels intuitive. You don't think about the technology at all. It just disappears into the background.

Maya

But when something isn't intuitive, you feel stupid, even though it's completely the designer's fault. Bad design blames the user.

Lena

My grandmother versus my nephew is the perfect test. If both of them can use an app without help, it's truly intuitive.

Maya

That's the ultimate test. Word nine: compatible. Compatible means able to work well together. This one causes me real pain.

Lena

Tell me about it. I bought a charger that wasn't compatible with my phone, and I had to drive all the way back to the store to return it.

Maya

Or when your old files aren't compatible with the new software version. Hours of work, suddenly unreadable. That hurts.

Lena

That's why standards matter so much. When everything is compatible, life feels seamless. When it's not, it's pure chaos.

Maya

I always check that my devices are compatible before I buy anything new now. Lesson learned the hard way, twice.

Lena

I heard some countries now require all phones to use the same charger type, so everything stays compatible. That's smart regulation.

Maya

Very smart. And our tenth and final word: streamline. To streamline means to make a process simpler and faster by removing unnecessary steps.

Lena

I love this word. We streamlined our checkout process at work last quarter, and customers noticed the difference immediately.

Maya

Companies streamline everything — hiring, shipping, customer service. The goal is always to remove the boring, repetitive steps.

Lena

I try to streamline my mornings, too. Coffee, news, gym — no wasted time, no decisions to make before I'm fully awake.

Maya

And honestly, the best way to streamline any workflow is often to automate the repetitive parts first. Let software do the boring work.

Lena

Full circle! We started with automate and we're ending with streamline. So let's do a quick recap of all ten words. Maya, hit me.

Maya

automate — let machines or software do the repetitive work for you.

Lena

innovate — introduce new ideas and change the game.

Maya

obsolete — old tech that something newer has replaced.

Lena

seamless — smooth and continuous, with no breaks or problems.

Maya

breakthrough — a big, important discovery or development.

Lena

malfunction — when technology stops working correctly.

Maya

accessible — easy for everyone to use and reach.

Lena

intuitive — easy to understand with no explanation needed.

Maya

compatible — able to work well together.

Lena

streamline — make a process simpler and faster.

Maya

And notice how they all connect. Companies innovate to create a breakthrough. That breakthrough makes old tools obsolete.

Lena

Then they automate the new process, streamline the workflow, and try to make everything seamless, compatible, and intuitive.

Maya

And if they do it right, the result is accessible to everyone — until the day it malfunctions, of course.

Lena

Ha! There's always a malfunction waiting around the corner. Well, that's our show for today. Thanks for cooking up words with us in the Word Kitchen.

Try to use these ten words this week — describe the tech in your life with them. We'll see you next time. Bye!

📝Grammar: Past and present habits and states (used to)

Use **used to** + infinitive for past habits and states that are no longer true. Most factories **used to** do everything by hand before they learned to **automate** production. In negatives and questions, use **didn't use to** and **did you use to**. **Did** offices **use to** print everything before email made paper **obsolete**? There is no present form of **used to** — never say "I use to". For present habits, use the present simple with an adverb of frequency like **usually** or **normally**. You may also use the past simple for repeated past actions. But use only the past simple — nev
