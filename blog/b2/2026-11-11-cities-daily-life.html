پادکست: شهر و زندگی روزمره

گفت‌وگوی امروز را گوش کنید.

📝 متن پادکست

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I am Maya, and today Lena and I are talking about cities and daily life, and about settling into a new home in a busy city.

Lena

Hello, everyone. I am Lena. My cousin Mina has just moved to a new flat, and her first week sounds like a perfect story for today. She has a small bathroom, a dark basement under the building, and neighbours who are still strangers to her.

Maya

Moving city is exciting, but daily life quickly becomes real. On her first morning, what happened?

Lena

Her alarm clock rang at six, but she turned the alarm clock off and fell asleep again. The second time the alarm clock rang, she jumped out of bed, ran to the bathroom, and washed her face with cold water.

Maya

I know that feeling. My alarm clock is my enemy and my best friend at the same time. If my alarm clock does not ring, I miss my bus, my class, and sometimes my breakfast too.

Lena

Mina said the bathroom in her new flat is tiny. There is a shower, a small mirror, and just enough space for one person in the bathroom at a time.

Maya

A tiny bathroom can still work well if it is clean and organised. Did she like the bathroom, or did the bathroom annoy her?

Lena

At first she complained about the bathroom, because the bathroom light was weak. Then she bought a brighter light, cleaned the bathroom tiles, and suddenly the bathroom felt fresh and calm.

Maya

That is a good lesson for city life. Small changes matter. What about the basement? Many city buildings have a basement for storage.

Lena

Her building has a long basement with old bikes, boxes, and winter coats. The basement smells a little damp, and the basement stairs are narrow, so she carries only one box down to the basement at a time.

Maya

I would be nervous in a dark basement alone at night. Is the basement safe?

Lena

Yes, the basement has a strong door and a new light now. Mina keeps her suitcase in the basement, her extra chairs in the basement, and she says the basement is actually very useful, even if the basement is not pretty.

Maya

Speaking of useful places in a city, Mina found something wonderful near her street, did she not?

Lena

She did. Two streets away there is an art gallery, and next door to the art gallery there is an adult education centre. She walked past the art gallery on Tuesday and saw bright paintings in the window.

Maya

An art gallery can make a neighbourhood feel alive. Did she go inside the art gallery?

Lena

She went inside the art gallery on Saturday morning. The art gallery was free that day, and a guide at the art gallery explained the photos of old city streets. Mina loved the art gallery so much that she wants to take a photography class.

Maya

And that is where the adult education centre comes in, right?

Lena

Exactly. The adult education centre offers evening classes for people who work during the day. At the adult education centre you can learn photography, cooking, computer skills, and languages. Mina signed up at the adult education centre for a photography course.

Lena

Mina said the staff at the adult education centre were kind. They did not bother to rush her when she asked many questions, and they gave her a map of the art gallery and the adult education centre building.

Maya

You used one of our words there in a natural way. To bother means to make an effort. People often say, do not bother, when they mean, do not trouble yourself.

Lena

Yes, and Mina is learning when to bother and when to rest. On her first evening, she did not bother to cook a big dinner. She just made soup.

Maya

Let me guess. She ate the soup from a bowl, sitting by the window, watching the city.

Lena

Exactly right. She filled a big blue bowl with tomato soup, put the bowl on the small table, and ate slowly. That bowl was a gift from her mother, so the bowl makes the new flat feel like home.

Lena

Mina broke a bowl on moving day, sadly. The bowl fell in the kitchen, and pieces went everywhere. Now she has only two bowls left, one bowl for soup and one bowl for salad.

Maya

Before we continue with Mina and her busy kitchen, let me share something that can help your daily learning. Lena

That is such a good habit. A few minutes every morning, maybe while your alarm clock is still ringing in your head, can change your English over time.

Maya

Now, back to Mina. How is she managing her mornings now?

Lena

Much better. She sets her alarm clock the night before, and she puts the alarm clock far from her bed, so she must stand up when the alarm clock rings. She goes straight to the bathroom, brushes her teeth in the bathroom, and plans her day.

Lena

She does bother to make breakfast now, because she learned that if she does not bother, she feels tired by ten. A bowl of oats, a bowl of fruit, or eggs and toast give her energy. She says, if I bother to start well, the whole day feels easier.

Maya

I like that idea. To bother is not negative here. It means to take the trouble to do something good. If you bother to practise English, if you bother to read, if you bother to speak, you improve.

Lena

And if you do not bother, nothing changes. Mina did not bother to explore her neighbourhood at first, because she was tired after moving. Then one walk changed everything. She found the art gallery, she found the adult education centre, and she met a neighbour in the basement who helped her carry a box.

Lena

His name is Sam. He was taking his bike out of the basement when Mina was struggling with a heavy box on the basement stairs. He carried the box to the basement for her, showed her where the basement lights were, and told her which day the basement is cleaned.

Lena

Sam volunteers at the art gallery on weekends. He told Mina that the art gallery has a free evening every month, and that students from the adult education centre can show their photos inside the art gallery in spring.

Lena

She is already practising. She took photos of her bathroom window in morning light, of a bowl of oranges on her table, and even of the basement door, which looks mysterious in black and white.

Maya

Smart. And what about her evenings at the adult education centre?

Lena

Mina laughed at that, because her own alarm clock is loud and ugly, but she will never throw that alarm clock away. It woke her for her first class, her first gallery visit, and her first week at work.

Maya

Before we finish Mina's story, here is a second reminder from me, and then Lena will tell you how the week ended. Lena

Thank you, Maya. So, how did Mina's first week end? On Sunday, she invited Sam and two classmates from the adult education centre to her flat. She made a big bowl of pasta, then a second bowl of salad, and they talked about the art gallery exhibition.

Maya

Did she bother to tidy the basement and the bathroom before her guests arrived?

Lena

She did bother to tidy everything. She cleaned the bathroom until it shone, she put extra boxes neatly in the basement, she set her alarm clock early to buy bread, and she placed a bowl of flowers, well, a bowl with flowers in water, in the middle of the table.

Maya

A bowl of flowers is a lovely centrepiece. It shows a bowl is not only for food.

Lena

Her guests loved the flat. They said the bathroom was bright, the basement was useful, and the neighbourhood, with its art gallery and its adult education centre, was perfect for a new start.

Maya

So Mina's daily life now has a rhythm. Alarm clock, bathroom, bowl of breakfast, work, class at the adult education centre, sometimes the art gallery, and home again.

Lena

Yes, and she told me, I do not bother to worry about being perfect in this city anymore. I just bother to try, every single day. That is her motto now.

Maya

That is a motto for English learners too. Do not worry about being perfect. Bother to listen, bother to speak, bother to repeat the shadowing story, and your English will grow.

Lena

And remember the small words of daily life. An alarm clock starts your day, a bathroom wakes you up, a bowl holds your food, a basement holds your extra things, an art gallery feeds your eyes, and an adult education centre feeds your mind.

Maya

Beautifully said, Lena. Thanks for listening today. Practise these words in your own sentences about your city and your daily life, and we will see you tomorrow with a fresh lesson.

Lena

Goodbye for now, and do not forget to set your alarm clock tonight.

📝گرامر: Used To, Would, Be Used To and Get Used To

Used to plus the base verb describes a past habit or state that is not true now. I used to live near an art gallery, but now my flat, with its small bathroom and basement, is across the city.

Would plus the base verb describes a repeated past action, not a state. Every Sunday we would visit the art gallery and eat soup from a big bowl afterwards.

For past states with verbs like be, have, live and like, use only used to. Say I used to have an alarm clock in the bathroom, never I would have an alarm clock as a state.

Be used to plus -ing or a no
