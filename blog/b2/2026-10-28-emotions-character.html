📚 Vocabulary

7 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
able /AY-bul/
adjective
having the skill or power to do something
"I am able to speak three languages."
abrupt /uh-BRUPT/
adjective
sudden and unexpected, often rude
"His abrupt departure surprised everyone."
absolutely /ab-suh-LOOT-lee/
adverb
completely, totally, or definitely
""Are you sure?" "Absolutely!""
age /AYJ/
noun
how old a person or thing is
"What is your age? I am ten."
aggressive /uh-GRES-iv/
adjective
behaving in an angry or violent way
"His aggressive behavior scared everyone."
ambitious /am-BISH-us/
adjective
Having a strong desire to succeed.
"She is an ambitious student who wants to go to university."
anger /ANG-ger/
noun
A strong feeling of being annoyed or unhappy.
"He felt a lot of anger about the unfair decision."

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I am Maya, and this is Lena. Today we are talking about emotions and character, and about seven useful words that describe how people feel and how people act.

Lena

That sounds like a great topic, Maya. We all feel strong emotions every day, and we all meet people with very different characters. So let us start with a story from real life.

Maya

Good idea. Last year, I met a young woman named Sara. Sara was only twenty years old, but she was already able to speak three languages. She was able to lead a small team at work, and she was able to solve problems calmly when things went wrong.

Lena

Wow, that is impressive. At such a young age, she was already able to do so much. What made her special?

Maya

I think Sara was ambitious. She was an ambitious student at university, and she stayed ambitious after she started working. An ambitious person wants to grow, wants to learn, and wants to reach big goals. Sara was ambitious, but in a kind and friendly way.

Lena

I like that. Being ambitious does not mean being cold or selfish. It means you have a clear dream, and you work hard for it. Do you remember what Sara wanted to do?

Maya

Yes. She wanted to start her own small business. She said, I am ambitious, and I want to build something useful for my community. She worked every evening after her job, and she never gave up, even when she was tired.

Lena

Thanks, Maya. Friends, if you love learning English the way we do it here, try the Muse English app. It is free, and it gives you new words every day with photos and clear pronunciation, plus this podcast, fun shadowing stories, quick quizzes, grammar lessons, and a leaderboard where you can earn points. Download it today and keep your English growing, one day at a time.

Lena

That is the good side of being ambitious. But sometimes ambition can turn into anger, right? When people feel blocked, they can feel a lot of anger.

Maya

Exactly. Let us talk about anger for a moment. Anger is a strong feeling. We feel anger when something is unfair, or when someone treats us badly. A little anger can help us say, this is not right. But a lot of anger can hurt us and hurt other people.

Lena

That is true. I remember a day at my old office. A manager gave an abrupt answer to a colleague, and the whole room went quiet. His abrupt tone surprised everyone, because he was usually polite. That abrupt moment changed the mood of the entire meeting.

Maya

An abrupt change like that is hard to handle. Abrupt means sudden and unexpected. When a person is abrupt, they speak quickly and sharply, and they do not explain. An abrupt message can sound rude, even if the person does not mean to be rude.

Lena

So what did your colleague do after that abrupt answer? Did she feel anger?

Maya

She did feel anger, yes. She felt anger because she had worked hard on the project, and the abrupt answer made her work feel small. But she did not show her anger in an aggressive way. She stayed calm, and later she asked the manager for a private talk.

Lena

That was wise. When anger turns aggressive, things get worse. Aggressive behavior means acting in an angry or forceful way, and it often scares people. An aggressive voice, an aggressive email, or aggressive body language can break trust in a team.

Maya

Absolutely. And here is something important: being angry is not the same as being aggressive. You can feel anger and still speak kindly. You can say, I feel anger about this decision, and I want to talk about it. That is honest, and it is not aggressive at all.

Lena

Absolutely, I agree with you. Let us pause here for a quick word about something that can help every listener with words like these.

Maya

Wait, Lena, before the break, let me add one thing. Age does not decide character. A young person can be wise, and an older person can still learn new ways to handle anger. Your age is just a number. What matters is how you act.

Maya

Now, back to our words. We talked about anger, and about being abrupt and aggressive. Let us look at the other side: calm, able, and ambitious people.

Lena

Yes. Think about a teacher who is able to explain a hard idea in a simple way. That teacher is able to listen, able to wait, and able to help every student. Being able to listen is a real skill, just like being able to speak well.

Maya

And think about an ambitious athlete. An ambitious athlete trains every morning, even in winter. That athlete is ambitious, but also patient. Ambition without patience can turn into anger when results come slowly.

Lena

That is so true. I once knew an ambitious young man who wanted a promotion in six months. When it did not happen, he felt anger, and he sent an abrupt email to his boss. The email was abrupt and a little aggressive, and it hurt his chances instead of helping them.

Maya

You are right, Lena. Friends, this is Maya. The Muse English app is waiting for you, and it is free. Every day you get fresh words with photos and pronunciation, this podcast, shadowing stories you can speak along with, quizzes, grammar lessons, and points on the leaderboard as you learn. Maya

Oh no. That is a classic mistake. When anger takes over, we act in an abrupt way, and we sound aggressive, even when we do not want to. The better move is to pause, breathe, and then speak.

Lena

Absolutely. And age can help here. With age comes experience. At a young age, we react fast. With more age and more practice, we learn to slow down. Of course, age alone does not fix everything, but age plus reflection is powerful.

Maya

Let us also talk about the word absolutely, because we use it all the time. Absolutely means completely or definitely. If someone asks, are you sure, you can answer, absolutely. It is a strong, friendly way to say yes.

Lena

Absolutely! I use it every day. Absolutely, I will help you. Absolutely, we can meet tomorrow. It sounds warm and confident, much warmer than a short yes.

Maya

And it works in serious talks too. If a friend asks, do you believe in me, you can say, absolutely. That one word can change how a person feels. It shows full support, with no doubt at all.

Lena

That is beautiful. Now, let us bring all seven words together in one little story. Imagine a woman named Nina. Nina is ambitious, and she is able to run a busy shop. One morning, a supplier gives her an abrupt phone call, and she feels anger rising.

Maya

Nina feels anger, but she does not become aggressive. She takes a deep breath. She says to herself, I am able to handle this. I am not going to be abrupt in return. I am going to stay calm and clear.

Lena

Then Nina calls the supplier back. She speaks politely, but firmly. She says, I felt anger about the sudden change, but I want us to find a solution. The supplier apologizes, and they agree on a new plan. Nina smiles and thinks, absolutely, this is how strong people solve problems.

Maya

I love that story, Lena. Nina shows character. She is ambitious, she is able to stay calm, she avoids being abrupt, she controls her anger, and she never turns aggressive. And she does it at her age, with grace and humor.

Lena

And that brings us to a second short message, because we want every listener to keep learning words like these every single day.

Maya

Before we recap, one last thought. Character is built in small moments. When you feel anger, you choose your answer. When a message is abrupt, you choose a kind reply. When you feel ambitious, you choose steady work over quick wins.

Lena

Yes, and being able to pause is a skill. You are able to learn it, at any age, with practice. Absolutely, it gets easier every time you try.

Lena

Now let us recap our seven words one more time, slowly, so you can remember them.

Maya

Able means having the skill or power to do something. If you are able to do it, you can do it. Ambitious means having a strong desire to succeed. An ambitious person dreams big and works hard.

Lena

Abrupt means sudden and unexpected, often rude. An abrupt answer can shock people. Anger is a strong feeling of being annoyed or unhappy. Anger is normal, but we must handle it well.

Maya

Aggressive means behaving in an angry or forceful way. Aggressive words can scare people, so we avoid them. Age means how old a person or thing is. Your age does not limit your growth.

Lena

And absolutely means completely or definitely. Absolutely is a warm, strong yes. So, absolutely, you can learn these words. Absolutely, you can use them tomorrow.

Maya

Let us finish with a question for you. Think about your own character. Are you ambitious? Are you able to stay calm when you feel anger? Do you ever sound abrupt without meaning to? Think about it, and try using these words in your own sentences today.

Lena

And remember, at any age, you can grow. You are able to change, you are able to learn, and you are absolutely able to become the person you want to be. Keep practicing, keep listening, and we will see you in the next episode of Muse English.

Maya

Goodbye, everyone, and absolutely keep learning.

📝Grammar: Verbs + Prepositions (depend on, belong to)

Many verbs are followed by a **fixed preposition** before their object. For example, we say **depend on**, **belong to**, and **wait for**. After the preposition, use a noun, a pronoun, or a verb with **-ing**. An ambitious student might apologise **for being** late to class. Some verbs take **no preposition** at all, even when it feels strange. We say **discuss the plan**, not discuss about the plan, and **marry someone**, not marry with someone. A different preposition can change the meaning completely. **Look at** means use your eyes, **look for** means try to find, and **look after** me
