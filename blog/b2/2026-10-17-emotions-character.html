📚 Vocabulary

7 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
resilient /ruh-ZIL-yunt/
adjective
able to recover quickly after problems or difficulties
"Resilient people do not give up after a failure; they learn and try again."
vulnerable /VUL-nuh-ruh-bul/
adjective
easy to hurt physically or emotionally; needing protection
"Newborn babies are vulnerable and need constant care."
charisma /kuh-RIZ-muh/
noun
a special personal quality that attracts people and makes them follow you
"The candidate's charisma won over the whole audience."
perseverance /pur-suh-VEER-unts/
noun
continuing to try even when something is very difficult; not giving up
"Her perseverance through years of training finally earned her a gold medal."
composure /kum-POH-zhur/
noun
staying calm and controlled, even in a stressful situation
"Despite the chaos, the captain kept his composure and gave clear orders."
restless /REST-lus/
adjective
unable to stay still or calm, usually because of worry, boredom, or excitement
"The restless crowd grew impatient as the concert started an hour late."
integrity /in-TEG-ruh-tee/
noun
the quality of being honest and having strong moral principles
"People trust a leader with integrity, even when they disagree with him."

📝Grammar: Mixed Conditionals

**Mixed conditionals** combine two different times. Both the condition and the result are imagined, but one is in the past and the other is in the present. Past condition, present result: use **if + past perfect, would + base verb**, often with now. If she **had shown** more **perseverance**, she **would be** more **resilient** now. Present condition, past result: use **if + past simple, would have + past participle**. If he **were** calmer, he **would have kept** his **composure** in that meeting. You can also use **might** in the result for a possible outcome: If he **had trained** harder
