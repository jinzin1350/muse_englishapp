📚 لغت‌ها

۷ لغت تازه لغت‌های تازه با معنی فارسی، مثال واقعی و تلفظ.
basket /BASS-kit/
اسم · noun
A container made of woven material.
سبد
"She put the fruit in a basket."
colour /KUL-ur/
اسم · noun
The appearance of something from light.
رنگ
"Red is my favourite colour."
credit card /KRED-it KARD/
اسم · noun
A plastic card used to buy things now and pay later.
کارت اعتباری
"I paid with my credit card."
credit limit /KRED-it LIM-it/
اسم · noun
The maximum amount of money you can borrow.
سقف اعتبار
"I reached my credit limit this month."
credit rating /KRED-it RAY-ting/
اسم · noun
An assessment of your ability to repay debts.
رتبه اعتباری
"A good credit rating helps you borrow money."
current account /KUR-unt uh-KOUNT/
اسم · noun
A bank account for daily money transactions.
حساب جاری
"I need to check my current account balance."
department store /dih-PART-munt STOR/
اسم · noun
A large shop selling many types of goods.
فروشگاه بزرگ
"We bought new clothes at the department store."

پادکست: پول و خرید

گفت‌وگوی امروز را گوش کنید.

📝 متن پادکست

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I am Maya, and today Lena and I are talking about money, shopping, and a very busy morning at a department store.

Lena

Hello everyone. I love a department store, but I need a plan. Last Saturday I walked into a department store with an empty basket, and I left that department store with a full basket and a worried smile.

Maya

That sounds familiar. A basket helps you carry things, but a basket also shows you how much you are buying. When my basket is full, I stop and look inside my basket again.

Lena

Exactly. My basket had a blue scarf, a red bag, and a gift for my sister. I chose the colour of the scarf first, because colour matters to her. She loves a bright colour, not a dark colour.

Maya

Colour is powerful in shopping. A shop changes the colour of a display, and suddenly everyone wants it. My favourite colour is green, but in that department store, every colour looked beautiful.

Lena

And then I went to pay. I did not have enough cash, so I used my credit card. I use my credit card for big shopping, and I always keep my credit card safe in my wallet.

Maya

A credit card is useful, but a credit card is not free money. When you pay with a credit card, you borrow money, and later you must pay it back. That is why I check my credit card bill every week.

Lena

You are right. My bank gave me a credit limit, and I must stay under my credit limit. My credit limit is not a target. If I reach my credit limit, my card may stop working.

Maya

A good rule is to use only a small part of your credit limit. If your credit limit is high, it does not mean you should spend it all. Staying far below your credit limit also helps your credit rating.

Lena

Yes, my credit rating is important. A good credit rating helps you borrow money for a car or a home. My credit rating improved when I paid every bill on time, every single month.

Maya

And a bad credit rating can make life expensive. With a bad credit rating, a bank may say no, or charge more. So protect your credit rating, check your credit rating, and build your credit rating slowly.

Lena

I also check my current account every morning. My current account shows my daily money, what comes in and what goes out. When my current account is low, I stop shopping.

Maya

A current account is for daily life. Rent, food, and transport come from my current account. Before I visit a department store, I look at my current account and decide a clear budget.

Lena

That is a smart habit. Shall we take a short break to tell our listeners about something that helps with all of this English?

Maya

Great idea. If you want to learn words like basket, colour, and credit card in a fun and easy way, try the free Muse English app. Every day you get new words with clear photos and pronunciation, this podcast, shadowing stories where you speak along, fun quizzes, grammar lessons, and a leaderboard with points. Install Muse English for free, study a little every day, and watch your English grow step by step with us.

Lena

I use the app on the bus, and the shadowing stories are my favourite part. Now, back to our shopping story. After I paid with my credit card, I checked my current account on my phone right there in the department store.

Maya

And what did your current account say?

Lena

It said I was safe, but close to my limit for the month. Not my credit limit on the card, my own personal limit. I put one item back on the shelf, and my basket became lighter.

Maya

That is the moment a basket teaches you something. An empty basket at the start, a careful basket at the end. I always ask, do I need everything in this basket, or do I just like the colour?

Lena

Colour again! The red bag was beautiful, but I already have a bag in the same colour at home. So the bag stayed in the shop, and only the scarf and the gift went into my basket.

Lena

Can I tell a story about my cousin? She opened a new current account last spring because her old current account charged high fees every month. She compared three banks, she asked about every fee, and she chose a current account that was simple and clear.

Maya

That is an important lesson. Many people never look at their current account fees. A small fee every month becomes a big cost every year. When you choose a current account, read the small print, ask questions, and keep the papers safe at home.

Lena

My cousin also learned about her credit rating at the bank. The bank worker explained that her credit rating was not a mystery number. It was a story about her habits. Did she pay on time? Did she stay under her credit limit? Did she use her credit card with care?

Maya

Exactly, and that story follows you. A landlord may check your credit rating before renting you a flat. A phone company may look at your credit rating too. So a good credit rating opens doors, while careless shopping with a credit card can quietly close them.

Lena

She changed her habits in small steps. She set a reminder for every credit card payment. She kept her basket smaller at the department store. She chose colour with her head, not only with her heart, and she checked her current account every Sunday evening.

Maya

Small steps, big results. After six months, her credit rating was better, her current account was healthier, and she could walk through a department store with a basket in her hand and feel completely calm. She enjoyed the colour, the music, and the windows, without buying everything she liked.

Lena

I love that picture. Shopping should be pleasant, not stressful. A basket should hold what you planned, a credit card should help you, not hurt you, and a credit limit should be a fence that protects your garden, not a wall you crash into.

Maya

And a department store should be a place of choice. You can try a colour, compare a price, ask a worker, and then decide slowly. Nobody in a department store should rush your money. Your current account will thank you when you take your time.

Maya

Let me share another story. My brother wanted a new phone from a department store. The department store offered him a new credit card with a high credit limit on the same day.

Lena

That sounds dangerous. A new credit card, a high credit limit, and a shiny phone in your basket is a risky mix.

Maya

He said no, thank goodness. He checked his current account, he thought about his credit rating, and he waited one month. Later he bought the phone with savings from his current account, and he felt proud.

Lena

Waiting is a superpower. Shops use colour, music, and special offers to make us hurry. A department store is designed to keep you inside, moving from colour to colour and basket to basket.

Maya

So here is our simple system. One, take a basket only when you have a list. Two, choose colour carefully, because colour at home matters more than colour in the shop. Three, if you use a credit card, know your credit limit before you shop.

Lena

Four, protect your credit rating by paying on time. Five, check your current account before and after shopping. And six, enjoy the department store, but let the department store serve you, not control you.

Maya

Before we finish, Lena, tell everyone once more about learning with us every day.

Lena

Of course. Learning English is like saving money in a current account. Small steps every day build a strong future, just like a good credit rating. With the free Muse English app, you learn daily words with photos and pronunciation, listen to this podcast, practise with shadowing stories, test yourself with quizzes, study grammar lessons, and earn points on the leaderboard. Install it for free today, open it tomorrow morning, and make English a happy daily habit.

Maya

Beautifully said. So remember, friends, a basket is just a basket, a colour is just a colour, and a credit card is just a tool. Your credit limit is a boundary, your credit rating is your reputation, your current account is your daily picture, and a department store is a place to practise smart choices.

Lena

Thank you for listening to Muse English. Tell us, what colour is in your basket today, and did you check your current account before you paid with your credit card?

Maya

We will see you tomorrow with new words, new stories, and new practice. Keep learning, keep smiling, and keep your credit rating strong and your basket light. Goodbye for now!

Lena

Goodbye everyone, and happy learning!

Maya

One last tip before we go, friends. At the end of every month, sit down with a cup of tea, open your current account, look at your credit card bill, remember your credit limit, and ask yourself one honest question. Did my basket this month match my plan, or did the colour and the lights of the department store decide for me? That one quiet minute protects your credit rating and your peace.
