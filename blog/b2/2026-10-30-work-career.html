📚 Vocabulary

7 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
arrangement /uh-RAYNJ-ment/
noun
A plan or agreement made for something.
"We made an arrangement to meet on Friday."
banker /BANG-ker/
noun
A person who works in a high position at a bank.
"My uncle is a successful investment banker."
banking /BANG-king/
noun
The business of banks, managing money and finances.
"She studied finance and now works in banking."
be laid off /bee LAYD OFF/
phrasal verb
Lose your job because there is no work.
"Many workers were laid off from the factory."
be made redundant /bee MAYD ree-DUN-dunt/
phrase
Lose your job because it's no longer needed.
"He was made redundant after 20 years."
be offered a job /bee OFF-urd uh JOB/
phrase
Be given the chance to work for a company.
"She was offered a job at the new company."
be offered work /bee OFF-urd WURK/
phrase
Be given the chance to do some work.
"He was offered work for the summer."

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! Today Lena and I are talking about work and careers, and along the way we will learn seven useful words about jobs, banks, and big changes at work.

Lena

I love this topic, Maya, because work is such a big part of life. Most adults spend a large part of every day at work, so it really helps to know the right words when we talk about jobs and careers. So, where should we start?

Maya

Let us start with banking, because my cousin works in banking. Banking is the business of banks, managing money and finances, and it is a huge part of the economy. My cousin studied finance at university, and now she works in banking in the city.

Lena

That sounds interesting. Is she a banker? A banker is a person who works in a high position at a bank, and I always imagine a banker in a smart suit, talking to customers about money.

Maya

Yes, she is a banker, and she loves her job. As a banker, she helps people and small businesses with loans and savings. She says working in banking is busy but rewarding, because every day is different. A good banker needs to be patient, careful, and good with numbers.

Lena

Before we go on, can you tell our listeners about the app?

Maya

I would love to. If you are enjoying this episode, you will love the Muse English app, because it is free and it makes learning English part of your daily life. Every day you get fresh words with photos and pronunciation, plus this podcast, shadowing stories, quizzes, grammar lessons, and a leaderboard where you collect points as you learn. Download the Muse English app today and start learning with us.

Lena

Thank you, Maya. Now, back to work. My cousin is also in banking, and last week we made an arrangement to meet for lunch. An arrangement is a plan or agreement made for something, so our arrangement was simple: we would meet at noon near her office.

Maya

That is a nice arrangement. Did you keep the arrangement, or did something change at the last minute?

Lena

We kept the arrangement, and over lunch she told me some news. Her friend at the bank was laid off last month. To be laid off means to lose your job because there is no work, and it was a shock for everyone in her office.

Maya

That is sad news. When a worker is laid off, it is not because they did a bad job. A person is laid off when the company has no work for them, or when the company needs to save money. It can happen to anyone, even to a very good worker.

Lena

Exactly. Her friend was laid off from the factory office where she worked, and now she is looking for a new job. She told me that being laid off was hard at first, but it also gave her time to think about what she really wants to do next.

Maya

That is a brave way to look at it. And it reminds me of another word that sounds similar but is a little different: to be made redundant. To be made redundant means to lose your job because it is no longer needed. For example, my uncle was made redundant after twenty years at the same company.

Lena

Twenty years! That must have been very hard. What happened? Why was he made redundant?

Maya

The company changed its way of working, and his old job was no longer needed. He was made redundant, but the company gave him some money and time to find something new. Being made redundant is different from being laid off, because the job itself disappears, not just the work for a while.

Lena

I see. So when you are laid off, there is no work at the moment, but when you are made redundant, the job is gone for good. That is a useful difference. Did your uncle find a new job?

Maya

Yes, and here is the happy part. After a few months, he was offered a job at a new company. To be offered a job means to be given the chance to work for a company, and he was so happy when he was offered a job in a new city.

Lena

That is wonderful news. When you are offered a job, it feels like a fresh start. Did he accept it at once, or did he think about it first?

Maya

He thought about it for a weekend, and then he accepted. He was offered a job with better hours and a kind team, and now he enjoys going to work again. Being offered a job after a hard time is one of the best feelings in working life.

Lena

It really is. And speaking of good news, I want to tell our listeners something before we continue.

Maya

Go ahead, Lena.

Lena

If you like learning words like these with us, try the Muse English app. It is free, and every day it gives you new words with clear photos and pronunciation, this podcast, shadowing stories to practise speaking, fun quizzes, grammar lessons, and a leaderboard with points to keep you going. Install the Muse English app for free and learn with us every day.

Maya

Thank you, Lena. Now, where were we? Ah yes, my uncle was offered a job, and that changed everything for him. But not everyone wants a full-time job. Some people prefer shorter work, and that brings us to our last word today.

Lena

Yes, to be offered work. To be offered work means to be given the chance to do some work, and it is often shorter or less formal than a full job. For example, a student can be offered work for the summer, just like my neighbour's son was offered work in a cafe last summer.

Maya

That is a good example. He was offered work for the summer, and he worked three days a week. When you are offered work, you can say yes or no, and you can often choose your hours. It is a great way to earn money and gain experience.

Lena

And it can lead to more. My neighbour's son was offered work in the cafe, he worked hard, and at the end of the summer he was offered a job there full-time. So first he was offered work, and then he was offered a job. That is a lovely story.

Maya

It is, and it reminds me of my friend Dana. Dana worked in banking for five years, and she knew a senior banker who always gave her good advice. When Dana heard that some workers in her office might be laid off, she made an arrangement to meet that banker for coffee and ask what she should do.

Lena

That was a smart arrangement. What did the banker say to her?

Maya

The banker told her to stay calm and to keep learning new skills, because banking changes fast. He said that even if a worker is laid off, new chances come, and a good banker is always in demand. Dana followed his advice, and she made an arrangement to take an evening course in finance.

Lena

Good for her. Did she stay in banking, or did she change her career?

Maya

She stayed in banking, but she moved to a different team. Her old team was closed, and a few people were made redundant, but Dana was lucky. She was not made redundant, and she was not laid off. Instead, she was offered work on a new project, and later she was offered a job leading that project.

Lena

That is a wonderful ending. So Dana made an arrangement to learn, she avoided being laid off, she was offered work, and then she was offered a job. It shows that one good arrangement can change your whole career.

Maya

Exactly. And Dana always says the same thing to young people who want to work in banking: make an arrangement to meet people, ask questions, and never stop learning. A banker who keeps learning will always find work, even in hard times.

Maya

It is. And it shows how these words connect in real life. A banker works in banking, friends make an arrangement to meet, workers can be laid off or made redundant, and then, with luck and effort, they are offered a job or offered work again.

Lena

Let us quickly remember our seven words. An arrangement is a plan or agreement, like our arrangement to meet for lunch. A banker is a person in a high position at a bank. Banking is the business of banks and money.

Maya

To be laid off means to lose your job because there is no work. To be made redundant means to lose your job because it is no longer needed. To be offered a job means to be given the chance to work for a company. And to be offered work means to be given the chance to do some work.

Lena

Perfect. And here is my tip for learners: use these words in your own sentences this week. Talk about an arrangement you made, or about someone who was offered a job. The more you use a word, the better you remember it.

Maya

That is great advice. Thank you for listening to Muse English today. Remember, an arrangement is a plan you make, a banker works in banking, and being laid off or made redundant is never the end of the story, because you can always be offered work or offered a job again. Keep learning, keep practising, and we will see you tomorrow with seven brand new words. Goodbye!

Lena

Goodbye, everyone, and good luck with your work and your English!

📝Grammar: So, Such, So...That and Such...That

Use **so** before an adjective or adverb, and **such** before a noun. **So** makes an adjective stronger, and **such a** or **such** makes a noun phrase stronger. Say **so** + adjective or adverb: *The banker was **so** busy.* Say **such a** + adjective + singular noun: *It was **such a** busy day.* Say **such** + adjective + plural or uncountable noun: *They are **such** kind people.* For amounts, use **so many** or **so few** with plural nouns, and **so much** or **so little** with uncountable nouns. **Such a lot of** works with both plural and uncountable nouns.
