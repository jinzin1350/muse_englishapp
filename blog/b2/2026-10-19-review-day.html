📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
perseverance /pur-suh-VEER-unts/
noun
continuing to try even when something is very difficult; not giving up
"Her perseverance through years of training finally earned her a gold medal."
adventurous /ad-VEN-chuh-russ/
adjective
willing to take risks and try new things
"She is an adventurous traveler."
markup /MARK-up/
noun
the extra amount a store adds to the price of a product above what it paid for it
"The markup on restaurant wine can be three times the original cost."
counterfeit /KOWN-ter-fit/
adjective
fake; made to look real, but not real
"The police seized thousands of counterfeit banknotes at the market."
vulnerable /VUL-nuh-ruh-bul/
adjective
easy to hurt physically or emotionally; needing protection
"Newborn babies are vulnerable and need constant care."
cashback /KASH-bak/
noun
money your credit card company gives back to you after you buy something
"Her credit card gives her two percent cashback on every grocery purchase."
charisma /kuh-RIZ-muh/
noun
a special personal quality that attracts people and makes them follow you
"The candidate's charisma won over the whole audience."
approach /uh-PROHCH/
verb
to come closer to something or someone
"The train is starting to approach the station."
B&B /BEE-und-BEE/
abbreviation, noun
bed and breakfast; a place to sleep and eat breakfast
"We stayed in a nice B&B."
reimburse /ree-im-BURS/
verb
to pay someone back for money they spent, usually for work
"The company will reimburse you for your travel expenses."

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I am Maya, and with me as always is my friend Lena.

Maya

Let us start with your weekend, because I know you did something fun. Were you feeling adventurous?

Lena

Maya

I did. I wanted to be more adventurous, so instead of staying home, I booked a tiny room in a family-run B&B. The B&B was an old house with blue windows, and the owner, Mrs. Allen, greeted me with tea and cake.

Lena

That sounds lovely. Did you travel by train?

Maya

Yes. I sat by the window and watched the fields go by. And as the train began to approach the coast, I could smell the sea. When you approach a new place for the first time, everything feels exciting. I saw the town approach slowly in the distance, with its little harbor and white houses.

Lena

Did you fly at all? I know small planes can be hard for you.

Maya

No flying this time, thank goodness. I get airsick on small planes. Last year, my airsickness was so bad on a short flight that I could not enjoy the trip at all. This time the train was perfect.

Lena

Smart choice. So what did you do in the town?

Maya

On the first day, I walked along the harbor. There was a small accident near the market — a delivery bike fell over — but nobody was hurt, and everyone helped pick up the boxes. It reminded me that even in a quiet town, life happens fast.

Lena

And were you traveling alone? Did you feel safe?

Maya

Mostly, yes, but I will be honest: at night, walking back to the B&B alone, I felt a little vulnerable. Mrs. Allen told me that vulnerable travelers should stay on the main streets after dark, so I did. She also said the town protects vulnerable visitors with good street lights, which made me feel better.

Lena

That is good advice. So, was the trip a success?

Maya

A big one. On the last evening, I joined a walking tour. Our guide had real charisma. With such charisma, she made the old town come alive — every street had a story. I think charisma in a guide matters more than facts. You can read facts in a book, but charisma makes you feel the place.

Lena

I agree completely. Before we continue, let me share something that helps me learn English every single day.

Maya

Go ahead!

Lena

It is the Muse English app. Every morning you get new words with real photos and clear pronunciation audio, just like the words we are reviewing today. You also get this podcast, shadowing stories that train your mouth to speak naturally, quick quizzes, grammar lessons, and a leaderboard where you earn points for daily practice. It is completely free, so install it today and learn with us. Your English will thank you.

Maya

Thank you, Lena! Now it is your turn. You said you did something fun this weekend too.

Lena

I did something brave. I started a tiny online store selling handmade bags.

Maya

Wow! That is ambitious. How is it going?

Lena

It is hard, but exciting. It takes real perseverance to start a business. In the first week, only two people bought anything. My perseverance was tested every day, but I kept going. My friend Sara says perseverance is the difference between people who dream and people who do. I think she is right.

Maya

I admire that. What did you learn about prices?

Lena

So much! First, I learned about markup. When a big store sells a bag, the markup can be huge — sometimes three times the real cost. I decided to keep my markup small and honest. Customers notice a fair markup, and they come back. A big markup might earn more once, but an honest markup builds trust.

Maya

That is smart business. How do you pay for your materials?

Lena

With my credit card, because I earn two percent cashback on every purchase. The cashback really adds up over a month. Last month I earned enough cashback to buy all my fabric for free. If your card offers cashback, use it wisely — it is like a small reward for spending.

Maya

Any problems so far?

Lena

One scary moment. A customer tried to pay with a counterfeit bill at a local market where I had a stand. I had learned how to spot a counterfeit note, so I checked it carefully. It was clearly counterfeit — the paper felt wrong. I refused it politely, and later the police told me counterfeit money is a real problem at markets. Now I only take card payments.

Maya

Good thinking. Starting a business really shows your integrity too.

Lena

Thank you. Integrity matters to me. I would rather earn less than lose my integrity. A shop with integrity keeps customers for years.

Maya

And what about money troubles? Did you ever worry about an overdraft?

Lena

Almost! In the second month, my account nearly went into overdraft because a supplier charged me twice. An overdraft would have cost me extra fees, so I called the bank fast. They fixed it, and they promised to reimburse the extra charge. They said they will reimburse me within three days. When a company makes a mistake, they should reimburse you quickly — that is only fair.

Maya

Did you stay calm through all that?

Lena

I tried. When the supplier overcharged me, I kept my composure. Losing your composure never helps in business. I spoke quietly, showed the receipts, and kept my composure until they agreed to fix the bill. Nobody respects you if you lose your composure, but everyone respects calm strength.

Maya

You sound very resilient.

Lena

I am learning to be resilient. A resilient person bends but does not break. My grandmother was resilient — she raised four kids alone and never complained. I want to be as resilient as she was.

Maya

I love that. Now, tell me — do you ever feel restless, like you want to quit your job and do the store full time?

Lena

Sometimes I feel restless at my desk, dreaming about the store. But a restless feeling is not a plan. I need to save more first — maybe for a mortgage one day, or a bigger apartment for my workshop. Right now my apartment is too small for all the fabric. A bigger apartment with good light would be perfect, and one day, with enough savings, even a small mortgage for a studio.

📝Grammar: Wish and If Only

We use **wish** and **if only** to talk about situations we want to be different. The verb after **wish** moves one step back in time, as in "I wish I had more time" when I do not have enough time now. **If only** means the same as **wish**, but it is stronger and more emotional. "If only I had listened to you!" shows a deep regret. For wishes about the present, use **wish + past simple**: "I wish I knew the answer."
