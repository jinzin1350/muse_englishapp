10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
home truth /HOME TROOTH/
noun phrase
An unpleasant fact about oneself that is true.
"My friend told me some home truths about my spending habits."
hit home /HIT HOME/
idiom
To be fully understood and have a strong effect.
"His words about poverty really hit home."
approachable /uh-PROH-chuh-bul/
adjective
Friendly and easy to talk to.
"Her approachable demeanor made students feel comfortable asking questions."
alarmed /uh-LARMD/
adjective
Frightened or worried.
"The sudden noise left her feeling alarmed."
appease /uh-PEEZ/
verb
To pacify someone by granting their demands.
"He tried to appease his angry boss with an apology."
household word /HOWSS-hold WURD/
noun phrase
A person or thing widely known by everyone.
"Coca-Cola has become a household word globally."
achieve economic growth /uh-CHEEV ee-kuh-NOM-ik GROHTH/
phrase
To increase a country's wealth and prosperity.
"Governments aim to achieve economic growth."
have the time of your life /hav thuh TYME uhv yawr LYFE/
idiom
To enjoy oneself immensely.
"We had the time of our lives at the party."

adjective

"His altruistic actions benefited the entire community."

📝Grammar: The Subjunctive (I insist that he be on time)

The **subjunctive** is a verb form for things **demanded, suggested or necessary**, not facts. It always uses the **base form** of the verb, for every person: *The manager insisted that he **appease** the angry client.* (not *appeases*) It follows **that**-clauses after verbs like **insist, suggest, demand, recommend, propose** and adjectives like **essential, vital, crucial**: *It is **vital that** every leader **be** approachable.* Notice — no *-s*, even with *he / she / it*. Negatives put **not** before the base form: *The lawyer suggested that the report **not contain** any home truths.*
