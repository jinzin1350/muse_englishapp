📚 Vocabulary

7 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
abhor /uhb-HOR/
verb
To intensely dislike or hate something or someone.
"She truly abhors any form of injustice."
alarmed /uh-LARMD/
adjective
Frightened or worried.
"The sudden noise left her feeling alarmed."
altruistic /al-troo-ISS-tik/
adjective
Showing selfless concern for the well-being of others.
"His altruistic actions benefited the entire community."
antipathy /an-TIH-puh-thee/
noun
A deep-seated feeling of dislike or aversion.
"His antipathy towards modern art was well-known."
appease /uh-PEEZ/
verb
To pacify someone by granting their demands.
"He tried to appease his angry boss with an apology."
approachable /uh-PROH-chuh-bul/
adjective
Friendly and easy to talk to.
"Her approachable demeanor made students feel comfortable asking questions."
astute /uh-STOOT/
adjective
Having an ability to accurately assess situations or people.
"An astute businessman, he quickly spotted the market opportunity."

Podcast: Emotions & Character

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I'm Maya.

Lena

And I'm Lena. Today we're diving into the vocabulary of emotions and character, with seven powerful words: abhor, alarmed, altruistic, antipathy, appease, approachable, and astute.

Maya

These are the words that take you beyond happy, sad, and angry. They let you describe exactly how people feel and who they are.

Maya

Think of the last time someone surprised you: a stranger's unexpected kindness, or a friend's sudden sharp temper. We are constantly reading feelings and character, but most learners reach for the same flat words: happy, angry, nice.

Lena

And at C2, flat words won't do. Saying you felt worried is fine, but saying you felt alarmed tells the listener the worry hit you suddenly. Saying someone is nice is fine, but calling her approachable and altruistic paints a whole person. That's the game today: precision.

Lena

Let's begin with the strongest emotion word. Maya, what does it mean to abhor something?

Maya

To abhor is to intensely dislike or hate something. Someone who abhors cruelty feels sick just hearing about it. She truly abhors any form of injustice, so she spends her weekends volunteering for human rights groups.

Lena

That's a strong word. So if I say I abhor lies, I mean I can't tolerate dishonesty at all.

Maya

Exactly. People who abhor violence refuse to watch violent films. They abhor it completely. And the noun is abhorrence, though our focus word is the verb abhor.

Lena

Quick pause. Want these words in your pocket, not just in your ears? The Muse English app gives you daily words with photos and pronunciation, this very podcast, shadowing stories, quizzes, grammar lessons, and points on the leaderboard. It is completely free to install. Open it after this episode and lock in today's seven words. Your future self will thank you.

Lena

Now a related feeling: antipathy. His antipathy towards modern art was well-known, as our lesson example says.

Maya

Antipathy is a deep-seated feeling of dislike or aversion. It's quieter than hatred but very real. You can have an antipathy toward cold weather, or toward meetings that start before nine in the morning.

Lena

I have a real antipathy toward alarm clocks. But seriously, some people feel an antipathy toward an entire cuisine.

Maya

And that antipathy can grow over years. If someone abhors cruelty but only feels antipathy toward long flights, you see the difference in intensity. To abhor is fire; antipathy is a cold wind.

Lena

You know what I notice? These words split into two families. Abhor, antipathy, alarmed: those are emotions, things that move through you.

Maya

And altruistic, approachable, astute: those are character traits, the kind of person you are. Emotions visit; character stays. People remember your character long after your mood has passed.

Lena

But the two feed each other. Someone who is deeply altruistic will feel alarmed when others suffer. And someone with a long antipathy toward unfairness may grow into an adult who truly abhors cruelty.

Maya

The astute observer sees the link: feelings shape character, and character shapes feelings. An approachable leader builds a team where people admit mistakes early, because nobody feels alarmed reporting bad news to her.

Lena

I love that. So the man who abhors dishonesty probably feels an antipathy toward office politics too, and his altruistic choices reveal the character behind the emotion.

Lena

Our third word is alarmed. The sudden noise left her feeling alarmed, says the example sentence.

Maya

Alarmed means frightened or worried. It's that jolt when something unexpected happens. Residents were alarmed by the news, and doctors were alarmed by the test results.

Lena

But it doesn't have to be dramatic. You might feel alarmed when you realize you've lost your keys, or alarmed by a strange email from your bank.

Maya

True. An alarmed voice, an alarmed crowd. The word always carries that quick flash of worry. And notice how different it is from our other words: you abhor and you feel antipathy over time, but you feel alarmed in a sudden moment.

Lena

Let's move to character now. Some people just radiate kindness. That's where altruistic comes in.

Maya

Altruistic means showing selfless concern for the well-being of others. His altruistic actions benefited the entire community, our example says.

Lena

An altruistic person donates without expecting thanks. Doctors who work in disaster zones are often driven by altruistic motives.

Maya

And here's the thing: you don't have to be a hero. An altruistic neighbor checks on the elderly lady next door. Small altruistic gestures, like paying for a stranger's coffee, make a street feel like a community.

Lena

So altruistic is about action. But what about how someone comes across? That brings us to approachable.

Maya

Approachable means friendly and easy to talk to. Her approachable demeanor made students feel comfortable asking questions.

Lena

The approachable professor is the one whose office door is always open. Customers love an approachable manager because they can raise problems without fear.

Maya

Approachable people build trust fast.

Maya

One more note before we continue. If you enjoy sharpening your eye for people like this, the Muse English app keeps it going daily: fresh words with photos and pronunciation, the podcast, shadowing stories to train your ear, quizzes and grammar to lock it in, and a leaderboard that keeps you motivated. It is free to download, so make it part of your morning routine.

Maya

We've covered five words. Now two more that are all about reading people: appease and astute.

Lena

Let's take appease first. He tried to appease his angry boss with an apology, our example says.

Maya

To appease is to pacify someone by granting their demands. You appease a crying child with a cookie. Governments sometimes appease angry crowds with promises of reform.

Lena

But appease can have a darker edge. You can appease a bully by giving in, which rarely works twice. Parents learn that you can't appease every tantrum or the tantrums never stop.

Maya

Still, in daily life, small acts to appease tension matter. An apology can appease a hurt friend. A fair compromise can appease both sides of an argument.

Lena

And our final word: astute. An astute businessman, he quickly spotted the market opportunity.

Maya

Astute means having an ability to accurately assess situations or people. An astute observer reads the room in seconds.

Lena

The astute investor sold before the crash. An astute interviewer asks the question nobody expects and learns the truth.

Maya

Astute is not the same as clever. Clever is quick; astute is sharp-eyed. An astute manager notices who is struggling before anyone complains. The astute listener hears what people mean, not just what they say.

Lena

One thing I love about appease is how everyday it is. We appease without thinking: the barista offers a free cookie to appease the customer who waited too long.

Maya

And businesses do it at scale. Airlines appease delayed passengers with meal vouchers. But the word carries a warning: if you appease everyone, you stand for nothing. Appease the wrong demand and the problem returns bigger.

Lena

Same with astute, but on the other side. Astute is the skill of not being fooled. The astute shopper reads the fine print. The astute voter watches what politicians do, not what they say.

Maya

And here is a beautiful pairing: the astute leader knows when to appease and when to stand firm. Appease a tired team with a day off, but never appease bad behavior. That judgment call is what makes a leader astute rather than just kind.

Lena

That is a great frame. Appease is the action, astute is the judgment behind it.

Lena

Quick challenge, listeners. Which word fits? She gave her whole bonus to the flood victims.

Maya

Altruistic. A purely altruistic move, and it inspired the whole office.

Lena

The room went quiet when the stranger walked in, everyone tense.

Maya

They felt alarmed. Sudden worry, exactly our word.

Lena

The new manager keeps her door open, warm smile, anyone can walk in.

Maya

Approachable. Her approachable style changed the whole office culture.

Lena

He gave the protesters everything they demanded, just to end the noise.

Maya

He tried to appease them. Appease is precisely that verb.

Lena

The detective who solved the case from one glance at the letters.

Maya

Astute. An astute mind sees patterns others miss.

Lena

And the critic who hates every new painting?

Maya

A deep antipathy toward modern art. Or simply: he abhors change.

Lena

Let's put all seven words into one scene. A town hall meeting. The mayor is famously approachable, and tonight she proves it.

Maya

Residents speak freely. One man says he abhors the new highway plan because it cuts through the park. Several homeowners feel alarmed that their houses might lose value.

Lena

Then a local builder stands up. He is famously astute, and the room listens. He proposes moving the route behind the hill, a compromise that would appease both commuters and park lovers.

Maya

The mayor thanks him. She admits she once felt antipathy toward him, back when they were rivals, but his altruistic work on the library changed her mind.

Lena

Nobody abhors compromise tonight. The alarmed homeowners relax, her approachable style wins the day, and the old antipathy fades. It takes an astute leader to read that room.

Lena

Let's do a lightning recap. Abhor: to intensely dislike, like injustice.

Maya

Alarmed: frightened or worried, often sudden.

Lena

Altruistic: selflessly caring for others' well-being.

Maya

Antipathy: a deep-seated dislike or aversion.

Lena

Appease: to calm someone by giving them what they want.

Maya

Approachable: friendly and easy to talk to.

Lena

Astute: sharp at assessing people and situations.

Maya

Seven words, each with its own flavor of emotion or character. Use them this week and notice how much more precise you sound.

Lena

Thanks for listening to Muse English. See you tomorrow.

Maya

See you tomorrow!

📝Grammar: Advanced Cohesion and Discourse Markers

**Cohesion** is what makes a text flow: the words that link sentences and show how ideas relate. **Discourse markers** like *moreover, consequently* and *admittedly* tell the reader what kind of idea comes next. At C2, the challenge is choosing the **exact** marker for the relationship, and punctuating it correctly.
