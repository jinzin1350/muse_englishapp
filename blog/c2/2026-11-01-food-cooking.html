additive /AD-uh-tiv/
اسم · noun
A substance added to food to improve it.
افزودنی، ماده افزودنی
"Many processed foods contain artificial additives."

اصطلاح · idiom
All the necessary qualities for something to happen.
تمام عناصر لازم
"The team has all the ingredients of a champion."
allergic /uh-LUR-jik/
صفت · adjective
Having an adverse physical reaction to certain substances.
حساسیت داشتن، آلرژیک
"She is allergic to peanuts."
allergy /AL-er-jee/
اسم · noun
A damaging immune response to a substance.
آلرژی، حساسیت
"His severe nut allergy requires an EpiPen."
animal welfare /AN-uh-mul WEL-fair/
اسم · noun

پادکست: غذا و آشپزی

گفت‌وگوی امروز را گوش کنید.

📝 متن پادکست

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I am Maya, and today we are talking about something we all deal with every single day, the food we eat, and the choices behind it.

Lena

And I am Lena. Maya, I have to confess something. Yesterday I stood in the supermarket for ten minutes just reading the back of a soup packet, and I still had no idea what half of it meant.

Maya

That is exactly where we should start, because food labels are full of words that sound scientific but affect our daily lives. Take the word additive. An additive is a substance added to food to improve it, maybe to keep it fresh longer, to give it color, or to make it taste stronger.

Lena

So when a label says it contains an additive, that is not automatically a bad thing?

Maya

Not automatically, no. Some additive products are completely harmless, like vitamin C added to juice to stop it going brown. But other people worry about a particular additive, especially an artificial one, because they feel they do not know what it really does to the body.

Lena

That makes sense. I think the real question is how much we trust what is in our food, and how much we actually cook ourselves.

Maya

Exactly. And that brings us to our first break, because there is something I want to tell you about.

Lena

If you are enjoying learning words like this, you should try the Muse English app. Every day you get fresh words with photos and pronunciation, plus this podcast, shadowing stories, quizzes, grammar lessons, and a leaderboard where you collect points as you learn. It is free to install, and it turns ten spare minutes into real progress. Go and give it a try today.

Maya

Welcome back. Lena, before the break you said you stood in the supermarket reading labels. Did you end up buying the soup?

Lena

I did not. I put it back, because I saw a long list of ingredients I could not pronounce, and I thought, I would rather cook something simple at home.

Maya

That is a very common reaction. Cooking at home gives you control, and it also means you eat less convenience food. Convenience food is pre-prepared food that needs very little cooking, like a frozen pizza or a ready meal you just heat up.

Lena

Convenience food saves time, though. After a long day at work, convenience food feels like the only realistic option.

Maya

It does, and there is no shame in that. But when convenience food becomes the default every night, you lose track of what you are eating. A home cooked meal does not have to be complicated. It can be rice, vegetables, and some beans, and it is still faster than you think.

Lena

Speaking of knowing what you eat, Maya, I have a friend who has to read every label very carefully, because she is allergic to peanuts. If she is allergic to something, even a tiny amount can make her seriously ill.

Maya

That is a really important point. When someone is allergic to a food, it is not a preference, it is a medical issue. And an allergy can develop at any age. An allergy is a damaging immune response to a substance, which means the body overreacts to something harmless, like a peanut.

Lena

Her allergy is so severe that she carries an EpiPen everywhere. She told me that eating at a restaurant feels stressful, because she has to explain her allergy to every waiter, every single time.

Maya

I can imagine. And it is not just peanuts. Some people discover an allergy to milk, or to shellfish, or to wheat, and suddenly their whole diet changes. Living with an allergy teaches you to cook carefully and to ask questions, which is actually a useful skill for everyone.

Lena

It also makes you think about the food system itself. Like, where does our food come from, and how are the animals treated?

Maya

That is the next big question, and it connects to two more of our words today. Animal welfare means the physical and mental well-being of animals. When shoppers care about animal welfare, they want to know that the animals lived decent lives, with space, fresh air, and proper food.

Lena

And the opposite of that is battery farming, right? Battery farming means keeping farm animals in small cages for mass production. Many consumers oppose battery farming because of ethical concerns.

Maya

Yes. Battery farming is efficient, and it keeps prices low, but critics say it ignores animal welfare completely. So shoppers face a real dilemma. Do you pay more for food that respects animal welfare, or do you buy the cheaper option?

Lena

I think a lot of people would choose better animal welfare if they could afford it. But when money is tight, convenience food and cheap meat win, even if you dislike battery farming.

Lena

Listeners, a quick word about the Muse English app. It gives you daily words with photos and pronunciation, this podcast, shadowing stories to practise speaking, quizzes to test yourself, grammar lessons, and a leaderboard with points to keep you motivated. It is free, it is fun, and it fits into your day. Download it and keep learning with us.

Maya

Thanks, Lena. Now, let us pull all of this together. Maya: That tension is real. And it is why some people say a good cook needs all the ingredients of a great meal, not just food, but knowledge too. When we say a team has all the ingredients of a champion, we mean it has everything it needs to succeed. In the same way, a good food system needs all the ingredients of fairness: honest labels, decent animal welfare, and affordable prices.

Lena

I love that expression. A person can have all the ingredients of a great cook, too. Curiosity, patience, and a willingness to try new flavors. You do not need fancy equipment, just all the ingredients of a good attitude.

Maya

Exactly. And here is something else worth saying before we finish this part.

Maya

Thanks for that thought, Lena. Now, let us pull all of this together. Food is not just fuel. Every meal is a set of choices, about health, about money, and about values.

Lena

Let us recap our words. First, additive. An additive is a substance added to food, and while one additive may be harmless, a long list of additives on a packet is worth questioning.

Maya

Second, convenience food. Convenience food is quick and easy, but relying on convenience food every day means you rarely know exactly what you are eating.

Lena

Third, allergic and allergy. If you are allergic to something, your body reacts badly to it, and an allergy is the medical name for that response. A nut allergy, for example, can be life threatening.

Maya

Fourth, animal welfare and battery farming. Animal welfare is about how well animals live, and battery farming is the intensive system that many people believe harms animal welfare.

Lena

And finally, all the ingredients of something. Remember, a team can have all the ingredients of a champion, and a cook can have all the ingredients of success. It means having everything necessary.

Maya

Now, here is my challenge for you this week. Cook one meal from scratch, read one food label properly, and ask yourself one question: does this food respect my health, my budget, and animal welfare?

Lena

That is a great challenge. And if you try it, tell us how it went. We love hearing from you.

Maya

And here is a practical tip for your next shopping trip. When you pick up a packet, look for the shortest ingredient list you can find. A short list usually means less processing, fewer additives, and food that is closer to its natural state.

Lena

That is good advice. I also try to buy food in season. Seasonal vegetables taste better, they cost less, and they rarely need an additive to stay fresh, because they have not travelled for weeks to reach the shop.

Maya

Exactly. And if you do buy convenience food sometimes, and we all do, just balance it. Have convenience food for lunch, then cook something fresh for dinner. Nobody is perfect, and one ready meal does not ruin a healthy diet.

Lena

What about eating out? If you have an allergy, eating out is stressful, but restaurants are getting better. Many menus now mark dishes clearly, and a good waiter will always check with the kitchen about your allergy.

Maya

That is true, and it shows how awareness is growing. Ten years ago, almost nobody talked about animal welfare in a restaurant. Today, menus proudly say where the meat comes from, and whether the farm respects animal welfare. That change happened because customers asked for it.

Lena

So our choices really do matter. Every time we choose food that respects animal welfare, or refuse to support battery farming, we send a message to the whole food industry.

Maya

And every time we cook at home, we practise the language of food, too. You learn words like additive and allergy naturally, because they are part of your real life, not just a list in a book.

Maya

Before we go, let us practise. Say these words with us. Additive. Allergic. Allergy. Animal welfare. Battery farming. Convenience food. And the phrase, all the ingredients of.

Lena

Beautiful. Keep practising, keep cooking, and keep learning. This is Muse English, and we will see you tomorrow with seven brand new words.

Maya

Goodbye, everyone, and happy cooking.

📝گرامر: Inverted Conditionals (Had I known, Should you need...)

In formal English, we can drop if and invert the subject and the auxiliary. Had I known means the same as if I had known, but it sounds more formal and more elegant.

Only three auxiliaries work this way: should, were and had. Should you need help covers a possible future event, were I you covers an unreal present, and had I known covers an unreal past.

For negatives, put not after the subject and never use a contraction. Had I not seen the label is correct, while Hadn't I seen it is wrong.
