📚 Vocabulary

7 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
biophilia /bye-oh-FIL-ee-uh/
noun
a deep love of living things and nature
"Her biophilia draws her to wild places every weekend, where she feels most at home among trees and birds."
anthropocene /AN-throh-puh-seen/
noun
the proposed geological age in which humans are the dominant force shaping Earth's systems
"Some scientists argue we have entered the Anthropocene, an era defined by human impact on the climate."
bioremediation /bye-oh-ruh-mee-dee-AY-shun/
noun
the use of living organisms to clean pollution
"The wetland project uses bioremediation: reeds and microbes absorb the toxins from the river water."
succession /suk-SESH-un/
noun
the natural sequence of changes by which an ecosystem develops over time
"Ecological succession turned the abandoned farmland back into oak forest within fifty years."
defaunation /dee-faw-NAY-shun/
noun
the loss of wild animals from an ecosystem
"Defaunation is emptying the world's forests of their large mammals, and scientists warn the effects cascade downward."
vernal /VUR-nul/
adjective
relating to spring or the beginning of a season
"The garden wakes each year with vernal energy: crocuses, daffodils, and the first bees."
riparian /ruh-PAIR-ee-un/
adjective
relating to the banks of rivers and streams
"The riparian woodland along the river shelters otters, kingfishers, and ancient willows."

Podcast: Nature & Environment

📝 Podcast Transcript

Lena

Welcome to Muse English! I'm Lena, and today we are walking into the wild with seven beautiful, precise words about nature.

Maya

And I'm Maya, here to ask the questions you are probably thinking. Our seven words today are biophilia, anthropocene, bioremediation, succession, defaunation, vernal, and riparian.

Lena

By the end of this episode, you will have heard each of them in real conversation, because that is how words stick. Maya, which one should we start with?

Maya

Start with biophilia. It sounds like a philosophy.

Lena

It almost is. Biophilia is a deep love of living things and nature. The biologist E O Wilson popularized the word, and it describes that pull many of us feel toward forests, oceans, and wild places.

Maya

So when I drive an hour out of the city just to sit under big trees, that is my biophilia speaking?

Lena

Exactly. People with strong biophilia feel calmer around plants and animals, and research shows that this love of nature is genuinely good for our health. A house full of plants, a weekend hike, a walk by the river, all of these are little acts of biophilia.

Maya

I love that. One word for a feeling I have had my whole life.

Lena

That is the magic of advanced vocabulary. It names feelings. And here is a word for the age that feeling lives in, the anthropocene.

Maya

The anthropocene. Break it down for me.

Lena

Anthropo means human, and cene is a geological ending, like the Holocene before it. The anthropocene is the proposed geological age in which humans are the dominant force shaping Earth's systems. It marks the moment when human activity, not glaciers or volcanoes, drives the planet's biggest changes.

Maya

So the anthropocene is not only about pollution. It is about our footprint becoming a geological layer.

Lena

Beautifully said. Future scientists may read our plastic and concrete as the signature layer of the anthropocene. And if your biophilia makes that idea sad, you are not alone. Many people carry both feelings at once, a love of nature and grief that the anthropocene is wounding it.

Maya

A heavy pair of words. Do we get any hope today?

Lena

We do, and it comes from our next word. But first, a quick break.

Maya

Wait, Lena, before we pause, let me try these two. Biophilia, my love of wild places. Anthropocene, the age of humans reshaping the planet. How did I do?

Lena

Perfect. You are already thinking in advanced English. And now, friends, a word about the app.

Lena

If you are learning something new today, the Muse English app is where today's words live on after the episode. Open the app and you will find today's words with photos and pronunciation, the full transcript of this podcast, shadowing stories to train your speaking, quizzes to test yourself, grammar lessons, and a leaderboard where you earn points every day. It is completely free to install. Make today's vocabulary yours forever.

Maya

And we are back. Lena, give me the hopeful word.

Lena

Bioremediation. Say it slowly, bio re me diation. Bioremediation is the use of living organisms, such as bacteria, fungi, or plants, to clean pollution from air, water, or soil. Instead of digging up poisoned earth with machines, we let nature heal itself, with a little help.

Maya

Give me a picture I can see.

Lena

Imagine a river stained by an old factory. Engineers plant reeds along its banks. The roots absorb heavy metals, and helpful microbes break down the chemicals. That is bioremediation in action. No smoke, no noise, just roots and microbes doing what they have done for millions of years.

Maya

So bioremediation is like medicine, but the patient is the land itself.

Lena

I will steal that sentence. And bioremediation is growing fast, because it is often cheaper and gentler than traditional cleanup. After bioremediation, a dead river can breathe again, and sometimes your biophilia gets a happy ending.

Maya

From cleanup to rebirth. What comes next?

Lena

Succession. In everyday speech, succession means following after, but ecologists use it for something gorgeous. Succession is the natural sequence of changes by which an ecosystem develops over time, for example from bare ground to mature forest.

Maya

So nature has a script, and succession is the plot?

Lena

Exactly. First, tough little weeds and mosses settle on bare rock. They die and build soil. Then grasses come, then shrubs, then trees. That whole long relay is succession. Leave an abandoned parking lot alone for a hundred years, and succession will quietly turn it into a young forest.

Maya

A hundred years? Succession is patient.

Lena

Very patient. And wise. Farmers and foresters study succession because fighting it is expensive and working with it is free. When you watch succession, you watch nature's memory at work, always returning toward balance.

Maya

Okay, I am tracking five words so far. What is defaunation? It sounds ominous.

Lena

It is. Defaunation is the loss of wild animals from an ecosystem, especially through hunting or habitat loss. Think of defaunation as deforestation's quiet sibling. The trees may still stand, but the forest is empty.

Maya

An empty forest. That is haunting.

Lena

Scientists call these silent forests. Defaunation happens when hunters take the large animals, the elephants, the big cats, the monkeys. Without them, seeds do not travel, trees do not regenerate, and the whole web weakens. Defaunation starts with animals but ends with plants, and eventually with people, because we depend on those systems too.

Maya

Can defaunation be reversed?

Lena

Sometimes, yes. Protect the animals, restore their corridors, and the land can recover. Rewilding projects are really just fights against defaunation. Every wolf returned to a valley, every rhino guarded from poachers, is a small victory over defaunation.

Maya

That is a word I will not forget. One more pause before the last two?

Lena

Yes, and friends, another word about the app.

Maya

Everything we cover here, the daily words, this podcast, the shadowing stories, the quizzes, the grammar lessons, they all live in the Muse English app, and it is free to install. Your points and streaks build on the leaderboard, so every day you practice moves you up. Ten minutes a day is all it takes. Grab the app and start building your streak tomorrow morning.

Lena

Two words left, and they are the gentlest of the seven. Vernal. It comes from the Latin word for spring. Vernal simply means relating to spring or the beginning of a season.

Maya

Vernal. It even sounds fresh.

Lena

Use it for that first week of April feeling. The garden wakes each year with vernal energy, crocuses, daffodils, and the first bees. Poets love the vernal light, that clear, hopeful brightness that arrives after winter. And vernal works for beginnings beyond nature too, the vernal season of a new career, a vernal morning in your life.

Maya

I will never say just spring again when I can say vernal. And the last one, riparian?

Lena

Riparian is a specialist's word, and it means relating to the banks of rivers and streams. The riparian woodland along a river shelters otters, kingfishers, and ancient willows. Those trees are called riparian vegetation, and they do important work, holding the soil, shading the water, feeding the fish.

Maya

So if I walk along a river and admire the trees on its banks, I am admiring riparian life.

Lena

Precisely. Next time you cross a bridge, look down at the riparian strip on each side. That green fringe is one of the richest habitats on Earth, a nursery for birds, insects, and fish. Riparian zones punch far above their weight.

Maya

Before the final review, give me one real sentence for each word, something I can steal for my own English.

Lena

Happily. Her biophilia is so strong that she plans every vacation around national parks. The scientists published a paper arguing that the anthropocene began in the nineteen fifties.

Maya

Keep going.

Lena

The city chose bioremediation over bulldozers, and the lake is swimmable again. Foresters waited patiently while succession did the replanting for free. Camera traps proved that the reserve had escaped defaunation, and the leopards were back.

Maya

Two left.

Lena

A vernal rain washed the city clean on the first morning of April. And the beavers built their dam in the riparian shallows, where the willows lean over the water.

Maya

Stealing all seven.

Lena

Then let me do a final review. Biophilia, a love of living things and nature. Anthropocene, the age of humans reshaping the planet. Bioremediation, healing pollution with living organisms.

Lena

Succession, nature's long relay from bare ground to forest. Defaunation, the emptying of wild animals from ecosystems. Vernal, of spring and new beginnings. Riparian, of riverbanks and their green fringe.

Maya

Seven words, and I can use every one of them. That is a good day.

Lena

It is. Open the Muse English app to review them with photos and pronunciation, take the quiz, and try the shadowing story. Until tomorrow, this is Lena.

Maya

And Maya. Keep learning, keep growing.

📝Grammar: Cleft Sentences (It was... that, What I need is...)

You met **cleft sentences** at C1; at C2 the focus is precision and range. A cleft sentence splits one idea into two parts to **emphasise** the part that matters most. *The reeds cleaned the river* becomes ***It was the reeds that** cleaned the river* — the reeds, not the machines. The two main types are **it-clefts** and **wh-clefts**. Use **it-clefts** to correct or contrast: *It's not the river I blame, **it's** the factory*. With people, use **who** rather than *which*: ***It was my sister who** told me*. After a **what** clause, the verb stays singular and you never add *it*: *What bio
