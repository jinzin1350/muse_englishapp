📚 Vocabulary

7 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
aloof /uh-LOOF/
adjective
Distant and reserved; not friendly.
"He remained aloof from the office gossip."
averse /uh-VURS/
adjective
Having a strong dislike or opposition to something.
"He is not averse to taking risks."
aversion /uh-VUR-zhun/
noun
Strong dislike or feeling of repugnance towards something.
"She has a strong aversion to public speaking."
bite your nails /BITE yaw NAYLZ/
idiom
To chew on one's fingernails, usually from nervousness.
"She tends to bite her nails when she's stressed."
bliss /BLIS/
noun
Perfect happiness or joy.
"Lying on the beach was pure bliss."
blissful /BLIS-ful/
adjective
Extremely happy; full of joy.
"They spent a blissful honeymoon in the Maldives."
blissfully /BLIS-fuh-lee/
adverb
In a state of perfect happiness.
"She smiled blissfully, enjoying the quiet morning."

📝Grammar: Passive Reporting Structures (It is said that..., He is thought to...)

In news and formal writing, we often report what people in general say, think, or believe without naming anyone. Instead of saying who said it, we use a passive reporting structure, and this keeps the tone formal and distant, even a little aloof. There are two main patterns. The first is **It + passive + that clause**, as in **It is said that** he is rich. The second is **subject + passive + to-infinitive**, as in He **is said to be** rich. Never mix the two patterns.
