📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
accelerationism /ak-sel-uh-RAY-shun-iz-um/
noun
the idea that technological and economic change should be accelerated rather than slowed down
"Silicon Valley accelerationism treats every regulation as an obstacle to inevitable progress."
lucubration /loo-kyoo-BRAY-shun/
noun
studious work or meditation, especially late at night; laborious study
"Her doctoral thesis was the fruit of years of quiet lucubration."
dark pattern /DARK PAT-urn/
noun
a deceptive design trick in an app or website that manipulates users
"The checkout page used a classic dark pattern, hiding the cancel button in pale grey text."
heuristic /hyoo-RIH-stik/
noun
a practical method or rule that helps you find answers quickly, even if it is imperfect
"A useful heuristic for essays is to write the introduction after the body."
cybernetics /sy-bur-NET-iks/
noun
the science of communication and automatic control in machines and living things
"Modern cybernetics treats the brain and the computer as variations on the same information-processing system."
technosolutionism /tek-noh-suh-LOO-shuh-niz-um/
noun
the belief that every social or political problem has a technological solution
"Technosolutionism tempts politicians to fund apps instead of fixing the underlying problem."
technocratic /tek-nuh-KRAT-ik/
adjective
decided or controlled by technical experts rather than by politicians or ordinary people
"The technocratic committee imposed the new data policy without consulting the public."
anthropocene /AN-throh-puh-seen/
noun
the proposed geological age in which humans are the dominant force shaping Earth's systems
"Some scientists argue we have entered the Anthropocene, an era defined by human impact on the climate."
bioremediation /bye-oh-ruh-mee-dee-AY-shun/
noun
the use of living organisms to clean pollution
"The wetland project uses bioremediation: reeds and microbes absorb the toxins from the river water."
vernal /VUR-nul/
adjective
relating to spring or the beginning of a season
"The garden wakes each year with vernal energy: crocuses, daffodils, and the first bees."

📝Grammar: Hedging and Distancing (it would appear that...)

**Hedging** makes a claim **less direct or less certain**, so you never assert more than the evidence supports: *It accelerates growth. → **It would appear that** it accelerates growth.* At C2, hedging is reflexive in academic, professional, and diplomatic English. The classic hedges cluster around a few tools: **seem / appear to** (*There **seems to be** a pattern*), the softest of all, **it would seem / appear that** (*This **would seem** to be the best **heuristic***), and modals like **may / might / could** (*This **may be** due to a **technocratic** committee overreaching*).
