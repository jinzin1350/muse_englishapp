📚 لغت‌ها

۷ لغت تازه لغت‌های تازه با معنی فارسی، مثال واقعی و تلفظ.
business class /BIZ-nis KLAHS/
اسم · noun
A more comfortable and expensive section on an airplane.
کلاس تجاری (هواپیما)
"For long flights, business class offers much better comfort."
cabin /KAB-in/
اسم · noun
A small room on a ship or airplane.
کابین، اتاقک
"Our cruise ship cabin had a lovely ocean view."
cancellation fee /kan-suh-LAY-shun FEE/
اسم · noun
Money charged when you cancel a booking or service.
هزینه لغو
"We had to pay a cancellation fee for the hotel room."
carriage /KA-rij/
اسم · noun
A separate section of a train for passengers.
واگن (قطار)
"We found our seats in the third carriage of the train."
chalet /SHA-lay/
اسم · noun
A wooden house or hut, especially in mountains.
کلبه چوبی (کوهستانی)
"We rented a cozy chalet for our ski holiday."
charter flight /CHAR-ter FLYT/
اسم · noun
A flight arranged for a specific group, not regular.
پرواز چارتر
"We took a charter flight directly to the resort."
clamp /KLAMP/
فعل · verb
To fix a device to a wheel to prevent movement.
چرخ‌بند زدن
"My car was clamped for parking illegally."

پادکست: سفر و حمل‌ونقل

گفت‌وگوی امروز را گوش کنید.

📝 متن پادکست

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I am Maya, and today Lena and I are talking about travel at the highest level, the small words and phrases that separate a good traveller from a truly fluent one.

Lena

Hello everyone. I love this topic, because travel English sounds simple until you are standing in an airport, tired and confused. Today we have seven advanced words, and every single one of them comes from real journeys.

Maya

Let us start with a story. Last spring, my cousin flew to Geneva for a conference. She had booked business class for the first time in her life, and she could not stop talking about it.

Lena

Business class will do that to a person. Wider seats, better food, and you actually sleep. But tell me, was the business class ticket worth the money for her?

Maya

She said yes, completely. In business class she arrived rested, and she went straight into her meeting. She told me, once you try business class on a long flight, ordinary seats feel like a punishment. Business class is not just luxury, it is energy management.

Lena

I agree, although I only travel business class when someone else is paying. Now, on that same trip, did she stay in a hotel?

Maya

No, and this is the lovely part. After the conference, she took a train into the mountains and rented a small chalet for three nights. A chalet is a wooden house, usually in the mountains, with a steep roof and a warm fire inside.

Lena

A chalet sounds perfect. I stayed in a chalet in Austria once, and the silence was incredible. Did her chalet have a view?

Maya

Her chalet looked straight at the lake. She said the chalet was tiny, but every window was a painting. Honestly, a good chalet beats a five star hotel for me, because a chalet feels like a home, not a product.

Lena

You know what, this is a good moment to pause. You can listen, read the transcript, and then shadow the story until the words feel natural. It is free, and it takes only a few minutes a day. Shall we continue?

Maya

Yes, let us talk about trains, because my favourite word today is carriage. In British English, a carriage is one section of a train. You might sit in the third carriage, or walk through the carriage looking for your seat.

Lena

Carriage is such an elegant word. An American would say car, but carriage sounds almost historical. I once slept through my stop because our carriage was so quiet and comfortable.

Maya

That happened to me too. The carriage was warm, the rhythm of the train is hypnotic, and suddenly you are three towns past your station. A quiet carriage should come with a warning, honestly. Every carriage on that line was quiet, so nobody woke me up.

Lena

And each carriage had those little tables by the window. I love a train carriage with a table, a coffee, and a book. For me, the carriage is where the holiday actually begins.

Maya

Trains are gentle. Planes, however, bring us to our next word: cabin. The cabin is the inside space of a plane where passengers sit, and also a small room on a ship. Pilots talk about the cabin crew, and announcements begin with, welcome to the cabin.

Lena

Cabin is a busy little word. On a ferry last summer, our cabin was so small that I could touch both walls at the same time. Still, that cabin was ours, and we slept like children.

Maya

My grandfather worked on ships, and he always said a good cabin is a quiet cabin. He would check his cabin first, before the restaurant, before anything. If the cabin was calm, the whole voyage was calm.

Lena

And in a plane, cabin pressure, cabin bag, cabin crew, the word cabin is everywhere. You cannot fly without meeting the word cabin at least five times.

Maya

Exactly. Now, Lena, tell everyone about your famous charter flight disaster, because our listeners deserve the full story.

Lena

Oh, the charter flight. A charter flight is not a regular scheduled flight. A company or a travel group hires the whole plane for one specific journey. Our charter flight was supposed to leave at seven in the morning.

Maya

Supposed to. I remember this. The charter flight left nine hours late, did it not?

Lena

Nine hours. Nine hours in a small airport with one cafe. And here is the painful part: with a charter flight, the rules are different. When we asked about compensation, the company smiled and pointed at the small print. A charter flight can be wonderful when it works, direct and cheap, but a charter flight gives you less protection when it fails.

Maya

That is an important lesson. A charter flight is a tool: brilliant for a group going to one resort, risky if your plans are tight. I would still take a charter flight for a ski trip with twenty friends, because the whole group travels together.

Lena

True. And when that charter flight finally landed, we were too happy to be angry. Almost.

Maya

Almost is honest. Now let me give you the opposite story, a cancellation fee story, because this word saves people real money. My brother booked a hotel, then his plans changed, and the hotel charged him a cancellation fee of almost half the price.

Lena

A cancellation fee is the money you pay when you cancel a booking. Hotels charge a cancellation fee, airlines charge a cancellation fee, even train companies quietly add a cancellation fee in the conditions.

Maya

The trick is timing. Cancel early, and the cancellation fee is often zero. Cancel at the last minute, and the cancellation fee can be the full price. My brother now reads the cancellation fee rules before he books anything.

Lena

I learned that lesson with a flight to Lisbon. I changed my dates, and the cancellation fee was bigger than the discount I was celebrating. Since then, I always ask one question before I pay: what is the cancellation fee, and when does it start?

Maya

That one question is pure C2 wisdom. Shall we take our second short break? Lena

Back to the show, and back to my favourite disaster category: parking. Our last word is clamp, and as a verb it means to fix a metal device to a car wheel so the car cannot move. Authorities clamp a car when it is parked illegally.

Maya

Nothing ruins a holiday like returning to your car and finding a clamp on the wheel. My uncle once said, they will clamp your car in London faster than you can buy a coffee.

Lena

He was right. In some streets, if you park on the wrong line, they clamp the car within the hour. And removing a clamp is expensive and slow, so one clamp can destroy a whole afternoon.

Maya

I always check the signs now, because I never want to pay to free my own car from a clamp. A clamp is small, heavy, and impossible to ignore, which is exactly the point of a clamp.

Lena

And notice the grammar: clamp is a verb here, they clamp your car, but in the sentence, a clamp on the wheel, it is a noun. Flexible words like clamp are everywhere in advanced English.

Maya

Let us pull the whole journey together. Imagine one perfect trip. You book business class because the flight is long, and business class lets you arrive human. On the plane, the cabin crew look after you, and your little corner of the cabin feels calm.

Lena

Then you land, take a train, and find your carriage. The carriage rolls through green valleys, and at the end of the line, a wooden chalet is waiting for you in the mountains.

Maya

Your friends arrive later on a charter flight, laughing about their adventure. Nobody paid a cancellation fee, because everybody read the rules, and nobody found a clamp on their car, because everybody parked legally.

Lena

That is the dream trip: business class comfort, a peaceful cabin, a quiet carriage, a warm chalet, a reliable charter flight, no cancellation fee, and definitely no clamp.

Maya

And if you can tell that story in English, with all seven words in the right places, your English is genuinely advanced. Say the words once more with us: business class, cabin, cancellation fee, carriage, chalet, charter flight, and clamp.

Maya

Before we finish, one quick game. I will describe a situation, and you name the word. You are on a train, sitting in a long section with rows of seats and a corridor. What is it?

Lena

That is a carriage, of course. My turn. You cancel a hotel room one hour before arrival, and the hotel takes money from your card. What did you pay?

Maya

A cancellation fee, sadly. Next one: a small wooden house under snowy mountains, with smoke rising from the chimney.

Lena

A chalet, my favourite answer today. And the last one: your plane section has wide leather seats, champagne, and space to lie completely flat.

Maya

Business class, the dream. You see, listeners, when the words live inside little stories like this, they stop being vocabulary and start being memories.

Lena

Thank you for travelling with Muse English today. Practise a little every day, and the words will follow you everywhere. See you tomorrow!

📝گرامر: Advanced Cohesion and Discourse Markers

Cohesion is what makes a text flow: the words that link sentences and show how ideas relate.

Discourse markers like moreover, consequently and admittedly tell the reader what kind of idea comes next.

At C2, the challenge is choosing the exact marker for the relationship. For adding, use moreover or furthermore; for a result, use consequently or as a result; for contrast, use however, nevertheless or on the other hand.
