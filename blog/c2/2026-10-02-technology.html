📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
obsolescence /ob-suh-LES-uns/
noun
the state of becoming outdated and no longer useful
"Engineers now design phone batteries with planned obsolescence in mind, knowing users will replace the device within three years."
disintermediation /dis-in-tur-mee-dee-AY-shun/
noun
the removal of middlemen between producers and consumers, made possible by technology
"Streaming platforms completed the disintermediation of the music industry, letting artists reach listeners without record labels."
panoptic /pan-OP-tik/
adjective
allowing everything to be seen or watched at once
"Smart cities are becoming panoptic environments where cameras track every pedestrian's movement."
skeuomorph /SKYOO-oh-morf/
noun
a design detail that copies the look of a real-world object into a digital product
"The leather texture on that old calendar app was a skeuomorph meant to comfort paper-and-pen users."
black box /BLAK BOKS/
noun
a complex system whose inner workings are hidden from its users
"Large language models are often black boxes: even their creators cannot fully explain every answer they produce."
hallucination /huh-loo-suh-NAY-shun/
noun
a confident but completely false output generated by an AI
"That chatbot's hallucination about a nonexistent law briefly fooled a busy lawyer."
deepfake /DEEP-fayk/
noun
a fake video or audio clip made with artificial intelligence
"The viral video of the mayor confessing to bribery turned out to be a crude deepfake."
singularity /sing-gyu-LAR-i-tee/
noun
the hypothetical moment when artificial intelligence surpasses human intelligence
"Some futurists predict the singularity will arrive within our lifetime; others call it science fiction."
neoluddite /nee-oh-LUH-dyte/
noun
a person who opposes or distrusts new technology
"The neoluddite in our office refuses to use the cloud and still backs up everything on printed paper."
datafication /day-tuh-fuh-KAY-shun/
noun
the process of turning every aspect of life into data that can be collected and analyzed
"Fitness trackers are a symbol of datafication: even sleep has become a set of numbers."

Podcast: Technology

Listen to today’s conversation.

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome back to Word Kitchen, everyone. I am Maya.

Lena

And I am Lena. Today we are cooking up something a little different: a full meal of words about technology. Ten delicious, slightly dangerous words about the gadgets and systems that run our lives.

Maya

And we picked a theme: the tech paradox. Every word today has a little tension inside it. Technology promises us freedom, speed, connection, but it always brings a shadow along with it. Shall we start with a word most of us feel every time we buy a new phone?

Lena

Oh, I know this one. Planned obsolescence. That is the word, right? Obsolescence?

Maya

Exactly. Obsolescence is the state of becoming outdated and no longer useful. Phone makers literally design for it. They know the battery will weaken in two or three years, the software will slow down, and you will feel that itch for the new model.

Lena

That is so cynical when you say it out loud. Obsolescence used to be an accident. A thing wore out. Now obsolescence is a business plan. The obsolescence of my last laptop felt scheduled, like it had an appointment to die.

Maya

Right. And at the same time, that planned obsolescence is what funds the next leap forward. Without it, companies might not invest in better cameras or faster chips. So is obsolescence a crime against consumers, or just the price of progress?

Lena

I would call it both. The tension is real. Anyway, next word. I have a love-hate relationship with this one because it changed my career. Disintermediation.

Maya

Disintermediation is a mouthful. Break it down for us.

Lena

Disintermediation means cutting out the middleman. A streaming platform lets a musician upload songs straight to listeners. No record label, no distributor. That is disintermediation in action.

Maya

So the internet's original promise was disintermediation. Bookstores, travel agents, taxi companies, all squeezed out by a direct connection.

Lena

Yes, but notice the twist. We got disintermediation, and then the platforms themselves became the new middlemen. They take a bigger cut than the old ones ever did. Disintermediation created a new boss, and that boss is an app.

Maya

That is the paradox again. We removed the gatekeepers and built bigger gates. Speaking of gates, our third word is one I find genuinely chilling. Panoptic.

Lena

Panoptic comes from the panopticon, that old prison design where one guard could watch every cell at once. Panoptic means allowing everything to be seen at once. A panoptic system sees all of you, all the time.

Maya

And modern cities are becoming panoptic environments. Cameras on every corner, sensors in the streetlights, your face scanned at the airport. It is safety, sure, but it is also a panoptic kind of safety. Who is watching, and what are they doing with what they see?

Lena

I felt it last month when my navigation app suggested a coffee shop before I had searched for anything. That panoptic awareness of my routine made me wonder whether convenience is worth being seen so completely.

Maya

Exactly. And every panoptic system is sold to us as protection. We trade privacy for a feeling of security, and we never quite get either. Now, let us lighten up a little with a word I adore. Skeuomorph.

Lena

A skeuomorph is a design detail that copies a real-world object into a digital product. Like the old iPhone bookshelf app with wooden shelves, or a camera shutter sound on a phone that has no shutter.

Maya

The skeuomorph is basically nostalgia baked into software. When we were all scared of touchscreens, designers gave us fake paper calendars and stitched leather notes to make us comfortable. The skeuomorph was a bridge.

Lena

And bridges are meant to be crossed and then forgotten. Today a skeuomorph looks charmingly outdated. It is funny: the skeuomorph itself suffered obsolescence. Designers moved on to flat, clean interfaces, and those leather textures look ancient now.

Maya

But I think every skeuomorph leaves a fossil in the interface. The save icon is still a floppy disk, and that skeuomorph survived because it became a symbol.

Lena

I love that. Now we are getting into the heavier territory, because our fifth word is a warning. Black box.

Maya

A black box is a system whose inner workings are hidden. You see the inputs and the outputs, but what happens in the middle is a mystery. Large A I models are often described as black boxes.

Lena

That is what scares me most about them. When a black box decides who gets a loan or which resume gets shortlisted, nobody can explain exactly why. The engineers built the black box, trained it on mountains of data, and now even they cannot trace every decision.

Maya

And here is the deep irony. We built tools to be perfectly logical, and the result is a black box we trust without understanding. We replaced human bias, which is at least visible, with machine bias hidden inside a black box.

Lena

Which leads us beautifully to word six, because one of the strangest products of the black box is the hallucination.

Maya

A hallucination, in A I terms, is a confident but completely false output. The model tells you a fact with total certainty, and the fact is invented.

Lena

I saw a famous example where a chatbot wrote a whole legal brief citing cases that never existed. That hallucination was so smooth and so authoritative that a busy lawyer nearly filed it in court. The hallucination did not stumble or hedge. It just lied, fluently.

Maya

That is what makes a hallucination so dangerous. It wears the costume of knowledge. A machine hallucination arrives polished, formatted, and ready to be believed.

Lena

And the paradox is that the same creativity that produces a hallucination also produces genuinely brilliant answers. You cannot keep the magic and remove the hallucination, because they come from the same place. Every black box dreams a little.

Maya

Beautifully said. Our seventh word takes that dreaming into darker territory. Deepfake.

Lena

A deepfake is a fake video or audio clip made with artificial intelligence. Someone's face, someone's voice, saying things they never said.

Maya

We started this conversation talking about planned obsolescence in phones, and now we are here. The technology that retouches our selfies can also put our words into someone else's mouth. A deepfake of a politician, released the night before an election, could swing votes before anyone proves it is a deepfake.

Lena

And the really cruel part is that once deepfakes exist, any real scandal can be dismissed as just another deepfake. Truth itself suffers a kind of obsolescence when every deepfake makes people doubt their own eyes.

Maya

So the same tools that let a teenager make a funny video let a state actor forge reality. Creativity and deception, same software. Okay, deep breath. Word eight is for the dreamers and the doomsayers. Singularity.

Lena

The singularity is the hypothetical moment when artificial intelligence surpasses human intelligence and starts improving itself beyond our control or understanding.

Maya

Some futurists talk about the singularity like it is a religious event, a kind of second creation. They say once we cross it, all bets are off. Jobs, economies, maybe even what it means to be human, everything transforms.

Lena

And the skeptics say the singularity is a story we tell ourselves to make the present feel more dramatic. They argue intelligence is not a ladder you climb, it is a landscape, and there is no single singularity moment, just decades of messy, uneven change.

Maya

But notice how the singularity debate absorbs all our other words. A black box that hallucinates, a panoptic network watching everything, datafication turning our lives into fuel, and at the end of it, maybe the singularity, maybe not. The story is irresistible either way.

Lena

Which is why word nine exists. For every prophet of the singularity, there is a neoluddite.

Maya

A neoluddite is a person who opposes or distrusts new technology. The original Luddites smashed textile machines two hundred years ago. A modern neoluddite might refuse smartphones, avoid social media, or keep everything on paper.

Lena

I have a friend who is a proud neoluddite. Flip phone, no cloud, reads actual newspapers. And honestly, after a week of doom-scrolling, part of me envies the neoluddite life. The neoluddite is not against progress, they are against being dragged along by it.

Maya

That is the nuance. A neoluddite is making a choice about attention, about who gets to interrupt them. But there is a cost. The neoluddite who avoids every tool also avoids the benefits, like the disintermediation that lets a small business reach the whole world.

Lena

Right. Total rejection is as extreme as total surrender. Most of us live somewhere between the hype and the neoluddite cabin in the woods. And that brings us to our final word, the one that quietly powers everything else. Datafication.

Maya

Datafication is the process of turning every aspect of life into data that can be collected and analyzed. Your steps, your sleep, your heartbeat, your shopping list, your friendships, all converted into numbers.

Lena

Datafication is the raw material of the panoptic city. You cannot have panoptic surveillance without datafication first. Every sensor, every app, every loyalty card is datafication in action, quietly translating your life into a database row.

Maya

And datafication is what makes the black box hungry. The models need data the way engines need fuel, so datafication keeps expanding, finding new parts of life to measure. Even your emotions are becoming data points now. Datafication does not ask permission. It just measures.

Lena

That is the paradox in its purest form. Datafication promises self-knowledge. Know your body, know your habits, optimize yourself. But the knowledge flows mostly one way. We get a dashboard. The companies get the keys to our behavior.

Maya

So let us do a quick recap of our ten words. Obsolescence, the planned death of our gadgets. Disintermediation, cutting out the middleman, until new ones appear. Panoptic, seen from everywhere at once. Skeuomorph, the fossil of an old object inside new software. Black box, a system nobody can fully explain.

Lena

Hallucination, a confident lie from a confident machine. Deepfake, a forged reality. Singularity, the moment machines outthink us, maybe. Neoluddite, the person who says no thanks. And datafication, the great conversion of life into numbers. Ten words, one paradox: every tool is a trade.

Lena

That is the meal, folks. Ten words about technology, each with a little shadow inside it. Maya, this was a rich one.

Maya

It really was. Keep an eye on the obsolescence of your phone, the disintermediation in your industry, the panoptic cameras on your street, and the skeuomorphs in your apps. Behind every black box there may be a hallucination, behind every deepfake a lesson, and somewhere between the singularity and the neoluddite, a life you actually chose.

Lena

Beautifully put. We will see you tomorrow in the Word Kitchen. Until then, stay curious, and maybe check your datafication dashboard, just this once.

Maya

Goodbye, everyone.

Lena

Bye.

📝Grammar: Ability and possibility at C2: can, could, could have, be able to

**CAN** is present or future ability, simple and direct: I **can** spot a deepfake after one viewing. It also covers general possibility: technology **can** surprise us all. **COULD** is the past of **can** for general ability: early phones **could** barely hold a charge. It also softens a present or future possibility: the singularity **could** arrive quietly, through updates. For everything **can** and **could** cannot do — perfect forms, infinitives, gerunds — use **BE ABLE TO**. I'd love to **be able to** explain that black box. She has **been able to** resist datafication so far. **COU
