📚 لغت‌ها

۷ لغت تازه لغت‌های تازه با معنی فارسی، مثال واقعی و تلفظ.
at your earliest convenience /at yor ER-lee-est kun-VEE-nee-ence/
عبارت · phrase
As soon as you are able to.
در اسرع وقت ممکن
"Please submit your forms at your earliest convenience."
bestow /beh-STOH/
فعل · verb
To present an honor, right, or gift.
اعطا کردن، بخشیدن
"The university will bestow honorary degrees next month."
better off /BET-er OFF/
صفت · adjective
In a more desirable or advantageous situation.
در وضعیت بهتری
"You'd be better off studying engineering than arts."
bullying /BUL-ee-ing/
اسم · noun
Repeated aggressive behavior intended to harm another.
قلدری، زورگویی
"The school has a strict policy against bullying."
bureaucratic /byoor-oh-KRAT-ik/
صفت · adjective
Relating to a system of administration with many rules.
بوروکراتیک، دیوان‌سالارانه
"Dealing with the bureaucratic paperwork was frustrating."
bursary /BUR-suh-ree/
اسم · noun
A grant, usually for students, to help with costs.
کمک هزینه تحصیلی
"She received a bursary to cover her university tuition fees."
bury yourself in your books /BER-ee yor-SELF in yor BOOKS/
اصطلاح · idiom
To study very hard and intensely, ignoring everything else.
غرق مطالعه شدن
"He had to bury himself in his books before the final exams."

📝 متن پادکست

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I am Maya, and today we are talking about education, opportunity, and the strange systems that surround learning.

Lena

And I am Lena.

Maya

Let us start with a student called Sara. Sara received a bursary last spring, and that bursary changed everything for her. Without the bursary, she would have left university. With the bursary, she could pay her rent, buy food, and actually study.

Lena

A bursary is such a practical kind of help. Sara told me the bursary felt like a hand reaching out at exactly the right moment.

Maya

And notice the verb we can use here. The university did not just give the bursary. It chose to bestow the bursary on her, and to bestow an honor is a formal, generous act. Universities bestow degrees, they bestow medals, and they bestow titles on scholars they respect.

Lena

I love that word bestow. When a college will bestow an honorary degree, the ceremony feels grand. But Sara said the quiet bursary mattered more than any grand ceremony, because the bursary paid for real life.

Maya

Exactly. A leader may bestow praise in a speech, but a bursary bestows something you can spend. Sorry, let me say that more naturally. A bursary helps in a way praise cannot. Still, when an institution decides to bestow support, it should bestow it fairly and on time.

Lena

On time is the key phrase, which brings us to our next expression. Sara wrote to the finance office and ended her email politely. She wrote, please send the documents at your earliest convenience. That phrase, at your earliest convenience, means as soon as you are able to.

Maya

It is polite, but it can also hide urgency. If I write at your earliest convenience in a work email, I am smiling on the surface. Underneath, I may need the answer today. Sara wrote at your earliest convenience because the office was slow, and she needed her bursary confirmed.

Lena

And the office replied with the same phrase. They said, please upload your proof at your earliest convenience. So Sara heard at your earliest convenience, and she acted at once. She told other students, when an office says at your earliest convenience, do not relax too much. It often means please hurry, politely.

Maya

Lena

You used our next word perfectly. Bureaucratic. The system around Sara was deeply bureaucratic. Every bursary form led to another bureaucratic form. Every bureaucratic rule needed a stamp, a copy, and a second copy of the stamp.

Maya

A bureaucratic process is not always evil, of course. Rules can protect fairness. But a bureaucratic office can forget the human being inside the file. Sara waited in a bureaucratic queue for three hours, holding a number, while her classes continued without her.

Lena

And when the process is too bureaucratic, students give up. That is the tragedy. A bursary exists to help, but a bureaucratic maze can hide the bursary from the very people who need it most.

Maya

Which raises a serious question. Are students better off with a simple system and slightly less checking, or better off with heavy checking and long delays? I think most students are better off when help arrives early. A late bursary is almost no bursary at all.

Lena

Better off is our next phrase, and it is everywhere in education choices. Are you better off studying engineering or arts? Are you better off living at home or near campus? Sara was clearly better off with the bursary, even with the paperwork, because she stayed at university.

Maya

My brother faced the same question. He thought he would be better off leaving college and working full time. A year later, he admitted he was not better off at all. His pay was low, his hours were long, and he missed learning. He went back, and now he says he is better off in every way that matters.

Lena

So better off is never just about money this month. You might be better off poorer for a year if the degree opens doors later. And a student with a bursary is better off than a student with debt and fear, even if their grades are the same.

Maya

It is free to install, and each day builds on the last, so your English grows in small, steady steps. Try it after this episode and keep your streak alive.

Lena

Sara had a rule before final exams. She would bury yourself in your books, as the idiom says. Well, she would bury herself in her books, for weeks. When friends asked how to pass, she laughed and said, first you bury yourself in your books, then you sleep, then you bury yourself in your books again.

Maya

The idiom bury yourself in your books means to study very hard and ignore almost everything else. My advice is, bury yourself in your books for focused hours, but come up for air. If you bury yourself in your books without rest, your brain stops learning.

Lena

Sara learned that balance. She did bury herself in her books every morning, but she took walks in the afternoon. She said, I can bury myself in my books and still be kind to my mind. That is mature studying. Bury yourself in your books when it counts, and rest when it counts too.

Maya

Now, a harder part of Sara story. At her old school, bullying nearly ended her education. The bullying was quiet at first, messages, whispers, laughter in corridors. Then the bullying became open, and teachers had to act.

Lena

Bullying is repeated aggressive behaviour intended to harm another person. One cruel joke is not always bullying, but a pattern is bullying. Schools that ignore bullying teach a terrible lesson, that power matters more than safety.

Maya

Sara school finally created a strict policy against bullying, and the bullying slowly stopped. She says anti bullying work saved her love of learning. No bursary and no degree can replace safety. A school without bullying is a school where minds can grow.

Lena

And adults must model that. A teacher who mocks a student is teaching bullying by example. A leader who listens and acts teaches the opposite of bullying. That culture is worth more than any building.

Maya

Let us connect all of this. Years later, Sara graduated, and the university chose to bestow an award on her for her work against bullying. They did not just bestow a medal. They invited her to speak, and she told new students that a bursary had kept her there.

Lena

What a circle. The bursary helped her, she fought bullying, and then the college wanted to bestow an honor she never expected. She said the decision to bestow that award belonged to every teacher who had helped her.

Maya

In her speech she thanked the office too, with a smile. She said, thank you for answering at your earliest convenience, even when earliest took three weeks. Everyone laughed, because everyone knew the bureaucratic truth inside the joke.

Lena

She also gave practical advice. Apply for every bursary early. Reply at your earliest convenience when offices ask for documents. Do not let a bureaucratic delay steal a week. And if you are better off asking for help, then ask for help.

Maya

Speaking of help that actually helps, here is a second reminder from us. The Muse English app is made for daily progress. You earn points, climb the leaderboard, and build a habit that carries you forward. Install it free today, open todays lesson, and let a few focused minutes move your English ahead.

Lena

Back to advice for students listening now. If money is your barrier, search for a bursary before you search for a loan. A bursary does not need to be repaid. One bursary can make you better off for years.

Maya

If paperwork is your barrier, make a folder, make copies, and write polite, firm emails. Use at your earliest convenience when you want to sound professional, and remember that a bureaucratic system respects clear records more than angry words.

Lena

If people are your barrier, report bullying, document bullying, and tell a trusted adult about bullying again if the first adult does nothing. Bullying thrives in silence. Your voice is part of the cure.

Maya

And if focus is your barrier, bury yourself in your books in short, deep sessions. Tell yourself, for the next hour I will bury myself in my books completely. Then reward yourself. Students who bury themselves in their books with a plan are better off than students who drift.

Lena

I would add one more thought about honours. We bestow too much honour on fame and too little on quiet service. A school should bestow its thanks on cleaners, counsellors, and the officer who processes a bursary kindly. When we bestow respect widely, culture changes.

Maya

Beautifully said. So, to review todays language in real life. A bursary is money that helps a student study. To bestow is to present an honour, a right, or a gift formally. Better off means in a more desirable or advantageous situation. Bullying is repeated aggressive behaviour intended to harm. Bureaucratic describes a system of administration with many rules. At your earliest convenience means as soon as you are able to. And bury yourself in your books means to study very hard and intensely, ignoring everything else.

Lena

If you remember those ideas through Saras story, you remember the words. She needed a bursary, she survived bullying, she fought a bureaucratic maze, she wrote at your earliest convenience, she chose the path that left her better off, she learned when to bury herself in her books, and in the end her university was proud to bestow its honour on her.

Maya

That is education and learning at C2 level, not just words, but judgment. Thank you for listening to Muse English. See you tomorrow.

Lena

See you tomorrow, everyone. Study well, be kind, and remember, you are better off learning a little every day than waiting for a perfect day that never comes.

📝گرامر: Future in the Past (was about to, was to, would)

Sometimes we talk about the past and need to say what was in the future at that time. *I knew she would call* looks forward from a moment that is already behind us.

Would is the past of *will*. Use would after reporting and thinking verbs in the past, for predictions, promises and reports seen from that earlier moment.

Use was about to for a very near future at that time, and was on the point of plus a verb ending in -ing for something even nearer, often interrupted. If a bureaucratic letter says reply at your earliest convenience, you may be about to miss a bursa
