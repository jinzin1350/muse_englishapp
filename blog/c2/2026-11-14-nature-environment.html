📚 لغت‌ها

۷ لغت تازه لغت‌های تازه با معنی فارسی، مثال واقعی و تلفظ.
brutal suppression /BROO-tul suh-PRESH-un/
گروه اسمی · noun phrase
Harsh and violent control or crushing of dissent.
سرکوب وحشیانه
"The government's brutal suppression of protests drew international condemnation."
carbon (dioxide) emissions /KAR-bun dy-OK-syde ih-MISH-unz/
گروه اسمی · noun phrase
Release of carbon dioxide into the atmosphere.
انتشار دی‌اکسید کربن
"Reducing carbon emissions is crucial for climate change."
carnivore /KAR-nuh-vor/
اسم · noun
An animal that eats only meat.
گوشتخوار
"Lions are carnivores, hunting other animals for food."
ceasefire /SEES-fyre/
اسم · noun
A temporary agreement to stop fighting in a war.
آتش‌بس
"Both sides agreed to a temporary ceasefire."
chuck it down /CHUK it DOWN/
فعل عبارتی · phrasal verb
To rain very heavily.
باران شدید باریدن
"It's really chucking it down outside; bring an umbrella."
civilian casualties /sih-VIL-ee-un KAZH-oo-ul-teez/
گروه اسمی · noun phrase
People not in the military killed or injured in conflict.
تلفات غیرنظامی
"The conflict resulted in a tragic number of civilian casualties."
clammy /KLAM-ee/
صفت · adjective
Unpleasantly damp and sticky, often due to humidity.
مرطوب و چسبناک
"The air felt clammy before the thunderstorm broke."

📝 متن پادکست

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I am Maya, and today Lena and I are travelling, at least in our imaginations, to a stormy coastline where nature, war, and weather all collide in one unforgettable week.

Lena

Hello, everyone. I am Lena. Our story begins in a small coastal region where a long conflict had only just paused, and where the first question on everyone's lips was whether the ceasefire would actually hold through the winter storms.

Maya

That word, ceasefire, is a perfect place to start. A ceasefire is a temporary agreement to stop fighting. In our story, both sides signed a ceasefire on Monday, and by Tuesday ordinary families were walking to the market again for the first time in months.

Lena

And yet a ceasefire is fragile. One loud explosion, one misunderstanding, and a ceasefire can collapse in an hour. That is why international observers stayed in the region, watching carefully and reporting every violation of the ceasefire the moment it happened.

Maya

The observers also wrote about something darker in their report. They described the brutal suppression of peaceful protests in a town just across the border, where people who had gathered with candles were driven from the square.

Lena

Brutal suppression means harsh and violent control, the crushing of dissent. When journalists use the phrase brutal suppression, they are signalling that force was not calm or measured, but deliberately severe.

Maya

In the report, the brutal suppression led to a debate in parliament. Some politicians defended the police, while others said that brutal suppression of unarmed citizens could never be justified, whatever the provocation.

Lena

And notice how the story connects to our second big theme, the environment. War is an environmental disaster too. Burning fuel depots raised carbon dioxide emissions across the whole region, and scientists measured those carbon dioxide emissions from satellites.

Maya

Exactly. Carbon dioxide emissions are the release of carbon dioxide into the atmosphere. Every factory, every truck convoy, and every fire adds to carbon dioxide emissions, and those carbon dioxide emissions trap heat and change the climate.

Lena

The observers calculated that one month of fighting produced more carbon dioxide emissions than the region's capital city normally produced in a whole year. That is a staggering figure, and it shows why climate scientists now study carbon dioxide emissions from conflicts as well as from industry.

Maya

Meanwhile, the human cost was impossible to ignore. The report counted civilian casualties in every district. Civilian casualties are people who are not in the military but are killed or injured in a conflict.

Lena

It is a phrase we hear on the news so often that we risk becoming numb to it. But behind the words civilian casualties there are teachers, bakers, grandparents, and children. Each statistic about civilian casualties is a family that will never be whole again.

Maya

In one village, the ceasefire arrived too late to prevent civilian casualties during the final night of shelling. Aid workers arrived the next morning, and their first task was simply to record the civilian casualties so that families could learn what had happened to their relatives.

Lena

That is heavy, Maya, but I think our listeners appreciate honesty. Shall we lighten the mood for a moment and talk about the weather in this coastal region, because the weather there was truly dramatic that week?

Maya

Gladly. On Wednesday the sky turned grey, the air grew clammy, and by noon it began to absolutely chuck it down. And when I say chuck it down, I mean rain so heavy that the streets turned into rivers within twenty minutes.

Lena

Chuck it down is a wonderfully British informal phrase. It means to rain very heavily. You might say, take an umbrella, it is going to chuck it down later, or, we cannot walk home now, look at it chuck it down out there.

Maya

And clammy, our other weather word, describes that unpleasant damp and sticky feeling on your skin before a storm. The air felt clammy in the aid workers' tent, clammy in the school, clammy everywhere, because the humidity was so high and there was no wind at all.

Lena

I remember you once described a tropical evening as clammy, Maya. Your shirt sticks to your back, your hands feel clammy, and even the paper in your notebook goes soft and clammy in the damp air.

Maya

That is exactly it. And in our story, after hours when it continued to chuck it down, the clammy air finally gave way to a cool, fresh breeze, and everyone agreed the storm had cleaned the sky.

Lena

Now, Maya, before we go any further, I want to pause and tell our listeners something useful, because learning words like these is much easier with the right daily habit, and that is exactly what our app gives you.

Lena

Beautifully said. Now, back to our coastline, because there is a nature reserve at the heart of this region, and that is where our next word lives. The reserve is famous for one magnificent carnivore, the coastal wolf.

Maya

A carnivore is an animal that eats only meat. A lion is a carnivore, a shark is a carnivore, and our coastal wolf is a carnivore too, hunting deer and rabbits across the marshes.

Lena

During the fighting, the carnivore population suffered badly. With no rangers in the reserve, poachers moved in, and every large carnivore, from the wolves to the wild cats, was suddenly at risk.

Maya

Biologists told the observers that protecting a top carnivore protects everything beneath it. If the carnivore disappears, the deer multiply, the young trees are eaten bare, and the whole ecosystem slowly unravels.

Lena

That is why, once the ceasefire held for a second week, the rangers returned. Their first survey found that at least one famous carnivore, an old female wolf the locals called Grandmother, had survived the war with her cubs.

Maya

I love that detail. And the rangers connected her survival to the climate story too. The marshes around the reserve absorb enormous amounts of carbon. When those marshes burn, carbon dioxide emissions rise sharply, so protecting nature is also a way of cutting carbon dioxide emissions in the long term.

Lena

Which brings us back to politics. At the peace conference, negotiators argued about who should pay for the environmental damage. One delegate said the brutal suppression of the protests had to be investigated before any money was discussed.

Maya

Another delegate answered that the ceasefire had to come first, because without a ceasefire there would be more civilian casualties, more fires, and even higher carbon dioxide emissions. First stop the fighting, she said, then argue about everything else.

Lena

It is a powerful argument. Every extra week of war means more civilian casualties, and every report of civilian casualties makes reconciliation harder. The phrase civilian casualties therefore appears again and again in the conference documents, and sadly, so does brutal suppression.

Maya

The conference also heard from a young doctor who had worked through the final bombardment. She said she never wanted to fill in another form recording civilian casualties, and she begged both sides to turn the temporary ceasefire into a permanent peace.

Lena

And outside the conference hall, as if the sky itself wanted to comment, it began to chuck it down again. Delegates ran for cover laughing, because after months of fear, ordinary rain, even rain that could chuck it down for hours, felt like a gift.

Maya

There is something poetic about that. The clammy conference room emptied, people stood under the awnings with coffee, their sleeves clammy from the humid air, watching the rain wash the dust from the streets.

Lena

Maya, that reminds me, we should give our listeners one more practical tip before we reach the final part of the story, because a daily routine really does change everything when you are learning a language at this level.

Maya

Absolutely. Do not just listen to English, use it daily in small ways. Points and a leaderboard keep you motivated, it takes only minutes a day, and installation is free.

Lena

I second every word of that. Now, the ending of our story. Three months later, the ceasefire was still holding. It was no longer called a ceasefire by the newspapers, in fact, they had started calling it the peace.

Maya

The investigation into the brutal suppression had begun, slowly and imperfectly, but it had begun. And in the reserve, Grandmother the wolf, our favourite carnivore, was photographed with four healthy cubs near the marsh.

Lena

The region also signed its first climate agreement, promising to measure carbon dioxide emissions every year and to replant the burned forests. It is a small promise, but small promises kept are how recovery starts.

Maya

And on the day the agreement was signed, the weather performed one last time. The morning was clammy, the clouds gathered by lunch, and by three o'clock it had started to chuck it down so hard that the signing ceremony moved indoors, with everyone laughing and shaking the rain from their coats.

Lena

What a journey these seven words have taken us on. From a ceasefire that gave people hope, to the brutal suppression that the world must not forget, to the civilian casualties whose stories deserve to be told with care.

Maya

And from the science of carbon dioxide emissions to a proud carnivore in the marshes, and from clammy air to rain that can truly chuck it down, this is the living English of news, nature, and everyday conversation.

Lena

Practise these words out loud this week. Use ceasefire in a sentence about the news, describe a clammy evening, warn a friend that it is about to chuck it down, and explain to someone why a wolf is a carnivore.

Maya

And when you report the news in English, remember the precise phrases, brutal suppression, civilian casualties, and carbon dioxide emissions, because precision is what advanced English is all about. Thank you for listening to Muse English, and we will see you tomorrow with seven more words.

📝گرامر: Nominalisation in Formal English

Nominalisation means expressing an action or quality as a noun instead of a verb or adjective. *Prices rose sharply* becomes *there was a sharp rise in prices*.

It is typical of academic, business and news writing. It packs more information into fewer words and sounds more objective, which is why reports speak of the suppression of protests or a reduction in carbon dioxide emissions.

Two things change: the verb becomes a noun, and the adverb becomes an adjective. *The company expanded rapidly* becomes *the company's rapid expansion*.
