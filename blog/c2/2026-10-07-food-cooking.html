📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
mise en place /meez ahn PLASS/
noun
everything prepared and in place before cooking begins
"Before service begins, every chef completes their mise en place."
amuse-bouche /uh-MYOOZ-BOOSH/
noun
a small free appetizer served by the chef before the meal
"The meal started with an amuse-bouche of chilled pumpkin soup."
degustation /deh-gus-tuh-SYON/
noun
a tasting menu of many small courses
"We booked a seven-course degustation at the new riverside restaurant."
terroir /teh-RWAHR/
noun
the soil, climate, and landscape that give food or wine its character
"This wine tastes of its terroir, the rocky hills of the region."
sommelier /suh-muhl-YAY/
noun
the wine expert in a restaurant who advises guests
"The sommelier suggested a light red to match the lamb."
umami /oo-MAH-mee/
noun
the rich savory taste, sometimes called the fifth taste
"Parmesan and mushrooms both add a deep umami flavor."
gustatory /GUS-tuh-tor-ee/
adjective
relating to the sense of taste
"The chef designed the evening as a gustatory journey."
concoction /kun-KOK-shun/
noun
a mixture of ingredients, often unusual or inventive
"Her strange concoction of mango and chili surprised everyone."
delectable /dih-LEK-tuh-bul/
adjective
extremely delicious
"The chocolate dessert was absolutely delectable."
infusion /in-FYOO-zhun/
noun
flavor drawn out by soaking ingredients in hot liquid
"She prepared a calming infusion of chamomile and honey."

Maya

Welcome back to Word Kitchen, the show where we savor the English language one delicious word at a time. I'm Maya.

Lena

And I'm Lena. And Maya, I have to say, today's theme might be my favorite one yet.

Maya

I think it's mine too. Today we're exploring the language of fine dining. Ten rich, flavorful words about great food, the people who make it, and the experiences around the table.

Lena

Food vocabulary is so much fun because every word comes with a taste or a smell attached. You don't just learn the word, you almost experience it.

Maya

That's so true. Let's start in the kitchen, before a single pan gets hot, with a phrase every professional chef swears by: mise en place.

Lena

Mise en place. I love how it sounds. It's French, and it literally means everything in its place.

Maya

Exactly. Mise en place is the practice of preparing everything before you start cooking. All the vegetables chopped, all the spices measured, all the sauces ready.

Lena

So instead of panicking halfway through a recipe, your mise en place keeps you calm and in control.

Maya

Precisely. In restaurant kitchens, mise en place is almost a religion. A chef's mise en place has to be perfect before the dinner service begins.

Lena

You know, I started doing mise en place at home last month, and it changed everything. No more burnt garlic while I search for the olive oil.

Maya

See? Mise en place works in a tiny home kitchen just as well as in a grand restaurant. That was our first word, by the way: mise en place. Everything prepared before cooking begins.

Lena

Beautiful. Now, let's move from the kitchen to the dining room. Imagine you sit down at an elegant restaurant, and before you even order, the waiter brings you a tiny, gorgeous bite, completely free.

Maya

Oh, I know this one. That's an amuse-bouche.

Lena

Yes. An amuse-bouche is a small complimentary appetizer from the chef, meant to amuse your mouth and wake up your appetite.

Maya

I love receiving an amuse-bouche. It feels like a little gift, a preview of the chef's creativity.

Lena

It really is a gift. A great amuse-bouche is tiny, maybe one or two bites, but it tells you so much about the chef's style.

Maya

I remember an amuse-bouche I had in Montreal once. It was a single spoonful of chilled corn soup with a drop of truffle oil. I still think about it.

Lena

That's the magic of a perfect amuse-bouche. Small, surprising, and unforgettable. So that's word two: amuse-bouche, the chef's little welcome gift.

Maya

Now, what if you want the full experience, not just one bite? Then you might order a degustation.

Lena

A degustation. That's a tasting menu, right? Many small courses, one after another.

Maya

Exactly. A degustation is a carefully designed sequence of small dishes, sometimes five courses, sometimes ten or more.

Lena

A degustation is such a wonderful way to explore a chef's imagination. Each course is like a chapter in a story.

Maya

And the pacing of a degustation matters so much. The dishes build on each other, from light to rich, from simple to complex.

Lena

I did a degustation for my birthday last year. Seven courses. By the fourth course I was in heaven, and I still remember the dessert.

Maya

A great degustation can take three hours, and you never want it to end. Word three: degustation, the multi-course tasting menu.

Maya

Let's talk about wine for a moment, because our next word belongs to both food and wine lovers: terroir.

Lena

Terroir. Another French word. It refers to the land itself, right? The soil, the climate, the hillside where something grows.

Maya

Exactly. Terroir is the complete natural environment that gives a food or a wine its unique character. Two vineyards a mile apart can produce completely different wines because their terroir is different.

Lena

So when someone says a cheese tastes of its terroir, they mean you can taste the meadow, the air, the place it came from.

Maya

Beautifully put. Terroir reminds us that great flavor starts long before the kitchen, out in the fields and on the hillsides.

Lena

I love that idea. The terroir is like the ingredient's birthplace, and it never fully leaves the flavor.

Maya

That's word four: terroir, the land and climate behind the taste.

Lena

Now, if you're enjoying that wine with its beautiful terroir, you might want some expert guidance. That's where our next word comes in: sommelier.

Maya

A sommelier is the wine expert in a fine restaurant. They help you choose the perfect bottle for your meal.

Lena

A great sommelier does so much more than pour wine. A sommelier understands how flavors work together.

Maya

Right. You might tell the sommelier what you're eating, and the sommelier will suggest a wine that makes both the food and the wine taste better.

Lena

I always feel a little nervous talking to a sommelier, but honestly, every sommelier I've met loves sharing their knowledge.

Maya

Same here. A good sommelier never judges. They just want you to have a wonderful evening. Word five: sommelier, your guide to the wine list.

Lena

Speaking of flavor, let's talk about the science of taste. Our next word is umami.

Maya

Umami. It's often called the fifth taste, alongside sweet, sour, salty, and bitter.

Lena

Umami is that deep, savory richness you taste in things like parmesan cheese, mushrooms, soy sauce, and slow-cooked meat.

Maya

Exactly. When a dish has wonderful umami, it tastes satisfying in a way that's hard to describe but impossible to miss.

Lena

I think umami is why we love so many comfort foods. That savory depth makes us want another bite.

Maya

Chefs work hard to build umami into their dishes, layering ingredients that each add their own savory note.

Lena

So the next time a soup tastes incredibly rich and you can't explain why, you're probably tasting umami. Word six: umami, the savory fifth taste.

Maya

Our next word is a beautiful adjective: gustatory.

Lena

Gustatory means relating to the sense of taste, right?

Maya

Yes. Anything gustatory is about tasting and flavor. A chef might describe an evening as a gustatory adventure.

Lena

I love that. A meal isn't just eating, it's a gustatory experience, engaging all your senses through flavor.

Maya

Food critics use gustatory when they want to sound precise. Instead of saying the food tasted good, they describe its gustatory qualities.

Lena

So a gustatory delight is a delight for your taste buds. Word seven: gustatory, all about the sense of taste.

Maya

Now for something playful. Imagine a bartender mixing mango, chili, and fresh mint into one surprising drink. That drink is a concoction.

Lena

A concoction is a mixture of ingredients, often unusual or inventive. It can be delicious or delightfully strange.

Maya

Exactly. Every new cocktail starts as someone's concoction. Some concoctions become classics, and some are never ordered twice.

Lena

I once tasted a concoction of espresso, orange peel, and tonic water. Strange, but I still remember it.

Maya

That's the fun of a concoction. It doesn't follow the rules. A bold concoction can surprise even an experienced palate.

Lena

Word eight: concoction, an inventive mixture.

Maya

When that mixture turns out perfectly, we need a word for how it tastes. Our next word is delectable.

Lena

Delectable means extremely delicious. It's stronger than just tasty.

Maya

Right. You might say a simple sandwich is tasty, but a dessert that makes you close your eyes in happiness is delectable.

Lena

I had a delectable chocolate cake in Toronto last spring. Three layers, dark and rich. Truly delectable.

Maya

When food is delectable, you slow down. You want every bite to last.

Lena

Delectable is one of those words that almost tastes good just saying it. Word nine: delectable, irresistibly delicious.

Maya

And our final word is infusion.

Lena

An infusion is flavor drawn out by soaking ingredients in hot liquid, like tea.

Maya

Exactly. When you steep chamomile flowers in hot water, you create a calming infusion.

Lena

Chefs make infusions all the time, not just for tea. An infusion of herbs in warm oil can transform a simple dish.

Maya

Or an infusion of citrus peel in hot cream for a dessert sauce. The gentle heat pulls the flavor out slowly.

Lena

I love ending the day with a warm infusion of ginger and honey. So simple, so soothing.

Maya

Word ten: infusion, flavor extracted by soaking. And that completes our ten words.

Lena

Let's do a quick review, Maya. Ten words, and I want to see if our listeners remember them all.

Maya

Challenge accepted. We started with mise en place, the chef's secret to calm cooking. Everything chopped, measured, and ready.

Lena

Then the amuse-bouche, that tiny free gift from the chef before the meal begins.

Maya

Then the grand degustation, course after course of the chef's best ideas.

Lena

We tasted the terroir in every glass, remembering that flavor begins in the soil and the climate.

Maya

And the friendly sommelier guided us to the perfect bottle.

Lena

We explored umami, the deep savory taste hiding in mushrooms and parmesan.

Maya

We celebrated every gustatory pleasure, every delight of the sense of taste.

Lena

We laughed over a strange but memorable concoction at the bar.

We savored every delectable bite of dessert.

Lena

And we finished with a soothing infusion of herbs and honey.

Ten words, one delicious theme. If you remember the amuse-bouche that starts the meal and the mise en place that starts the cooking, you're already thinking like a chef.

And if you can taste the terroir, thank the sommelier, and describe the umami, you're speaking like a true gourmet.

📝Grammar: First conditional and future time clauses

We use the **first conditional** for real possibilities in the future. The structure is **if + present simple**, **will / won't + infinitive**. Never use **will** after **if**. Say "If the sommelier recommends it" — never "If the sommelier will recommend it". **Unless** means **if not**, and it takes the present simple. We won't serve the degustation unless every amuse-bouche is perfect. After **when**, **as soon as**, **until**, **before**, and **after**, use the present tense for future meaning.
