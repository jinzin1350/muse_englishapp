📚 Vocabulary

limerence /LIM-uh-runss/
noun
an intense, almost obsessive romantic attraction to someone
"His limerence for his colleague made it impossible for him to concentrate at work."
tryst /TRIST/
noun
a secret meeting between lovers
"The two arranged a midnight tryst at the old boathouse."
rapprochement /ra-prohsh-MAHN/
noun
the rebuilding of friendly relations after a disagreement
"There has been a cautious rapprochement between the two estranged brothers."
estrangement /iss-TRAYNJ-munt/
noun
the state of no longer being close to someone you were close to
"Years of silence led to a painful estrangement between father and son."
camaraderie /kahm-uh-RAH-duh-ree/
noun
warm friendship and trust among people who spend time together
"The camaraderie among the old school friends was obvious at the reunion."
fickle /FIK-uhl/
adjective
changing feelings or opinions quickly and without reason
"Voters can be fickle, supporting a leader one year and rejecting him the next."
unrequited /un-ruh-KWY-tid/
adjective
(of love or feelings) not returned by the other person
"She wrote poems about her unrequited love for her best friend."
avuncular /uh-VUNG-kyuh-lur/
adjective
kind, warm and friendly, like an uncle
"With his avuncular smile, the old professor put every nervous student at ease."
scion /SY-un/
noun
a young member of a rich or famous family
"As the scion of a banking dynasty, she was expected to join the family firm."
uxorious /uk-SOR-ee-uss/
adjective
(of a husband) excessively devoted to his wife
"He was so uxorious that he cancelled every trip that would take him away from his wife."

📝 Grammar: 7B: Second conditional, choosing between conditionals

The second conditional imagines an unreal present or future. Its classic shape is **if + past simple, would + infinitive**. In formal style, use **were** for every subject. **If the scion were** present sounds more polished than **if the scion was** present.
