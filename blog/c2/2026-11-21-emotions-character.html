📚 لغت‌ها

۷ لغت تازه لغت‌های تازه با معنی فارسی، مثال واقعی و تلفظ.
dejected /deh-JEK-ted/
صفت · adjective
Sad and depressed, especially after a disappointment.
افسرده، دل‌شکسته
"She felt dejected after failing her driving test."
devastated /DEV-uh-stay-ted/
صفت · adjective
Feeling extremely shocked, sad, or disappointed.
ویران، دلشکسته
"She was utterly devastated by the news of her loss."
diffident /DIF-uh-dent/
صفت · adjective
Lacking self-confidence; shy and hesitant.
کمرو، خجالتی
"His diffident manner made it hard for him to speak up."
diligent /DIL-uh-jent/
صفت · adjective
Showing care and conscientiousness in one's work.
کوشا، سخت‌کوش
"She is a diligent student, always completing her assignments."
disdainful /dis-DAYN-ful/
صفت · adjective
Showing contempt or lack of respect.
تحقیرآمیز، با بی‌اعتنایی
"He gave a disdainful look at the cheap imitation."
distraught /dis-TRAWT/
صفت · adjective
Deeply upset and agitated.
پریشان، آشفته
"She was distraught after losing her pet."
dogged /DOG-id/
صفت · adjective
Showing persistent determination and tenacity.
سرسخت، پیگیر، مصمم
"Her dogged pursuit of justice finally paid off."

پادکست: احساسات و شخصیت

گفت‌وگوی امروز را گوش کنید.

📝 متن پادکست

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I am Maya, and today Lena and I are exploring the hidden language of emotions and character, the words we reach for when feelings run deep and personalities reveal themselves under pressure.

Lena

Hello, everyone. I love this theme, because ordinary words like sad or shy often fail us. Today we have seven precise words, and by the end you will hear the difference between feeling dejected, being truly devastated, and becoming completely distraught.

Maya

Let us begin with a small story. Imagine a young singer named Sara who has prepared for months for an important audition. She is naturally diffident, which means she lacks self confidence and hesitates to speak or perform in front of strangers.

Lena

Diffident is such a useful word. A diffident person is not rude and not lazy. She simply doubts herself. Sara's diffident manner made the judges think she did not care, although inside she cared enormously.

Maya

Exactly. And Sara was also diligent. Every morning she practised before school, she checked every note, and she finished every exercise her teacher gave her. A diligent student does careful, steady work, even when nobody is watching.

Lena

I admire that. My grandfather was the most diligent person I knew. He kept neat notebooks for forty years. People sometimes confuse diligent with boring, but diligent work is often the quiet engine behind every success.

Maya

On the day of the audition, Sara sang beautifully, but the panel chose someone else. On the train home she felt completely dejected. Dejected means sad and depressed, especially after a disappointment. She stared out of the window, and even her favourite song could not lift her mood.

Lena

Dejected is stronger than a little disappointed, is it not? If a football team loses a final, the players sit dejected in the dressing room. Their shoulders fall, their voices drop, and hope feels far away.

Maya

Yes, and context matters. You might feel dejected after failing a driving test, or dejected when a plan you loved is cancelled. It is a heavy, discouraged feeling, but it usually passes as life moves forward.

Lena

That distinction helps, because our next word, devastated, is much stronger. When Sara reached home, she learned that the community theatre she loved would close forever. She was devastated by the news. Devastated means extremely shocked, sad, or disappointed, as if something inside you has been destroyed.

Maya

A family can be devastated by the loss of their home. A writer can be devastated when years of work disappear. And Sara, who had grown up in that theatre, sat on the steps and cried, utterly devastated.

Lena

I remember feeling devastated when my best friend moved to another continent. Ordinary sadness was not enough to describe it. I felt devastated for weeks, and every familiar street reminded me of her.

Maya

Before we continue with Sara, this is a good moment to talk about how you can practise words like these every day. In the Muse English app, each daily lesson gives you new words with clear photos, natural examples, and pronunciation you can listen to and repeat. You hear the words in a podcast like this one, you practise them in a shadowing story, and then a short quiz checks what you remember. It takes only a few minutes, it is completely free to install, and those small daily steps build a vocabulary that actually stays with you when you need it most.

Lena

Thank you, Maya. Now, back to Sara. Her older brother, Daniel, reacted very differently to the theatre closing. He became distraught. Distraught means deeply upset and agitated, unable to think calmly. He paced the kitchen, checked his phone again and again, and could not settle.

Maya

Distraught is dejected and devastated with restless energy added. A distraught parent waits outside a hospital room. A distraught traveller searches an airport after losing a passport. The mind keeps racing, and calm advice is hard to hear.

Lena

Sara told me she had once been distraught after losing her dog in a crowded park. She called his name until her voice hurt. Luckily a kind stranger found him, and her distraught tears turned into tears of relief.

Maya

Meanwhile, the theatre director held a public meeting. One wealthy sponsor was openly disdainful. Disdainful means showing contempt or a lack of respect. He gave a disdainful look at the old building and called the actors amateurs who were wasting money.

Lena

That disdainful tone made the room angry. Nobody minds honest criticism, but a disdainful remark says, I am above you and you are worth nothing. His disdainful smile hurt more than his refusal to help.

Maya

Sara, although still diffident, stood up. Her hands shook, because a diffident speaker fears attention, but her diligent preparation gave her facts. She listed every class, every ticket, and every child the theatre had helped.

Lena

That is a beautiful point. Being diffident does not mean being weak. A diffident person can be brave precisely because speaking costs her so much. And being diligent meant Sara had the evidence ready when the moment came.

Maya

The sponsor listened, still a little disdainful, but less certain. Then Daniel, no longer distraught, presented a plan to save the building. A week earlier he had been too distraught to sleep, yet now his energy had found a direction.

Lena

Emotions change, and our words should change with them. At first the whole company felt dejected after the bad news. Then many felt devastated when they thought the theatre would close. Some became distraught during the uncertain days that followed.

Maya

And through all of it, one quality carried them forward. Our final word is dogged. Dogged means showing persistent determination and tenacity. Sara showed dogged determination. She knocked on doors, wrote letters, and organised concerts, and she refused to give up.

Lena

Dogged is a word I want learners to keep. A dogged pursuit of justice can change a community. A dogged student revises after a failure. A dogged athlete trains through a long winter. It is determination that keeps going when enthusiasm fades.

Maya

The theatre staff were dogged as well. Their dogged efforts raised half the money in one month. Even the sponsor, who had been so disdainful at the meeting, admitted that such dogged commitment deserved respect.

Lena

Let me test myself with a quick contrast. If I miss my bus, I might feel annoyed, not devastated. If I fail an exam I worked hard for, I might feel dejected. If a loved one is in danger, I might become distraught. Does that sound right?

Maya

Perfect. And a shy student who avoids raising her hand is diffident, not disdainful. A disdainful student would look down on the lesson itself. A diligent student prepares carefully, and a dogged student keeps preparing after everyone else has stopped.

Lena

I also notice how these words describe character over time. Diligent and dogged are habits. Diffident and disdainful can be manners or attitudes. Dejected, devastated, and distraught are emotional states that rise and fall with events.

Maya

That is an advanced observation, and it will help you use the words naturally. You can say, she is a diligent researcher, or he made a disdainful comment, or they remained dogged in their campaign. Each word fits a slightly different grammar pattern, so listen for those patterns in the examples today.

Lena

Before we finish Sara's story, here is another thought for your learning routine. The Muse English app is designed for busy people who want steady progress without pressure. Every day you receive fresh words for your level, from beginner to advanced, with Persian meanings, clear pronunciation, and a podcast recorded just for learners. The shadowing stories train your speaking, the quizzes train your memory, and the leaderboard and points keep you motivated alongside thousands of other learners. Install Muse English free, open today's lesson, and let tomorrow's you thank you for starting today.

Maya

Thank you, Lena. So, did the theatre survive? Yes, it did. The council offered a grant, local businesses helped, and that once disdainful sponsor quietly paid for new seats. He never apologised in words, but he stopped being disdainful and started listening.

Lena

And Sara? She was no longer the dejected singer on the train. She still felt diffident before big performances, but she had learned that a diffident heart can hold great courage.

Maya

Her teacher called her the most diligent and dogged student she had ever taught. Diligent in every rehearsal, dogged in every setback, Sara had turned a devastating season into a beginning.

Lena

Wait, let us recap all seven words one final time, slowly and clearly. Dejected, sad after a disappointment. Devastated, extremely shocked and sad. Diffident, shy and lacking confidence. Diligent, careful and conscientious in work. Disdainful, showing contempt or no respect. Distraught, deeply upset and agitated. Dogged, determined and persistent.

Maya

Excellent recap. Notice how each word earns its place. A dejected player, a devastated family, a diffident speaker, a diligent worker, a disdainful critic, a distraught parent, and a dogged campaigner. Seven pictures, seven precise meanings.

Lena

My advice for this week is simple. Choose the word that describes your own life today. Are you feeling dejected about a result? Are you being diligent with a project? Are you staying dogged when progress feels slow? Use the word in a real sentence about yourself, and it will become yours.

Maya

And if you meet a diffident learner, be patient. If you hear a disdainful voice, stay calm. If a friend is distraught or devastated, listen first and judge later. Language for emotions teaches us empathy as well as vocabulary.

Lena

That is why I love words at this level. They do not just make your English sound advanced. They help you notice people more accurately, and they help you describe your own heart with honesty.

Maya

Thank you for listening to Muse English. Practise today's words in the app, try the shadowing story, and take the quiz while the story of Sara is still fresh in your mind. We will be back tomorrow with seven more words and another conversation.

Lena

Goodbye for now, everyone. Stay diligent, stay dogged, and be kind to every diffident beginner you meet, including yourself.
