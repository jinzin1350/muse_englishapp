📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
equanimity /ee-kwuh-NIM-uh-tee/
noun
calm, balanced composure, especially under pressure or in a crisis
"The surgeon faced the crisis with remarkable equanimity, calmly directing her team while the alarms blared."
magnanimous /mag-NAN-uh-mus/
adjective
generous and forgiving, especially toward a rival or someone who has wronged you
"In a magnanimous gesture, the victorious candidate invited her defeated rival to join the new cabinet."
obstinate /OB-stuh-nut/
adjective
stubbornly refusing to change your opinion or course of action
"His obstinate refusal to accept the committee's findings stalled the reform for another year."
reticent /RET-uh-sunt/
adjective
reserved and reluctant to speak freely about your thoughts or feelings
"Usually reticent about her private life, the novelist surprised everyone by publishing a candid memoir."
mercurial /mur-KYOOR-ee-ul/
adjective
subject to sudden, unpredictable changes of mood or behavior
"The director's mercurial temperament made every rehearsal unpredictable — brilliant one day, furious the next."
effusive /uh-FYOO-siv/
adjective
expressing feelings with overwhelming enthusiasm and warmth
"The critics were effusive in their praise, calling the premiere the most electrifying of the decade."
stoic /STOH-ik/
adjective
enduring hardship or pain without complaint; calmly accepting what cannot be changed
"She bore the months of painful treatment with stoic resolve, never once complaining to her family."
impetuous /im-PECH-oo-us/
adjective
acting quickly without careful thought; impulsive and rash
"His impetuous decision to invest his savings in the untested startup nearly cost him everything."
sanguine /SANG-gwin/
adjective
optimistic and confident, especially in a difficult situation
"Despite the gloomy forecasts, the analysts remain sanguine about the country's long-term growth."
truculent /TRUH-kyoo-lunt/
adjective
aggressively defiant and eager to argue; hostile in manner
"The debate turned truculent when one panelist began shouting over every rebuttal."

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome back to Word Kitchen, everyone. I'm Maya.

Lena

And I'm Lena. Today's menu is rich and emotional. We're serving ten advanced words about emotions and character, the words that describe how people feel, how they behave, and how they carry themselves through life.

Maya

Exactly. These are precision tools. Let's start with equanimity. Equanimity is calm, balanced composure even when everything around you is falling apart. Picture a surgeon in an emergency room. The alarms are blaring, the stakes are life and death, and she keeps her equanimity.

Lena

I love equanimity because it's contagious. A leader with real equanimity steadies the whole team. Last year my manager handled a disastrous product launch with remarkable equanimity, and honestly, her equanimity is what stopped the rest of us from panicking.

Maya

That's the magic of equanimity. When one person holds their equanimity, the room borrows it. Diplomats need equanimity during tense negotiations. The ambassador's equanimity during those long nights of talks genuinely impressed both sides.

Lena

Beautiful. Next up: magnanimous. A magnanimous person is generous and forgiving, especially toward a rival or someone who has wronged them. It's generosity with dignity.

Maya

A perfect example. After a bitter election, the winning candidate gave a magnanimous speech, praising her opponent and inviting him to help shape the new policy. That magnanimous gesture healed a divided country faster than any law could.

Lena

True magnanimous behavior costs something. It's easy to be generous when you're comfortable. A magnanimous leader forgives public criticism and still promotes the critic. I try to be magnanimous in arguments with my brother, though I'll admit my magnanimous moments are rarer than I'd like.

Maya

Ha! Same here. Now a tougher one: obstinate. An obstinate person stubbornly refuses to change their mind or course, even when the evidence is against them. My grandfather was obstinate about his old car. Mechanics begged him to retire it, but he stayed obstinate.

Lena

There's a fine line between principled and obstinate. The committee chair was so obstinate about the original plan that the reform stalled for a year. Her obstinate refusal to hear new data frustrated everyone.

Maya

But obstinate isn't always bad. The scientist was obstinate in defending her hypothesis, and she turned out to be right. Sometimes an obstinate streak is just conviction wearing work boots.

Lena

Well said. Next: reticent. A reticent person is reserved, reluctant to speak freely about their thoughts or feelings. My neighbor is lovely but reticent. It took three years before she told me anything personal.

Maya

Reticent people aren't cold; they're careful. The usually reticent novelist surprised everyone by publishing a painfully honest memoir. Interviewers who expected a reticent guest got the most open conversation of the year.

Lena

I was reticent about my promotion at first, not wanting to seem boastful. But being reticent became lonely, so I finally told my friends. Have you ever been reticent about good news, Maya?

Maya

Oh, constantly. I'm naturally reticent with strangers. Okay, word five: mercurial. Mercurial describes someone subject to sudden, unpredictable changes of mood. Brilliant one minute, furious the next.

Lena

The classic mercurial boss. My first editor was mercurial. Monday he'd praise my article as genius, Tuesday he'd call the same draft a disaster. Working for a mercurial manager taught me to never take either mood personally.

Maya

Artists are often called mercurial, though that's a bit of a stereotype. The conductor's mercurial temperament made every rehearsal electric. One moment he'd stop the orchestra to weep at a beautiful phrase, the next he'd demand they play it again, harder.

Lena

I think we all have mercurial days. My energy is mercurial before coffee. After coffee, I'm merely dramatic.

Maya

Agreed, and same. Next: effusive. Effusive means expressing feelings with overwhelming enthusiasm and warmth. Gushing, but sincere.

Lena

The critics were effusive after the premiere, calling it the most electrifying performance of the decade. Their effusive praise filled three pages of the morning paper.

Maya

I love effusive people. My aunt writes the most effusive birthday cards, full of underlines and exclamation marks. When she hugs you, it's an effusive, whole-body hug. Her effusive warmth makes every family gathering better.

Lena

There's a difference between effusive and fake. Effusive joy is real; you can feel it. When the team won the championship, the fans were effusive in the streets, singing until sunrise.

Maya

Word seven: stoic. A stoic person endures hardship without complaint, calmly accepting what they cannot change.

Lena

The marathon runner was stoic about her injury, finishing the race on a sprained ankle without a word of complaint. That stoic determination inspired the whole crowd.

Maya

Stoic doesn't mean feeling nothing. The nurse remained stoic through the hardest night of the outbreak, holding patients' hands while hiding her own exhaustion. True stoic strength is quiet.

Lena

My grandmother was stoic in the old sense. She raised four kids through a war and never once described herself as suffering. There's something deeply admirable about that stoic grace.

Maya

Now: impetuous. Impetuous means acting quickly without careful thought. Rash, impulsive, leaping before you look.

Lena

His impetuous decision to quit his job and move abroad with no plan shocked everyone. That impetuous leap worked out, but it could easily have been a disaster.

Maya

Youth is often impetuous. The impetuous young trader risked the firm's money on a hunch and lost a fortune. An impetuous apology can also backfire, by the way, saying sorry before you understand what you did wrong.

Lena

I've sent some impetuous text messages in my time. My rule now: write the impetuous draft, sleep on it, delete it in the morning.

Maya

Wise. Almost done: sanguine. Sanguine means optimistic, especially in the face of difficulty. Confident that things will work out.

Lena

Despite the gloomy economic forecasts, the analysts remain sanguine about long-term growth. Their sanguine outlook is based on decades of data, not wishful thinking.

Maya

Doctors often stay deliberately sanguine with patients. The surgeon's sanguine tone before the operation calmed the whole family. Being sanguine isn't naive; it's a choice to focus on what's possible.

Lena

I'm trying to be more sanguine. My default is anxious planning, but a sanguine friend of mine talks me down. Her sanguine attitude reminds me that most crises pass.

Maya

Last word, and it's a spicy one: truculent. Truculent means aggressively defiant, eager to argue, ready for a fight.

Lena

The debate turned truculent when one panelist started shouting over every rebuttal. His truculent tone killed any chance of real discussion.

Maya

Truculent behavior usually masks insecurity. The truculent customer at the cafe wasn't really angry about the coffee; he was having a terrible day. Still, nobody enjoys a truculent confrontation.

Lena

Sports crowds can turn truculent fast. The referee made one bad call and the truculent chanting started. Security had to step in before the truculent mood spread further.

Maya

Okay, lightning round. Lena, give me a sentence with equanimity and obstinate together.

Lena

The mediator kept her equanimity even when the obstinate delegates refused every compromise.

Maya

Nice. Now magnanimous and truculent.

Lena

She gave a magnanimous reply to his truculent email, and he actually apologized.

Maya

Beautiful. Reticent and effusive?

Lena

He's reticent with strangers but effusive with his grandchildren.

Maya

Mercurial and stoic?

Lena

Her mood is mercurial, but her work ethic is stoic. She never misses a deadline.

Maya

Impetuous and sanguine?

Lena

His impetuous investments worry me, though he's sanguine they'll pay off.

Maya

Perfect. That's our ten: equanimity, magnanimous, obstinate, reticent, mercurial, effusive, stoic, impetuous, sanguine, and truculent. Use them this week. Describe someone's character with precision.

Lena

And notice how they pair up. Equanimity versus truculent. Magnanimous versus obstinate. Reticent versus effusive. Stoic versus impetuous. Mercurial versus sanguine. Character is a whole spectrum.

Maya

Lovely closing thought. Thanks for cooking with us on Word Kitchen. I'm Maya.

Lena

And I'm Lena. See you tomorrow, and stay sanguine out there.

📝Grammar: Obligation and prohibition - have to, must, should

**have to** + infinitive expresses obligation that comes from an outside rule or a general situation, not from the speaker's own choice. An obstinate employee still **has to** follow the safety regulations. **must** + infinitive also expresses obligation, but it comes from the speaker's personal decision or a specific situation. I **must** thank my magnanimous host before I leave — nobody ordered me to; I decided it. **mustn't** + infinitive is prohibition: something is forbidden. You **mustn't** be truculent with the referee. Careful: **mustn't** does NOT mean the same as **don't have to**.
