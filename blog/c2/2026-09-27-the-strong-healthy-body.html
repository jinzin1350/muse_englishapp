📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
invigorating /in-VIG-uh-ray-ting/
adjective
making you feel healthy, fresh, and full of energy
"A cold morning swim is surprisingly invigorating."
debilitating /duh-BIL-uh-tay-ting/
adjective
making someone very weak and unable to function normally
"Chronic fatigue can be utterly debilitating."
stamina /STAM-uh-nuh/
noun
the physical or mental strength to keep going for a long time
"Building cardiovascular stamina takes months of consistent training."
sedentary /SED-un-teh-ree/
adjective
involving little movement or physical activity; sitting a lot
"A sedentary lifestyle increases the risk of heart disease."
wholesome /HOLE-sum/
adjective
natural, pure, and good for your health
"She prefers wholesome, unprocessed foods over packaged snacks."
ailment /AYL-munt/
noun
a mild illness or health problem
"The clinic treats every minor ailment, from colds to sprains."
remedy /REM-uh-dee/
noun
a treatment or solution that cures a health problem
"Rest and hydration remain the simplest remedy for a common cold."
longevity /lon-JEV-uh-tee/
noun
living a long, healthy life
"Regular exercise is linked to greater longevity."
immunity /ih-MYOO-nuh-tee/
noun
the body's natural protection against disease
"Vaccination strengthens your immunity against the virus."
convalescence /kon-vuh-LESS-unts/
noun
the period of recovering from an illness or operation
"During his convalescence, he took long walks by the sea."

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome to Word Kitchen, everyone. I am Maya, and this is Lena, and today we are cooking up something really special for your health vocabulary.

Lena

That is right, Maya. Today is all about the body, about feeling strong, staying healthy, and talking about all of it like a truly advanced English speaker.

Maya

Exactly. We have ten wonderful, sophisticated words for you today, all connected to health and the body. By the end of this episode, you will not just know them, you will feel them.

Lena

Oh, I like that. Let us start with something energizing. Maya, what did you do this morning?

Maya

Well, I went for a cold morning swim, actually, and it was so invigorating. There is nothing quite like cold water to wake up every cell in your body.

Lena

A cold swim sounds invigorating, but also terrifying. I am more of a slow, gentle walk person in the morning.

Maya

You should try it once. The feeling afterwards is invigorating in a way coffee never manages. It is like your whole body is humming with energy.

Lena

I believe you. An invigorating start to the day can change your whole mood. What makes an activity invigorating, do you think?

Maya

I think it is anything that wakes your body up and makes you feel alive and refreshed. A brisk walk in the cold air can be invigorating too. It does not have to be extreme.

Lena

That is a good definition. So, invigorating means giving you energy and freshness. Now, what is the opposite? Let us talk about the word debilitating.

Maya

Oh, that is a heavy one. Debilitating describes something that takes all your strength away, something that leaves you weak and unable to function.

Lena

Right. We often hear it with chronic conditions. Chronic pain can be debilitating. Severe flu can feel debilitating for a week.

Maya

Yes, and at an advanced level you can use it metaphorically too. Stress can be debilitating. A toxic work schedule can be truly debilitating for your health.

Lena

That is so true. I read once that sleep deprivation has a debilitating effect on concentration. So debilitating is about being weakened, drained of power.

Maya

Exactly. Invigorating gives you strength, debilitating takes it away. A perfect pair. Now, Lena, you mentioned your morning walks. How long do you walk?

Lena

About forty minutes every day. I am trying to build my stamina.

Maya

Stamina is such an important word. It is your ability to keep going, physically or mentally, over a long time.

Lena

Yes. When I started, after fifteen minutes I was done. Now my stamina is much better. Building stamina takes patience and consistency.

Maya

It really does. Runners talk about stamina all the time, but office workers need stamina too. Mental stamina matters as much as physical stamina.

Lena

Absolutely. A long exam, a demanding project, a full day of caregiving, all of these require stamina. How do you build yours?

Maya

Slowly. You increase the effort a little at a time, and your stamina grows. That is the secret of stamina: gradual, steady challenge.

Lena

Beautiful. And speaking of challenge, let us talk about the enemy of stamina: the sedentary life.

Maya

Oh yes. Sedentary is one of those words every advanced learner should own. It describes a lifestyle with very little movement, lots of sitting.

Lena

Desk jobs make us sedentary. Long commutes make us sedentary. Even relaxing on the sofa all evening is sedentary behavior.

Maya

And the research is clear: a sedentary lifestyle is linked to all kinds of health problems. The body was built to move.

Lena

It was. Breaking up sedentary time is so important. Even standing up every hour helps. Small movements fight the sedentary trap.

Maya

I love that phrase, the sedentary trap. Now, when we do eat, what should we eat? That brings us to wholesome.

Lena

Wholesome is such a warm word. It means natural, pure, and genuinely good for you, especially food.

Maya

Yes. A bowl of vegetable soup, fresh bread, fruit, these are wholesome foods. Nothing artificial, nothing overprocessed.

Lena

And it goes beyond food, does it not? We talk about wholesome habits, wholesome entertainment, even a wholesome lifestyle.

Maya

Exactly. Wholesome describes anything simple and healthy and good. A wholesome breakfast sets up the whole day.

Lena

So wholesome is natural goodness. Now let us move to a word doctors use a lot: ailment.

Maya

An ailment is a mild illness or health problem. Not something dramatic, just a small thing that bothers you.

Lena

Right. A cold is an ailment. A headache is an ailment. A sore back after gardening is an ailment.

Maya

It is a gentle, slightly formal word. Instead of saying I am sick, you might say I have a minor ailment. It sounds calm and precise.

Lena

Every family has its little ailments. My grandfather always says, at his age, some small ailment is just part of life.

Maya

That is a lovely attitude. And when you have an ailment, what do you look for? A remedy.

Lena

Yes. A remedy is a treatment, a cure, a solution that makes the ailment go away.

Maya

Hot tea with honey is an old remedy for a sore throat. Rest is the simplest remedy for exhaustion.

Lena

And again, we can use it metaphorically. Laughter is a remedy for a bad mood. A walk in nature can be a remedy for stress.

Maya

I love that. Finding the right remedy is an art. Sometimes the best remedy is simply time and patience.

Lena

Beautifully said. Now, Maya, let us zoom out. What is the big goal of all this healthy living?

Maya

Longevity, of course. A long, healthy life.

Lena

Longevity is such a rich word. It is not just living long, it is living long and well.

Maya

Researchers study longevity in places where people routinely live past a hundred. They look at diet, movement, community.

Lena

And the secrets of longevity are often simple: wholesome food, daily movement, strong relationships, purpose.

Maya

Exactly. Chasing longevity is not about fear of aging. It is about loving life so much you want more of it.

Lena

That is a wonderful way to put it. More years, and more life in those years. That is the dream of longevity.

Maya

Now, a word our bodies understand deeply: immunity.

Lena

Immunity is your body's defense system, its protection against disease.

Maya

Yes. When you are vaccinated, your immunity learns to recognize the virus. Good sleep and nutrition support your immunity too.

Lena

Strong immunity does not mean you never get sick. It means your body fights back fast and effectively.

Maya

Exactly. Building immunity is a long game: consistent habits, not quick fixes. Your immunity is your silent guardian.

Lena

I like that image. And when immunity fails and illness wins, what follows? Our final word: convalescence.

Maya

Convalescence is the quiet period of recovery after an illness or an operation. It is the body's time to heal.

Lena

It is such an elegant word. During convalescence, you rest, you eat well, you take gentle walks, and slowly you return to yourself.

Maya

Hospitals discharge people quickly now, so much of convalescence happens at home. A peaceful convalescence needs support and patience.

Lena

Rushing convalescence is a mistake. The body knows its own timetable. Respecting convalescence is respecting yourself.

Maya

So true. Before the recap, let us play a quick game, a little rapid round to test our own vocabulary.

Lena

Oh, I love this. You give me a situation, and I answer with one of our ten words.

Deal. Situation one: you climb three flights of stairs and you are barely out of breath, because you have been training for months.

That is stamina. Months of training build real stamina.

Maya

Situation two: you drink a glass of icy fresh orange juice after a run on a hot day.

Lena

Invigorating. That first cold sip is pure invigorating refreshment.

Maya

Situation three: your grandmother is ninety four, she gardens every day, and her doctor says her heart is perfect.

Longevity. She is the very picture of longevity.

Situation four: you sit at your computer for ten hours straight without standing up once.

Lena

A completely sedentary day. My back is already complaining just thinking about it.

Situation five: a bowl of lentil soup made from scratch, with fresh vegetables from the market.

Lena

Wholesome. Nothing could be more wholesome than that.

Maya

Situation six: you wake up with a scratchy throat and a runny nose, nothing serious.

Just a minor ailment. A small ailment that needs rest, not panic.

Debilitating. That kind of exhaustion is genuinely debilitating.

Ten out of ten. You are a Word Kitchen champion, Lena.

Great idea. One, invigorating: full of fresh energy, like that cold swim.

Two, debilitating: draining your strength, like chronic fatigue.

Honestly, stamina. I am training for a long hike, and building stamina is my daily project. What about you?

📝Grammar: Choosing between comparatives and superlatives

Use **comparative adjectives + than** to compare two people, places, or things. Short adjectives take **-er**; longer ones take **more**. Spelling matters: **big → bigger**, **easy → easier**, **hot → hotter**. One-syllable adjectives ending in **-ed** take **more**: **more tired**, **more stressed**. Three adjectives are irregular: **good → better**, **bad → worse**, **far → further** (use **farther** only for physical distance). To show equality, use **(not) as + adjective + as**: her recovery was as swift as his. **The same as** marks identical things: his symptoms were the same as mine.
