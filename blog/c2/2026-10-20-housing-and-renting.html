7 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
have the time of your life /hav thuh TYME uhv yawr LYFE/
idiom
To enjoy oneself immensely.
"We had the time of our lives at the party."
high-rise (flat) /HY-ryze FLAT/
noun phrase
A tall building with many floors.
"They live in a modern high-rise flat downtown."
hit home /HIT HOME/
idiom
To be fully understood and have a strong effect.
"His words about poverty really hit home."
home truth /HOME TROOTH/
noun phrase
An unpleasant fact about oneself that is true.
"My friend told me some home truths about my spending habits."
household word /HOWSS-hold WURD/
noun phrase
A person or thing widely known by everyone.
"Coca-Cola has become a household word globally."
hovel /HOV-uhl/
noun
A small, dirty, and miserable dwelling.
"They lived in a tiny, dilapidated hovel."
lead a sheltered life /leed uh SHEL-turd LYFE/
phrase
To live protected from difficult or unpleasant experiences.
"She led a sheltered life, unaware of real-world challenges."

📝Grammar: Ellipsis and Substitution

Natural English avoids repeating words. We either **replace** them with a short word (**substitution**) or **leave them out** (**ellipsis**). The most common substitutes are **one / ones** for nouns, **do / does / did** for verbs, and **so / not** for whole clauses. I don't like this hovel. Can I see the renovated **one**? She works in a high-rise, and he **does** too. **So** and **not** replace entire clauses after verbs like hope, think, and be afraid. Is the estate agent coming? I hope **so**. Will the rent rise? I'm afraid **not**. Ellipsis drops words the listener can already recover.
