Muse English یک اپلیکیشن رایگان آموزش زبان انگلیسی است، با درس روزانه برای هر شش سطح، از A1 (مبتدی) تا C2 (تسلط کامل). هر روز لغت‌های تازه با معنی فارسی، یک پادکست واقعی، تمرین شدوئینگ، گرامر و آزمون دارید. دربارهٔ اپ

📚 لغت‌ها

۱۰ لغت تازه لغت‌های تازه با معنی فارسی، مثال واقعی و تلفظ.
debit card /DEB-it kard/
اسم · noun
A card used to pay directly from a bank account.
کارت بانکی، کارت بدهی
"I paid for groceries with my debit card."
cabin /KAB-in/
اسم · noun
A small room on a ship or airplane.
کابین، اتاقک
"Our cruise ship cabin had a lovely ocean view."
chalet /SHA-lay/
اسم · noun
A wooden house or hut, especially in mountains.
کلبه چوبی (کوهستانی)
"We rented a cozy chalet for our ski holiday."
debtor nation /DET-er NAY-shun/
گروه اسمی · noun phrase
A nation that owes money to other nations.
ملت بدهکار
"The debtor nation faced economic challenges due to its loans."
carriage /KA-rij/
اسم · noun
A separate section of a train for passengers.
واگن (قطار)
"We found our seats in the third carriage of the train."
bureaucratic /byoor-oh-KRAT-ik/
صفت · adjective
Relating to a system of administration with many rules.
بوروکراتیک، دیوان‌سالارانه
"Dealing with the bureaucratic paperwork was frustrating."
at your earliest convenience /at yor ER-lee-est kun-VEE-nee-ence/
عبارت · phrase
As soon as you are able to.
در اسرع وقت ممکن
"Please submit your forms at your earliest convenience."
debt relief /det ri-LEEF/
گروه اسمی · noun phrase
The partial or total cancellation of debts.
بخشودگی بدهی
"The organization advocates for debt relief for poor countries."
bestow /beh-STOH/
فعل · verb
To present an honor, right, or gift.
اعطا کردن، بخشیدن
"The university will bestow honorary degrees next month."
credit card fraud /KRED-it kard frawd/
گروه اسمی · noun phrase
Illegal use of someone else's credit card information.
کلاهبرداری کارت اعتباری
"He was a victim of credit card fraud last month."

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I am Maya, and today is a review day, so we are going back over three rich lessons about money, travel, and education, and we are going to use every word again in a real story.

Lena

I am Lena, and I love review days. Instead of learning new words, we lock in the words we already met, and we hear them in new situations until they feel natural and easy.

Maya

Our story follows a young researcher called Dara. Dara is travelling to a mountain conference, studying on the train, and helping her family at the same time. Her journey begins with a simple debit card. She pays for her ticket with her debit card, checks the payment on her phone, and keeps the debit card in a safe pocket.

Lena

A debit card sounds ordinary, but Dara learned to protect it after a frightening warning. Her bank sent a message about credit card fraud in her city. Thieves were copying numbers, and credit card fraud was rising. She had never been a victim of credit card fraud, but the warning made her careful with every card she owned.

Maya

Exactly. She turned on alerts, she covered her number in public, and she told her younger brother that credit card fraud is not a distant crime. It can happen in a small shop, on a busy train, or on a website that looks perfectly honest.

Lena

A quick word for our learners. If you are listening in the Muse English app, you get fresh daily words with photos and pronunciation, this podcast, shadowing stories, smart quizzes, grammar lessons, and points that keep you coming back. Tell a friend who is learning alone that the app is free to install and their first lesson is waiting today. A daily habit changes everything, one small day at a time.

Maya

Thank you, Lena. Now, Dara boards her train and walks through the carriage. The carriage is warm and quiet, students are reading, and an old man is sleeping near the window. She likes this carriage because the seats face the mountains, and everyone in the carriage seems calm.

Lena

She finds her seat in the carriage, puts her bag above her head, and takes out her notes. The carriage moves slowly out of the city, past rivers and small villages, and Dara begins to review her conference paper about debt and fairness in the modern world.

Maya

Her paper asks a hard question. What happens to a debtor nation when its loans grow too large? A debtor nation owes money to other nations, and every year the interest grows. Dara writes that a debtor nation is not just a number in a report. A debtor nation is millions of families, schools, and hospitals waiting for a fair chance.

Lena

And she compares a debtor nation with a debtor country, which is almost the same idea in different words. She also studies the debt burden on ordinary people, because a heavy debt burden can close clinics and leave whole towns in deep poverty, where families cannot afford food, heating, or medicine.

Maya

Deep poverty is not a statistic for Dara. She grew up near a street where deep poverty shaped every day. She argues that debt relief can give a poor country room to breathe, paying for teachers instead of interest.

Lena

She uses that phrase again and again in her paper. Debt relief, she writes, is not charity. Debt relief is justice delayed for too long. And if a debtor nation receives fair debt relief, its children can stay in school, its farmers can borrow safely, and its towns can slowly escape deep poverty.

Maya

On the train, an older passenger reads her screen and smiles. He asks about her work, and she explains it simply. She says, imagine your family owes so much that every salary disappears. That is what a heavy debt burden feels like for a whole country, and that is why she believes in debt relief.

Lena

The train climbs into the mountains, and the plan changes. Dara was supposed to sleep in a hotel, but the hotel is full, so a colleague offers her a cabin by the lake and a chalet in the village. The cabin is small, just one room on the edge of the forest, and the chalet is larger, with wooden balconies and a view of the snow.

Maya

She chooses the cabin for the first night because she loves quiet places. The cabin has a wood fire, a narrow bed, and a window full of stars. In the morning she walks to the chalet for breakfast, and later she works in the chalet with her colleagues, drinking tea beside a warm stove.

Lena

I would choose the chalet every time. A chalet feels like a storybook house, warm wood and snow on the roof. But a tiny cabin forces you to rest and read. Dara slept in the cabin, worked in the chalet, and loved them both.

Maya

At the conference, the first problem is painfully bureaucratic. A bureaucratic officer cannot find her registration. The system is bureaucratic, slow, and full of stamps and forms. Dara stays polite, but inside she is frustrated, because every bureaucratic delay steals an hour from real work.

Maya

You get this podcast, a shadowing story, quizzes, and grammar lessons that build step by step. It is free to install, so finish one short lesson today, earn your points, and watch your streak grow.

Lena

She has seen this before at university. A bureaucratic office once delayed her grant for a month. This time, she keeps every email, every receipt, and every form, so no bureaucratic mistake can stop her. She tells younger students, respect the process, but never let a bureaucratic system make you feel small.

Maya

Her presentation goes beautifully. The audience asks about education, and she talks about bullying in schools, about how bullying destroys confidence, and about a small bursary that once saved her own studies. That bursary paid her fees in her hardest year, and she still remembers the letter that brought the bursary to her door.

Lena

And she gives advice that sounds simple but is powerful. She tells the students, if exams are coming, bury yourself in your books for a short, focused season. Not forever, and not alone, but bury yourself in your books, ask for help, and you will be better off than you expect. Students who plan are better off, families who save a little are better off, and travellers who check their bags are better off too.

Lena

Back on the mountain, Dara receives an email from the conference director. The email ends with a polite phrase, please send your final slides at your earliest convenience. She smiles, because at your earliest convenience sounds gentle, but everyone knows it means soon. She replies at your earliest convenience is fine, and she sends the slides that same evening.

Maya

That phrase follows her all week. The hotel writes, please confirm your chalet booking at your earliest convenience. Her bank writes, please update your debit card details at your earliest convenience. Even her brother writes it as a joke, please call me at your earliest convenience, because he wants news about the cabin and the famous chalet.

Lena

Ha, families always find the formal phrases funny. But Dara uses the phrase seriously in her final letter. She writes to a minister and says, please act on debt relief at your earliest convenience, because a debtor nation cannot wait another decade. It is polite, it is professional, and it carries real urgency.

Maya

At the closing ceremony, the university decides to bestow a special award on Dara. They bestow the award for her research and for her work with poor students. The president says, we bestow this honour on someone who never forgot where she came from, and the whole hall stands and claps.

Lena

To bestow something is formal and generous. A university can bestow a degree, a king can bestow a title, and a teacher can bestow praise that changes a young life. Dara says the greatest thing we can bestow is time. When we bestow time and attention on learners, we bestow confidence as well.

Maya

Her journey home is not simple. Her flight is cancelled, and she refuses to pay a cancellation fee for a flight the airline itself cancelled. She argues politely, shows her ticket, and the cancellation fee disappears. Then the airline offers a charter flight the next morning, a special charter flight for stranded passengers, and she accepts it with relief.

Lena

Before that, she had dreamed of business class, with a wide seat and hot food, but business class was far too expensive for a student budget. So she travels simply, reads in her seat, and saves her money. At the airport car park, she sees a clamp on a car that parked badly, and an officer explains that the clamp stops the car from moving until the fine is paid.

Maya

It is a funny ending to a serious trip. She thinks about the clamp, the cancellation fee, the charter flight, and business class, and she laughs, because travel teaches vocabulary faster than any classroom. Every delay, every ticket, and every strange sign becomes a new word you will never forget.

Lena

Let us review our key words one final time, naturally. Dara paid with her debit card, slept in a cabin, worked in a chalet, and rode in a quiet carriage. She studied a debtor nation, fought a bureaucratic system, warned travellers about credit card fraud, and argued for debt relief with real passion.

Maya

And she wrote at your earliest convenience without fear, and she watched a university bestow its highest honour. If you can hear those words and picture the story, the review has worked. The debit card, the cabin, the chalet, the carriage, and even the bureaucratic forms are now part of your English, not just words on a page.

Lena

One more picture. Imagine the carriage at sunrise, Dara reading in the carriage with her debit card safe in her bag, the chalet waiting in the village, and her speech about the debtor nation in her notebook.

Maya

Thank you for reviewing with us today. Keep your streak alive in the app, try the shadowing story, and speak these words out loud. We will see you tomorrow with a fresh lesson.

📝گرامر: The Subjunctive (I insist that he be on time)

The subjunctive is a verb form for things that are demanded, suggested or necessary, not facts. It uses the base form of the verb for every person: the doctor insisted that she stay in bed.

Use it after verbs such as insist, suggest, recommend, demand, propose and require, followed by that. A bureaucratic officer might insist that a debtor nation submit its forms at your earliest convenience.
