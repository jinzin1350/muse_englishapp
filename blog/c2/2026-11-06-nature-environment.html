📚 Vocabulary

7 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
biodiversity /bye-oh-dy-VUR-si-tee/
noun
The variety of life in a particular habitat.
"Protecting biodiversity is crucial for ecosystem health."
biomass /BY-oh-mass/
noun
Organic matter used as fuel or for energy production.
"Burning biomass can be a renewable energy source."
bird sanctuary /BURD SANK-choo-air-ee/
noun phrase
A protected area for birds to live safely.
"We visited a bird sanctuary to observe rare species."
blood sport /BLUD SPORT/
noun phrase
A sport involving the killing or injuring of animals.
"Bullfighting is considered a blood sport by many activists."
blowy /BLOH-ee/
adjective
Windy; characterized by strong breezes.
"It was a blowy day, perfect for flying kites."
breadline /BRED-line/
noun
The state of being extremely poor.
"Many families are living on the breadline due to unemployment."
breezy /BREE-zee/
adjective
Pleasantly windy; light and fresh.
"It was a breezy afternoon, perfect for a walk."

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I am Maya, and this is Lena. Today we are going out into the wild, because our words today are all about nature and the environment, and I have a story to tell you.

Lena

Oh, I love a nature story. Where are we going?

Maya

To a bird sanctuary. A real one, on the coast, about two hours from here. I went there last weekend, and honestly, it changed how I think about the natural world.

Lena

A bird sanctuary? That sounds peaceful. What is a bird sanctuary, exactly, for our listeners who have never heard the phrase?

Maya

A bird sanctuary is a protected area where birds can live safely. No hunting, no building, no loud machines. Just marshes, reeds, and thousands of birds. The bird sanctuary I visited protects a whole coastline, and the people who work there guard it like a treasure.

Lena

Beautifully said. Now, before we go further, let me pause for a moment, because I want to tell our listeners something useful.

Maya

Go ahead, Lena.

Lena

If you are enjoying this episode, you will love the Muse English app. It gives you daily words with photos and pronunciation, this very podcast, shadowing stories you can practise with, quizzes, grammar lessons, and a leaderboard where you can earn points. It is free to install, and it turns ten quiet minutes a day into real progress. So after this episode, go and download the Muse English app and start learning with us.

Maya

Thank you, Lena.

Lena

I can picture it. And the weather? Was it a calm, sunny day?

Maya

Not at all! It was a blowy day. A really blowy day, with strong wind coming in off the sea. My hair was flying everywhere, my jacket was flapping, and the guide just laughed and said, welcome to a blowy morning on the coast.

Lena

And after the wind, did it calm down?

Maya

Yes! By the afternoon it turned breezy. A lovely breezy afternoon, light and fresh, perfect for walking. The difference between blowy and breezy is the feeling. Blowy is strong and a little rough. Breezy is gentle and pleasant. A breezy day makes you want to walk for hours.

Lena

That is a beautiful distinction. So the morning was blowy, and the afternoon was breezy. I like that.

Maya

And the breezy afternoon was the best time to see the birds. In the breezy light, with the sun low over the water, you could see hundreds of them, ducks, geese, herons, all feeding in the shallow water of the bird sanctuary.

Lena

That sounds wonderful. And what about biodiversity? I know that word is one of today's words, and it matters so much in a place like that.

Maya

Biodiversity is the variety of life in a particular habitat. And this bird sanctuary is a perfect example of biodiversity. It is not just birds. There are fish, frogs, insects, wild flowers, and reeds, all living together. That variety, that richness, is biodiversity.

Lena

So biodiversity means many different kinds of life in one place.

Maya

Exactly. And biodiversity is fragile. If you drain the marsh or build a road through it, the biodiversity collapses. The birds leave, the fish die, and the whole system falls apart. Protecting biodiversity is the whole reason the bird sanctuary exists.

Lena

I read that protecting biodiversity is one of the most urgent jobs in the world right now. We are losing species faster than ever before.

Maya

We are. And that is why places like this bird sanctuary matter. Every time I visit one, I feel how precious biodiversity is. You can see it, you can hear it, thousands of wings in the air, and you understand that biodiversity is not an abstract idea. It is alive.

Maya

Now, back to the coast. Because my visit to the bird sanctuary was not only about birds. I also met a man called Tom who works on a biomass project nearby.

Lena

Biomass? That is another of today's words. What is biomass?

Maya

Biomass is organic matter used as fuel or for energy production. So, plants, wood, crop waste, things that were once alive, burned or processed to make energy. Tom's project turns farm waste into biomass energy for the local village.

Lena

So biomass is a renewable energy source?

Maya

It can be. Burning biomass can be a renewable energy source, because plants grow again. But Tom was honest with me. He said biomass is only green if you manage it carefully. If you cut down forests to make biomass, you destroy biodiversity, and that is a terrible trade.

Lena

That is a real tension. Biomass sounds clean, but the way you produce biomass matters.

Maya

Exactly. Tom showed me the plant where they process the biomass. It is small, it uses waste from local farms, and it powers the visitor centre at the bird sanctuary. That is good biomass.

Lena

Let me jump in here with our second little break, because I have something to share too.

Maya

Please do.

Lena

Learning English is a journey, and the Muse English app walks it with you. Maya

Thank you, Lena.

Lena

Wise words. Now, Maya, I have a harder question. While you were at the coast, did anyone talk about blood sport?

Maya

Yes, actually. And it was a difficult conversation. A blood sport is a sport involving the killing or injuring of animals. Bullfighting is the famous example. Fox hunting is another. And some people in the village were arguing about it.

Lena

What were they saying?

Maya

One older man said a blood sport is tradition, part of the culture, and people have done it for centuries. But a young woman from the bird sanctuary said no, a blood sport is cruelty, and tradition is not an excuse. She said every blood sport teaches people that animals do not matter.

Lena

That is a strong argument. And what do you think?

Maya

I think a blood sport belongs to the past. We now understand biodiversity, we understand that animals feel pain, and we have better ways to enjoy the countryside. A blood sport might be tradition, but so many cruel things were once tradition. The world changes, and a blood sport should change with it.

Lena

I agree with you. And it is interesting how the same village can hold both ideas, the bird sanctuary protecting life, and a blood sport taking life.

Maya

That contradiction is everywhere. People love nature, but they also defend a blood sport because their grandfather did it. Change is slow. But the young people I met, they were clear. They said, no more blood sport. Protect the birds, protect the animals, protect biodiversity.

Maya

Now, one more story from the coast, and this one is about people, not birds.

Lena

Go on.

Maya

Near the bird sanctuary, there is a small village, and some families there are living on the breadline. The breadline is the state of being extremely poor. These families have almost no money, and every week is a struggle.

Lena

That is sad. Living on the breadline means you cannot afford the basics.

Maya

Yes. Many families are living on the breadline because there are so few jobs in the village. The fishing is gone, the farms are small, and young people leave. The ones who stay are often on the breadline, and it breaks my heart.

Lena

But the bird sanctuary brings visitors. Does that help?

Maya

It does, a little. The sanctuary hires local guides, and visitors buy food and stay in small guest houses. One woman told me the sanctuary gave her a job, and for the first time in years her family is not on the breadline. She said, the birds saved us.

Lena

And what about the weather there, for the people who work outside all day?

Maya

They live with it. A blowy morning, a breezy afternoon, that is normal for them. The guides say a blowy day keeps the crowds away, so the birds are calmer. And a breezy evening is the best time for a walk along the marsh. They love the wind, blowy or breezy, because it is part of their home.

Lena

I love that. So, let us remember our seven words today. Biodiversity, the variety of life in one place. Biomass, organic matter used for energy. Bird sanctuary, a protected area where birds live safely. Blood sport, a sport that kills or injures animals. Blowy, windy and strong. Breadline, the state of being extremely poor. And breezy, pleasantly windy, light and fresh.

Maya

Beautifully summarised, Lena. And think about how they connect. A bird sanctuary protects biodiversity. Biomass can help or harm that biodiversity. A blood sport works against it. And the people who live near the sanctuary, some on the breadline, some working in the wind, blowy mornings and breezy afternoons, they are part of the story too.

Lena

That is the heart of it. Nature is not separate from us. The biodiversity of a marsh, the biomass in a field, the birds in a bird sanctuary, they are all part of our lives.

Maya

And if you visit a bird sanctuary one day, go on a breezy afternoon if you can. But even a blowy morning is worth it. The wind, the birds, the wildness, it reminds you why biodiversity matters.

Lena

Thank you for that wonderful story, Maya. And thank you, listeners, for joining us on Muse English. Practise these seven words, use them in your own sentences, and come back tomorrow for more.

Maya

Goodbye, everyone, and enjoy a breezy day wherever you are.

📝Grammar: Fronting and Word Order for Emphasis

**Fronting** means moving part of a sentence to the **beginning** for emphasis or drama. A castle stood on the hill becomes **On the hill stood** a castle. After a fronted place or direction, a simple verb can come **before a noun subject**. **Into the room walked** a tall man. **Down came** the rain. With a **pronoun** subject, do not invert. Say **Here it comes**, not Here comes it. Fronted adjectives and participles invert with **be**. **Gone are** the days of cheap flights. **So loud was** the music **that** we could not hear each other. **Such was** the demand **that** tickets sold ou
