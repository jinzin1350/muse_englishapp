📝 متن پادکست

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! Hello everyone, I am Maya, and today is a special review day. No new words today. Instead, we are going to practise the words from the last three days, and use them again and again until they feel easy and natural.

Lena

Hi, I am Lena, and I love review days. Last week I felt ill for two days. I had the flu, and my head was painful, really painful. When you are ill like that, even a small illness feels big, and you just want to rest.

Maya

Oh no, the flu is terrible. When I am ill, I cannot work at all. My brother was ill last winter too, and his illness lasted a whole week. Did you call a doctor, or did you just rest at home?

Lena

I did call the doctor, and she told me to rest and drink water. She also said a scientist in her hospital is studying the flu. That scientist explained that rest helps the body fight an illness. It was only a bit painful by day three, just slightly painful, not too bad.

Maya

That is good news. And speaking of health, a scientist on the news this morning talked about obesity. He said obesity is growing in many cities, and we need to reduce sugar and reduce fast food. If families reduce the quantity of sugar at home, a scientist believes obesity will slowly fall.

Lena

Yes, I heard that too. That scientist was clear. To reduce obesity, we should reduce big meals and move more. Even a slightly smaller quantity helps. A bit less sugar every day can reduce risk, and it does not need to be painful at all.

Maya

Exactly. Small steps work. Now, let me tell you something funny. Yesterday I wanted to buy a book online. I opened my browser, I started to browse a book shop, and I found a great book about a soldier. I clicked add to basket, then I had to type my name, and my browser was so slow.

Lena

Ha, that gave me a laugh. The same thing happened to me. I browse shops online every week. I read a blog by a famous blogger, I click add to basket, and then I forget to pay. That blogger always writes, remember to add to basket before the sale ends.

Maya

That blogger is funny. His blog always gives me a laugh. But Lena, before you add to basket again, please back up your computer. Last month my browser closed suddenly and I lost everything because I did not back up my work.

Lena

You are right. I always back up my photos now, and I back up my blog notes too. A good blogger should back up everything. If your browser breaks and you cannot type, you lose your work, so back up first, then type.

Maya

Before we continue, let me share something that helps me learn. In the Muse English app, I review words every morning with clear photos and friendly pronunciation. I listen to this podcast, I practise the shadowing stories, and I answer the short quizzes. My grammar lessons are there too, and I earn points on the leaderboard. It is free to install, and my English feels stronger every single week.

Lena

I use the app in exactly the same way. So, back to our stories. My cousin is a soldier. He is a brave soldier who works far from home. Last year he was ill in winter, and he said the cold wind was painful on his face. A soldier must stay strong, even when he feels ill.

Maya

A soldier has a hard job. My neighbour is a vet, and she helps the dogs of a soldier near our street. She told me a soldier once brought a small dog that was ill, and the vet worked all night. That is a kind story.

Lena

That is lovely. A vet is a kind of scientist too, in a way. A vet studies animals like a scientist studies nature. And talking about work, can you type fast, Maya? I type very slowly. When I type an email, I make mistakes, and I have to type it again.

Maya

I type quite fast now because I practise. Open your browser, type a short blog post every day, and you will get faster. First type slowly, then type with music, then type a story. Practice helps you type without thinking.

Lena

Good idea. I will type a little every day. Now here is my tip for shopping. When I browse a shop, I compare the quantity and the price. I only add to basket when I am sure. Last week I did add to basket for a warm coat, and I am very happy with it.

Lena

And now a quick word from me about learning. If you want English to stay in your memory, try the Muse English app every day. You get fresh words with photos, clear pronunciation, this podcast, fun shadowing stories, grammar lessons and quick quizzes, all in one place. You can see your progress, earn points and climb the leaderboard. Install it free today and make English a happy daily habit.

Maya

Yes, do it. It really works. So, Maya, let us finish with health. What is the best way to reduce a painful back? My back is slightly painful after work, just a bit painful when I sit and type all day.

Maya

A scientist on a health blog said stand up every hour. Do not sit and type for three hours. Move a bit, walk a bit, and reduce your sitting time. That will reduce the pain, and a painful back will feel better. Also back up your chair with a pillow, ha, that is a joke, but do back up your files while you stand.

Lena

That made me laugh again. We have had a laugh, we talked about a soldier, a vet, a scientist and a blogger, and we did not forget obesity. To reduce obesity, reduce sugar, and if you feel ill with the flu or any illness, rest, call a doctor if you need one, and take care.

Maya

Perfect summary. Today we reviewed our words in real stories. We used them when we browse, when we type in a browser, when we add to basket, and when we back up our work. Keep practising, keep smiling, and join us tomorrow for fresh new words. Goodbye for now.

Lena

Goodbye everyone. Stay healthy, do not get ill, and see you tomorrow on Muse English.

📝گرامر: Present Perfect with For, Since, Just, Yet, Already

We use the present perfect (have/has + past participle) with for and since to say how long something has continued until now.

Use for with a period of time, like for ten minutes or for two weeks. Use since with the starting point, like since Monday or since 2015.

Ask about time with How long...? For example, the scientist in today's story has worked in her city for three years, since 2023.

We use just for a very short time ago. It goes between have/has and the past participle: *She has just left.*

Already means sooner than
