📝 متن پادکست

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! Hello everyone, and welcome back to our daily podcast. I am Maya.

Lena

And I am Lena. It is great to be with you again. Today we are talking about health, because winter is coming and a lot of people around us feel ill at this time of year.

Maya

That is true. My neighbour felt ill last week. She had the flu, and she stayed in bed for four days.

Lena

Oh no, the flu is terrible. When my brother had the flu last winter, he felt ill, tired, and weak, and he could not go to work at all.

Maya

The flu is a common illness, but it can feel much worse than a simple cold. With the flu, your body hurts, your head hurts, and even getting up can feel painful.

Lena

Yes, my brother said his throat was painful and his legs were painful too. He said the whole illness felt painful from the first day to the last day.

Maya

That sounds awful. What did he do to get better?

Lena

He rested, he slept a lot, and he drank a large quantity of warm water and tea every day. The doctor told him that a good quantity of water really helps the body fight an illness.

Maya

I agree. When I had the flu two years ago, I felt ill for almost a week. My doctor said, rest, drink water, and reduce your work. She told me to reduce stress and reduce long hours in front of a screen.

Lena

That is good advice. So many people try to keep working when they are ill, and then the illness lasts longer.

Maya

Exactly. If you are ill, your body needs time. And now, Lena, I think this is a good moment to tell our listeners something useful.

Lena

Yes, let me share this. If you want to remember words like flu and illness, the Muse English app can help you every day. Maya

Thank you, Lena. That was a perfect reminder. Now, let us go back to health. We talked about the flu, but doctors also talk a lot about another problem, obesity.

Lena

Yes, obesity is a serious health problem in many countries today. Obesity means a person is very overweight in a way that harms their health.

Maya

And obesity is not only about how a person looks. Obesity can make walking painful, it can make the knees painful, and it can lead to other kinds of illness later in life.

Lena

That is right. A friend of mine works in a hospital, and she says obesity often comes with other problems. People with obesity may feel ill more often, and a small illness can become a bigger problem for them.

Maya

So what can people do about obesity?

Lena

Doctors usually give simple advice first. Reduce the quantity of sugar in your food. Reduce the quantity of fried food you eat each week. And move your body a little more every day.

Maya

I like that, because it is practical. You do not need a perfect plan. Just reduce one unhealthy habit at a time. For example, reduce sweet drinks from three cans a day to one can a day.

Lena

Yes, and watch the quantity of food on your plate too. A smaller quantity of rich food, with more vegetables, can slowly reduce the risk of obesity.

Maya

And if you feel ill or if exercise feels painful at the start, talk to a doctor. A doctor can check if there is an illness behind the problem.

Lena

Absolutely. Never ignore a painful knee or a painful back. Pain is a message from your body, and a long illness can begin with a small painful problem that nobody checks.

Maya

Let us review our words so far. Flu is the illness like a bad cold. Ill means not feeling well. An illness is a disease or a period of being sick. Painful means causing pain. Quantity means an amount of something. And reduce means to make something smaller or less.

Lena

And obesity is the state of being very fat and unhealthy. I think our listeners are doing great, because we have used every word many times today.

Maya

We have. And speaking of learning, here is one more idea for everyone listening.

Wonderful. Now, before we finish, let me tell a short story. Last month, my cousin felt ill at work. She thought it was only the flu, but the illness did not go away.

Lena

Oh, what happened next?

Maya

She went to the doctor, and the doctor said it was the flu, but a strong kind of flu. He told her to rest, to drink a good quantity of soup and water, and to reduce her screen time so her painful eyes could rest.

Lena

Did she get better?

Maya

Yes, after ten days the illness was gone. She said she learned an important lesson. When you are ill, slow down early, and your body will thank you.

Lena

That is a lesson for all of us. Rest early, drink a healthy quantity of water, reduce stress, and see a doctor when something feels painful for too long.

Maya

Perfect summary. And remember, obesity is a long term problem, so small healthy choices every day can reduce that risk over time.

Thank you for listening, everyone. Stay healthy, take care of your body, and keep learning English with us.

📝گرامر: Adjectives + Prepositions (good at, married to)

Many adjectives are followed by a fixed preposition. For example, we say good at, interested in, and married to.

The preposition is often different from your language, so learn each adjective together with its preposition.

Use at with good at and bad at. Use in with interested in.

Use of with afraid of, tired of, proud of, and full of. When Tom was ill, he was tired of staying in bed with the flu.

Use about with worried about and excited about. Use to with married to, similar to, and kind to.

Use **for
