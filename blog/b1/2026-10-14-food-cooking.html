Muse English: A Kitchen Full of Flavor

Maya

Welcome to Muse English! I am Maya, and with me is Lena. Today's ten new words are all about cooking and food, so put on your apron and join us in the kitchen.

Lena

I love cooking, and I love eating even more. Let us start with the first word, simmer. To simmer means to cook something gently over very low heat. The soup is not boiling hard. It is just simmering, with tiny little bubbles.

Maya

Exactly! When I make chicken soup, I simmer the soup for ten minutes. If you simmer slowly, the flavor gets deeper and richer. Never boil it fast, because a hard boil ruins the taste.

Lena

Our second word is roast. To roast means to cook in the oven with dry heat. She roasted chicken for dinner last night. Do you roast vegetables, Maya?

Maya

I roast vegetables every single week. I roast potatoes with olive oil and salt. A very hot oven makes them perfect. Roast at a high temperature, and turn them halfway through.

Lena

The next word is chop. Chop means to cut food into small pieces. Chop the onions before cooking, or your pan will not be ready. Can you chop onions without crying?

Maya

Not really! I chop onions fast and I cry every time. I also chop garlic and peppers for the pan. If you chop everything before you start, cooking is much easier and calmer.

Lena

Word number four is stir. Stir means to mix food with a spoon. Stir the sauce every five minutes, or it will stick. If you do not stir the rice, it burns on the bottom.

Maya

Stirring is my favorite job in the kitchen. I stir the soup, I stir the curry, I stir everything. Stir gently so the vegetables do not break. A wooden spoon is the best tool.

Lena

Now crispy. Crispy means hard, crunchy, and beautifully browned. The fries are hot and crispy. I love crispy chicken skin, do you?

Maya

I do! Crispy pizza edges are the best part of the pizza. To make food crispy, use hot oil and do not crowd the pan. Crispy fries need patience, but they are worth it.

Lena

The sixth word is aroma. Aroma means a pleasant food smell. The aroma of fresh bread filled the kitchen. A wonderful aroma can make you hungry in one second.

Maya

Oh yes! The aroma of coffee in the morning wakes me up. The aroma of a roast chicken brings everyone to the table. A good aroma tells you the food is almost ready.

Maya

Lena

Thanks, Maya! Let us continue with our seventh word, appetizer. An appetizer is a small dish served before the main meal. We ordered soup as an appetizer. Appetizers are small, but they are full of flavor.

Maya

At home, I make a simple appetizer with bread, cheese, and olives. A great appetizer should not be too big, because the main dish is coming. After the appetizer, everyone is ready for dinner.

Lena

Our eighth word is tender. Tender means soft and easy to chew. The meat is tender and juicy. Tender beef almost melts in your mouth.

Maya

Slow cooking makes meat tender. Simmer it long and low, and tough meat becomes tender. Nobody likes dry, tough steak. Everyone loves tender, juicy meat.

Lena

Now bitter. Bitter means a sharp, not-sweet taste. Dark chocolate tastes bitter. Black coffee is bitter without sugar or milk.

Maya

Bitter foods can be healthy. Arugula and grapefruit taste bitter, but they are good for you. A little bitter taste balances a sweet sauce. Too bitter is not nice, but a little bitter is interesting.

Lena

The last word is marinate. To marinate means to soak food in sauce before cooking. Marinate the chicken for one hour, and it will taste amazing. To marinate is to give the food extra flavor and make it tender.

Maya

I marinate fish with lemon, garlic, and herbs. Marinate overnight for the best result. You can marinate beef, chicken, or even tofu. The longer you marinate, the deeper the flavor.

Lena

Wonderful! Let us practice with a little story. Maya, how do you make your soup taste so good?

Maya

First, I chop the vegetables into small pieces. Then I simmer the soup for ten minutes on the lowest heat. A gentle simmer keeps the vegetables tender. The aroma rises from the pot, and it smells wonderful.

Lena

And the main dish? Tonight I will marinate the chicken for one hour in yogurt and spices. Then I will roast it in a hot oven. I hope the skin gets crispy and golden.

Maya

Do not forget to stir the sauce every five minutes while it cooks! Then, for dessert, dark chocolate tastes too bitter for me, so I will bake something sweet. A small appetizer first, a sweet ending last.

Let us review all ten words: simmer, roast, chop, stir, crispy, aroma, appetizer, tender, bitter, marinate. Can we use them all in one dinner story? Challenge accepted!

Lena

Tell me everything, step by step.

Maya

Last Sunday I cooked a big dinner for friends. First I chopped the onions, carrots, and peppers. Then I marinated the chicken for one hour in lemon and herbs.

Lena

Then what did you do?

Maya

Then I roasted the chicken in a very hot oven until the skin was crispy and golden. The aroma filled the whole house! I served a small appetizer first, warm bread with cheese.

Lena

The main dish sounds delicious. Did you simmer anything too?

Maya

Yes! I simmered a tomato soup for ten minutes on low heat. I stirred the sauce every five minutes so nothing burned. The meat was tender, the vegetables were tender, everything was perfect.

Dessert was not bitter at all. I know dark chocolate tastes bitter, so I made a sweet apple pie instead. The bitter black coffee after dinner was the perfect finish.

They really are. Chop your vegetables, marinate your chicken, roast it until crispy, simmer your soup low and slow, stir the sauce, and enjoy the aroma. That is cooking, and that is English!

📝Grammar: Present Perfect with For, Since, Just, Yet, Already

The **present perfect** connects the past to the present. We form it with **have** or **has** plus the past participle. Use **for** with a length of time. It answers the question how long — ten minutes, one hour, or three days. Use **since** with the starting point of time. It answers the question when it started — morning, Monday, or 2010. **Just** means the action finished very recently. It stands between **have** and the participle. **Already** shows something happened before now, often sooner than expected. It stands in the same middle position. **Yet** is for questions and negative s
