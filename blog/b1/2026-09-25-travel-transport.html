📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
itinerary /ih-TIN-uh-rer-ee/
noun
a detailed plan of a trip, showing places and times
"Our itinerary includes three cities in Italy."
boarding /BOR-ding/
noun
the act of getting on a plane, train or ship
"Boarding starts forty minutes before the flight."
platform /PLAT-form/
noun
the raised area beside the track where you get on a train
"Our train leaves from platform nine."
transfer /trans-FUR/
verb
to change from one vehicle to another during a trip
"We transferred from the bus to the metro downtown."
aisle /ILE/
noun
the long passage between rows of seats on a plane or bus
"She prefers an aisle seat so she can stand up easily."
customs /KUS-tumz/
noun
the place at an airport where officers check travelers' bags
"We waited in a long line at customs."
layover /LAY-oh-vur/
noun
a short stop between two connecting flights
"We had a three-hour layover in Istanbul."
carry-on /KAR-ee-on/
noun
a small bag you take with you onto the plane
"I only travel with a carry-on."
shuttle /SHUT-ul/
noun
a bus or plane that travels regularly between two places
"The airport shuttle leaves every twenty minutes."
connection /kuh-NEK-shun/
noun
a flight or train that continues your journey
"We missed our connection in Frankfurt."

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome back to Word Kitchen! Today we're talking about traveling smart — getting through airports and train stations without stress.

Lena

Great topic, Maya. I love traveling, but the airport part always makes me nervous.

Maya

Totally understandable. Airports are full of announcements, long lines, and signs in a foreign language. But a few key words make everything much easier.

Maya

Me too, until I learned some smart travel words. Let's start with itinerary. Your itinerary is your detailed travel plan — the list of places you will visit, and when.

Lena

So like, my itinerary for next month is Rome, then Florence, then Venice?

Maya

Exactly. I always print my itinerary before a big trip. Actually, do you share your itinerary with your family?

Lena

Yes! I send my whole itinerary to my mom so she knows where I am. Having a clear itinerary makes the trip feel real.

Maya

A good itinerary also saves money. If your itinerary is planned in advance, you don't waste time deciding every morning.

Lena

Okay, next word: boarding. That's when passengers get on the plane, right?

Maya

Right. Boarding usually starts about forty minutes before the flight. When they call your group, you show your ticket and walk onto the plane.

Lena

I always arrive early because I hate rushing during boarding. Last time, the boarding was delayed and everyone stood in line forever.

Maya

Oh, that's annoying. I like to wait in my seat until the final boarding call. Then there's no long line.

Lena

Smart. What about platform? That one is for trains.

Maya

Yes. The platform is the raised area next to the track where you get on the train. You check the departure screen to find your platform number.

Lena

Like platform nine at a big station?

Maya

Exactly. I once ran to the wrong platform and almost missed my train. Always double-check the platform on the screen.

Lena

Good advice. And when you change trains, that's a transfer.

Maya

A transfer is when you change from one vehicle to another during your trip. For example, you might take a bus and then transfer to the metro.

Lena

In London I had a transfer from the train to the underground. The signs for the transfer were really clear.

Maya

Some transfers are tight. My last transfer gave me only ten minutes, and I had to run!

Lena

Stressful. Speaking of seats — aisle or window?

Maya

I'm team aisle. The aisle is the long passage between the rows of seats. An aisle seat means you can stand up easily.

Lena

I love the window, but on a long flight the aisle is better. Last time I sat in the middle and climbed over two people every hour. Never again.

Maya

Ha! With an aisle seat you control your own space. The flight attendant walks down the aisle with the drink cart, so you get served first too.

Lena

True. Now customs — the place at the airport where officers check your bags?

Maya

Yes. After you land in a new country, you go through customs. Officers may ask what you bought abroad or look inside your bags.

Lena

The line at customs can be so long. Once I waited at customs for almost an hour in New York.

Maya

Same. Pro tip: have your passport ready before you reach customs. It speeds everything up.

Lena

What about layover? That's a short stop between connecting flights.

Maya

Right. A layover is the waiting time between your first flight and your next one. A two-hour layover is perfect — enough time to relax, not enough to get bored.

Lena

My favorite layover was in Istanbul — five hours, so I explored the airport food court. But a twelve-hour layover? That's too much.

Maya

For a long layover, some airports let you leave and see the city. Always check the rules before a long layover.

Lena

Before we move on, what's the difference between a layover and a connection? They sound similar.

Maya

Good question. A connection is the flight itself — the plane that takes you onward. A layover is the waiting time between the two flights.

Lena

So the layover is the boring part, and the connection is the next flight. Got it.

Maya

Exactly. And if your layover is long enough, you can sometimes turn it into a mini adventure in a new city.

Lena

Carry-on — the small bag you take onto the plane.

Maya

Yes! A carry-on is the bag you keep with you instead of checking it in. I travel with only a carry-on, so I never wait at baggage claim.

Lena

Same here. Packing a small carry-on forces you to pack light. My carry-on has everything for a whole week.

Maya

And a good carry-on fits in the overhead bin. If your carry-on is too big, they make you check it.

Lena

Shuttle — like the airport bus?

Maya

A shuttle is a bus or small plane that travels regularly between two places. The airport shuttle runs every twenty minutes between the airport and the city center.

Lena

Hotels often have a free shuttle too. When I land late, I take the hotel shuttle instead of an expensive taxi.

Maya

Smart. Some shuttles need booking in advance, so check the shuttle schedule before your trip.

Lena

Last one: connection. That's the next flight that continues your journey, right?

Maya

Exactly. If you fly from Tehran to Paris with a stop in Istanbul, the Istanbul flight is your connection. Miss your connection and you wait for the next one.

Lena

I once missed a connection because my first flight was late. Now I always choose a connection with at least two hours between flights.

Maya

Good rule. A tight connection is exciting only in movies. A comfortable connection means coffee, not running.

Lena

Let's review all ten: itinerary, boarding, platform, transfer, aisle, customs, layover, carry-on, shuttle, connection.

Maya

Quick challenge, Lena. I'll describe a situation, and you name the word. You're at the airport and they call your group to get on the plane.

Lena

Boarding!

Maya

You change from a bus to a train downtown.

Lena

A transfer!

Maya

The detailed plan of your whole trip.

Lena

Itinerary.

Maya

The small bag you keep with you on the plane.

Lena

Carry-on.

Maya

You're an expert traveler now. Try using all ten words this week — plan your itinerary, arrive early for boarding, and always give yourself a comfortable connection. See you next time on Word Kitchen!

📝Grammar: Present perfect and past simple

The **present perfect** is **have** or **has** + the **past participle**. Use it for past experiences when you don't say when, like a strange customs check or a long layover. Also use it for recent actions with a present result, often with **just**, **already**, or **yet**. Our boarding has just started, so the aisle is still empty. The **past simple** is for finished actions when we say, ask, or know when they happened. We missed our connection last Friday because the shuttle was late.
