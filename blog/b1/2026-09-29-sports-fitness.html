Podcast: Sports & Fitness

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Hello everyone, and welcome back to Word Kitchen, the show where we cook up English words and serve them with real conversation. I am Maya.

Lena

And I am Lena. Today's menu is all about sports and fitness, Maya. Ten fresh words for our listeners, and we will use every single one again and again, so by the end of the show, you will really know them.

Maya

Exactly. And here is our first word: workout. A workout is a period of physical exercise that you do to stay healthy. So Lena, what does your workout look like these days?

Lena

My workout is pretty simple. I do a thirty-minute workout every morning before work. Fifteen minutes of running, then fifteen minutes of weights.

Maya

Nice. My workout is in the evening. I finish a long day, and a good workout clears my head. Do you think a short workout is enough, or does a workout need to be long to work?

Lena

A short workout counts. Honestly, any workout is better than no workout. The best workout is the one you actually do, right?

Maya

True. And before any workout, we need our second word: warm-up. A warm-up is the gentle exercise you do before the hard part. Why is a warm-up so important, Lena?

Lena

Because your body needs time to wake up. A ten-minute warm-up gets your blood moving. If you skip the warm-up, you can hurt yourself. My warm-up is always jogging in place and arm circles.

Maya

Mine too. A good warm-up tells your muscles, get ready, we are about to work. Some people think a warm-up is boring, but a proper warm-up makes the whole workout better.

Lena

Absolutely. And speaking of muscles, let's talk about our third word: stretching. Stretching means doing slow exercises that make your muscles longer and more relaxed. When do you do stretching, Maya?

Maya

After every workout. Stretching is my favorite part of the day. I do stretching for my legs, my back, my shoulders. Stretching after exercise stops the soreness the next morning.

Lena

I agree. Stretching before running helps too. Gentle stretching in the morning wakes me up better than coffee. Do you think people skip stretching too often?

Maya

All the time. They finish a hard workout and walk straight home. But five minutes of stretching makes such a difference. Stretching is slow, calm, and your body thanks you.

Lena

Now, our fourth word: strength. Strength is the power your muscles have. How do you build strength, Maya?

Maya

With weights and with time. Strength does not come in one week. You build strength slowly, workout after workout. Lifting, pushing, climbing stairs, all of that builds strength.

Lena

Right. And strength is not only for athletes. An older person needs strength to carry groceries. A parent needs strength to lift a child. Strength is useful for everyday life.

Maya

That is such a good point. People think strength means big muscles, but real strength is just being able to do what your life asks of you.

Lena

Our fifth word is balance. Balance is the ability to stay steady and not fall over. Which sports need the most balance, do you think?

Maya

Gymnastics, for sure. And surfing. Yoga too. Yoga improves your balance and your posture. When I started yoga, my balance was terrible. Now my balance is much better.

Lena

Balance matters in daily life too. Carrying a baby while opening a door takes balance. Walking on ice in winter takes balance. Good balance keeps you safe.

Maya

Exactly. And you can train balance just like strength. Stand on one foot while you brush your teeth. That simple habit builds balance over time.

Lena

Word number six: coach. A coach is a person who trains athletes or a sports team. Maya, have you ever had a coach?

Maya

Yes. My swimming coach in school changed everything for me. A good coach sees what you cannot see. My coach told me my arms were crossing over, and that one tip from my coach made me twice as fast.

Lena

A coach can push you further than you would push yourself. But the best coach also knows when to rest you. A great coach mixes hard work with kindness.

Maya

True. Even professional athletes need a coach. A coach plans the workout, checks the warm-up, and watches your stretching. A coach is part teacher, part cheerleader.

Lena

Number seven is goal. A goal is something you want to achieve. What is your fitness goal right now, Maya?

Maya

My goal is to run ten kilometers without stopping. I set that goal in January. Every workout brings me closer to my goal. Do you have a goal, Lena?

Lena

My goal is to do a full pull-up. One clean pull-up. That is my big goal for the year. A goal gives your training a direction. Without a goal, exercise feels random.

Maya

And in football, a goal is also the thing you kick the ball into. So the word goal has two meanings, but both are about aiming at something and trying to reach it.

Lena

Love that. Now word eight: teamwork. Teamwork is when people work together as a group to reach a shared goal. Why does teamwork matter so much in sports?

Maya

Because no one wins alone. Winning a match needs teamwork, not just one star player. Good teamwork means passing the ball, trusting each other, and covering for mistakes.

Lena

And teamwork is not only in sports. A good workout class needs teamwork too. People cheer each other on. That teamwork energy makes everyone try harder.

Maya

Even your coach will tell you: talent wins games, but teamwork wins championships. Real teamwork is built in training, day after day.

Lena

Word nine: flexibility. Flexibility is the ability to bend and move your body easily. How do you improve flexibility, Maya?

Maya

Stretching, stretching, stretching. Daily stretching gives you more flexibility than any magic trick. When I was young, my flexibility was great. Then I sat at a desk for years, and my flexibility disappeared.

Lena

Same here. Now I do ten minutes of stretching every night for flexibility. Flexibility protects your joints. Good flexibility means fewer injuries when you play sports.

Maya

And flexibility is useful as you get older. Bending down to tie your shoes, reaching a high shelf, that is all flexibility. Keep working on flexibility and your body stays young.

Lena

Our final word, number ten: endurance. Endurance is the ability to keep doing something difficult for a long time. Marathon runners have amazing endurance.

Maya

They do. But endurance is built slowly. Long, slow runs build endurance. You cannot rush endurance. If you try to build endurance too fast, you burn out.

Lena

Endurance is mental too. The last kilometer of a race is all endurance of the mind. Your legs want to stop, but your endurance, your will to continue, carries you to the goal.

Maya

Beautifully said. So let's do a quick review. We had workout, your exercise session. Warm-up, the gentle start. Stretching, the slow muscle work. Strength, your muscle power. Balance, staying steady. Coach, your trainer. Goal, what you aim for. Teamwork, winning together. Flexibility, bending easily. And endurance, lasting long.

Lena

And remember, every workout starts with a warm-up, includes stretching, builds strength and balance, follows your coach's plan, moves you toward your goal, grows with teamwork, improves flexibility, and slowly builds endurance.

Maya

That was one sentence with all ten words. We did it. Thanks for cooking with us today on Word Kitchen. Keep practicing, keep moving, and we will see you next time.

Lena

Bye everyone.

📝Grammar: Obligation and prohibition - have to, must, should

We use **have to** and **must** to talk about obligation. Obligation means something you need to do. Use **have to** for general rules that come from the outside. These are rules at work, at school, or in a gym. Use **must** for a personal decision or a strong feeling inside you. It is your own choice, not a rule. **mustn't** is a prohibition. It means something is NOT allowed. Be careful: it is very different from **don't have to**. **don't have to** means something is NOT necessary. You can do it if you want, but nobody says you need to.
