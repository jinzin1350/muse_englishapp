📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
neighborhood /NAY-bur-hood/
noun
a part of a city where people live
"I love our neighborhood; everyone is friendly."
resident /REZ-ih-dunt/
noun
a person who lives in a particular place
"The residents of this building held a meeting yesterday."
skyscraper /SKY-skray-pur/
noun
a very tall building in a city
"You can see the whole city from the top of the skyscraper."
sidewalk /SYD-wawk/
noun
the paved path beside a street where people walk
"Please do not ride your bike on the sidewalk."
suburb /SUH-burb/
noun
a quiet area of houses near a city
"They moved to a suburb to have a bigger garden."
fountain /FOWN-tin/
noun
a structure that sends water up into the air
"Children like to play near the fountain in the square."
bridge /brij/
noun
a structure built over a river or road
"We walked across the old bridge in the evening."
crosswalk /KRAWS-wawk/
noun
a marked place where people cross the street
"Always use the crosswalk when you cross a busy street."
rooftop /ROOF-tawp/
noun
the top surface of a building
"We had dinner on the rooftop and watched the sunset."
traffic jam /TRA-fik jam/
noun
a long line of cars that cannot move
"We were late because of a big traffic jam."

Podcast: Cities & Daily Life

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome back to Word Kitchen, everyone. I am Maya, and this is my friend Lena.

Lena

Hi there. Maya, I have been thinking about city life all week, and I have so much to tell you.

Maya

Oh, perfect topic for today. City life it is. Tell me, what has been going on in your neighborhood?

Lena

Well, you know my neighborhood has been changing a lot lately. Last month a new cafe opened on our street, and now the whole neighborhood feels different, in a good way.

Maya

I love that. A good neighborhood really changes how you feel about a city. When I moved to my neighborhood three years ago, I did not know anyone, but the neighborhood welcomed me right away.

Lena

Exactly. The people make the neighborhood. Actually, that reminds me of something funny. A new resident moved into the apartment next to mine, and on his very first day he knocked on my door with a box of cookies.

Maya

That is the sweetest thing. Every resident should do that. You know, as a long-time resident of this area, I can tell you that our street has the friendliest residents in the whole city.

Lena

I believe that. And speaking of the street, have you noticed the new skyscraper going up near the park?

Maya

Oh my God, yes. That skyscraper is getting taller every week. I walk past that skyscraper on my way to work, and I keep thinking, how do they build a skyscraper that high?

Lena

I have no idea, but I want to visit the top of that skyscraper when it opens. Imagine the view from the top of a skyscraper like that. You could see the whole city.

Maya

Definitely. You know what I love about tall cities though? Even with a giant skyscraper on every corner, life still happens down here, on the sidewalk.

Lena

That is so true. The sidewalk is where the city really lives. I was walking down the sidewalk this morning, and I saw a street musician playing guitar right on the sidewalk.

Maya

I love that. I take a long walk on the sidewalk every evening after dinner. The sidewalk near the river is my favorite place to think.

Lena

And it is so much safer when the sidewalk is wide and clean. Our neighborhood association actually asked the city to repair the sidewalk near the school, and they finally did it last week.

Maya

That is great news. Now tell me, do you ever think about moving out of the city? A lot of my friends are moving to a suburb.

Lena

Funny you ask. My sister just moved to a suburb last spring. She says life in a suburb is quieter, but she misses the city energy.

Maya

I have thought about it too. A suburb with a garden sounds lovely, but I think I would get bored in a suburb after a month. I need the noise and the people.

Lena

Same here. But my sister says her suburb has a lovely park, and every suburb near us has a train station now, so she is only twenty minutes from downtown.

Maya

Twenty minutes is not bad at all. And you know what every good suburb has? A central square with a fountain.

Lena

Yes. A beautiful fountain. The fountain in her town square is famous. At night the fountain lights up with different colors, and people sit around the fountain to talk.

Maya

That sounds magical. We have a fountain in the park downtown too, but our fountain has been broken for a year. Every time I pass that fountain, I feel sad.

Lena

They should fix that fountain. A working fountain makes any public space feel special. I took my niece to see a fountain last weekend, and she could not stop laughing at the water.

Maya

Kids love a fountain. Okay, so tell me about your trip across town yesterday. Did you take the new bridge?

Lena

I did. That new bridge is beautiful. The old bridge was always crowded, but this new bridge has wide lanes for cars and a separate path for walkers.

Maya

I have not crossed that bridge yet. Is the bridge open to bikes too?

Lena

Yes, the bridge is open to everyone. I walked across the bridge at sunset, and the view of the river from the bridge was amazing.

Maya

I will definitely walk across that bridge this weekend. Speaking of walking, I have a story about a crosswalk.

Lena

A crosswalk story? This should be good.

Maya

Yesterday I was waiting at a crosswalk, and the light turned green, but a cyclist crossed right through the crosswalk without stopping. I was so surprised.

Lena

That is dangerous. Every driver and cyclist should stop at a crosswalk. In my neighborhood they painted a new crosswalk near the school, bright yellow lines, so the crosswalk is impossible to miss.

Maya

That is smart. A clear crosswalk saves lives. I always wait for the green man at the crosswalk, even when the street looks empty.

Lena

Good habit. Now, change of topic. Guess where I had dinner last night.

Maya

Where?

Lena

On a rooftop. My friend has access to a rooftop in the city center, and we watched the sunset from that rooftop.

Maya

A rooftop dinner? That is the dream. I have never eaten on a rooftop. Every summer I say I want to visit a rooftop restaurant, and I never do.

Lena

You have to try it. From the rooftop you can see the whole skyline. We brought pizza up to the rooftop, and honestly it was the best evening of my month.

Maya

I am jealous. Okay, next summer, rooftop dinner, you and me. It is a plan.

Lena

Deal. But let me complain about one thing first. Getting to that rooftop took me an hour because of a terrible traffic jam.

Maya

Ugh, a traffic jam. The worst. I was stuck in a traffic jam on Tuesday too, and I missed the start of my favorite class.

Lena

A traffic jam can ruin your whole evening. My brother says the traffic jam on the highway is worse every year. Last Friday the traffic jam added forty minutes to his drive.

Maya

That is why I love walking. No traffic jam on the sidewalk, right?

Lena

Exactly. No traffic jam, no stress. Just you, the sidewalk, and the city.

Maya

You know, Lena, this whole conversation made me realize something. A city is really just a collection of small things. A friendly neighborhood, a kind resident, a shiny skyscraper, a busy sidewalk.

Lena

A quiet suburb, a sparkling fountain, a strong bridge, a safe crosswalk, a windy rooftop.

Maya

And even a traffic jam, because that is part of city life too.

Lena

It is. Every resident of this city has a story about a traffic jam.

Maya

And every neighborhood has that one perfect sidewalk for an evening walk.

Lena

And every suburb dreams of having its own fountain and bridge.

Maya

Maya and Lena, signing off from the city. If you live near a skyscraper, look up today. If you pass a crosswalk, use it. And if you ever get invited to a rooftop, say yes.

Lena

Say yes to the rooftop, say no to the traffic jam, and enjoy your city. See you next time on Word Kitchen.

📝Grammar: Second conditional, choosing between conditionals

We use the **second conditional** to talk about imaginary or unreal situations in the present or future. The form is simple: **if + past simple, would / wouldn't + infinitive**. For example: If I lived in that suburb, I would walk to work. With I, he, she, or it, we can use **was or were**: If the skyscraper were taller, we could see the sea. **Were** sounds more formal and correct. We often say **If I were you** to give advice: If I were you, I'd take the bridge, not the tunnel. Choosing: the **first conditional** is for real, probable plans (If it rains, I'll take a taxi).
