📚 Vocabulary

symptom /SIMP-tum/
noun
a sign that you are sick, like a fever or a cough
"A fever is a common symptom of the flu."
prescribe /pruh-SCRYBE/
verb
when a doctor tells you which medicine to take
"The doctor prescribed some pills for my cold."
diagnosis /dy-uhg-NOH-sis/
noun
when a doctor finds out what your illness is
"The diagnosis was just a simple cold."
recovery /ree-KUH-vuh-ree/
noun
getting healthy again after being sick
"His recovery took about two weeks."
allergy /AL-uhr-jee/
noun
when your body reacts badly to something like pollen
"She has an allergy to peanuts."
vitamin /VY-tuh-min/
noun
a pill or food that keeps your body healthy
"Oranges have a lot of vitamin C."
infection /in-FEK-shun/
noun
when germs get into your body and make you sick
"He had an infection in his throat."
immunity /i-MYOO-nuh-tee/
noun
your body's ability to fight sickness
"Good sleep helps build your immunity."
nutritious /noo-TRI-shus/
adjective
food that is healthy and good for your body
"A nutritious breakfast gives you energy."
remedy /REM-uh-dee/
noun
something that makes you feel better when you are sick
"Hot tea is a simple remedy for a cold."

📝 Grammar: 6B: Modals of deduction - might, can't, must

Sometimes we guess about the present. **might** shows a guess that is possible. Use **might** when you are not sure. Your nutritious soup might be cold now. Use **must** when you are almost sure something is true. Your immunity must be strong — you never get sick. Use **can't** when you are sure something is NOT true. That can't be an allergy — you never sneeze. Careful: the opposite of **must** (sure) is **can't**, NOT **mustn't**. **mustn't** means "don't do it" — a rule, not a guess. We often add **be + -ing** after these words. Your body must be fighting the infection.
