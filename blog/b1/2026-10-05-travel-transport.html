📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
transit /TRAN-zit/
noun
the system of buses, trains, and subways in a city
"The city's transit is fast and clean."
commuter /kuh-MYOO-tur/
noun
a person who travels to work or school every day
"The train was full of tired commuters."
timetable /TYME-tay-bul/
noun
a list showing when buses or trains arrive and leave
"Check the timetable before you leave home."
fare /FEHR/
noun
the money you pay to travel by bus, train, or taxi
"The taxi fare was higher than I expected."
conductor /kun-DUK-tur/
noun
a person who checks tickets on a train or bus
"The conductor asked to see our tickets."
escalator /ES-kuh-lay-tur/
noun
moving stairs that carry people up or down in a station or building
"We took the escalator to the second floor."
detour /DEE-toor/
noun
a longer road you take when the usual road is closed
"We had to take a detour because of the accident."
intersection /in-tur-SEK-shun/
noun
a place where two or more roads meet
"Turn left at the next intersection."
highway /HY-way/
noun
a wide, fast road between cities
"The highway was empty this morning."
passport /PASS-port/
noun
a small official book you need to travel to other countries
"Don't forget your passport at home."

Podcast: Travel & Transport

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome to Word Kitchen. I am Maya.

Lena

And I am Lena. Today we are talking about getting around, buses, trains, cars, and travel.

Maya

I love this topic. Every day, millions of people use public transit. Transit is the system of buses, trains, and subways in a city.

Lena

A good transit system changes everything. In my city, the transit is clean and fast. I take the transit to work every single morning.

Maya

Same here. When the transit is on time, my day starts well. Cities with great transit feel alive. Do you know who uses the transit most?

Lena

The commuter, of course. A commuter is a person who travels to work or school every day.

Maya

Every commuter has a routine. A smart commuter leaves home early. I was a commuter for five years, and every commuter learns the best seat on the train.

Lena

Commuters are creatures of habit. A tired commuter just wants a quiet ride. Every commuter I know has a podcast or a book for the trip.

Maya

And every commuter needs a timetable. A timetable is a list showing when buses or trains arrive and leave.

Lena

The timetable is my morning bible. I check the timetable while I drink my coffee. If the timetable changes, my whole plan changes.

Maya

Modern timetables live on our phones. A clear timetable means no long waits in the rain. I always save the timetable for the way home too.

Lena

Then there is the fare. The fare is the money you pay to travel by bus, train, or taxi.

Maya

Fares go up, fares go down. The bus fare here is two dollars. A monthly card is cheaper than paying the fare every day.

Lena

Students pay a lower fare in many cities. If the fare is fair, more people ride. A high fare pushes people back into cars.

Maya

On the train, you meet the conductor. A conductor is a person who checks tickets on a train or bus.

Lena

Our conductor is wonderful. When the conductor comes, everyone shows a ticket. A friendly conductor makes the trip better.

Maya

The conductor knows all the regulars. A good conductor helps lost tourists too. I once saw a conductor return a lost bag to its owner.

Lena

At the station, you take the escalator. An escalator is moving stairs that carry people up or down.

Maya

I love escalators. The escalator at the main station is so long. Stand on the right of the escalator, walk on the left.

Lena

Every escalator has that rule. Kids ride the escalator just for fun. The new escalator near my office is made of glass.

Maya

Sometimes the road is closed, and you need a detour. A detour is a longer road you take when the usual road is closed.

Lena

Detours test your patience. Last week a detour added twenty minutes to my trip. But a detour can also show you a pretty street.

Maya

My driver took a clever detour yesterday and beat the traffic. A bad detour wastes your morning. Always ask why there is a detour.

Lena

In the city, every road meets at an intersection. An intersection is a place where two or more roads meet.

Maya

Intersections are busy. At every intersection, watch the lights. The intersection by my office has the longest red light ever.

Lena

Be extra careful at a big intersection. Cars, bikes, and people share the intersection. I always look both ways at an intersection.

Maya

For longer trips, take the highway. A highway is a wide, fast road between cities.

Lena

I love highway driving. The highway to the beach takes two hours. Early on a Sunday, the highway is completely empty.

Maya

A highway at rush hour is a different story. Every highway has rest stops for coffee. We sang songs all the way down the highway.

Lena

And for travel abroad, you need a passport. A passport is a small official book you need to travel to other countries.

Maya

My passport is full of stamps. I keep my passport in a special pocket. Check that your passport is valid before you book a flight.

Lena

A passport opens the world. Without a passport, no international trip. Every traveler should know where their passport is.

Maya

Before we finish, let's share our best travel tips. Lena, what is your number one rule for the transit?

Lena

Always check the timetable first, and keep some coins for the fare. If the fare machine is broken, coins save the day. A prepared commuter never gets stuck.

Maya

Great tip. Mine is about stations. Take the escalator when you have luggage, but learn where it ends. And smile at the conductor, because a happy conductor remembers you.

Lena

I like that. My tip for drivers is this. When there is a detour, stay calm and follow the signs. A detour is temporary. And at every intersection, slow down, even if the light is green.

Maya

Solid advice. My last tip is for road trips. Plan your highway stops before you leave. A good highway plan means good coffee. And always check your passport the night before a flight, not at the airport.

Lena

I learned that the hard way. One more tip from me. If you are a new commuter in a new city, ride the transit for fun on the weekend. Learn the lines, learn the fare system, and you will feel at home fast.

Maya

Wonderful. Travel is easier when you know the words. Let's say them together one more time, nice and slow.

Lena

Transit, commuter, timetable, fare, conductor.

Maya

Escalator, detour, intersection, highway, passport.

Lena

Nicely done.

Maya

Let's review. Transit moves the city. The commuter rides every day. The timetable gives the times. The fare is the price. The conductor checks tickets. The escalator carries you up. A detour saves you when roads close. The intersection is where roads meet. The highway links cities. The passport opens the world.

Lena

Use these words this week. Describe your transit, or tell a friend about a funny detour.

Maya

Thanks for listening to Word Kitchen. See you next time.

📝Grammar: The Passive (be + past participle)

Use the **passive** when the action matters more than who does it. In travel news we say the train **was delayed** — we do not say who delayed it. Form the passive with **be + past participle**: **is cleaned**, **was closed**, **has been checked**. The object of the active sentence becomes the new subject: the conductor checks the tickets → the tickets **are checked**. Add **by** only when the person is important: the bridge **was built** by a famous engineer.
