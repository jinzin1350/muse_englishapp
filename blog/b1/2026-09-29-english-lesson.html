📚 Vocabulary

workout /WURK-out/
a period of physical exercise to stay healthy
"I do a thirty-minute workout every morning."
warm-up /WORM-up/
gentle exercises you do before harder exercise
"A ten-minute warm-up gets your body ready for exercise."
stretching /STRETCH-ing/
slow exercises that make your muscles longer and more relaxed
"Stretching after a run stops pain the next day."
strength /STRENGTH/
the power your muscles have
"Hill runs build strength in your legs."
balance /BAL-uhnss/
the ability to stay steady and not fall
"Yoga improves your balance and posture."
coach /KOHCH/
a person who trains athletes or a sports team
"Our coach plans every workout for the team."
goal /GOHL/
something you want to achieve
"My goal is to run ten kilometers without stopping."

working together as a group to reach a shared goal
"Winning needs teamwork, not just one star player."
flexibility /flek-suh-BIL-uh-tee/
the ability to bend and move your body easily
"Daily stretching gives you more flexibility."
endurance /en-DYOOR-uhnss/
the ability to keep doing something difficult for a long time
"Long slow runs build your endurance step by step."

📝 Grammar: 4A: Obligation and prohibition - have to, must, should

We use **have to** and **must** to talk about obligation. Obligation means something you need to do. Use **have to** for general rules that come from the outside. These are rules at work, at school, or in a gym. Use **must** for a personal decision or a strong feeling inside you. It is your own choice, not a rule. **mustn't** is a prohibition. It means something is NOT allowed. Be careful: it is very different from **don't have to**. **don't have to** means something is NOT necessary. You can do it if you want, but nobody says you need to.
