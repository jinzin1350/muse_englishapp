bunch /BUHNCH/
noun
a group of things held together
"I bought a bunch of bananas."
delicious /duh-LIH-shuhs/
adjective
Having a very pleasant taste.
"This cake is delicious!"
good/bad at sth /GOOD (or BAD) at SUHM-thing/
phrase
able or unable to do something well.
"I am good at cooking pasta."
taste [flavour] /TAYST/
verb
Sense the flavor of food or drink.
"Can you taste the salt in this soup?"
(a) bit of sth /uh-BIT-uhv-SUHM-thing/
phrase
a small amount of something
"I need a bit of sugar for my tea."
(I) could not believe my eyes /kuhd-NOT-buh-LEEV-my-AIZ/
idiom
to be very surprised by what you see
"When I saw the huge cake, I could not believe my eyes."
(I'm/you're, etc.) kidding /KID-ing/
phrase
saying something not true as a joke
"You won the lottery? You're kidding, right?"

I added a bit too much sugar and a bit too little flour. A bit of a mistake in the recipe, a bit of bad luck, and the cake was a rock.

You're kidding! A rock?

I am not kidding. When my mom cut into it, I could not believe my eyes. We could not believe our eyes! It was so hard.

Lena

That is hilarious. I would not have believed my eyes either. Did you taste it?

But you're good at cooking now! That disaster taught you something. You're good at learning from mistakes, clearly.

Exactly. Nobody starts out good at anything. I was bad at cooking once too. I was really bad at it.

And now? Are you good at baking too?

Maya

I'm getting better. I'm good at pasta, I'm good at soup, and I'm almost good at cakes. Almost.

Lena

That's inspiring. Maybe I'll buy a bunch of vegetables this weekend and try a soup. Just a bunch of carrots and a bunch of onions to start.

A bunch of carrots is a perfect start. And remember: add a bit of salt, taste as you go, and have fun. Cooking should be delicious in every way, including the process.

Even if it's not delicious, I'll learn something. And I will not be kidding myself that it's easy, but I'll try.

Maya

That's the attitude. One last tip: when your soup is finally delicious, take a photo. When you look at it later, you could not believe your eyes that YOU made it.

Lena

I love that. I could not believe my eyes that I, Lena, cooked something delicious. What a day that will be!

Maya

And that is our show! Today we cooked up some great English. Keep practicing, keep tasting life, and we'll see you tomorrow on Muse English. Bye!

📝Grammar: Modals of Deduction: Must, Might, Can't

Sometimes we are not sure about something, so we make a guess. We call this a deduction. Use **must** when you are almost sure something is true: the food **must be** delicious because the line is so long. Use **might**, **may** or **could** when something is possible: he **might be** at the market; she **could be** cooking now. Use **can't** when you are almost sure something is NOT true: that **can't be** her cake — it looks too perfect! The form is simple: **modal + base verb** (she **must know** the recipe). After these modals, never use *to* and never add -s: *she **must be** happy*,
