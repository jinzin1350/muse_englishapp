📚 Vocabulary

10 new words Learn new words with real examples and audio.
checkout /CHEK-out/
noun
the place in a store where you pay for your items
"There was a long line at the checkout."
installment /in-STAHL-ment/
noun
one of several small payments you make over time
"I bought the phone in twelve monthly installments."
invoice /IN-voys/
noun
an official paper or email that asks you to pay
"The company sent me an invoice for the repair."
deposit /duh-PAH-zit/
noun
money you pay in advance to keep a service or place
"We paid a deposit to rent the apartment."
purchase /PUR-chiss/
verb
to buy something
"She purchased a new laptop yesterday."
warranty /WAR-un-tee/
noun
a promise to repair or replace a product if it breaks
"The TV comes with a two-year warranty."
loyalty /LOY-ul-tee/
noun
a store program that rewards regular customers
"Show your loyalty card to get extra points."
tax /taks/
noun
money you pay to the government on things you buy
"The price includes eight percent tax."
fee /fee/
noun
money you pay for a service
"The bank charges a monthly fee for the account."
payment /PAY-ment/
noun
the act of paying money for something
"You can make the payment by card or in cash."

Podcast: Money & Shopping

📝 Podcast Transcript

Maya

Welcome back to Word Kitchen, the show where we cook up English words you can actually use. I am Maya.

Lena

And I am Lena. Today's recipe is all about money and shopping. Maya, I have a story for you. Last weekend I bought a new laptop, and the whole purchase took longer than I expected.

Maya

Oh, I love a good shopping story. Tell me everything. How did you pay for the purchase? All at once?

Lena

Not all at once. The store offered me a plan: twelve monthly installments. The laptop was too expensive for one payment, so I said yes to the installments.

Maya

Installments are great when the price is big. So you pay one installment every month for a year?

Lena

Exactly. The first installment comes out next month. And get this, each installment is small enough that I can still save money.

Maya

That is smart shopping. Did they ask for a deposit too?

Lena

Yes. They asked for a small deposit before they ordered the laptop. I paid the deposit, and the deposit counts toward the total price.

Maya

A deposit is normal for big purchases. It shows the store you are serious. So what happened at the checkout?

Lena

The checkout was busy, but the cashier was friendly. At the checkout, she asked if I wanted a loyalty card.

Maya

Oh, loyalty cards are my favorite thing. Do you have their loyalty card now?

Lena

I do. She said every purchase with the loyalty card gives me points. My last purchase gave me fifty points already.

Maya

Nice. Points from one purchase can really add up. So after the checkout, what did you do with the invoice?

Lena

She sent the invoice to my email. I always keep the invoice because the invoice shows exactly what I paid for.

Maya

Good habit. An invoice is your proof. Did you check the warranty on the invoice too?

Lena

I did. The invoice says the laptop has a two-year warranty. The warranty covers repairs, so I do not have to worry.

Maya

A good warranty is worth a lot. My phone's warranty saved me once when the screen broke. What about the tax? Did the price include tax?

Lena

Yes, the tax was already in the price. The tax on the laptop was about eight percent of the total.

Maya

Tax always surprises people. In some places the tax is added at the checkout, and the tax can change the final price a lot.

Lena

True. I asked about extra fees too. There was a small fee for the payment plan, but no fee for the card payment itself.

Maya

So the fee for the installment plan, is the fee big?

Lena

The fee is tiny, just a few dollars. And the final payment is the twelfth installment. After that payment, the laptop is all mine.

Maya

One thing I always ask about is the fee. Once a hotel charged me a strange fee just for paying with a card. So now I always ask: is there a fee for this payment?

Lena

Oh yes, surprise fees are the worst. But this time every fee was clear. The invoice even listed the fee next to the tax, so the invoice helped me understand the full price.

Maya

That is what a good invoice does. It breaks down the purchase, the tax, the fee, all of it. Speaking of big purchases, my friend just paid a deposit on an apartment. A deposit for a rental can be a full month of rent.

Lena

Right, and you get the deposit back if nothing is broken. A deposit protects both sides. Did your friend read the warranty for the appliances too?

Maya

He should. A warranty on the fridge is important. And you know what else he asked about? Loyalty programs at the furniture store. His loyalty points from an old purchase gave him a real discount on a sofa.

Lena

That is the power of loyalty. Stores love loyalty because it keeps you coming back. I have one loyalty card that I have used for years.

Maya

Me too. But tell me, at the checkout, was there a long line at the checkout? I hate waiting at the checkout when it is crowded.

Lena

It was busy, but the second checkout opened, so it moved fast. And the cashier at the checkout explained the installment plan again, one installment at a time.

Maya

Good service. So the last payment is in twelve months, and then the laptop is yours. Will you make every payment on time?

Lena

Of course. If I miss a payment, there could be a late fee. I set a reminder for each payment. Twelve payments, no late fees, no stress.

Maya

And what about the warranty? Two years is a nice warranty. If anything breaks, the warranty will fix it. I always say: never skip the warranty on a big purchase.

Lena

Agreed. A strong warranty plus a clear invoice makes me feel safe. And a small deposit, fair installments, no hidden fee, honest tax: that is how a store earns my loyalty.

Maya

I love it. Let us review our words, because every word we used is one you should know. First: purchase. A purchase is the thing you buy. Lena's laptop purchase took a while.

Lena

Second: installment. An installment is one small payment of many. Twelve installments for one laptop.

Maya

Third: deposit. A deposit is money you pay in advance. It counts toward the price.

Lena

Fourth: checkout. The checkout is where you pay in the store. Busy checkout, friendly cashier.

Maya

Fifth: loyalty. A loyalty card rewards regular shoppers with points on every purchase.

Lena

Sixth: invoice. An invoice is the official paper or email that shows what you paid.

Maya

Seventh: warranty. A warranty promises repairs if the product breaks. Two-year warranty on the laptop.

Lena

Eighth: tax. Tax is money added to the price, paid to the government.

Maya

Ninth: fee. A fee is a small charge for a service, like the fee for the payment plan.

Lena

Tenth: payment. A payment is the act of paying. Lena's last payment is in twelve months.

Maya

Ten words, one great story. That is your Word Kitchen for today. Keep practicing these words, and your English will grow like a good recipe. See you next time.

Lena

Bye, everyone. And happy shopping.

📝Grammar: Future forms - present continuous, be going to, will / won't

Use **be going to + infinitive** for plans you have already decided. I have decided: I am **going to make** the final payment on Friday. Use **be going to** for predictions with evidence, when you can see it coming. Look at that total: this purchase is **going to be** expensive. Use the **present continuous** for future arrangements, plans with a fixed time and place. I am **paying** the deposit at the checkout tomorrow morning.
