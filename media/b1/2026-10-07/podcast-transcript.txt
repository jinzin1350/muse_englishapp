Word Kitchen: Our Green Planet

Maya: Hello and welcome to Word Kitchen! I am Maya.
Lena: And I am Lena! Today we are cooking up ten wonderful words about nature and the environment.
Maya: That is right. Our planet is beautiful, but it needs our help. Let us start with the word wildlife.
Lena: Wildlife means animals and birds that live in nature, not in homes or zoos.
Maya: Last summer I visited a national park, and I saw so much wildlife there. There were deer, rabbits, and many colorful birds.
Lena: That sounds lovely. But in many places, wildlife is in danger. When people cut down forests, wildlife loses its home.
Maya: Yes, and pollution hurts wildlife too. Pollution is our next word. Pollution means dirty air, water, or land that is bad for people and animals.
Lena: Big cities often have air pollution from cars and factories. And water pollution hurts fish and other wildlife in rivers and lakes.
Maya: So what can we do about pollution? We can recycle! To recycle means to use old paper, plastic, or glass again to make new things.
Lena: I recycle all my plastic bottles and newspapers. It is easy. When you recycle, you help reduce pollution and you protect wildlife.
Maya: Great habit, Lena. Now let us talk about the landscape. Landscape means everything you can see in a natural place: the hills, fields, and rivers.
Lena: The landscape changes with the climate. Climate is our next word. Climate means the usual weather of a place over a long time.
Maya: Exactly. For example, the climate in Canada is cold in winter, but the climate in Egypt is hot and dry all year.
Lena: And a changing climate can bring a drought. Drought means a long time with no rain, so plants cannot grow.
Maya: Droughts are terrible for farmers. Without rain, there is no harvest. Harvest means collecting crops from the fields when they are ready.
Lena: Farmers collect the harvest in autumn. But when a drought comes, the harvest is small and food becomes expensive.
Maya: And too much rain brings the opposite problem: a flood. Flood means water covering land that is usually dry.
Lena: A flood can damage homes near a river, and a flood can also destroy the harvest in the fields.
Maya: So the climate can give us a drought or a flood, two opposite problems for farmers and for wildlife.
Lena: That is true. Now, what about the word endangered? Endangered means an animal or plant that may disappear forever.
Maya: The panda is an endangered animal. Tigers are endangered too. Many kinds of wildlife are endangered because of pollution and lost homes.
Lena: But there is good news. Some endangered animals are coming back because people protect them. National parks keep wildlife safe.
Maya: Our last word is solar. Solar means from the sun, or using energy from the sun.
Lena: Solar energy is clean. When we put solar panels on our roofs, we make less pollution. Solar power does not hurt the wildlife or the landscape.
Maya: I love that picture: a green landscape with solar panels on the houses. No smoke, no pollution, just clean solar energy.
Lena: And with clean energy, the climate stays more stable. We get fewer droughts and floods, and farmers get a good harvest every year.
Lena: Before the game, here is a question from a listener. Sara asks: how can I remember all these new words?
Maya: Great question, Sara. Use the words in real life. When you read the news about a drought or a flood, say the words out loud.
Lena: Yes! And when you recycle at home, say to yourself: I recycle because I want less pollution and more wildlife.
Maya: You can also look at the landscape on your way to work and think about the climate in your city.
Lena: And when you see solar panels on a roof, remember the word solar. Words are easy to remember when you connect them to your own life.
Maya: Exactly. Now let us review our ten words: wildlife, landscape, climate, pollution, recycle, endangered, drought, flood, harvest, and solar.
