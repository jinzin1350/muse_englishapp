Lena: I love cooking, and I love eating even more. Let us start with the first word, simmer. To simmer means to cook something gently over very low heat. The soup is not boiling hard. It is just simmering, with tiny little bubbles.
Maya: Exactly! When I make chicken soup, I simmer the soup for ten minutes. If you simmer slowly, the flavor gets deeper and richer. Never boil it fast, because a hard boil ruins the taste.
Lena: Our second word is roast. To roast means to cook in the oven with dry heat. She roasted chicken for dinner last night. Do you roast vegetables, Maya?
Maya: I roast vegetables every single week. I roast potatoes with olive oil and salt. A very hot oven makes them perfect. Roast at a high temperature, and turn them halfway through.
Lena: The next word is chop. Chop means to cut food into small pieces. Chop the onions before cooking, or your pan will not be ready. Can you chop onions without crying?
Maya: Not really! I chop onions fast and I cry every time. I also chop garlic and peppers for the pan. If you chop everything before you start, cooking is much easier and calmer.
Lena: Word number four is stir. Stir means to mix food with a spoon. Stir the sauce every five minutes, or it will stick. If you do not stir the rice, it burns on the bottom.
Maya: Stirring is my favorite job in the kitchen. I stir the soup, I stir the curry, I stir everything. Stir gently so the vegetables do not break. A wooden spoon is the best tool.
Lena: Now crispy. Crispy means hard, crunchy, and beautifully browned. The fries are hot and crispy. I love crispy chicken skin, do you?
Maya: I do! Crispy pizza edges are the best part of the pizza. To make food crispy, use hot oil and do not crowd the pan. Crispy fries need patience, but they are worth it.
Lena: The sixth word is aroma. Aroma means a pleasant food smell. The aroma of fresh bread filled the kitchen. A wonderful aroma can make you hungry in one second.
Maya: Oh yes! The aroma of coffee in the morning wakes me up. The aroma of a roast chicken brings everyone to the table. A good aroma tells you the food is almost ready.

Maya: At home, I make a simple appetizer with bread, cheese, and olives. A great appetizer should not be too big, because the main dish is coming. After the appetizer, everyone is ready for dinner.
Lena: Our eighth word is tender. Tender means soft and easy to chew. The meat is tender and juicy. Tender beef almost melts in your mouth.
Maya: Slow cooking makes meat tender. Simmer it long and low, and tough meat becomes tender. Nobody likes dry, tough steak. Everyone loves tender, juicy meat.
Lena: Now bitter. Bitter means a sharp, not-sweet taste. Dark chocolate tastes bitter. Black coffee is bitter without sugar or milk.
Maya: Bitter foods can be healthy. Arugula and grapefruit taste bitter, but they are good for you. A little bitter taste balances a sweet sauce. Too bitter is not nice, but a little bitter is interesting.
Lena: The last word is marinate. To marinate means to soak food in sauce before cooking. Marinate the chicken for one hour, and it will taste amazing. To marinate is to give the food extra flavor and make it tender.
Maya: I marinate fish with lemon, garlic, and herbs. Marinate overnight for the best result. You can marinate beef, chicken, or even tofu. The longer you marinate, the deeper the flavor.
