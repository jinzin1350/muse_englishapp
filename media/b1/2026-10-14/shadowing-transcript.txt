Sara's Special Dinner

Sara wanted to cook a special dinner for her family.
She started by planning the menu in the morning.
First, she decided to chop the onions, carrots, and peppers.
She carefully chopped everything into small pieces.
The recipe said to chop two onions and four carrots.
She loved to chop fresh herbs for extra flavor, and she asked her son to chop the tomatoes too.
After she chopped the vegetables, she washed the knife she used to chop them.
Next, she decided to marinate the chicken for the main dish.
She placed the chicken in a bowl of lemon and herbs to marinate.
She knew that if you marinate meat longer, the flavor is deeper.
She hoped one hour would be enough to marinate the chicken well.
While the chicken was in the fridge to marinate, she started the soup.
She put the pot on the stove and let the soup simmer.
A gentle simmer was perfect for the tomato soup.
She left the soup to simmer for ten minutes on low heat; a soft simmer is always better than a hard boil.
While the soup continued to simmer, she prepared a small appetizer.
For the appetizer, she sliced bread and added cheese on top.
She placed the warm appetizer on the table for her guests.
She thought the simple appetizer would make everyone happy.
A delicious appetizer filled the room with a warm aroma.
When the hour passed, she took the chicken out to roast.
She put the chicken in a hot oven to roast until golden.
A slow roast would make the skin wonderfully crispy.
Crispy skin was her favorite part of the whole chicken, so she always cooked it until it was perfectly crispy.
She opened the oven, and the wonderful aroma of chicken and herbs filled the kitchen with a rich aroma.
The aroma of roast chicken made everyone hungry at once.
Her son said the sweet aroma was the best smell in the world.
She used a wooden spoon to stir the soup one last time.
She had to stir the soup every five minutes so the carrots would stay tender.
She stirred carefully, and reminded herself to stir slowly, so the vegetables would stay tender.
She knew she must stir often to keep the bottom from burning, and she continued to stir with love.
The soup was ready, and the vegetables were soft and tender; the meat in the pot was tender too.
The roast chicken was perfect: crispy skin outside, tender meat inside.
For dessert, she served fruit and crispy cookies, because dark chocolate tastes bitter to the children, but the sweet cookies balanced the bitter taste, and nobody wanted a bitter ending to the meal.
Her husband loved bitter chocolate and drank bitter black coffee after the meal.
