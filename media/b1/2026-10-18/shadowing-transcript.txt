Sara wanted to get fit, so she decided to do exercise every day.
On Monday, she went to the gym for the first time.
The gym was big and full of friendly people.
She did not know what to do, so a trainer helped her.
On Tuesday, she chose to go swimming instead.
When you go swimming, your whole body works hard.
Sara loved the water, and she went swimming again on Thursday.
Her brother is a footballer, and he gave her good advice.
The footballer told her, "Start slow and never give up."
Sara wrote those words in her notebook: never give up.
On Wednesday, she tried to do exercise at home.
She learned that you can do exercise anywhere, not only at the gym.
Her legs hurt, and she wanted to give up.
But she remembered the footballer's words and kept going.
On Friday, she watched her brother play football.
The young footballer ran fast and scored two goals.
Sara felt proud of her brother, the hard-working footballer.
On Saturday, she learned about cricket from a friend.
A century in cricket means one hundred runs in one game.
Scoring a century takes many hours of hard work.
Sara thought, "A century is like fitness: one step at a time."
She hopes to see a real century on TV one day.
On Sunday, she went to the gym again, stronger than before.
At the gym, she met a woman who plays ice hockey.
The woman said ice hockey is the fastest team sport.
Ice hockey players must skate very fast.
Sara wants to watch an ice hockey game this winter.
She also plans to go swimming every weekend now.
In winter, she will go swimming at the indoor pool near her home.
When life gets hard, she tells herself, "Don't give up."
She will never give up on her health and her dreams.
Sara's strong week taught her that small steps win.
Every morning, Sara is happy that she chose to do exercise.
