Sara's Big Race
Sara woke up early because she had a workout planned.
Every workout made her a little stronger and a little faster.
She never skipped her workout, even on rainy days.
Before the workout, she always did a careful warm-up.
Her warm-up was ten minutes of slow jogging.
A good warm-up woke up her legs and her lungs.
She knew a warm-up could stop injuries before they started.
After the warm-up, she did gentle stretching.
Stretching for her shoulders helped her run tall and relaxed.
She learned that stretching after a workout stopped pain the next day.
Slow stretching in the evening helped her sleep better too.
Her coach said stretching was just as important as running.
Coach Dana had trained runners for more than ten years.
Her coach believed in easy days and hard days.
Every Friday, her coach watched her run and gave her advice.
Sara wanted to build strength in her legs.
Hill runs built strength faster than flat roads.
Real strength came slowly, week after week.
Her coach said strength and balance were a perfect pair.
Running on soft sand improved her balance.
Good balance helped her stay steady on the rocky path.
She practiced balance by standing on one foot while brushing her teeth.
Her goal was to finish a ten-kilometer race in October.
She wrote her goal on a piece of paper and put it on her fridge.
Every workout brought her closer to her goal.
She shared her goal with her running club.
The club believed in teamwork more than winning.
Teamwork made the long Sunday runs feel easy and fun.
In their teamwork, the fast runners waited for the slower ones, and that teamwork spirit kept everyone smiling.
Sara worked on flexibility after every run.
Better flexibility meant her legs felt light, and her coach praised her growing flexibility.
She noticed her flexibility improved after a month of stretching.
On race day, her endurance carried her through the final kilometer.
Long slow runs had built her endurance step by step.
Endurance lived not only in her legs, but in her mind, and she was proud of the endurance she had built.
