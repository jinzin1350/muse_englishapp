My neighbor Sam loves cooking more than anything in the world.
Last Friday he bought a bunch of fresh vegetables at the market.
The bunch was so big that he could not carry it alone.
He also bought a bunch of fruit for a delicious dessert.
When I saw the size of that bunch, I could not believe my eyes.
"You are kidding," I said, "that is enough food for a week."
He laughed and said, "I am not kidding, I am cooking for twenty people."
Sam is very good at cooking, and everyone knows it.
He is good at soups, he is good at pasta, and he is good at desserts.
I asked him to taste my sauce, because he has a great taste for flavors.
He tasted it carefully and said it needed a bit of salt.
I added a bit of salt and tasted it again myself.
Now the taste was perfect, rich and full of flavor.
"You see," he said, "if you taste as you cook, you always win."
He is never kidding when he talks about food.
We chopped the bunch of vegetables together, talking and laughing.
The kitchen smelled delicious, and my mouth was watering.
The soup looked so delicious that I could not believe my eyes.
When the guests arrived, they tasted the soup and smiled.
"This is delicious," they all said, asking for more.
Sam served a second course that was equally delicious.
Even the dessert was delicious, sweet and cold.
One guest said, "Sam, you are good at everything."
Sam answered, "I am not good at everything, but I am good at this."
He added, "You would be good at it too, with a bit of practice."
He explained that a bit of patience changes everything in the kitchen.
My friend tried the cake and could not believe her eyes.
"You are kidding," she whispered, "did you really make this?"
"I am not kidding," Sam said, "and you can make it too."
When she tasted the last bite, she could not believe her eyes again.
That evening, I learned that a bit of care makes good food taste even better with good friends.
