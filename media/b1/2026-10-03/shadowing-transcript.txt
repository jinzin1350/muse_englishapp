Last month, my friend Sarah wanted to find a new job, so she sent her resume to twenty companies.
She updated her resume with help from a career workshop, and her new resume looked fantastic.
One morning, a kind manager called her and said, "We read your resume and we want to meet you."
The manager asked Sarah to come to a meeting at the office on Friday morning.
Sarah was nervous before the meeting, but during the meeting she answered every question calmly.
After the meeting, the manager smiled and said, "We would like to hire you for our team."
Sarah was happy because a big company wanted to hire her as a designer.
The manager explained that they try to hire people who are creative and friendly.
They also wanted to hire one more person for the sales team, so Sarah told her brother about the job.
Companies often recruit new workers in the spring, and they recruit through job fairs and the internet.
Sarah signed a one-year contract with the company, and she read every page of the contract.
The contract said she would work forty hours a week, and the contract promised fair pay.
On her first day, she joined a morning meeting with the whole team.
The meeting was short and clear, and every meeting ended with a plan for the day.
Her manager gave her a small project to start with, and Sarah loved the project.
The project was to design a new logo, and she finished the project in one week.
Later, she gave her first presentation to the team, and the presentation made everyone excited.
She practiced her presentation three times, and her presentation was clear and simple.
After the presentation, her manager gave her honest feedback about her work.
The feedback was positive, and Sarah used the feedback to make her designs even better.
The company also ran a weekend workshop for new workers, and Sarah joined the workshop.
In the workshop, she learned new skills, and the workshop helped her feel confident.
Her manager asked, "How was the workshop?" and Sarah said, "The workshop was amazing!"
Now Sarah helps the company recruit new people, and she loves to recruit creative designers.
She tells new workers, "Update your resume, prepare for your meeting, and ask for feedback."
Sarah is happy at work because her manager trusts her and her project is going well.
Every week, she leads a short meeting, gives feedback to friends, and plans the next project.
