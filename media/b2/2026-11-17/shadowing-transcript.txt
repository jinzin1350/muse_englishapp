The small village by the sea was worried about climate change.
Climate change brought stronger storms and hotter summers to the village.
Old Tomas wanted to reduce his carbon footprint, so he stopped driving to the market.
His daughter Ana also cut her carbon footprint by cycling to school every day.
Ana believed in conservation, and she joined a conservation group at the weekend.
The conservation group planted trees to protect the beach from the wind.
One morning, Ana found a young eagle with a hurt claw near the rocks.
The eagle could not hunt because its claw was weak and painful.
A vet cleaned the claw gently and wrapped it in a soft bandage.
Ana watched the sharp claw move slowly as the eagle began to feel better.
Far away, the radio reported a civil war in another country.
The civil war had closed schools and damaged the water system in many towns.
Because the water was dirty, cholera began to spread in one crowded camp.
Doctors said cholera was dangerous, but clean water could stop cholera quickly.
The news also said every casualty from the fighting needed urgent help.
One casualty was a teacher, and another casualty was a young farmer.
Ana felt sad for each casualty and wished the civil war would end soon, because a civil war hurts everyone.
Back home, Tomas checked his carbon footprint again and smiled at his progress.
He told his neighbours that a smaller carbon footprint could help slow climate change.
The conservation team agreed, and their conservation work grew stronger every month.
They protected the forest, because conservation kept the river clean and safe.
Clean water in the village meant cholera stayed far away, and no child caught cholera in their homes.
When the eagle was strong, its claw gripped the branch with real power.
Ana opened the cage, and the eagle spread its wings above the sea.
She promised to continue conservation and to teach others about climate change.
Tomas promised to keep his carbon footprint small for the rest of his life.
The village learned that climate change, conservation, and kindness are all connected.
And every person, like every casualty they had heard about, deserved care and respect.
