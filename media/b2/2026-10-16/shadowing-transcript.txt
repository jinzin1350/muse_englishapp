Daniel's Money Lesson

Daniel loved shopping, but he never watched his bank balance carefully.
One Saturday morning, he bought a new jacket, a pair of shoes, and a smart speaker.
By noon, his account had slipped into overdraft without him noticing.
The bank charged him a forty-dollar fee for the overdraft.
An overdraft fee is the most expensive way to borrow money, his father always said.
Daniel promised he would never pay another overdraft fee again.
He set up an alert so his phone would warn him before an overdraft happened.
At the shoe store, the clerk admitted the shoes carried a huge markup.
Daniel learned that a markup is simply the store's profit on each item.
He checked the same jacket online and saw the markup was nearly double the factory price.
He asked the manager for a discount, and she lowered the markup a little.
At home, Daniel paid his bills with a credit card that offered cashback.
Every month, his cashback grew a little bigger.
Cashback rewards felt like a small gift for spending wisely.
Daniel paid in full every month, so his cashback stayed pure profit.
That evening, Daniel and his wife talked about their mortgage.
Their mortgage payment took a third of their income every month.
They had chosen a fixed-rate mortgage, so the payment never changed.
A smaller mortgage had seemed impossible five years ago, but they managed it.
Daniel was proud they had never missed a single mortgage payment.
On Sunday, Daniel took his car to a new mechanic for a simple repair.
The mechanic quoted a price that was clearly an overcharge.
Daniel refused the overcharge and asked for an itemized bill.
When the mechanic saw Daniel checking prices, the overcharge quietly disappeared.
Never let anyone overcharge you without asking questions first.
At the market, Daniel bought a suspiciously cheap phone charger.
The charger was counterfeit, and it died after only two days.
A counterfeit product often looks perfect but hides dangerous flaws.
He reported the seller of the counterfeit goods to the market manager.
From now on, no counterfeit bargain would ever fool Daniel again.
On Monday, Daniel traveled to another city for a work meeting.
The company promised to reimburse all his travel expenses.
He photographed every receipt so the company could reimburse him quickly.
Within a week, they did reimburse every dollar, and Daniel felt respected.
A company that will not reimburse its workers does not deserve their loyalty.
