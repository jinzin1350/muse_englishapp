The Valley That Heals

The small town sits in a green valley below tall mountains.
High above the town, a glacier shines white in the morning sun.
Every summer, the glacier melts a little more than the year before.
Old villagers remember when the glacier reached the edge of the forest.
As the glacier continues to thaw, the river below grows louder every spring.
The spring thaw brings fresh water down to the thirsty fields.
But if the thaw comes too early, the river can flood the low roads.
Last year the thaw arrived in March, and two small bridges almost broke.
Far beyond the mountains lies a desert of burning sand.
In the middle of the desert, tired travelers find a cool oasis.
The oasis holds tall palm trees around a pool of clear water.
Birds rest at the oasis before they continue their long flight.
After the monsoon, even the desert blooms, and the oasis overflows with green life.
On the coast, fishermen watch the tide every single morning.
They sail out on the high tide and return before the evening tide turns.
At low tide, the top of the reef rises above the water like a hidden garden.
The reef shelters fish, crabs, and slow sea turtles.
Divers call the reef the most beautiful place they have ever seen.
If the reef dies, the tiny fish that live only there could go extinct.
The spotted frogs of the valley are now nearly extinct.
Scientists fear the frogs will go extinct within ten years.
Volunteers guard the wet nests day and night so the frogs will not go extinct.
In the rainy season, dark monsoon clouds gather over the hills.
The monsoon fills the wells and rivers with weeks of heavy rain.
Before the monsoon, farmers spread compost on the fields to feed the soil.
In the school garden, students turn lunch waste into rich compost.
They mix dry leaves and fruit peels, and the compost feeds the young plants.
Their teacher calls compost nature's clever way of wasting nothing.
In the dry hills, a single spark can start a wildfire.
The wildfire races forward when the hot wind blows through the trees.
A single wildfire can push a rare animal one step closer to extinct.
During the wildfire, thick smog covered the town for three whole weeks.
In winter, thick grey smog settles over the town again.
Cars and factories feed the smog until it hides the distant hills.
The first monsoon rain washes the winter smog out of the sky.
