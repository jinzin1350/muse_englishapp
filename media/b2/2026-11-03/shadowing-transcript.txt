Sara moved into a new flat last month, and the first thing she needed was broadband.
Without broadband, she could not work from home or stream her favourite films.
The technician installed broadband on Friday, and suddenly everything felt faster.
Sara opened her browser and smiled when the pages loaded in seconds.
Her browser had saved all her old bookmarks, so nothing was lost.
She clicked a bookmark for her favourite cooking blog and found a new recipe.
The blog belonged to a chef in Toronto, and Sara read the blog every week.
That evening, Sara decided to start a blog of her own about life in a new city.
She wrote her first blog post about setting up broadband in a new flat.
To find photos for the blog, she liked to browse through free picture websites.
She used her browser to browse for images of cosy kitchens and busy streets.
When she wanted to browse quickly, she opened a bookmark for her picture site.
A friend sent her an email with an attachment full of photos for the blog.
Sara opened the attachment and saved every photo on her computer.
She replied to thank him, and she added an attachment of her own to the email.
Her brother warned her about risks associated with sharing photos online.
He explained that privacy problems are often associated with public posts.
Sara listened carefully, because she knew the risks associated with new technology.
She checked her blog settings and kept her home address private.
The next day, she used her browser to browse for tips on safe blogging.
She found a bookmark for a safety guide and read it twice.
The guide said that strong passwords are associated with safer accounts.
She never opened an attachment from a stranger, because safety came first.
Sara changed her passwords and felt much safer about her blog.
With fast broadband, she uploaded her photos in seconds.
Her readers loved the new blog, and one reader sent an attachment with a kind message.
Sara added a bookmark for the message, so she could read it again later.
She opened her browser each morning to check her blog and answer comments.
To browse for new ideas, she visited other blogs about food and travel.
Every blog she loved got a bookmark in her browser for quick access.
Sara was proud: her blog, her broadband, and her careful habits were all associated with a fresh start in her new city.
