Sara's Healthy Week

Sara works in a busy office in the city.
Every morning, she wakes up early and stretches her arms and legs.
Last Monday, her back started to ache after a long meeting.
The ache stayed with her all afternoon, and she could not focus on her work.
By the evening, she had a bad backache from sitting at her desk all day.
Sara knew that a backache was her body's way of asking for movement.
On Tuesday, she decided to walk to work instead of taking the bus.
As she walked through the park, she twisted her ankle on a wet stone.
Her ankle hurt, and she sat on a bench to rest it.
At home, she put ice on her ankle and raised her foot on a pillow.
While she rested, Sara read a book about ageing and good health.
The book said that ageing skin needs special care and gentle sunlight.
It also said that ageing does not mean giving up the things you love.
Sara thought about her grandmother, who loved to garden even as she was ageing.
Her grandmother had an operation for an artificial hip two years ago.
Before the artificial hip, her grandmother could hardly walk to the garden.
After the artificial hip, she walks among her flowers every single morning.
The artificial hip gave her a new life, full of walks and gardening.
Sara was amazed by how medical science continues to advance.
Doctors advance their skills every year, and hospitals advance their care too.
Because medicine can advance so quickly, people live longer and healthier lives.
Sara also learned that her body works automatically in wonderful ways.
Her heart beats automatically, and her lungs breathe automatically, day and night.
The door of her office opens automatically when she walks towards it.
Sara laughed, because her body heals automatically when she rests well.
On Wednesday, her backache was gone, and her back did not ache.
Her ankle felt stronger, and she walked slowly around the park again.
She noticed that a small ache can teach a big lesson about health.
Sara now stands up every hour, so her back will not ache again.
She wears good shoes to protect her ankle, and she stretches every morning.
Sara's backache taught her to move more and sit less.
She eats good food, sleeps well, and enjoys learning about ageing.
She is grateful that medicine continues to advance and that an artificial hip helped her grandmother.
Now Sara tells her friends that a backache is a message, not a punishment.
Most of all, Sara trusts her body, which works automatically and asks only for a little care in return.
