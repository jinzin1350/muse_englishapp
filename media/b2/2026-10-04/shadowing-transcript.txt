The Paperless Office

When Sara joined the small delivery company, everything was done on paper.
She wanted to automate the boring paperwork that wasted hours every day.
Her boss agreed to automate invoicing first, and it saved them a fortune.
Next, they decided to automate delivery tracking for every customer.
The dusty filing cabinets soon became obsolete, and nobody missed them.
A fax machine from the nineties sat in the corner, completely obsolete.
Sara loved to innovate, so she proposed a brand-new scheduling app.
The team began to innovate with small experiments every Friday.
To innovate faster, they invited young designers to join them.
Their first breakthrough came when the app cut delivery times in half.
That breakthrough made the local newspaper, and new clients called daily.
The transition felt seamless because all the new tools worked together.
Drivers said the switch was seamless after just one morning of training.
Good design should be accessible to every employee, not just experts.
They made the dashboard accessible with big buttons and clear colors.
The interface was so intuitive that nobody needed a training manual.
An intuitive layout helped the older drivers feel confident at once.
Before launch, they checked the app was compatible with all phones, which helped streamline the launch.
One old tablet wasn't compatible, so they replaced it immediately.
To streamline the morning routine, Sara removed three useless forms.
They continued to streamline packing, and boxes left twice as fast.
Then one rainy Monday, the main server began to malfunction.
When the old scanners malfunction, the compatible spares take over instantly.
Paper receipts became obsolete within a single busy year.
Their second breakthrough was a route planner that saved fuel daily.
The help videos were accessible in five languages for all drivers.
Even the skeptics called the final design intuitive and seamless.
Making every device compatible took a full week of testing.
Each month they streamline one more process to save money.
A short reboot fixed the malfunction within a single hour.
Sara also chose to automate customer emails after the old system began to malfunction weekly.
The team planned to innovate the returns process before winter.
Soon the old printers looked obsolete next to the new tablets.
Nobody expected a third breakthrough so early in the year.
Today their workflow feels seamless, intuitive, and accessible to all.
