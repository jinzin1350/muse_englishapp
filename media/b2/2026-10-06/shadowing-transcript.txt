Sara's New Balance

When Sara became a team leader, she was excited about the new challenge.
She showed her dedication from the very first day.
Her quiet dedication impressed the whole team.
Her dedication was strong, but her workload grew heavier every week.
The rising workload meant late nights at the office.
She asked her manager to review the team's workload, because an unfair workload hurts everyone.
Sara tried to multitask through every busy morning.
She would multitask between calls, emails, and meetings.
To multitask all day without rest is a fast road to burnout.
By winter, Sara was close to real burnout, and burnout was changing her personality.
She knew the signs of burnout, but she ignored them.
Her friend warned her about burnout and told her to network with other managers.
To network well, Sara asked honest questions and listened.
She networks online too, sharing articles and advice.
At a networking dinner, a mentor told her to learn to delegate.
She began to delegate small tasks to her assistant.
When she learned to delegate, her team felt trusted, and she learned to delegate bigger projects too.
A leader who learns to delegate helps the whole team thrive.
With less pressure, Sara started to thrive again.
She thrives when she has quiet time to think.
Watching her team thrive was a milestone she would never forget.
Reaching one year as leader was another proud milestone, a milestone she had earned.
She celebrated every milestone with her team.
For the last milestone, the company added a new perk: extra training days.
That perk helped everyone learn new skills.
The best perk, though, was simply sleeping well again, a perk money can't buy.
Thanks to that perk, Sara found the courage to pivot her style toward balance.
A smart pivot can save a career, she told her team.
She had once wanted to pivot to a new job, but after her brave pivot, she stays.
Her bold pivot proved that her dedication included taking care of herself.
Colleagues admired her dedication and her calm.
With a fair workload, she no longer needed to multitask all day.
She stopped trying to multitask and focused on one thing at a time.
She still networks every month, and she continues to thrive.
And that is how Sara built a career she truly loves.
