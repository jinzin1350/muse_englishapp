The Move to Maple Street

When Sara moved to her new city, she had to make a difficult choice.
She wanted a small bungalow near the park, but the rent was too high.
The bungalow had a lovely garden, yet the commute was forty minutes long.
Her second option was a top-floor apartment with a bright attic.
The attic had a round window and the perfect reading corner.
She imagined her desk under the attic window, writing letters home.
But the apartment had almost no storage, and that worried her.
The third option was an old house with a deep, dry cellar.
The cellar had tall shelves for boxes, winter clothes, and sports gear.
She checked the cellar walls for dampness and found them completely dry.
The house also had a big kitchen with a strong wooden chopping board.
Every morning she would slice fresh bread on that chopping board.
The landlord left a clean cloth in the kitchen for the new tenant.
She wiped the counters with the soft cloth and smiled.
A spare cloth hung by the sink, ready for washing dishes.
In the bedroom closet she found exactly one coat hanger.
She bought a dozen new coat hangers and hung up all her jackets.
A good coat hanger keeps a coat's shape, the landlord told her.
Now the choice was between the bungalow, the attic flat, and the cellar house.
She loved the bungalow, but the price made the choice hard.
She loved the attic light, but where would she put her boxes?
The cellar solved the storage problem in one simple move.
She called the landlord and said she would take the cellar house.
On moving day she cleaned every room with a damp cloth.
She carried her boxes down into the cool, quiet cellar.
Upstairs she unpacked the kitchen and found the old chopping board again.
She decided to keep the chopping board because it had character.
She hung her winter coats on the new coat hangers in the hallway closet.
That evening she sat in the garden, happy with her choice.
A home is not just rooms, she thought; it is the right choice at the right time.
Her friend still dreams of a bungalow, but Sara would not trade her cellar for anything.
