The Office with Two Managers

Sara started a new job on Monday, and her attitude was bright and hopeful.
She wanted to learn, to help, and to grow, and her positive attitude made everyone smile.
Her first manager, Mr. Lee, was calm and assertive.
He spoke clearly, he listened well, and he was never rude.
Sara loved his style, because an assertive leader gives clear directions without shouting.
In the next office, however, worked Mr. Grant, a man with a very different attitude.
Mr. Grant was arrogant, and everyone knew it.
He believed he was better than the whole team, and his arrogant attitude made people unhappy.
His team stayed silent, because an arrogant boss never listens to new ideas.
Sara's friend Tom was argumentative, and he argued with Mr. Grant every single day.
Tom was argumentative about the schedule, argumentative about the coffee, and argumentative about everything.
An argumentative person can make a whole office tired, and Tom made everyone tired.
One afternoon, something happened that appalled the entire office.
Mr. Grant shouted at a young intern in front of twenty people, and the scene appalled Sara.
It appalled her because the intern had done nothing wrong at all, and the memory appalled her all evening.
Sara felt sick, because cruelty in an office should appal anyone with a kind heart.
Sara thought of her sister Emma, who became anorexic due to stress at university.
Emma was anorexic for a year, and the family worried about her every single day.
With a doctor's help and her family's love, Emma slowly recovered, and she is healthy and strong today.
Emma often says that being anorexic is a serious illness, not a choice, and that kind words help more than advice.
Sara learned that an anorexic person needs kindness and professional help, not comments about their body.
She never jokes about being anorexic, because health is more important than a joke.
That evening, Sara spoke to Mr. Lee, and she was brave and assertive.
"This behaviour is wrong," she said in a clear, assertive voice, "and we must change it."
Mr. Lee listened, and his calm attitude gave Sara courage.
The next morning, Mr. Lee called a meeting, and he spoke about respect and kindness.
"A good attitude appeals to everyone," he said, "and respect appeals to every person in this room."
He was right: a friendly office appeals to workers, and a quiet lunch appeals to tired minds.
The idea of a better office appeals to Sara, and it appeals to Tom as well.
Mr. Grant said sorry to the intern, and his attitude slowly began to change.
Tom also changed, and he became less argumentative and more open to other ideas.
"I want to listen more," Tom said, "because being argumentative all day helps nobody."
Sara smiled, because her positive attitude had helped her whole team.
She learned that an assertive voice is stronger than an arrogant voice, and a kind attitude is stronger than a loud argument.
A good office needs people who are assertive, not arrogant, and open, not argumentative.
