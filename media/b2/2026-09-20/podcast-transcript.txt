Lena: I love that word. The Arctic is the natural habitat of the polar bear, and the coral reef is the habitat of thousands of colorful fish.

Lena: True. And remember our first word, erosion. Coastal erosion can destroy a habitat too, washing away the beaches where sea turtles lay their eggs. Protecting each habitat is part of protecting biodiversity.
Maya: Speaking of protecting nature, our sixth word is renewable. Renewable means coming from a source that never runs out, like the sun or the wind.
Lena: Renewable energy is one of the most hopeful stories in the news. Solar panels and wind turbines turn sunlight and wind into electricity, and since the sun and wind never run out, this energy is truly renewable.
Maya: More and more countries are investing in renewable power. The cost of renewable energy has fallen so much that in some places it is now cheaper than coal or gas.
Lena: And renewable energy helps fight climate change, which is important because climate change makes both drought and erosion worse. So going renewable is a smart way to protect every habitat we talked about.
Maya: Our seventh word is deforestation. Deforestation is the cutting down of forests over a large area.
Lena: Deforestation is heartbreaking. Every year, huge areas of rainforest are lost to deforestation, and with them we lose the incredible biodiversity that lived in those trees.
Maya: Deforestation also makes erosion worse. The roots of trees hold the soil together, so when deforestation clears the hills, the next heavy rain washes the soil straight into the rivers.
Lena: And deforestation adds to climate change, because trees absorb carbon dioxide. When we stop deforestation, we also slow down the warming that causes more frequent drought.
Maya: Our eighth word is contaminate. To contaminate means to make something dirty or poisonous.
Lena: Last year there was a story about a factory that continued to contaminate the local river. The chemicals would contaminate the water for miles downstream, and fishermen had to stop fishing there.
Maya: That is awful. When a river is contaminated, the pollution does not just stay in one place. One spill can contaminate drinking water, kill fish, and damage the whole habitat for years.
Lena: It really shows why we must not contaminate our rivers and soil. Once chemicals contaminate the land, cleaning it up can take decades and cost millions of dollars.
Maya: Our ninth word is sustainable. Sustainable means able to continue without harming the environment.
Lena: Sustainable farming is a great example. Sustainable methods protect the soil and use less water, so the farm can stay productive for generations.
Maya: I like that. A sustainable lifestyle is also about small choices, like buying less plastic and choosing renewable energy. If millions of people live in a more sustainable way, the impact is enormous.
Lena: And sustainable tourism is growing fast. Travelers visit natural parks in a sustainable way, enjoying the biodiversity without damaging the habitat they came to see.
Maya: Our tenth and final word is migrate. To migrate means to move from one region to another with the seasons.
Lena: Birds are the most famous example. Every autumn, millions of birds migrate south to warmer places, and every spring they migrate back north to raise their babies.
Maya: It is an amazing journey. Some birds migrate thousands of kilometers, crossing deserts and oceans without a map. They just know the way.
Lena: But migration is changing. When a drought dries up the lakes where birds stop to rest, or when a damaged habitat no longer offers food, the birds that migrate through that area suffer.
Maya: That is the connection between all our words today. Let us review them one more time. Erosion wears away the land. A drought brings long months without rain.
