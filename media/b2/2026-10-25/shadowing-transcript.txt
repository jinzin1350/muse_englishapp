Sara's Shopping Saturday

Sara opened a new bank account last Saturday.
She wanted to know the exact amount she could spend.
First, she walked to a cash machine near her house.
The cash machine asked her for the amount she wanted.
She took a small amount because she wanted to save money.
The cash machine gave her the exact amount she asked for.
Then she went to the market to compare prices.
The market was very competitive, and every shop tried to win her money.
One shop was more competitive than the others, so she bought her jacket there.
She paid the full amount at the counter and smiled.
Later, she remembered her three small debts.
She decided to consolidate her debts into one payment.
To consolidate means to combine many things into one stronger whole.
She called her bank and asked them to consolidate the three loans.
Now she pays one amount every month instead of three.
But she always checks what the bank will charge in interest.
A good bank will not charge too much interest on a loan.
Banks charge fees for many services, so she reads every contract.
If a bank tries to charge too much, she will move her bank account.
A competitive bank will charge less, and that saves money.
On Sunday, Sara read the news about corporation tax.
Corporation tax is the tax that companies pay on their profits.
The government plans to reduce corporation tax for small shops.
When corporation tax is low, shops can offer more competitive prices.
She learned that corporation tax affects every shop in town.
Sara liked that idea, because competitive prices help shoppers.
She went back to the market and checked the amount of every price tag.
She passed a cash machine on every street near the market.
She found a cash machine near the market, just in case she needed cash.
Her new bank account made shopping easy and safe.
Her bank account statement showed every amount clearly.
She told her friend to consolidate old debts, too.
"Consolidate your debts," she said, "and check what they charge you."
Her friend opened a bank account the next day.
Now both friends shop with a plan, and they always know the amount they spend.
