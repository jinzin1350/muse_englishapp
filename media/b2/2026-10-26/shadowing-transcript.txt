Sara wanted to learn how to use the Web for her studies.
She opened her laptop and searched the Web for a good English course.
On the Web, she found many free lessons and helpful videos.
First, she had to create an account on the learning website.
She typed her name and her email address to create the account.
The website sent a message to her email address to confirm the account.
Sara was happy because her new account worked on the first try.
Then she wanted to alter her profile, so she changed her photo.
She decided to alter the settings to make the text bigger and clearer.
It is easy to alter a setting when you know where to look.
Next, Sara wanted to analyse her progress after one week.
She looked at her scores and tried to analyse what she had learned.
When you analyse your results, you can see what to practise next.
Sara used a new app on her phone to practise words every morning.
The app showed her five words, and the app tested her at night.
She loved the app because the app made learning feel like a game.
One day, Sara wrote an application for a summer school in London.
Her application asked for her name, her address, and her experience.
She checked her application three times before she sent it.
Sara remembered to analyse the course details before she finished the application.
She did not want to alter her application after sending it.
While she waited, Sara searched the Web for photos of London.
She found a nice app that showed maps and famous places.
The app helped her plan her trip, and the app saved her favourite places.
Sara also had to alter her travel dates because the course started later.
She sent a message to the school's email address from her account to explain the change.
The school replied to her email address within two days.
Her application was accepted, and Sara felt proud and excited.
Now Sara tells her friends to analyse their goals and use the Web wisely.
