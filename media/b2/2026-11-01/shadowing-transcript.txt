Sara loved the small park near her home.
The atmosphere of the park in the morning was calm and fresh.
She walked past the baseball field where a boy practised with his bat.
The boy swung his bat and hit the ball high over the trees.
A little dog ran after the ball and began to bark loudly.
The dog continued to bark until its owner called it back.
On a bench nearby, a robin used its beak to eat a seed.
The bird cleaned its beak on the edge of the bench.
Sara smiled, because the atmosphere of the park always made her happy.
But Sara also worried about the trees, because acid rain had damaged their leaves.
Acid rain falls when dirty air mixes with rain, and acid rain hurts forests and lakes.
Air pollution from the busy road made the air grey on some days.
Air pollution also made Sara cough, and she wished the city were cleaner.
She remembered the news about research to find a cure for AIDS.
Doctors said that the fight against AIDS showed how science could save lives.
Sara believed that protecting nature and fighting AIDS both needed care and science, and news about AIDS reminded her that research saves lives.
In the afternoon, the atmosphere of the park changed, and families arrived with picnics.
The boy returned with his bat, and his friends cheered every time he swung the bat.
The dog came back too, and it started to bark at a squirrel in a tree.
A blackbird landed on the grass and used its beak to dig for worms.
Sara watched the bird, and she admired how strong its beak was.
She thought about acid rain again, and she decided to plant a tree in her garden.
A new tree would clean the air and reduce air pollution near her home, and less air pollution meant healthier lungs for everyone.
It would also give birds a safe place to use their beaks and build nests.
As the sun went down, the atmosphere of the park became golden and quiet.
The boy put his bat away, and the dog stopped to bark one last time before going home.
Sara walked home slowly, thinking about acid rain, air pollution, and the fight against AIDS.
She felt hopeful, because small actions, like planting trees, could change the atmosphere of a whole city.
That night, she dreamed of a clean park where no dog needed to bark in anger, and every bird sang with a healthy beak.
The next morning, she woke up early and returned to the park with a bag of seeds for the birds.
She scattered the seeds, and the robin used its beak to eat them at once.
