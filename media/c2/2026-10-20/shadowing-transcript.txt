Where We Live

Marta had lived in the same hovel for sixty years, and she loved every stone of it.
The hovel had one room, a small stove, and a roof that sang in the rain.
Visitors called it a hovel, but Marta called it home.
No hovel in the village was as old as hers, and no hovel was as full of memories.
Across the country, in the capital, a glass high-rise touched the clouds.
The high-rise had forty floors, and each high-rise apartment cost a fortune.
Jonas had grown up far from any high-rise, in a quiet street of small houses.
He decided to film the hovel and the high-rise in the same documentary.
The contrast between the hovel and the high-rise would be the heart of his film.
Marta agreed to visit the high-rise for one day of filming.
She rode the elevator to the top of the high-rise and laughed like a child.
From the high-rise roof, her whole village looked like a toy town.
That single sentence hit home for everyone who watched the film.
It hit home because it was simple, honest, and impossible to argue with.
The critics said the scene would hit home with any viewer, rich or poor.
A good documentary makes its message hit home without shouting.
Jonas became a household word almost overnight after the premiere.
To become a household word is the dream of every young filmmaker.
His name became a household word in schools, cafes, and parliament debates.
But Jonas knew that being a household word would not last forever.
The film also told a home truth about the country's housing crisis.
A home truth is painful, but a home truth is necessary.
The home truth was this: houses had become investments, not homes.
Nobody in the government wanted to hear that home truth.
Jonas saved his hardest home truth for himself, in the final scene.
He admitted that he had led a sheltered life for thirty years.
To lead a sheltered life, he said, is to never worry about rent or repairs.
He had led a sheltered life in the suburbs, far from any hovel or high-rise.
Making the film ended his sheltered life, and he was grateful for it.
At the premiere party, on the high-rise rooftop, everyone had the time of their lives.
To have the time of your life is to be completely, perfectly happy.
Marta danced under the city lights and had the time of her life.
The crew said they had never had the time of their lives like that night.
Jonas watched them and felt he was having the time of his life too.
A hovel, a high-rise, and one unforgettable night above the city.
