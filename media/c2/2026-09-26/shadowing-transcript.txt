The Long Road Home

Last year I began a peregrination that changed my life forever.
I had no strict itinerary, only a backpack and an open heart.
My first stop was a short sojourn in a fishing village.
During that sojourn I learned to cook with the local fishermen.
Then I walked on, feeling like a true wayfarer.
The wayfarer finds friends on every lonely road.
I crossed rivers and began to traverse the high mountains.
To traverse those mountains, I needed strong boots and stronger patience.
Each evening I wrote in my journal about the odyssey so far.
What started as a holiday had become a real odyssey.
In the third month, I lived a peripatetic life between small towns.
The peripatetic traveler learns to sleep anywhere.
Once I took a quick jaunt to the coast for a festival.
That jaunt gave me some of my happiest memories.
After the festival, I updated my rough itinerary.
A flexible itinerary leaves room for wonderful surprises.
In the desert I met an old globetrotter with silver hair.
The globetrotter told me stories from forty countries.
Every globetrotter I have met says the same thing: slow down.
We took a train together and talked through a long layover.
A layover is a gift if you share it with a friend.
Then I continued my long peregrination south, and the layover felt like a small rest inside it.
That peregrination taught me more than any classroom.
I decided to traverse the great valley on foot.
It took nine days to traverse the valley completely.
On the ninth day I made my final sojourn in a mountain inn.
The sojourn lasted only a week, but it felt like home.
The innkeeper was a retired wayfarer with kind eyes.
Every wayfarer who passed left a note on the wall.
I read about their odysseys late into the night, and each odyssey inspired my own.
Saying goodbye to my peripatetic months was harder than I expected, and I knew my peripatetic spirit would never fully rest.
Even my last jaunt to the lake felt like a closing chapter.
That final jaunt reminded me why I had started.
I burned my old itinerary and wrote a new one, because my peregrination was far from over.
A true globetrotter never really stops, and this layover was just the beginning of my next peregrination and my next odyssey.
