Sara sat at her desktop every morning before sunrise.
Her desktop was old, slow, and noisy, but it was her window to the world.
On her desktop she read the news about digital technology and the future of farming.
Digital technology had always fascinated her, ever since she was a child.
She believed that digital technology could change the destiny of her village.
Her village grew wheat, but a disease had destroyed half the harvest.
The farmers needed a disease-resistant wheat, and they needed it soon.
Sara read on her e-reader every night about disease-resistant crops.
Her e-reader held hundreds of articles, and she finished one after another.
On her e-reader she kept a list of every farmer who needed help.
One article warned of a doomsday scenario for farmers across the region.
The doomsday scenario was simple and frightening: no rain, no wheat, no food.
The newspapers repeated the doomsday scenario every morning, and fear spread through the village.
Sara refused to accept the doomsday scenario as her destiny.
She told her brother that destiny is not something that happens to us.
She said our destiny is something we build with our own hands.
She wrote to a team of scientists who worked on disease-resistant seeds.
The scientists answered her from their lab, and they invited her to visit.
At the lab, Sara saw computers, greenhouses, and fields of disease-resistant wheat.
The scientists explained that digital technology helped them find the strongest plants.
Every day, digital technology analysed thousands of seeds on a single desktop.
Sara returned home with a small bag of disease-resistant seeds.
The village leaders said the plan needed drastic measures, not small steps.
The oldest farmer said that drastic measures were the only answer.
Sara knew that drastic measures would be hard, but she also knew that small steps would fail.
So the village took drastic measures: they replanted every field in one week.
Sara worked at her desktop late into the night, tracking the weather and the soil.
Her e-reader became her teacher, and her desktop became her office.
The new wheat grew strong, and the disease could not touch it.
By harvest time, the village had more wheat than ever before.
The doomsday scenario never came, because the village had acted in time.
The drastic measures worked, and the village never looked back.
Sara smiled at her desktop and closed her e-reader for the night.
She knew now that destiny was not a mystery, and digital technology was not magic.
Destiny was a choice, and she had chosen well.
