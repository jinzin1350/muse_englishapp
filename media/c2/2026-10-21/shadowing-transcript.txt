The New Neighbor

Mrs. Hale is a retired teacher who lives alone on Maple Street.
She abhors gossip, so she never listens at fences.
Her new neighbor, Daniel, moved in with loud music and late parties.
Mrs. Hale felt alarmed when the noise shook her windows at midnight.
She knocked on his door the next morning, ready to complain.
Daniel opened the door with an approachable smile that surprised her.
He apologized at once and offered to appease her with fresh bread.
Mrs. Hale admitted she had felt a quiet antipathy toward loud young people.
Daniel laughed and said he abhors conflict even more than she does.
He explained that the party was his sister's birthday, a one-time event.
Mrs. Hale's antipathy began to fade as they talked over tea.
She learned that Daniel was an astute engineer who built bridges.
He told her he felt alarmed when the bridge cables sang in the wind.
An astute engineer, he said, never ignores a warning sound.
Mrs. Hale respected people who were astute about safety.
Daniel asked why she had looked so alarmed at the door.
She confessed that she abhors surprises before breakfast.
They both laughed, and the last of her antipathy disappeared.
Daniel turned out to be remarkably approachable and easy to talk to.
He invited her to dinner to appease her fully for the noisy night.
She brought a pie, because altruistic neighbors share what they bake.
Daniel's friends were as approachable as he was, warm and funny.
One friend worked for a charity with a truly altruistic heart.
She raised money for clinics, and Daniel abhorred anyone who mocked her work.
Mrs. Hale felt alarmed for a moment when the dog knocked over a lamp.
But Daniel was astute enough to catch it before it broke.
His quick move appeased the worried guests in seconds.
By autumn, Mrs. Hale's quiet antipathy had turned into real friendship.
Daniel remained the most approachable person on the street.
He helped her carry groceries, a small altruistic habit of his.
She still abhors loud music after midnight, and he respects that.
When the holidays came, he did not need to appease anyone; the street was at peace.
She no longer felt alarmed by his car in the driveway at night.
Instead she felt grateful for an astute, approachable, altruistic friend.
