Last spring, Anna visited a bird sanctuary on the coast.
The bird sanctuary protects hundreds of birds, and Anna wanted to see them all.
She arrived on a blowy morning, and the wind pushed hard against her coat.
It was a blowy day, but Anna loved the wild weather.
By noon the wind softened, and the afternoon turned breezy and bright.
A breezy walk along the marsh is the best way to see the birds.
Anna learned that biodiversity means the variety of life in one place.
In this bird sanctuary, the biodiversity is amazing: fish, frogs, flowers, and birds everywhere.
Protecting biodiversity is the first job of every bird sanctuary.
Anna met Tom, a guide who showed her the reeds and the nesting birds.
Tom explained that biomass can power the visitor centre.
Biomass is organic matter used for energy, and here it comes from farm waste.
Tom said biomass is useful, but only when it does not harm biodiversity.
If we burn forests for biomass, we lose the biodiversity we want to protect.
Good biomass comes from waste, and good biomass never destroys a forest.
In the evening, Anna and Tom talked about blood sport.
A blood sport kills or injures animals, and Tom said it has no place near a bird sanctuary.
Some people defend a blood sport as tradition, but Anna disagreed.
Tradition is not an excuse for a blood sport, she said.
A blood sport belongs to the past, Tom agreed.
The wind rose again, and the evening turned blowy and cold.
Anna pulled her coat tight against the blowy wind and kept walking.
The next morning was breezy and calm, perfect for watching birds.
On a breezy morning, the birds sing, and the whole bird sanctuary feels alive.
Tom told Anna about families in the village living on the breadline.
The breadline means extreme poverty, and many families there are on the breadline.
Jobs at the bird sanctuary help families leave the breadline behind.
One woman found work at the sanctuary, and her family is no longer on the breadline.
Living on the breadline is hard, but the sanctuary gives hope.
Anna thought about biodiversity again as she watched the sun set.
Without biodiversity, the marsh would be silent, and the bird sanctuary would be empty.
She promised to return on a breezy day, and to tell everyone about the blowy coast, the biomass project, and the birds.
