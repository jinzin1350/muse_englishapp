Lena: Now let us turn to a more formal word, cessation. Cessation means the stopping of something, or a pause in something. You might read about the cessation of hostilities in a newspaper, which means that fighting has stopped. A university might announce the cessation of a course that no longer attracts students. And after years of smoking, a person might celebrate the cessation of a harmful habit.
Maya: Cessation is a formal word, so you will meet it in news reports, contracts, and academic writing more often than in everyday conversation. In conversation, people usually just say stopping or ending. But when you read serious English, cessation appears again and again. The cessation of funding can end a research project. The cessation of rain can save a harvest. Understanding cessation helps you follow the news with confidence.
Lena: And notice how cessation connects to our theme. When students carry out research, they sometimes face the cessation of the very support they depend on.
