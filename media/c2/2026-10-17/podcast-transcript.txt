Maya: Welcome back. Word five is a mouthful, technosolutionism. Technosolutionism is the belief that every social problem has a technological fix. Traffic? Build an app. Loneliness? Ship a chatbot. A failing school? Hand every child a tablet. Technosolutionism is seductive because it promises change without conflict, and technosolutionism is dangerous for exactly the same reason.
Lena: Give me a concrete example, Maya.
Maya: A city faces a housing crisis. Technosolutionism says build a platform to match roommates, while the real fix is building houses, which costs money and upsets landlords. Technosolutionism lets leaders look busy without spending political capital. It is the technocratic dream in software form, every messy human problem reduced to a product launch.
Lena: And technosolutionism has a partner in crime, our sixth word, dark pattern. A dark pattern is a design trick that manipulates you. The hidden unsubscribe link buried in tiny grey text. The cancel button you can never find. The countdown timer that restarts every time you reload the page. A dark pattern is not bad design, it is design working exactly as intended, against you.
Maya: Everyone has met a dark pattern. You sign up in one click and need a phone call and a supervisor to cancel, that is a dark pattern at work. Lena: And our last word, accelerationism. Accelerationism is the idea that change should go faster, not slower. To an accelerationism believer, pausing is the real danger, because pausing lets rivals win.
Maya: Accelerationism is the mirror image of luddism. Where luddism says slow down, accelerationism says speed up.
