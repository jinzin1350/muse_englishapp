The Town That Chose Wisely

In the valley behind the mountains, a small town once lived in abject poverty.
The mayor promised the people that they would achieve economic growth within ten years.
But first, she had to admit that the town's factories were ailing.
Her first decision was to alleviate debt for the struggling families.
Then she asked the council to allocate funds for new schools and clean water.
She warned everyone to check the A P R before borrowing money for new tools.
Slowly, these careful steps began to boost the economy of the whole valley.
Visitors could hardly believe that such a hopeful place had known abject poverty.
The town did not achieve economic growth by luck; it earned it with patience.
Even the ailing textile mill found new life making fabric for hospitals.
When the bank agreed to alleviate debt for small shops, the main street woke up again.
The council voted to allocate funds for a bridge that connected the farms to the market.
Young couples learned that a low A P R can save a family thousands over the years.
New jobs and fair wages continued to boost the economy month after month.
The mayor often said that numbers mean nothing if people still suffer.
She had seen abject poverty in her own childhood, and she never forgot it.
Every nation wants to achieve economic growth, but few ask who it serves.
An ailing port was rebuilt, and soon ships from three countries arrived each week.
The new trade helped the government alleviate debt without raising taxes.
Teachers watched the town allocate funds wisely, and they trusted the plan.
The credit union offered loans with a fair A P R, and families finally felt safe.
Tourists came for the festivals, and their spending helped boost the economy further.
The mayor kept one photograph from the hardest year on her desk.
It showed children playing in a street marked by abject poverty.
She would point at it and say: we will achieve economic growth, but never like this again.
The ailing railway was repaired, and the first train brought books, not just goods.
To alleviate debt for farmers, the town bought their harvests at honest prices.
Each spring, the people gathered to decide how to allocate funds for the coming year.
Their choices continued to boost the economy while protecting the river and the forests.
And whenever someone asked about the A P R on a loan, the elders smiled: the town had learned its lesson.
