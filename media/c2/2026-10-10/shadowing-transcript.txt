1. Sara founded her startup to fight enshittification before it ever touched her users.
2. She had watched beloved platforms decay into enshittification, and she refused to repeat the pattern.
3. Resisting enshittification was the whole business model, her investor deck said on page one.
4. She promised the team that growth would never justify enshittification in their product, and she backed that promise with sandboxing discipline from day one.
5. Two years on, users still praised the company for escaping the enshittification that ruined its rivals.
6. Their first product was an agentic assistant that could plan an entire trip by itself.
7. It had to be genuinely agentic, so the team wrapped it in strict sandboxing from day one.
8. Every agentic experiment ran inside sandboxing first, far away from customer data.
9. The sandboxing lab reset itself nightly, no matter what the agentic model had attempted.
10. Customers soon trusted the agentic helper with bookings, emails, and weekly reports, after it survived another year of sandboxing without incident.
11. What delighted users most was how multimodal the assistant felt in daily use.
12. Its multimodal brain could read receipts, listen to voicemails, and even answer zero-shot questions about photos.
13. The zero-shot skill surprised the founders most, since they had never trained the model for half these tasks, and each zero-shot success still made them grin.
14. When a zero-shot answer looked shaky, the multimodal checker simply asked for more context.
15. Competitors looked dated once zero-shot and multimodal became the standard everyone expected.
16. Behind the scenes, tokenization quietly prepared every message for the model.
17. They tuned tokenization for three languages, so nothing ever arrived garbled.
18. A tiny bug in tokenization once split a surname in two, which made the team laugh and then fix it fast.
19. Without careful tokenization, the model would have stumbled over every rare name it met.
20. The lead engineer called good tokenization invisible plumbing, and doubled the tests around it.
21. To reach cheap phones, they applied quantization to their flagship model.
22. The quantization cut the model's size in half while keeping its answers sharp.
23. A farmer in Kenya ran the app offline, which was only possible because of quantization.
24. Through clever quantization, the same agentic assistant that needed a data center now fit in a pocket.
25. The engineers celebrated when their quantization passed every benchmark they had set.
26. Sara loved vibe coding, and she sketched new features by describing them aloud at midnight.
27. One Friday, she went full vibe coding and shipped a working prototype before dinner.
28. The purists warned that vibe coding produced messy code, so she hired an engineer to review every line.
29. Used wisely, vibe coding let the smallest team outbuild companies ten times their size, and the designer sketched the multimodal interface by describing it aloud too.
30. For trust, the team added watermarking to every generated image, and a journalist soon used that watermarking to expose a viral fake.
31. Their watermarking stayed invisible to the eye but clear to any checker, which is why Sara called honest watermarking a future selling point.
32. The founders also knew that training on machine-made text invites model collapse.
33. They had measured early model collapse in a lab run, where the outputs grew blurrier with each round, a textbook preview of full model collapse.
34. To prevent model collapse, they filtered every dataset, keeping only fresh human writing, and they used watermarking to prove their own outputs stayed traceable.
35. So the startup thrived: no enshittification, a disciplined agentic helper, Sara still vibe coding new features at midnight, and zero fear of model collapse, because humans stayed at the center.
