The museum had a hall of old machines, and Sara loved the analogue radio best.
An analogue signal moves smoothly, like a river, without breaking into pieces.
Sara turned the analogue dial slowly, and the music faded in like a sunrise.
Her brother Amir laughed, because his phone found music instantly through Bluetooth.
Bluetooth linked his headphones to his phone, and no cables got in the way.
Amir said Bluetooth was magic, but Sara said the analogue radio had a warmer soul.
At lunch, Amir asked how AI could help doctors find illness early.
AI can study thousands of scans, and AI can notice tiny changes that tired eyes miss.
Their teacher explained that AI is short for artificial intelligence, a field that builds machines that learn.
Artificial intelligence can read handwriting, translate speech, and even drive cars carefully.
Sara asked whether artificial intelligence could also help farmers grow more food.
Artificial intelligence can predict rain, warn about pests, and guide tractors through fields.
In the afternoon, the class visited a laboratory that studied biotechnology.
Biotechnology uses living cells to make medicines, and biotechnology helps doctors heal wounds faster.
The guide said biotechnology also helps farmers grow crops that survive dry summers.
Amir asked about cloning, and the guide spoke slowly, because cloning raises deep questions.
Cloning makes an exact genetic copy of a living thing, and cloning already helps scientists study disease.
The class debated cloning for a long time, because cloning an animal feels very different from cloning a person.
Sara asked about a designer baby, and the room went quiet.
A designer baby is a baby whose genes are chosen before birth, sometimes to prevent a serious disease.
The ethics of a designer baby are debated everywhere, because a designer baby could also be chosen for looks.
Amir said a designer baby chosen to avoid suffering sounded kind, but a designer baby chosen like a product sounded wrong.
Back home, Sara played her analogue radio again, and the warm sound filled the kitchen.
She connected her phone with Bluetooth and compared the two sounds side by side.
Bluetooth gave her clear digital music, while the analogue radio gave her a soft golden sound.
Sara wrote in her notebook that AI and artificial intelligence will shape the future, but people must guide them wisely.
She wrote that biotechnology and cloning can heal and help, but they need honest rules.
She finished with one sentence: technology should serve people, never replace their choices.
