Daniel worked in a busy office with a conceited manager called Mr. Grant.
Mr. Grant was so conceited that he never listened to anyone in the room.
Every morning, the conceited manager told the team how brilliant he was.
Daniel's colleagues were tired of the conceited speeches, but nobody dared to speak.
One Monday, the conceited manager blamed the whole team for his own mistake.
Voices rose, faces turned red, and Daniel knew someone had to conciliate the angry team.
Daniel stood up slowly and tried to conciliate his furious colleagues with calm words.
To conciliate angry people, Daniel always listened first.
First, he agreed with one small point, and that simple agreement helped to conciliate the room.
Then he told a gentle joke, and the joke helped to defuse the tension in seconds.
A good joke can defuse an argument faster than a long speech.
Daniel knew how to defuse a difficult situation, and his colleagues were grateful.
When the meeting ended, the team thanked him for helping to defuse the conflict.
That evening, Daniel's conscience bothered him, because he had stayed silent for too long.
His conscience told him that silence was not kindness, and his conscience was right.
A clear conscience matters more than a quiet life, Daniel thought.
He decided to listen to his conscience and speak up kindly the next day.
The next morning, Daniel looked at Mr. Grant's big office, and he realised he did not covet it at all.
He did not covet the manager's loud confidence or his expensive car.
People who covet other people's success often forget their own strengths.
Daniel did not want to covet anything, because coveting only made people bitter.
What Daniel really craved was respect, and he craved a team where people listened to each other.
After a long day, he craved a hot cup of tea and a quiet walk home.
We all crave rest after hard work, and we all crave kindness from the people around us.
In the next meeting, Daniel sat up straight, and he did not cross his legs like a shield.
He remembered that when you cross your legs tightly, you look closed and nervous.
Some people cross their legs without thinking, but Daniel kept both feet on the floor.
When Daniel felt nervous, he told himself, do not cross your legs, stay open and calm.
His open posture helped him to conciliate the team once again.
His calm words helped to defuse the old tension, and his conscience felt clear at last.
Mr. Grant noticed the change, and even the conceited manager began to listen.
Daniel learned that a conceited attitude pushes people away, but a kind heart brings them closer.
He also learned that his conscience was his best guide, and that he should never covet what others have.
He craved simple things now: honest work, good friends, and a peaceful evening at home.
And every morning, he reminded himself to sit tall, breathe deeply, and keep his body language open and confident.
