The Cartographer's Daughter

1. When Elena was twelve, her father began a peregrination across three continents, and he never really came home again.
2. A peregrination, he wrote in his letters, is not an escape but a way of paying attention to the world.
3. Elena grew up tracing his peregrination on the maps that papered their kitchen wall.
4. Years later, she decided to repeat his peregrination, step by step, city by city.
5. Her friends called it madness, but Elena called her plan a peregrination of the heart.
6. She began with a short sojourn in the port city where his journey had started.
7. During that sojourn, she learned to read tide charts and to bargain in two new languages.
8. The sojourn lasted six weeks, and by the end of the sojourn she felt like a different person.
9. Every sojourn teaches you something, her father had written, so learn quickly and leave kindly.
10. Elena had always lived a peripatetic childhood, moving between her divorced parents in different countries.
11. That peripatetic beginning made her restless, and the peripatetic rhythm of trains and border crossings felt like home.
12. She told herself she would stop being peripatetic one day, but the peripatetic life kept calling her back.
13. In the old harbor she met a sailor from the Armenian diaspora, a man whose family had scattered across five ports.
14. The diaspora, he told her, is a family that lives in many harbors at once.
15. He introduced her to a diaspora supper club where every dish came with a story of the diaspora journey behind it.
16. Through the diaspora network she found a captain willing to take her east.
17. Among the passengers was an émigré poet returning to the country he had fled forty years before.
18. The old émigré spoke of exile the way other men speak of weather, constantly and without bitterness.
19. Elena interviewed the émigré for her notebook, and the émigré gave her his father's compass as a gift.
20. Their ship followed a circuitous course to avoid the autumn storms, looping far south of the direct lane.
21. The captain apologized for the circuitous route, but Elena loved the circuitous detour and its unexpected islands.
22. A circuitous voyage, the captain said, is the sea's way of making you patient.
23. By the third week, the journey had become a true odyssey, full of strange ports and stranger kindnesses.
24. Elena wrote that an odyssey is measured not in miles but in the number of times you are changed.
25. Every odyssey needs a storm, and hers arrived off a nameless coast at midnight.
26. When the storm passed, the crew joked that their little odyssey had earned them sailor status at last.
27. Near the strait, pirates tried to waylay the ship, cutting across its bow in the dark.
28. The captain had been warned that gangs would waylay slow vessels there, so he kept the deck lights burning all night.
29. You cannot waylay a prepared crew, he laughed afterward, and Elena wrote the line in her notebook.
30. At dawn came the embarkation at the eastern port, with drums on the quay and gulls screaming overhead.
31. Elena had dreamed of this embarkation for years, and the real embarkation was louder and brighter than the dream.
32. During the embarkation she lost her bag, found it again, and laughed until she cried.
33. Finally the ship reached its last harbor, and the passengers prepared to disembark.
34. To disembark after two months at sea is to remember how steady the earth is supposed to feel.
35. Elena watched the old émigré disembark first, kissing the ground like a man greeting an old friend.
36. She was the last to disembark, carrying her father's compass and a notebook full of storms.
37. Her peregrination was over, but she knew another peregrination was already beginning in her mind.
38. The captain's warning stayed with her: in narrow waters, thieves will waylay any ship that travels alone, so she promised she would never let anyone waylay her again.
39. She ended her notebook with a single line about every odyssey, every embarkation, and every brave disembark she had yet to make.
