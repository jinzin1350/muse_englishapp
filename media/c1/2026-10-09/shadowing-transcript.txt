The Wayfarer's Map

Long ago, a young wayfarer left her village with nothing but a map and a dream.

Her itinerary listed twelve towns, but she secretly hoped to ignore it.

On the third day, a broken bridge forced her onto a long detour.

The detour led her through a valley she had never seen on any itinerary.

There she met a caravan of spice merchants resting by a river.

The caravan was preparing for an expedition deep into the mountains.

Join our expedition, the leader said, if you can traverse the high pass.

The wayfarer agreed, though no wayfarer had crossed that pass in years.

At dawn, the caravan began to traverse the steep rocky trail.

To traverse the pass, they walked single file through the snow.

Halfway up, the storm forced another detour around a frozen lake.

The weary wayfarer almost turned back, but the caravan would not leave her.

After four hard days, they finally began to disembark from the high plateau.

One by one, the travelers began to disembark onto the green valley floor.

The wayfarer decided to make a short sojourn in the valley village.

Her sojourn lasted three weeks, and the village felt like home.

During her sojourn, she joined a daily excursion to the waterfalls.

Each excursion revealed a new secret of the valley.

She wrote to her family: This sojourn has become the best part of my odyssey.

When the caravan returned, she took one last excursion to the old temple.

Then she rejoined the caravan for the final leg of the expedition home.

The road home, the last expedition of the season, was an odyssey of its own.

They had to traverse a wide river on a bridge of boats.

No detour could save them this time, so they crossed with courage.

At the port, the tired travelers finally got to disembark from the river boats.

To disembark after such a journey felt like waking from a dream.

She tore up her old itinerary and wrote a new one for the next adventure.

This time, her itinerary had only one line: follow the horizon.

Her odyssey had taught her that plans matter less than courage.

She would always be a wayfarer at heart, and her odyssey was far from over.

Every odyssey ends, she thought, only to begin again.

And every future excursion, expedition, and sojourn would begin with a single brave step.
