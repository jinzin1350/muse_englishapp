The Chef's Grand Opening

Daniel had dreamed of opening his own restaurant for years.
On opening night, he planned a menu full of umami, because he loved that deep savory taste.
The first dish was a mushroom soup with rich umami from roasted tomatoes.
His sous chef said the umami in the soup would impress every guest.
Daniel tasted the soup and smiled, because the umami was perfect.
For the main course, he chose a beautiful cut of lamb that had to be succulent, with a sauce from deglazing the pan.
He knew that a succulent roast would make the evening unforgettable.
The secret to keeping the lamb succulent was resting it while he deglazed the pan with red wine.
When the first guest cut into the lamb, the succulent juice ran across the plate.
The piquant mustard sauce beside the lamb had a sharp, lively flavor.
Daniel had tested the piquant sauce three times to get the balance right.
A piquant dressing can wake up even the simplest plate of greens.
He drizzled the piquant sauce over the lamb just before serving.
His friend warned him that one salad was bland, almost insipid, and an insipid salad would shame the kitchen.
Daniel refused to serve anything insipid, so he added fresh herbs and lemon at once.
Even the bread was tasted twice, because no insipid bite could leave his kitchen.
The dessert worried him most, because a sweet dish can easily become cloying, and a cloying dessert would ruin a perfect dinner.
He reduced the sugar three times until the cake was no longer cloying.
He tasted the frosting and decided it was still a touch too cloying.
For the second main dish, Daniel decided to braise beef in red wine.
First he seared the beef hard, then he deglazed the pan to save every bit of flavor.
To braise the beef properly, he cooked it low and slow for four hours.
He had learned to braise from his grandmother, who always told him to deglaze the pan before the slow cooking began.
When you braise meat with patience, it falls apart with a fork.
The vinaigrette for the starter salad needed care, because he had to emulsify it well.
If you fail to emulsify a dressing, the oil and vinegar separate on the plate.
Daniel whisked slowly until he could emulsify the oil and lemon juice completely.
He always said the moment you emulsify a dressing is the moment it comes alive.
A famous food connoisseur had booked a table for the opening night.
Daniel was nervous, because a connoisseur notices every tiny mistake.
But the connoisseur, who could deglaze a pan in his sleep, tasted the soup and nodded with quiet approval.
After the meal, the connoisseur asked to meet the chef personally.
Daniel cared about mouthfeel as much as taste in every dish.
The creamy mouthfeel of the soup made the guests close their eyes.
He believed that perfect mouthfeel separates good food from great food, and the wonderful mouthfeel of the evening stayed with everyone long after midnight.
