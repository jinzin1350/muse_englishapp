Sara decided to change her life through education, and she meant it.
She enrolled in a night program with a demanding curriculum.
On the first day, the professor handed out a long syllabus.
She studied the syllabus carefully and marked every deadline.
The professor was famous for an erudite style of teaching.
His erudite lectures made even difficult theories feel clear.
He explained that his pedagogy favored questions over answers.
The students loved this pedagogy because it kept them curious.
Sara admired her erudite teacher more than anyone she knew.
For every exam, she invented a clever mnemonic.
Each mnemonic turned long lists into funny little stories.
Thanks to one smart mnemonic, she memorized all the dates.
She refused to cram, because she wanted real understanding.
Instead of a last-minute cram, she reviewed a little each evening.
Her friends would cram the night before and risk a flunk, but she stayed calm.
She knew that if she tried to cram everything, she might flunk.
Nobody in the class wanted to flunk the final exam.
The professor warned that anyone caught in plagiarism would fail.
He said plagiarism showed disrespect for honest work.
The school used software that could detect plagiarism instantly.
By her final year, Sara began writing her thesis.
Her thesis explored new ways to teach science.
Writing the thesis taught her more than any single class.
When her thesis was approved, her pedagogy professor called it brilliant.
Years later, she became an autodidact in a new field, medicine.
As an autodidact, she designed her own curriculum.
This personal curriculum mixed videos, books, and practice.
She wrote a second syllabus just for herself.
Following her own syllabus, the autodidact learned faster than before.
People called her erudite, but she called herself curious.
She still used a mnemonic whenever memory failed her.
She never had to flunk a test, because she prepared with care.
Her honest work was the opposite of plagiarism.
Her pedagogy as a teacher now helps thousands of students.
The curriculum she built as an autodidact is used in schools today.
