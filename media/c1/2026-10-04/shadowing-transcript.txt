The Clinic That Never Sleeps

1. Dr. Amara started her day by reviewing each patient's prognosis, hoping the prognosis would be kind.
2. A good prognosis can give a frightened family real hope.
3. But an honest prognosis is never a promise, only a careful judgment.
4. In the next room, nurses worked to palliate a patient's severe pain, doing all they could to palliate her fear as well.
5. They could not cure her illness, but they could palliate her suffering.
6. Learning to palliate discomfort is a skill every young doctor must master.
7. One elderly man arrived with a debilitating back condition, a truly debilitating injury.
8. His pain was so debilitating that he could barely stand.
9. Years of hard labor had left him with debilitating joint damage.
10. The team discussed whether the new drug outperformed a placebo, or whether the placebo itself deserved the credit.
11. In the trial, the placebo group improved almost as much as the treated group.
12. Even a simple placebo can calm a patient who truly believes in it.
13. The researchers then studied the pathogenesis of the rare infection, tracing its pathogenesis step by step.
14. Understanding its pathogenesis helped them choose the right medicine.
15. Once the pathogenesis was clear, a targeted treatment became possible.
16. During lunch, the doctors joked about their own sedentary habits and their worryingly sedentary weekends.
17. A sedentary routine of long shifts and desk work was wearing them down.
18. They agreed that such a sedentary life could harm their own hearts.
19. A young patient showed signs of severe malnutrition, and his malnutrition worried the whole team.
20. His malnutrition had weakened his immune system for months.
21. Treating malnutrition required both food and patient education.
22. Suddenly, an alarm rang, and the team rushed to resuscitate a patient, fighting to resuscitate him in time.
23. They managed to resuscitate him within four minutes.
24. Knowing how to resuscitate someone is the most urgent skill in the ward.
25. After the crisis, his body slowly returned to homeostasis, and maintaining that homeostasis became the priority.
26. Restoring homeostasis after such stress takes careful monitoring.
27. Every vital sign pointed toward a stable homeostasis again.
28. Later, the doctor warned about an iatrogenic risk of the new drug, a possible iatrogenic complication.
29. She explained that an iatrogenic side effect could damage the liver.
30. Preventing iatrogenic harm is part of every safe prescription.
