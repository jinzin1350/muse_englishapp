Daniel was a frugal young man who saved every coin he earned.
He never tried to squander money on things he did not need.
When he shopped at the old market, he always tried to haggle.
The sellers respected him because he knew how to haggle politely.
One spring, Daniel received a small windfall from his late uncle.
He refused to squander the windfall on a lavish holiday.
Instead, this thrifty planner invested half of the unexpected money.
He was thrifty by habit, not by pressure from anyone else.
Daniel avoided shops with an enormous markup on basic goods.
He had learned that a high markup often hides average quality.
Some restaurants in his city charged exorbitant prices for tiny meals.
He thought those exorbitant menus were designed for tourists.
His wealthy neighbor lived in the most affluent part of town.
Even that affluent neighbor clipped coupons every single week.
He once watched a stranger squander a sudden windfall at the casino tables.
It reminded him that a windfall can vanish in a single night, and that lost money is hard to recoup.
He decided to stay frugal even as his savings quietly grew.
Being frugal had become his identity, not just a temporary phase.
At the market, he would haggle with a smile and a joke.
Nobody ever felt insulted when Daniel tried to haggle for a fair price.
He skipped the lavish hotel and chose a cozy family guesthouse.
The lavish suite would have cost five times more for the same view, mostly markup.
A thrifty traveler, he packed snacks instead of buying airport food.
His thrifty choices left him money for things that truly mattered.
When his laptop broke, he sold the old parts, hoping to recoup some of the cost.
He managed to recoup nearly half of the repair cost that way.
Daniel believed patience helps you recoup losses faster than panic ever could.
The affluent district nearby had beautiful parks he enjoyed for free.
True wealth, he thought, was being frugal without feeling deprived.
He never let pride push him to squander savings on showing off.
He believed a lavish lifestyle was the fastest road to worry.
He always asked what justified the markup before paying more.
He walked past the exorbitant boutique without a second glance.
To him, exorbitant fees were simply a tax on impatience.
He believed affluent or not, everyone benefits from careful spending.
