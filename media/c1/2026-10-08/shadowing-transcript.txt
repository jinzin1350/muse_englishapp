The Bridge Back Home

The old house stood quiet because the family had become estranged.
Two brothers lived in the same city but were completely estranged.
Their feud had started over something nobody could remember.
A long feud can make even kind people forget their kinship.
The patriarch of the family refused to speak about the past.
The proud patriarch believed that apologizing was a weakness.
His harsh words had started to alienate his own children.
Nobody wants to feel alienated at a family dinner, and harsh words only alienate people further.
The fallout from the argument touched every holiday, and the fallout grew heavier each year.
Even the grandchildren felt the fallout, though they were innocent.
Their upbringing had taught them to be proud and silent.
But a strict upbringing does not have to decide your future.
One day, the younger brother decided to reconcile.
He knew it would take more than words to reconcile.
He wrote a letter full of empathy and honesty.
Empathy means trying to feel what the other person feels.
His brother read the letter and felt their old kinship return.
Kinship is stronger than any feud, he realized.
He wanted to nurture this small new hope.
To nurture a relationship, you must give it time and patience, and nurture it with care every day.
They met for coffee, and he tried to nurture the conversation with gentle questions.
They agreed the old feud was not worth the pain, and both wanted the feud to end.
Slowly, the estranged brothers became friends again, and nothing about their estranged years felt worth it.
No family should stay estranged forever.
The patriarch watched them and said nothing, though the old patriarch's eyes were wet.
But his eyes showed empathy, not anger.
He had never learned to nurture feelings, only to hide them.
His own upbringing had been cold and distant, and that upbringing still shaped him.
That night, the old patriarch knocked on his son's door.
"I want to reconcile," he said quietly.
The fallout of so many silent years could finally end.
They refused to let pride alienate them ever again.
Instead, they chose empathy and honest conversation, because empathy heals what pride cannot.
A family that can reconcile can survive anything, and to reconcile is to begin again.
Their shared upbringing reminded them of their kinship, and the last fallout faded away.
