The Rooftop and the Alley

1. When I first moved to the city, I rented a small room on the top floor of a high-rise flat.
2. From the window of that high-rise flat, I could see a crooked little hovel squeezed between two new towers.
3. The hovel looked like it had survived a hundred winters, and nobody knew who lived there.
4. On my first Saturday, my neighbors invited me to a rooftop barbecue, and I had the time of my life.
5. I had the time of my life meeting people from twelve different countries on that roof, and Rosa's lentil soup was already a household word among them.
6. An old teacher named Rosa told me a home truth that night: the city rewards the curious.
7. That home truth stayed with me, because it was simple and completely true.
8. Rosa had grown up in a hovel near the docks, and unlike those who lead a sheltered life, she was proud of every step she had taken since.
9. Her name was a household word in our neighborhood, because she had taught three generations of children.
10. When she spoke about her childhood in the hovel, her words hit home for all of us.
11. She said she had not led a sheltered life, and she would not trade her hard lessons for anything.
12. Most people who lead a sheltered life never learn how strong they can be.
13. The next week, the city announced that the hovel would be replaced by a small park.
14. Some neighbors were happy, but the news hit home for Rosa in a different way.
15. "A home truth," she said, "is that progress always costs someone their memories."
16. That home truth hit home for the whole street, and we stood quietly for a moment.
17. We decided to record her stories before the hovel disappeared, and everyone volunteered to help.
18. The local newspaper wrote about our project, and suddenly Rosa's name became a household word across the city.
19. A phrase from her interview, "every wall has a memory," became a household word among students.
20. On the last evening before the demolition, we held a small concert in front of the hovel.
21. Hundreds of people came, and they all had the time of their lives singing her favorite songs.
22. Rosa laughed and said she was having the time of her life, surrounded by her city.
23. Looking down from my high-rise flat that night, I saw the lights of the concert glowing in the alley.
24. I realized that a high-rise flat can give you a view, but only people can give you a home.
25. Rosa told the crowd one more home truth: gratitude is a daily practice, not a feeling.
26. Her home truth hit home for me, because I had taken so much for granted.
27. She reminded us that she had never led a sheltered life, and that struggle had made her generous.
28. Anyone who chooses not to lead a sheltered life will collect stories worth telling.
29. Now, whenever friends visit my high-rise flat, I take them down to the alley first, so they can have the time of their lives the way I did.
30. I tell them Rosa's story, because it is a household word in my heart, and it always hits home.
