Sara moved to Toronto with two suitcases and a big dream.
Her first home was a small furnished basement apartment near the station.
The word furnished sounded promising, but the reality was disappointing.
The advertisement had promised a fully furnished home, but the furniture was old and broken.
The sofa was broken, the lamp did not work, and the walls were damp.
She worked all day and came home to a cold, dark room, and it honestly felt like a dog's life.
For three months, Sara felt she was living a dog's life.
Nobody should have to live a dog's life, she told herself.
She promised herself she would never again accept a dog's life.
One rainy evening, she saw an advertisement for a granny flat.
The granny flat stood behind a quiet family house with a garden.
Sara visited the granny flat the very next morning.
The owners were kind, and the little granny flat felt like a palace.
She signed the lease and moved into her granny flat that weekend.
The flat was empty, not furnished, so she bought secondhand furniture.
She was glad her new place was not furnished, because now she could choose everything herself.
A neighbor gave her a table, and she found a comfortable chair online.
Slowly, she began to breathe new life into the little place.
With plants and warm light, she could breathe new life into every corner.
Her friends came to help, and together they breathed new life into the garden too.
After a month of work, the old flat got a new lease of life.
It was the same building, but with fresh paint and a garden it had gotten a new lease of life.
Sara herself got a new lease of life in her sunny new home.
Her friends said the new Sara had truly gotten a new lease of life.
Her brother Amir called from London, where he still lived close to the council estate of their childhood.
Amir had grown up on a council estate in the east of the city.
Life on that council estate had taught him the value of community.
Their parents had waited years to get into council housing.
Once they moved into council housing, the whole family felt secure.
Council housing had given the family low rent and real peace of mind.
Even now, Amir spoke proudly of growing up in council housing.
Amir said the council estate was being renovated that spring.
A new park would breathe new life into the old council estate, he hoped.
Sara laughed and said every home deserves a new lease of life, and anyone can breathe new life into their own corner of the world.
From a dog's life in a damp basement to a sunny granny flat, she had come far.
