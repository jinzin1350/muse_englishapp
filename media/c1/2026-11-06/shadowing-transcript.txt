Sara's Tight Month

Sara looked at her bank account on Monday morning.
She was completely broke, and payday was still ten days away.
When you are broke, even a cup of coffee feels expensive.
Being broke taught Sara to plan every dollar she spent.
Sara decided to cook at home until she was not broke anymore.
Last month, her rent cheque had bounced, and she never forgot that feeling.
A cheque can bounce when there is not enough money in the account, and a bounced cheque costs extra money.
Sara checked her balance twice, so no cheque would bounce again.
Sara never wanted a cheque to bounce again in her life.
She paid for her groceries with a contactless card.
A contactless card is fast, and Sara liked that.
But a contactless card can also make you spend too much, and Sara knew her contactless card was a small danger.
Sara kept her contactless card in a small wallet by the door.
She needed new shoes, but she did not want to charge them to a credit card.
If you charge something to a credit card, you must pay it back soon.
Sara remembered the time she had charged a holiday to a credit card.
Now she only charges a purchase to a credit card in a real emergency.
She would never charge a holiday to a credit card again.
Her friend asked if the government would ever cancel debt for students.
To cancel debt means the money does not have to be paid back, and to cancel debt is a big decision for any government.
Sara hoped the government would cancel debt for young families one day.
If the government could cancel debt, many students could breathe again.
Her insurance did not cover her broken phone.
A good policy should cover the things that matter most.
Sara asked the company, will your plan cover a new phone?
They said no, the plan would not cover it.
One day, her savings would cover a short trip to the sea.
The news said the country was slowly coming out of recession.
To come out of recession takes time, but shops were opening again.
Sara felt hopeful when she heard the country would come out of recession.
Economists said it would take two more years to fully come out of recession.
Sara believed her own life would also come out of recession soon.
She was still broke, but she had a plan, and that plan gave her hope.
